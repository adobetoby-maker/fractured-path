# Chapter 22 — The Scout

The crowd took the better part of an hour to fully clear, the Ironyard's usual post-bout current of arguing gamblers and re-hashed exchanges thicker than Cael had ever seen it, and by the time it finally thinned to nothing he was sitting where the fourth exchange had put him, still working through the specific exhausted stillness that followed a fight he'd genuinely needed everything he had to win. Lira sat beside him, one hand loosely resting near his shoulder without quite touching it, the way she did when she wanted him to know she was there without crowding a body that had just taken real damage. Brom stood a few feet off, arms crossed, watching the last stragglers filter out through the main doors with the same unhurried attention he brought to everything.

Nobody had noticed the woman approaching until she was nearly at the circle's edge — which was, Cael understood the moment he registered her properly, itself a kind of skill. She moved through a thinning crowd the way he imagined a genuinely careful observer moved through any room: present, unremarkable, gone from anyone's attention half a second after they'd looked away.

She was perhaps forty, dressed plainly enough to have passed for a circuit regular at a glance, except that nothing about her carried a circuit regular's specific wear — no bruising, no calluses visible on the hands, the bearing of someone who'd spent a career observing rather than fighting. She stopped a respectful distance away and waited, without impatience, for Cael to notice her fully before she spoke.

"Halven," she said, introducing herself directly, no hesitation in it at all. "Senior teaching-practitioner, Greyvane Academy. Silver-tier. I specialize in irregular Path development assessment." She said this with the plain efficiency of someone who'd delivered the same introduction many times before and had long since stopped needing to soften it, the sentences arriving in the exact same order they'd probably arrived in a hundred times before this one. "I've attended this circuit six times over the past two months. Dace knew my pass was academy-issued. He didn't know which academy until tonight."

Cael sat up straighter despite the ache in doing so, cataloguing her the way he cataloged anything new and potentially significant — even now, even exhausted, the habit didn't switch itself off. "Six times," he said. "Watching me specifically?"

"Watching the circuit generally, at first. You specifically, for the last four of the six." She said this without any performance of secrecy, as though the surveillance itself weren't the interesting part, merely the necessary preamble to whatever came next. "I don't waste institutional travel budget on curiosity alone. By the second visit I'd narrowed my attention considerably."

She turned to Cael fully, and delivered the offer without further preamble, the way Brom might have delivered it — no warming up, no softening, simply the information laid out plainly for him to evaluate on its own merits.

"Greyvane has a provision called the demonstration-provision track. It was written, originally, for unkindled prospects under observation — young practitioners whose ability has shown itself before an Arbiter has formally classified it, admitted on the strength of demonstrated capability rather than a paper designation that doesn't exist yet." She paused, letting that land before continuing. "You're demonstrably beyond unkindled. But the provision's actual language — I've read it more times than most people who wrote it, I suspect — applies to any practitioner without a standard Arbiter-issued classification. Which you qualify for, whatever the specific reason happens to be. We've been looking for the right candidate to test that reading of the provision for some time. I believe you're it."

"The Compact monitors me," Cael said. It felt important to say plainly, before anything else, rather than let her discover it later and wonder why he'd withheld it.

"We're aware. We have institutional legal staff who exist specifically to handle exactly this category of complication — it isn't the first time Greyvane has admitted a student the Compact took an interest in, and I doubt it will be the last."

"My Path classification is—"

"Our assessment staff will evaluate you monthly, by demonstration, not by declaration." She held up a hand, not unkindly, cutting off the explanation before he could offer it. "That's the provision's actual requirement, in full. We're not asking what your Path is. We're not asking you to name it, defend it, or produce paperwork that doesn't exist. We're asking whether you can demonstrate it consistently, month after month, under observation, to a standard our assessment staff considers meaningful." She glanced toward the circle, still marked in the packed dirt from the night's bout. "Tonight qualified. Comfortably."

---

Lira spoke before Cael could respond, her voice carrying an edge of something that wasn't quite hope and wasn't quite defensiveness — a year of watching doors close on her own advancement, arriving all at once at a door that might, possibly, be about to open. "What about expelled practitioners?"

Halven turned to her with the specific attention of someone who'd already done the relevant research well before tonight, no visible surprise at the question at all. "Wind Path, Copper-tier, expelled from Venmire Academy. Insubordination, on the record — though the file I read suggested the actual dispute was about a technique variation your examiners found more threatening than incorrect."

Lira went very still. "You read my file."

"I read whatever's publicly accessible about anyone I'm seriously considering. It's a courtesy to the candidate, honestly — I'd rather arrive already informed than waste your evening asking questions I could have answered myself." Halven said this without apology, the flat practicality of someone who'd long since stopped feeling the need to soften how thoroughly she did her job. "Yes. We have a re-certification candidate provision. Different track from Cael's, same academic access — full enrollment, full facilities, monthly reassessment required to maintain standing, demonstrated continued advancement rather than a single qualifying result. It exists for exactly your situation: a practitioner whose formal record doesn't match their actual capability, through no fault in the capability itself."

"I can do that."

"I assumed you could. I watched your bout three weeks ago, the one against the formally-trained Wind opponent. That's not a Copper-tier performance. That's a Copper-tier classification attached to considerably more than Copper-tier ability, which is precisely the gap this provision exists to correct." She said this plainly, no flattery in the delivery at all, simply an assessment offered because she believed it accurate.

Brom, who had said nothing throughout the entire exchange, finally spoke — a single question, direct as everything else about him. "What about someone with a standard classification? No irregularity at all. Just a Copper-tier practitioner who wants in."

Halven glanced at him, recalibrating slightly. "Standard enrollment provision. No special designation required — an application, a demonstrated performance record, faculty review. Slower than the other two tracks, but available to anyone who qualifies on ordinary merit." Something in her expression suggested she'd already guessed why he was asking, though she didn't say so directly.

Cael asked for a day to think it over — not because the answer was genuinely in doubt, but because a decision this size deserved at least the appearance of the deliberation it actually required. Halven considered the request for a moment, then nodded.

"Three days," she said. "I'll be at the district inn until then. After that, I have other circuits to visit and other candidates to consider, and I'd rather not leave an offer open indefinitely — it tends to make people less honest with themselves about what they actually want." She said this without any particular pressure behind it, simply a fact about how she conducted her own business. "Find me if the answer's yes. Don't, if it's no. I won't take either answer personally."

She left as unceremoniously as she'd arrived, folding back into the emptying warehouse's shadows with the same unremarkable economy that had brought her across the floor in the first place, gone from sight within a dozen steps.

---

Once she was gone, the three of them sat in the emptied Ironyard for a long moment, none of them immediately reaching for a response the size of what had just happened.

"She wasn't surprised by any of your questions," Cael said, eventually, to Brom.

"No."

"She's done this before."

"Or she prepared very carefully for us specifically." Brom considered his own alternative for a moment, weighing it seriously rather than simply offering it as a debate point. "Both, probably. People who are genuinely good at something tend to look practiced even the first time they do it new, because the underlying skill transfers."

A silence settled over the three of them, weighted with everything the offer actually meant — not just for Cael, whose situation had prompted it, but for all three of them, sitting together in a warehouse that had become, over the past year, considerably more than a place they trained.

"It's not really a question, is it," Lira said, finally, quiet in a way that wasn't quite a question itself. "Not really. Not underneath."

"No," Cael admitted. "I don't think it is."

"So," she said.

"So," Cael agreed, and found, saying it, that the single small word was somehow carrying the entire weight of a decision none of them had actually spoken aloud yet — the specific, quiet gravity of something everyone in the room already understood without needing it confirmed.
