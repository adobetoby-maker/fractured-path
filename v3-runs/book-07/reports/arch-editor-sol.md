# Editor Report — Book 7 Architecture

## Verdict: STRUCTURAL_HOLD

## Findings

### E01

- Severity: BLOCKER
- Gate: `CANON_RULES.md` Reveal Schedule — “The Fractured Path predates the classification system” reveals in Book 8 and “The Fractured Path is primordial — what all Paths were” reveals in Book 13; `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, “What the Fractured Path actually is — revealed in layers”; `VOICE_CHARTER.md` §1 Decision Hierarchy.
- Evidence: Book 7 moves from observation into reserved ontology and purpose in character-facing text. Oryn says in Chapter 13, **“There’s no *shape* to you. Everyone has a shape — a Path’s a channel, I can feel the banks. You’ve got... currents. Nine of them. Ten. They don’t have banks. They don’t *need* banks.”** In Chapter 14 she theorizes, **“the reason I couldn’t read you is that you’re made of what I was reading with.”** Karis says in Chapter 15, **“Somebody made a floor that turns Paths off, and made it before there was a word for what you are, and it doesn’t turn you off.”** The Chapter 24 narration goes beyond the mandated alignment finding and assigns recipient-purpose: **“the oldest thing out here, which had never read anything else — and had left, for whoever could walk its floors, a road.”** The conflicting sources reserve the meanings: the bible’s Book 7–9 layer says only, **“The Fractured Path predates the classification system”** and explains that the [UNBOUND] distinction matters because it **“implies the absence of a container that was never supposed to be there”**; the Book 13–15 layer reserves, **“The Fractured Path is the primordial source — the raw, unlimited, individual potential that existed before anyone decided it needed to be structured.”** The series’ locked Book 7 ending stops at **“Someone made this pattern.”**
- Consequence: Book 8 loses the discovery that Cael’s condition does not require the system’s “banks,” Book 13 loses the raw-potential ontology, and the sites already read as an intentionally left route for a practitioner like Cael. Calling these statements guesses does not protect the schedule: a character theory can disclose a secret as effectively as a confirmed finding.
- Repair target: Keep the observable data (identical failures, Cael unaffected, unreadable currents, alignment) but remove on-page claims that Cael needs no container, is made of the substrate Tide reads, or that the sites were left for someone like him. Preserve the locked final sentence, “Someone made this pattern,” as the ceiling.

### E02

- Severity: BLOCKER
- Gate: `THE_FRACTURED_PATH_SERIES.md`, Book 7, “The Quieting — first encounter”; `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, Book 7 arc specification.
- Evidence: At the first Quieting encounter, Chapter 11 states, **“Oryn is not with them; the book is honest that this is the chapter that needed her, and she is nine days north on her route.”** The series source requires the opposite within that first encounter: **“An ancient ruin site where Path abilities stop working. The Arbiters go silent within 200 meters of the site perimeter. Oryn can’t access her Tide Path healing. Cael, notably, is unaffected — his Fractured Path operates normally throughout. He doesn’t mention this immediately.”** The Book 7 plant ledger also falsely claims, **“Oryn cannot access her Tide healing inside it (Ch11, staged at the worst possible moment).”**
- Consequence: One of the bible’s defining Book 7 images is absent. Oryn’s contrast with Cael is delayed to a later site, so the first encounter no longer performs the required healer/Quiet/Cael comparison and the ledger certifies a beat the card explicitly denies.
- Repair target: Put Oryn at the first Quieting encounter, or relocate the actual first encounter so that Oryn’s failed Tide healing, Cael’s unaffected state, the 200-meter boundary, and his delayed disclosure occur in the same canonical sequence.

### E03

- Severity: HIGH
- Gate: `THE_AUTHOR.md` §1.1 Fairness Law / Unified Gate 1; Book 7 PROVISIONAL Tide Path rule, “cannot heal what she cannot read”; Book 7 internal state consistency.
- Evidence: Chapter 6 establishes that Oryn’s reading of Cael returns nothing: **“the instrument returning nothing.”** Chapter 7 repeats the result on the third attempt: **“she reads Cael a third time and gets, again, nothing.”** With no intervening change to Cael or the reading, Chapter 13 suddenly gives her detailed access: **“There’s no *shape* to you... You’ve got... currents. Nine of them. Ten.”** The Tide reference says, **“a Tide practitioner cannot heal what she cannot read — architecture that returns no legible current is architecture she cannot touch.”** The count also conflicts with Chapter 22, where the pre-existing anomaly is **“Not a current. A *stillness*,”** while the tenth confirmed current does not exist until the acquisition completes later in Chapter 13.
- Consequence: The acquisition’s enabling condition looks invented at the payoff, and the “nine... ten” line either counts a fragment that has not yet been acquired or misclassifies the anomaly the book later insists is not a current. Readers cannot keep the promised character sheet.
- Repair target: Establish before Chapter 13 what materially changes between the failed readings and the successful one, including its cost/limit, and reconcile exactly what Oryn can count before the tenth fragment exists.

### E04

- Severity: HIGH
- Gate: Book 7 PROVISIONAL Quieting and Tide Path mechanics; `THE_AUTHOR.md` §1.1–1.2 / Unified Gates 1–2.
- Evidence: Inside the Long Stair’s quiet ground, Chapter 19 has **“Oryn, at the chamber’s back, both hands on Brom’s shoulder, *reading* — and getting the current the way she has always got it, and unable to mend a thing.”** The reference defines the reading as Tide use — **“the healer’s own current run *through* the patient’s architecture”** — and then gives the boundary rule: **“Inside quiet ground: nothing. She is a woman with a good kit and strong hands.”** The Quieting reference likewise says **“every Path declaration fails to render.”**
- Consequence: The boundary selectively disables mending but not a Tide-current diagnostic, although the architecture defines both as Tide operations. Later books would not know whether the Quieting suppresses declarations, energy flow, healing only, or all Path techniques.
- Repair target: Make the Chapter 19 diagnosis purely mundane, or revise and teach the boundary/reading distinction before the scene. Do not let current-based reading function inside quiet ground under the present rule.

### E05

- Severity: BLOCKER
- Gate: `THE_AUTHOR.md` §1.1 Fairness Law, §1.2 Limitations Over Powers, and Unified Gates 1, 2, and 14; `VOICE_CHARTER.md` §4.4; Book 7 Tide Path and fauna rules.
- Evidence: The Tide reference says, **“the reading takes minutes, not seconds, and the patient must be still.”** The fauna reference says, **“No creature speaks, thinks, or carries a Path.”** Chapter 21 resolves the wold-wyrm fight by having Cael touch a moving, armored, non-Path animal and, **“in one second of contact,”** read **“current, architecture, a body’s flow”** and find **“the place where the pressure-sense lives.”** Seln then bolts that exact location, removing the wyrm’s ability to sense the floor.
- Consequence: The weakest fight wins by an untaught acceleration, an untaught non-human target domain, and diagnostic precision beyond the established technique. The terrain is excellent, but the decisive move violates the reader’s rules and makes the new fragment a universal anatomical scanner on first combat use.
- Repair target: Rebuild the winning decision around a capability and target domain already taught, or plant and cost a distinct fast/non-human mode before Chapter 21. The solution must still be writable as removing the wyrm’s floor-sense without overriding the reading’s minutes-and-stillness limit at the turn.

### E06

- Severity: HIGH
- Gate: `THE_AUTHOR.md` §1.1 Fairness Law / Unified Gate 1; `VOICE_CHARTER.md` §4.4 winner-removes-options rule.
- Evidence: In Chapter 11, Cael becomes the only declaration and draws the stillhounds as established, but the actual escape comes when **“Ember-adjacent, one ignition point on fused stone... does something to the pack... they *break*, not away from him but *out* — past the line, into the scrub, gone. The chapter does not explain it.”** The Fairness Law requires, **“before any scene where a character solves a problem with a special ability, locate the earlier passage that taught the mechanism now being used.”**
- Consequence: The central Quiet fight is resolved by a reaction the card expressly leaves unexplained. Cael’s target-taking does not itself defeat the pack; the unexplained Ember/fused-stone interaction does.
- Repair target: Teach the relevant animal/material reaction before the turn, or replace the break with an already established way of stripping the pack’s pursuit or geometry.

### E07

- Severity: HIGH
- Gate: Book 6 Continuity Checkpoint, Shadow-adjacent seal bullet; Book 6 Clue / Plant Ledger, “Seln comes inside.”
- Evidence: Book 7’s seam claims, **“Shadow-adjacent’s *public* seal [is] still logically in force (out here there is no public — the seal is moot beyond the Line... Shadow-adjacent is used freely inside quiet ground and on the void roads).”** Book 6’s binding source says, **“The seal STAYS on publicly after this book — the logic updates (a defector’s tradecraft in Cael’s hands would confirm the surveillance-era acquisition) but the discipline holds.”** Book 7 itself establishes a board ledger, hold witnesses, monthly couriers, a Compact-shaped broker, a registry-forwarded courtesy copy, and an off-channel Compact team; “no public” is therefore not true.
- Consequence: Free public use can burn Seln as the source, validate the surveillance-era acquisition, and leak mechanism-adjacent evidence back west through channels the same architecture makes plot-bearing.
- Repair target: Preserve the public seal wherever non-circle witnesses or report chains exist. If any use must be public, explicitly establish why the source cannot be inferred or why the information cannot travel; being east of the Line is not sufficient.

### E08

- Severity: HIGH
- Gate: `NAME_REGISTRY.md` Rule 2, aural distinctness; `VOICE_CHARTER.md` §8.1 audio-first names.
- Evidence: Book 7 introduces **“Ghent’s crew”** in Chapter 2 and uses Ghent throughout the edge-territory arc. Book 6 already has **“Procurator Jent”** as a recurring named Compact counsel. The registry rule is exact: **“Aural distinctness is required, not just spelling distinctness... Judge by ear.”** “Ghent” and “Jent” are near-homophones with the same vowel, coda, syllable count, and only a close initial-consonant distinction.
- Consequence: In audio, a listener can hear the honest rival captain as the returning Compact procurator, especially because both are authority-adjacent men who price Cael and recur in institutional contexts.
- Repair target: Rename the cheaper/new Book 7 side against the full B1–B6 registry. Pike/Fiske, Oryn/Oxhollow, and wold-wyrm/Withrow were read aloud and are acceptable; the RESERVED cross-universe list is clean.

### E09

- Severity: MEDIUM
- Gate: Book 7 header and B6-thread ledger; internal antagonist-state consistency.
- Evidence: The header limits the Compact to **“one off-channel reach across the Line, Chapters 17–19.”** The ledger repeats, **“The faceless faction — reaches across the Line exactly once (Ch17–19).”** But Chapter 8’s first Fallow Ring contract already bears registry prose; Chapter 16 says its completion report is **“forwarded through a registry office that should have no interest in Lowmarch’s board”** to Vastin; and Chapter 17 identifies the Long Stair lure as **“a second quiet-ground posting, from the same broker, for the same ‘instruments.’”**
- Consequence: The architecture simultaneously treats the Fallow posting as Compact-connected and denies that it is an earlier reach. This muddies when the faceless faction finds the crew, what it knows before designing the trap, and whether the first contract was bait, recovery, surveillance, or coincidence.
- Repair target: Decide the first contract’s provenance and count. Either separate it demonstrably from the off-channel faction, or acknowledge two linked reaches and make the knowledge progression explicit.

### E10

- Severity: HIGH
- Gate: `VOICE_CHARTER.md` §9 Scene-closer rule and Editor’s Gate 10: **“no two consecutive chapters may end on the same structural move (log entry, single-line paragraph, observed-from-distance image).”**
- Evidence: Chapter 1 closes on **“the Power Log”** and its quoted entry. Chapter 2 immediately closes on **“the Power Log, end of chapter”** and another quoted entry. The repetition continues through large runs: Chapters 4–11 all finish with log text or a written-log/note variant; Chapters 14–18 do likewise; Chapters 20–21 do likewise. The checkpoint’s claim, **“no two consecutive chapters end on the same structural move,”** is false.
- Consequence: Audio chapter rhythm becomes predictably “scene, then Cael explains/files it,” flattening suspense and muting otherwise distinct chapter promises.
- Repair target: Reassign final beat types across all 24 cards so that no adjacent pair shares the same closer structure. Preserve the Power Log’s importance while moving some entries earlier or letting action, dialogue, object, or landscape carry the chapter break.

### E11

- Severity: MEDIUM
- Gate: `VOICE_CHARTER.md` §4.3 and `THE_AUTHOR.md` Unified Gate 13, Injury Persistence.
- Evidence: Chapter 3 explicitly records **“Karis’s hands cut by her own knife”** and **“a bite through [Cael’s] calf,”** but neither injury recurs or is resolved in either character’s next appearance or any later card. The source rule requires, **“Every wound, exhaustion state, or injury is continuity state: it recurs or is explicitly resolved (healed, bound, narratively closed) in the character’s next appearance.”** Lira’s arithmetic also skips a beat: Chapter 6 says her ankle **“has now gone twice,”** while Chapter 11 calls it **“the ankle that has now gone three times”** without staging a third turn in Chapters 7–10.
- Consequence: The opening fight’s declared bill partly vanishes, and the ankle history cannot be reconstructed. This weakens the series’ signature cost accounting and makes later one-armed/one-shouldered states harder to trust.
- Repair target: Carry or explicitly resolve every Chapter 3 injury at the next appearance, and stage or correct the ankle’s third event/count. Keep the otherwise strong Lira-arm and Brom-shoulder continuity.

### E12

- Severity: MEDIUM
- Gate: `THE_FRACTURED_PATH_SERIES.md`, Book 8 Vastin arc; Book 7 B6-thread ledger’s explicit deferral.
- Evidence: The Book 7 ledger says Vastin’s **“departure and the classified-history material are Book 8’s and are not staged, hinted, or scheduled.”** Chapter 16 nevertheless has him **“recognize a designation from a classified index he has never had cause to open.”** The series assigns the move to Book 8: Vastin shares what he found in **“the Compact’s classified history section”** and later **“obtains the Compact’s complete Quieting records.”**

- Dimension C (Book 6 seam): age, opening inventory, closing count, tiers/standing, three boxes, Seln’s case, asset restriction, Ephram/Hesk/Vell/Daeva/Reydan altitude, and the nine-plus-anomaly → ten-plus-anomaly arithmetic are clean. Quiet-ground folklore made practical through road etiquette, crossbows, fauna that hunt declarations, survey stakes, and Seln’s map blanks is the third keep. - Dimension I (fight audit):
  - Chapter 3 has a genuinely new problem: fight predators that acquire targets through Path discharge. The ford is load-bearing through current, submerged footing, and the pack’s concealment. The intended win is: **Cael removed the pack’s ability to preserve its leader-led geometry by becoming the first/loudest mover, letting Seln remove the leader.** Preserve this.
  - Chapter 6 changes category: survive a herd-generated landslide rather than defeat an opponent. Ledge, downhill flight, outcrop, cart, and gorge cause every major beat. The intended win is: **Karis removed the herd’s ability to overrun the outcrop by forcing its line to split.** It is distinct from the B6 Anchor lattice and B5 Daeva duel.
  - Chapter 11’s boundary-retreat problem is new and the lip/den/200-meter line are load-bearing. The image of Cael as the only declaration in the Quiet is excellent. Its final removal sentence is not currently fair because the unexplained Ember reaction supplies the escape (E06).
  - Chapter 19 is the most memorable fight: Cael walking out of the Quiet already declaring turns the enemy’s prepared boundary into the attack vector. The stair, bowl, rim, crossbow lanes, and Anchor line all matter. The win is: **Cael removed the off-channel team’s ability to hold the dead-Path bowl by unbinding its only controlled exit and attacking from the impossible side.** Although it reuses B6 Anchor knowledge and the B5 Daeva suite, the boundary inversion is genuinely new.
  - Chapter 21 has a new pressure-sense/water problem and the flooded walls are load-bearing, but it is the weakest fight because its win depends on the invalid one-second/non-human reading (E05). Its intended sentence is clear: **Cael and Seln removed the wyrm’s ability to sense weight through the floor.** Preserve that tactical objective while rebuilding the means.
- Dimension J (voice/register): clean at architecture level. Lira remains kinetic and blunt, Brom weighted and minimal, Karis method-first, and Seln operational. Oryn’s clinical reserve, consent vocabulary, and route arithmetic distinguish her from both Karis and Lira. Pike is quieter and more outcome-practical than Dace, while his independence from a parent institution distinguishes him from Vell. No verified companion line is simply Cael dialogue reassigned.

## Taste concerns (NEEDS_HUMAN_JUDGMENT — not defects)

- The fused grey stone, unreadable carvings, broken uprights, ancient stair, drowned hall, and buried wyrm are the least proprietary worldbuilding. The board, maps, and quiet-ground practices are unmistakably Valdris; the ruin surfaces could appear in many fantasy series. Consider whether each site needs one additional mechanism-of-use that is as specific and retellable as the board or the void-map convention.
- Pike is structurally well differentiated from Vell and Dace, but he is still the third ledger-keeper delivering third-party verdicts in closely related “pricing” language. An audio rehearsal should decide whether performance alone supplies enough separation.
- Oryn is distinct overall, but several correction constructions — **“That’s not an answer. That’s a finding,” “That’s worse. That’s a healer’s sentence,” “That’s not what I meant”** — use the series’ shared X/not-X cadence. A human ear should decide whether the clinical noun set is enough or whether one of those beats belongs to Cael/Karis’s register.
- The Tide anomaly remains canonically unresolved, but converting it from an “entry” into a precisely located “still place” removes a large amount of ambiguity. This is a pacing/taste decision rather than a continuity breach; preserve only if later books benefit from that much specificity now.
- The board ceasing to number the crew is satisfying but closely rhymes with prior “unscorable / no column” verdicts. Human judgment should decide whether this is deliberate series motif or one repetition too many.
