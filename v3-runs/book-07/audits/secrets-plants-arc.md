### BORDERLINE — maker attribution (four sentences). Verdict: NOT a leak; doc conflict flagged.

Four sentences in the book attribute agency (not identity, not purpose, not intelligence) to the
sites:

- ch10 L55 — Karis: "A circle is a made shape." Framed by narration as "a fact about a shape and nothing else," and re-limited at ch15 L45: "I said it as geometry... I'm not going to walk one step past *shape* in the small hand."
- ch15 L159 — Karis, privately, to Cael only, after the table is cleared: "The circle's perfect," she said... "Perfect isn't natural. Somebody made a floor that turns Paths off. It's older than anything I can date. And it doesn't turn you off. Three facts. I'm not putting a verb between them." (She adds at L165: "I'm not entering the hypothesis... I'm saying it to you. That's different.")
- ch23 L139 (Cael's log) — "...a still place at the center of me, and a line on a map that somebody drew before there were maps."
- ch24 L127 — Karis: "Someone made this pattern." And ch24 L133 — Cael: "And a line on a map that somebody drew before there was a registry to draw one."

**Assessment.** None of these names, describes, or ascribes intent to a maker; each is a claim about
a *shape* or a *line* plus an indefinite agent, and each is explicitly barred from becoming a
finding. The ch24 formulation is the one the series bible and the Book 7 series entry both
*mandate* ("Someone made this pattern.", SERIES L214 and L224), so the register is canon-sanctioned.
The ch15 L159 line is the same register, said once, off the record, to one person.

**Doc conflict to fix (architecture, not prose).** `CHAPTER_ARCHITECTURE.md` L30 asserts of the
Quieting: "no character theorizes a maker, a purpose, or an intelligence." Its own L32 then requires
"**Someone made this pattern.**" These two lines cannot both be true. The prose follows L32 (and the
bible). L30's absolutism should be narrowed to "no character theorizes a *purpose* or an
*intelligence*; indefinite agency is permitted and is the ending's own sentence."

## PART 3 — Arc 3 promise audit

**Promise (SERIES L204 / ARCHITECTURE L14):** *Make the reader feel the edge territories as the
first place Cael has ever been fully free — and then make that freedom complicated.*

**Arc statement (ARCHITECTURE L18):** *No instrument out here reads me* → *Nothing out here reads
me — and the oldest thing out here stops every Path but mine.*

### Pole 1 — the relief. Written Ch2, lived Ch5.

**Written, ch02 L217** (the log, the lower inn, forty-ninth day):

> *No instrument out here reads me. No Arbiter station, no assessment panel, no registry seat with a seal on it. The board reads what we did. I've spent four years being the case the instruments couldn't parse, and it turns out the cure was a country with no instruments. Write it plainly, because it's true: I'm free. First time.*

Narration lets it stand deliberately, ch02 L219: *"He read it back once. The sentence was there, in his
own hand, plain, and he let it stand, and noticed himself letting it, and did not argue with the
noticing either."*

Seeded earlier, ch01 L237: *"Six weeks. Four contracts. Nobody has asked what I am."* and ch01 L11:
*"No tier. No rank. No enrollment. And ... no file I can see."*

**Lived, ch05.** The pole is enacted as work rather than restated. Ch05 L17: *"He had never once
been paid to catalogue."* Ch05 L91: *"I've written eleven widths since noon and none of them mine,
and it's the first day of my life I've been paid by the line."* And, twice, the sentence the chapter
is built to earn — ch05 L209 and again ch05 L245:

> *First honest job I've ever had that didn't have a fight at the end of it.*

Ch05 L209 adds the tell that he knows the freedom is fragile: *"I'm writing that before it's
finished, which I don't do. Note that I did."* Ch05 L117 is the same register at the herd: *"He had
never in his life been given a day to look at anything that was not about to hit him."*

The pole holds unbroken for eight chapters exactly as the architecture claims, and the hinge is
written before the reader reaches the line — ch08 L255:

> *For twelve weeks nothing out here has read me. I'd like to see what a place that reads nothing does with that.*

### Pole 2 — the turn. Ch9 at the stone, then Ch11, Ch16, Ch19, Ch22, Ch24.

**Ch9, at the stone** — the first inversion, and it lands in the same words as the relief, meaning
the opposite. Narration, ch09 L203: *"He stood on the road beside Karis, at the stride where four
people had felt a thing, and he felt nothing change, and he did not say so."* Then the log, **ch09
L229**:

> *Quiet ground. Karis's Arbiter stopped at a line she can mark with a rock. So did Lira's, Brom's, Seln's, Oryn's — same line, same step. Mine didn't. Mine's been stopped since I was fourteen. I don't know what that means. Nobody asked. Tomorrow we go in.*

**Ch11 L249** — the inversion stated as a status, not a relief:

> *Everyone else's went to sleep at the stone. Mine has been asleep since I was fourteen. Out here that makes me the only one who's awake.*

**Ch16, at the wall** — the promise's complication named aloud, ch16 L25–L27:

> "Out there I was the thing the system couldn't hold." He had not known he was going to say it in that order until it came out in that order. "In there I was the only thing that *worked*. I don't know which one's worse."
>
> Lira said, "The second one. You're allowed to say so."

And the arc statement's second pole, written in the log, **ch16 L209**:

> *Nothing out here reads me. And at a line you can mark with a rock, every Path in this camp stops, and mine doesn't. Karis won't write her hypothesis. I won't write mine.*

**Ch22 L99–L103** — the second complication, moral rather than metaphysical: the freedom to be
unmeasured has produced an instrument that measures other people. Teague's wrist a foot from his
hand; *"He could know. Not everything. Enough."* → *"He did not put his hand on the wrist."* The
rule was written for exactly this at ch16 L173: *"The reading turned outward. I ask first. Every
time. If I ever don't, that's the day I became the instrument."*

**Ch19 L203 and Ch24 L29** — the third complication, institutional, and the one that most directly
complicates the *promise* rather than the arc. The board is the mechanism by which the edge
territories made him free (ch02 L37: the ledger page has no column for what you are). Teague, ch19
L203: *"Then you're not on the board anymore. You're something the board doesn't have a column for."*
Pike, ch24 L29: *"Teague says you need a column I don't have. I've kept this ledger thirty years.
First time it's been wrong about a crew by being too small."* — and ch24 L31, he leaves the line
blank.

**Ch24 L143** — the close:

> Nothing out here had read him. At three floors older than any registry, every Path in the party had stopped at a line and his had not; a fourth had been paced from outside and not crossed. Nobody alive — least of all him — knew why. Someone had made the pattern. That was as far as anyone could say.

### Verdict (≤10 sentences)

The drafted book delivers the promise. The freedom is not asserted but built out of an institution
— Pike's board, whose columns say what you did and not what you are — so that ch02 L217's "I'm
free. First time." is a conclusion the reader has watched him check, and ch05 makes him live in it
for two chapters of paid cataloguing with no fight at the end. The complication then lands in three
distinct registers rather than one, which is why it holds. Metaphysically at ch09 L229 and ch16 L25:
the same fact — nothing reads him — flips from relief to the thing he cannot say a sentence about,
and Lira names the worse of the two on the page. Morally at ch16 L173 and ch22 L101: freedom from
being measured produces the power to measure, and the book stages the temptation at Teague's wrist
and pays it by refusal. Institutionally at ch19 L203 and ch24 L29: the one honest ledger on the
continent runs out of room for him, and the freedom's own instrument is what breaks — the sharpest
version of "complicated," because the reader has spent twenty chapters learning to trust that
column. The complication therefore lands hardest not at the Ring but at the plank in the snow, where
Pike puts the pen down and leaves the line blank. The arc statement's first pole is exactly where
the architecture says (ch02 L217, lived ch05); its second pole is present twice — verbatim in the
log at **ch16 L209**, eight chapters earlier than the architecture claims, and again in close
narration over the map at **ch24 L143** — so the architecture's "The second sentence is written in
Chapter 24" is accurate as to placement over the map but not as to it being a log entry, and
understates that the pole is already fully written at Ch16. That early landing is a strength, not a
fault: it frees Ch24 to be the pattern rather than the confession.
