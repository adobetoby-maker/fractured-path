Reading it: the physical objects (ledger, log, notebook) run at roughly 22–28 hits per 10k words in every book — a ledger word every 400 words, from Book 1 on. That alone explains fatigue by Book 3. Worse, the *register* spreads: from Book 5 the accounting verbs take over the narration itself — "priced" ×13/10k in Book 5, "count/counted" ×23/10k in Book 7 and ×18 in Book 8, "catalogued" ×10 in Book 8, and the withholding beat ("said nothing / did not say") rising from 0 to 7/10k. By Books 7–8 every character talks like the ledger: things are "priced", "entered", "noted", "filed", "a fact about a ruler", and the narrator "catalogues" every halt.

## 3. Character and relationship growth — what the prose does
- The companions' wants exist in the series document (Lira the ladder; Brom the family; Karis the ranking; Seln the cache; Oryn the route) and they are *stated* in prose, but mostly through the same device: Cael catalogues them, prices them, files them. The characteristic move — "which was, from Brom, an essay" / "which from Lira was a paragraph" — measures a person instead of letting them speak. The census of that construction alone would be dozens per book.
- Relationship beats are recorded rather than played: Lira "said nothing, which was yes"; Oryn's whole verdict "was a nod". Restraint is a virtue once per chapter; as the default grammar for every pair, it flattens all six relationships to the same temperature.
- Cael's interiority is one register — inventory. A thirteen-year-old reader gets the same sentence shape for grief, fear, love and a fight.

## 4. Root causes (in the harness, not the prose)
1. The voice charter and author SOUL make "the cataloguer who cannot not count" the narrator's identity and the LOG the default chapter closer; the packets require a log closer every third chapter and "costs ledgered forward" in every aftermath.
2. The editor gates reward it: continuity gates check that every injury is "entered", every cost "priced" — so the author writes the accounting on the page to pass the gate.
3. The combat cap (≤8 combat-primary chapters) plus "fight silent / nobody declares" doctrine pushed fights toward stillness and summary; the Book 5 arena book made fights into debriefs.

## 5. Recommendations
**A. Device budget (hard caps, per chapter, enforced in loop.sh gates):** ledger/log/notebook objects ≤3; "catalogued/entered/noted/priced/filed" ≤2 combined; "said nothing / did not say" ≤1; the "was, from X, a [unit]" measuring clause 0; italic log entries only at true structural points — no more than one per three chapters, and never as a substitute for the scene.
**B. Fights:** at least one dramatized fight per movement (not per book) with the charter's geography-first rule; the fight is shown, not reported to the log; no arithmetic during a fight; each fight has a different shape (terrain, weapon, opponent's craft, a visible decision that wins it); costs shown on the body in the next scene, not "entered".
**C. Character growth:** each companion gets one scene per book where they act against their own pattern and it changes what they want, played in dialogue and action; Cael's interiority allowed at least two other registers (want, fear) besides inventory.
**D. Relationship growth:** two people talking with friction and warmth in every movement; disagreement allowed to stay unresolved for a chapter; at least one relationship per book moves to a new footing on the page (a name used for the first time, a thing asked for, a thing refused).
**E. Where to apply it:** Book 9 forward is the cheap path (write it into the Book 9 architecture, the charter and the gates). Books 3–8 would need a scene-level revision pass, not a line pass: ~40 chapters where a fight or a growth beat was reported instead of played would have to be re-dramatized. That is a real cost and a real choice.

## 6. Decision needed from the owner
Apply the new rules (a) from Book 9 forward only, (b) Book 9 forward plus a revision pass on Books 7–8 (the two most recent, where fights already exist and the tic layer is thickest), or (c) the full Books 3–8 revision.
