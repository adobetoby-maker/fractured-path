## 1. Reader response (unscored)

Interest caught at once: a note at breakfast that is not for Cael, and Lira going on eating, "which was how Cael knew it mattered." The annex scene is the best thing in the movement — one sheet, four paragraphs, nineteen words, and the sentence that was never written down. I believed every beat of it, and the shape of the reveal (she braced for weight and got a slip of paper) is exactly the right humiliation for her to turn into a weapon on Thursday. The review itself pays: two hits spent like coins, the barrow turn nobody at Greyvane has seen, and the notation read in the record's flat cadence. Brom's wall landing on the same day, in the same register of honest measurement, is a strong pairing.

Interest held through Gerda more than I expected. "Correctly" is a real idea — a procedure that hurt her without anyone doing wrong — and Lira deciding to be the one person who argues is the movement's cleanest character turn. Hobb's three lines ("Eleven. Yesterday, none." / "Better." / "Didn't go back.") are perfect.

Interest slipped twice. First, in chapter 28's opening stretch: sessions five through ten summarised in one paragraph, then a long paragraph of Karis's tells, then the full push — the texture goes to report for about a page. Second, across the chapter 29 → 30 boundary, where the calendar stops making sense (see §5): chapter 29 shows a normal Tuesday and Wednesday, then chapter 30 opens on a Monday night in which Cael collapses on Brom's floor and cannot sit up. I had to stop and re-read to work out when I was.

The person who matters most by the end is Karis — the line under the column, the mark, the witnessed volunteering. The event I expect next is Quenna's room after the fourth sitting, and I know what she is going to propose; the movement makes that wait feel earned rather than stalled. I would continue.

**Thirteen-year-old lens.** Would follow all of it; the bells, the bouts and the ledger entries are clear on one pass. Would love the barley clause and the pen withheld. Might not catch why the nulls matter until Cael writes the hypothesis in chapter 29 — and that is the right moment for it. Would be unsettled, in a good way, by "Part of me is waiting for it." Would not notice the week-11 calendar problem consciously but would feel the chapter 30 jolt.

**Adult genre reader lens.** Would admire the discipline of twenty-two nulls that never once fake a flicker, and the hypothesis being withheld from the one person who could not test it. Would notice the long-sentence drift in the cutaway and the reflections. Would notice that Lira's private speeches to Cael are elided three times running (ch27, ch28, ch30) and might start reading something into the elision that the edition does not want read (see §6).

### Continuity defects (all line-level; none alter an end state)

a. **ch28 ¶1** — "By the end of the first week of the nulls" → should be the second week (or "By the end of the tenth week"). Session one was Wednesday of week 9; sessions 2–4 that weekend; ch29 calls week 11 "the third week."

b. **ch28 ¶2, session arithmetic** — "a session nearly every day of the tenth week, and on two of the days they ran two" cannot hold sessions 5–14 (ten sessions). Seven days plus two doubles is nine at most; "nearly every day" implies fewer. Also the read-back of "all fourteen sessions" is "at the end of the tenth week, on the last evening," the day after the three-hour twelfth (Saturday), so 13 and 14 both fall on Sunday before the read-back — possible, but it should be said, since Karis was grey at the fingertips the day before.

c. **ch29 §7 → ch30, week 11** — Session 15 is "that night" after the Tuesday afternoon Wray/Edran scene; session 17 is "Wednesday morning, before the first bell"; session 16 has no slot. And the AUTHOR-REPORT places the fast catch on Monday night of week 11, but chapter 29's Tuesday (Oona at the table, Wray's floor, session 15) shows a Cael with no trace of a night on Brom's floor, a numb hand and "sitting up was not yet a thing he could do"; chapter 30 then opens on that Monday night and follows with "The Tuesday session in section four was a bad one." The reader is carried forward through Wednesday and then dropped back two days into an injury the Tuesday scenes never showed. Cleanest fix: move the fast catch to Wednesday night of week 11 (after session 17) and make the bad supervised session the Thursday one (section four runs Tuesday/Thursday); Quenna's "The fourth sitting is next week" still holds. Alternatively keep Monday and let Tuesday's scenes carry the aftermath — more lines to touch.

d. **ch31 §6** — "where Oona would go down in a little under seven weeks." From Thursday of week 10 she had "seven weeks and two days" (ch28), i.e. Saturday of week 17 (BOOK_MAP week 17). From the last day of week 11 that is a little under **six** weeks.

e. **ch29 §3** — "in a formal yard a week ago" (Lira's review was Thursday of week 9; this is Monday of week 11 — eleven days). "the week before" or "the other week" reads true.

f. **Drawn Channel in the air (ch25 §5, ch28 §5, ch31 §2).** Karis's declaration as minted in M2 and recorded in STATE_LEDGER (After Movement 2 §3) reads: "Join two of your standing ignition points with a line of heat *along any surface* between them." In this movement the channel runs "along the air" between two points at shoulder height, and in the long session "at every height." A standing point in the air is not constrained by the text; the channel is. Either lay the channelled pairs on the floor or wall (session seven already puts points on the floor) or have Karis note that the quiet room's whitewash is the surface she uses. M5's match depends on channel geometry ("her fourth channel broken from inside"), so this is worth settling now rather than after.

g. **ch27 §7 — Prynn certifies on Monday morning at the second bell.** M2 minted that Prynn certifies archive copies on the second and fourth afternoons. A student's one-clause copy out of hours is believable for Prynn; one clause ("out of her hours, and said nothing about it") would square it.

h. **Lira's shoulder** — "a bruise for a week" (ch26) is strapped through Friday of week 9, "bruised" on Monday of week 11, and "unstrapped at last… purple-yellow" on the last day of week 11, sixteen days on. Soft, not wrong; a storm slap that turned her a quarter round can bruise deep. Leave unless touched for another reason.

i. **Full push "after the eighth bell"** (ch28) — the eighth bell is lamps out in the residence wing (M1 canon). Brom's room by a shaded lamp is fine; the brief never forbids it. No change needed.

## 6. Reader Standard (thirteen-year-old; Gate 27)

- **Language:** clean. No oaths or softer cousins. "you idiot" (ch30, Lira, "Breathe, you idiot") is family register and acceptable.
- **Violence:** with cost, fear and consequence; no gore. The fast catch's cost (white flash, breath gone, numb hand) is the most frightening passage in the movement and is exactly how the brief wants cost shown.
- **Moral goodness:** the man who added a sentence out loud is named as wrong and gains nothing; Lira helps Gerda unasked at her own cost; Karis writes a rule to protect herself and then volunteers anyway; nobody is cruel and rewarded.
- **No romance or innuendo — one ambiguity to tighten, not a violation.** Three times running, what Lira says or feels about Cael is withheld from the page: ch27 §4 (one pace and the heat on his face — "he had been told a great deal and none of it was going to be said aloud at a table"); ch28 §3 ("short and sharp and not repeatable at a table with second-years near it"); ch30 §1–2 ("something to you tomorrow that I can't say tonight with Brom in the room… not repeatable anywhere," then her forehead against his shoulder). Each on its own reads as fear or fury. Together, elided, they can be read by an adult as a feeling Lira is not saying, which the edition expressly does not want (BOOK_MAP §11: "Lira is partner and family, not a love interest"). "Not repeatable… with second-years near it" also implies language the Reader Standard bans, even though no word appears. Repair is small: let one of the three carry its content on the page (what it looked like from the floor; "I counted six breaths and you weren't taking any of them"), and change "not repeatable" to "sharp," so the elision stops doing the work.
