## Verdict

The book holds as one arc. Read end to end it does what the map asked: the observer cage becomes a vantage point, the vantage point becomes a hypothesis, the hypothesis is tested the only honest way there was, and the boy who was written down by everybody stands up and reads the writers their own book. The calendar is clean from the road's third morning to the last free afternoon of week 24; every protected line is exact and lands where the map puts it; the knowledge boundaries hold without a single leak into Cael's head; the four cutaway voices stay inside what their owners could know; the Reader Standard is met everywhere. The two central set pieces, the match (ch36) and the two sittings (ch54–59), are the best writing in the book, and the quiet seventh movement earns its quiet. The weaknesses a straight-through read shows are not structural. They are habits: a repertoire of about ten similes and gesture-tags that recur so often across 61 chapters that by the last third a reader hears the author rather than the character (the explanatory "the way a X does Y" simile alone appears 237 times); a mild sag across ch27–31, where Gerda's arc and the second week of nulls run on parallel tracks with Cael's own stakes paused; and eight small continuity seams (two of which the lanes already named) that a line pass closes. Twenty-four line fixes below; three cross-movement priorities for one same-author pass. Nothing here needs a rebuild.

## 3. Book-wide repetition — the worst ten

Counts are `grep -oiE` over all 61 chapters (hits / chapters). "Keep" names the instances that earn their place; everything else is a candidate for the same-author pass.

1. **The explanatory simile "the way a X does Y" / "as a X does Y"** — 237 / 58. The book's dominant tic; by M6 nearly every gesture is glossed. Keep the ones that are character-specific and first: Brom "the way a smith considers a bar of iron" (ch1), Prynn "as a farmer looks at a stray dog" (ch33, the instructor), Lira "the way she would have taken a heel of bread off a market stall" (ch54). Target: no more than two per chapter, none on a gesture the sentence has already made clear.
2. **"as if (it/she/he) had/were…"** — 138 / 57. Same family; often stacked with (1) in one sentence.
3. **"for a long time / moment / while"** — 112 / 49. Pauses are measured this way in almost every dialogue scene. Keep in the hearing (ch55–58), where length is the point.
4. **"exactly"** — 263 / 59. "Exactly where", "exactly as", "exactly the same". Keep where exactness is the subject (Karis, Prynn, the deposition); thin everywhere else.
5. **The triple reading** — "read it twice / three times / again" 54 / 28 and "three times" 52 / 31. The P6 callback (the category line, cold tea) is deliberate and pays in ch49 and ch55; but by midbook every notice, letter, slip and ruling is read "twice, and then a third time". Keep: ch2 (origin), ch16 (the petition sentence), ch36 (the notice, "four… a fifth"), ch49 (deposition), ch61 (Hesk). Elsewhere read it once.
6. **"did not look up / without looking up"** — 49 / 28. Prynn's and Karis's signature; it has leaked to everyone.
7. **Measures by the hand** — "a finger's width / a hand's width / a hair's breadth" 38 / 21, "a hair" 24 / 16. Keep for the Ember contact test (ch40–41), where the hair's gap is the finding.
8. **"squared … to the edge" / "square to the table's edge"** — 23 / 21. Karis and Prynn both do it; once each per scene is plenty.
9. **"went (very) still"** — 22 / 17, and specifically Karis "went still, as she went still before an ignition" (ch25, ch37, ch51) — keep the first (ch25).
10. **Oona's tags** — "with her whole (serious) face / the whole of her face" 13 / 12 and "slate under her arm" 22 / 19. Keep the whole-face tag at ch12 (first), ch17 and ch41; keep the slate where it does work (ch5, ch41, ch59).

## 6. Reader Standard — met

No oaths, none of the softer cousins; the harshest word in the book is Lira's "Breathe, you idiot" over a boy who has stopped breathing (ch30). Violence costs and is felt (the burn, the fast catch, Gerda's shoulder) and is never lingered on. No romance, no innuendo; Lira is partner and family; Karis's paragraph asks for a place at a table. Wrong is named as wrong by the people wronged and by the book: the Fenmark reader who "made it up" (ch24), the custom that walked four people out of their lives with no hearing (ch58), and the Compact's practice, which the ruling itself calls what it is. Coss is allowed to be decent without the book excusing the machine (ch42, ch59).

## 9. Three cross-movement priorities for one same-author pass

1. **Thin the simile and gesture repertoire (M1–M8, every chapter).** Items 1–10 of §3, plus the small verbatim repeats. Scope: one reading pass, sentence by sentence, no script; cap the explanatory "the way a X does Y" and "as if" at two per chapter; cut "for a long time/moment", "exactly" and "did not look up" where the sentence stands without them; let a document be read once unless the reading is the beat; give Oona, Karis and Prynn one tag each per scene. The first instance of each figure stays; the rest are rewritten in the character's own register or cut. Expect to lose two to three thousand words across the book and to gain the characters back.

2. **Tighten the two slow stretches (ch27–31; ch47–50).** Scope: ch28's reported week of nulls and ch31's twenty-second session overlap with the ledger reading-back already done in ch28 — lose about a thousand words between them and let Cael's "part of me is waiting" surface once more in ch30 so his stakes stay live while Gerda's are paid.
