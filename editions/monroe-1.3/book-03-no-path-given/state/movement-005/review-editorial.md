## 1. Reader response (unscored)

Interest caught at the clean sheet in Quenna's room (ch32): a girl refusing a standard waiver and writing her own, then drawing a line under three sentences, is the kind of specific act that makes a reader lean in before the stakes are explained. It held through Hobb stepping across the channel because nobody told him not to (ch33, "Wanted to know"), through Lira's chalk circles and Brom's pea bags (ch34, the freshest training sequence in the book so far), and through the archive scene with no notebook (ch35). The match itself (ch36) is the best single stretch of the edition I have read: I could track where his feet were in every exchange, and the end came out of things I had been shown.

Where it slipped: the second half of ch33 (the instructor's "grammar" lecture is a lecture, short but still a lecture); the taper paragraphs of ch35 that re-list her tells for the third time; the ch37 warm-fragment reflection, which is beautiful but idles; and in the match, two places where I had to take the mechanics on trust (why the read is suddenly reliable at the start of exchange two; why already-lit channels die when one point fails). Those two are in the repair brief.

The person who matters most by the end is Karis, and that is a surprise the movement earns: her cutaway (ch38) turns the book's instrument into a person without a word of romance. Lira runs a close second through the wall scenes. The event I expect next: the first small Ember touch on Tuesday with somebody in the room, and Oona's Kindling on Saturday, with Quenna's unanswered question underneath both. I would continue without hesitation.

## 2. Two lenses

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** Everything load-bearing is sayable: lay, hold, join, let go; a point won't light where it's already hot; the shove first, then the heat; pick faster. The chalk-and-peas drill is exactly the kind of thing a reader this age will want to try in a yard. The match is readable at speed and the ending (he lights the spot first, so her fire can't stand there) is a rule the reader was handed two chapters earlier and will feel clever for remembering. The crying scene is short and unembarrassing. Nothing on the page is unsafe for this reader; the burn is one plain sentence and then a bandage. The slow patches for this reader are the ch37 reflection and the ch39 columns, both of which could lose a third of their sentences and keep their meaning.

**Adult genre reader.** This is the strongest movement so far on the owner's own terms: a learned capability tested under changed conditions; a loss that buys knowledge and is not disguised as a win; a cost ledger (burn, misfires, grey fingertips, Quenna's question) that the following chapters carry. The adult reader will notice two things the younger one may not: a cluster of sentences in ch36's reset and aftermath that sit very close to the source edition's phrasing (§8), and a prose texture that has gone short enough to flatten the difference between reflection and action (§9). Neither spoils the movement; both are fixable without touching the fight's beats.

## 5. The match (ch36) judged as a high-stakes fight

Against the seat's VOICE criteria for a major fight (terrain, starting positions, reach, objectives, techniques, injuries and limits established; the contest rendered as changing decisions; the opponent also learns; reversals follow from planted information; the decisive exchange expanded; costs carried):

**Fair and readable.** Terrain (swept earth, chalk, eight paces, north and south marks, three chairs), starting state (four fragments inventoried; the fifth space not reached for; forearms wrapped; binder handed to Lira), and limits (the framework's seam; the read's close range; the drop takes a shove not heat; "Nothing I have takes heat") are all on the page before Wray's finger drops. Position is stated at each halt (south chalk; her middle distance; the east corner forming). A listener can keep the floor in mind.

**A learned capability tested under changed conditions.** This is the movement's spine and it works: six weeks of study against her public draft, then the first thing the match teaches is that the lag he charted does not exist at full economy ("she had not let a single point go"). The drop (ch34, two of three, told to Lira before) becomes the crossing. "Pick faster" (ch34) becomes the three escapes. "Out-early her" (ch33) becomes the keystone.

**Reversals that follow from planted information.** Exchange one: the lag is gone — reversal of his model, planted by the ch33 lag demonstration ("for symmetry"). Exchange two: the read gives points as pressure before light, planted in M4's nulls (three in twelve; twice in session 22); she answers by moving her floor outward, which is the opponent learning; the crossing follows from Hobb's report and the drop test. Exchange three: pairs aimed at his lateness, the bait channel (planted by Lira's "stand there deciding until the bag made the decision for you"), out of angles, and the break on the ch33 cold-air rule. Karis's recognition and laugh are the correct emotional payoff for a researcher who wanted the thing to be true.

**Two places where the chain has a soft link**, both repairable in a sentence or two without touching a beat:

1. *The read's reliability (opening of exchange two).* In the quiet room the density came three times in twelve; in the match it is "faint and sure" on every near point for the rest of the fight. The text marks the jump ("The world changed texture") but does not tie it to anything shown. The formula's rule is that an unexplained ability must not quietly resolve the decisive problem; the read is not the decisive solution (the reach is), but it carries exchanges two and three and supplies the coordinate for the keystone. One clause of earned cause would close it: at full economy every point is a whole commitment, laid close and fast, and a committed point leans harder on the air than a demonstration point ever did; or, he has never once had the read open under a real fight. Either is consistent with M4's plant and with Karis's own account that it was her trying that changed things.

2. *Why the lit channels die (end of exchange three).* The keystone logic is sound and planted: her second point for the fourth channel cannot take in standing heat (ch33); the channel therefore cannot be drawn (her own declaration: "If either point fails, the channel fails with it"); the sequence breaks. But the page then has the *already-lit* channels "falter and go dark" too, and nothing shown makes them do that. Her declaration says a channel fails when one of its own points fails, and those points did not fail. The honest cause is on the page a few lines later: "Karis stopped." Make the dying explicitly hers: she was holding sixteen points on a count that no longer existed, and when the count broke she lost the hold, or let go. Ch38's cutaway ("the assembly had lost its sequence and gone out") can carry the same clause from her side. This matters because the instructor planted the question ("I've never seen a Ternhall one broken from inside") and the answer should be checkable.

**Trackable for a listener.** Yes, with one small snag: "The first point bloomed on the earth a pace to his right, at the height of his ankle" — on the earth or at ankle height; pick one.

**Cost carried.** The burn is examined by Wray, cooled, dressed, kept loose, aches through ch39, and reaches Book 4's "a while healing" via the Hesk letter. The breath and strap of the drop come due on the walk back. Two Wind absences at dawn. Karis's grey fingertips. Quenna's question. All carried.

**Two small internal tensions inside the fight:**
- Ch34 sets the drop's size: "I'm choosing the size first. A quarter. Fast." In the crossing the fragment closes on "a quarter of it, more than a quarter, as much as it could hold," at the same small bill. M2 canon says a joint misroute follows reaching for more than was chosen; that applies to sending, and a drop gives the force no road, so this is arguably consistent. But "choose the size first" is one of Cael's few hard rules and the match breaks it silently. Either keep to the quarter or have the excess cost him one visible thing.
- "However the next quarter of an hour went" (ch36, the gateway). Book 4 ch9 has Karis say the ratio that worked was "six weeks of demonstration sessions plus *one hour* of genuine engagement." Three exchanges with resets can plausibly run an hour; the edition should not pin it at a quarter. Remove the duration or make it "the next hour."
