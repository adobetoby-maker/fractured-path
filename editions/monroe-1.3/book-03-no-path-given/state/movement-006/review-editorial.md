# Editorial review — Book 3, Movement 006 ("Registry Law"), chapters 40–46

**Seat and standing.** This is a fresh-context editorial review, not self-review. The review seat is Claude Fable 5.1, standing in for the usual Sol seat (out of usage). The manuscript author was Claude Opus 5.5, a different model; I have no drafting history with this text and read it cold, in chapter order, before reading the author's report. It is a simulated cold read by a model, not a human read. Coverage: the seven supplied chapters (36,701 words by the supplied metrics), the preceding chapter 39 for the seam, the packet and its coordinator notes, the brief's working ranges and the 8-word reuse gate, the supplied canon evidence, the author's report (read as the author's map, not as evidence), and source chapters 15–17 for the reuse question. Measured metrics are the supplied ones; I did not re-estimate them. Everything else I measured myself is labelled with its method.

## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Breakfast on the Tuesday, with Lira counting terms on her fingers and Brom supplying "Buckets," and then the cook's woodpile: "She calls it kindling." That is the first laugh of the movement and it is earned by three people's characters at once. The first deliberate test in the quiet room is the best-written stretch of the seven chapters: the candle "not lit and then is, with nothing in between," the cost arriving *after* like a missed stair in the dark, Lira chafing his hand and saying "You looked like you were thinking about your dinner," and Karis kneeling at the post and saying, to nobody, "That isn't my point." The bench in the grey building (ch41) is the emotional high point: Denvash coming up "the way weather comes, into his body, without asking," two hands on a thin rigid shoulder, taken away as she stands and not before, and "Anchor. Anchor Path. Copper." followed by "It's ordinary." I would read the book for that scene alone. Later, D6 with a pen moving at the back of the hall, and Coss stopping writing, is a quiet scene that carries real suspense; the gauntlet is a clean, trackable fight with a loss that buys knowledge.

**Where it slipped.** Chapters 42 through 45. The events are right and the lines that matter land, but the register changes: paragraphs lengthen into reflective essay ("The machine was not run on cruelty..."; "That was the worst thing about institutions, he was beginning to learn..."), the camera pulls back from the body into summary, and the same structural idea (four layers, each resting on the one above) is explained twice in two chapters. A reader who does not have the source will feel only that these chapters are more *told* than the ones either side of them. A reader who does have the source will recognise the sentences. More on that in §4.

**Which person matters.** Oona, by a distance, and then Prynn. Oona is the category's true user walking out of it, and the author lets that fact do its own work without a speech. Prynn's affection arrives entirely through tea, an unlocked door, a dusty laugh and "It's a good road. It's only the wrong one."

**Which event I expect next.** The delegation, and the first time Coss and Cael are in the same room after D6. I also expect Karis to find nothing in the schedule, and to say so.

**Would I continue.** Yes. The movement ends on a true line ("It leaves them later. On whoever loses.") with the argument built and the week counted.

**Two lenses.** A fluent thirteen-year-old will follow the whole legal argument, because the author has reduced it to one checkable sentence ("the word isn't on the list") and taught the reader the list first; the same reader will drift in the scars passage (ch44) and the four-layers passage (ch45), which both explain the same idea at length before the clause arrives. An adult genre reader will enjoy the argument's construction and the fight-reading metaphor, and will notice that ch42–43 lean on authorial summary where ch40–41 trusted the scene.

**Findings.**
1. **Coss's calendar cannot reach the gate (ch41, lines 541–599; canon: BOOK_MAP §3 "19 — Coss at the gate").** The regional registry enters Oona's line "eight days later" than the Tuesday of week 18, i.e. the Wednesday of week 19; Coss reads it that day, requests in-person service that night, is approved "by the middle of the next morning," is assigned an aide "the next morning," and sets out "before dawn on the Tuesday" to reach the academy "on the Monday following." Yet he is at Greyvane's gate on the Monday of week 19 (ch42, line 609), before any of that could have happened. Oona Kindled on the Saturday of week 17 and her sheet left on the Monday post; no reading of "eight days" puts the line in his hands before the Tuesday of week 18, and a six-day road from that Tuesday lands him on Monday week 19 only if he read the line by the Sunday before — impossible. This is a verified continuity contradiction, not plan adherence. Related, lesser: "He had been at the regional office nineteen days" (line 547) sits oddly beside the M2 ledger ("Coss is on the road to the regional office," week 5–6); ten weeks are unaccounted for. That one is an apparent inconsistency for the planner, not a contradiction.
2. **Oona's age at her Kindling (ch41, line 435).** "a thirteen-year-old girl who had been afraid for seven weeks and two days." UNIVERSE_BIBLE: "At age 14, every person undergoes Kindling"; BOOK_MAP §5.5.1: "The child's Kindling arrives at fourteen in week 17"; M1 ledger: "Oona Kindles 'in winter' at fourteen." She is fourteen on the bench. One word.
3. **Doubled sentence (ch45, lines 1279–1281).** "That was why the schedule had no scars." then, after a break, "That was why the schedule carried no scars." Both versions of a rewritten line survived. Concrete defect.
4. **Soft lean on dating (ch45, line 1321).** Cael to Prynn: "a few hundred years of everybody treating it as if it were in the schedule." BOOK_MAP §10 forbids a date on the rename; this is not the rename, and "a few" is vague, but Book 6 dates the flag's existence to about two centuries. "Generations" would carry no risk. Optional.
5. **Image to weigh (ch41, line 435).** "told by the oldest instrument in the world that she was exactly who she had decided to be." The Arbiter's "instrument" is established usage; "oldest in the world" is Cael's flourish and nudges toward Book 9's secret. I would trim "in the world." Optional.
6. **Physical state.** The left-forearm burn is kept out of use and in a loose sleeve throughout; the right shoulder is not mentioned (acceptable, nothing loaded it). Ember cost is cumulative in a session (grey round the mouth by the fourth) and Lira caps it at three — a new limit, consistent with the notice. The ledger should record it.
7. **New canon to confirm (all acceptable on the evidence):** Oona as Anchor Path, Copper (Anchor is on M1's hardening family chart and among the eleven Paths watched — an existing name, as the packet required); the charter rule admitting the Compact's officer of record to a pending respondent's assessment "as an observer, without voice" (nothing in canon forbids it; it is the cheapest way to make Book 4's thin exhibit true); Coss lodging over the draper's shop; the taxonomy master as Prynn's former reader (see above — consistent with M1 prose); Brom raised "on an estate where people were told what they'd seen."

## 4. Source reuse — the lead concern

**Method.** Beyond the supplied 8-word list (99 unprotected runs, concentrated in ch42–46), I measured two things myself against the source edition, using the overlap tool's tokeniser: (a) maximal shared runs of six or more words per chapter against the whole source book, and (b) a sentence-skeleton alignment: every manuscript sentence of eight or more tokens matched to its best source sentence in chapters 15–17 by ordered-token similarity (difflib ratio, prefiltered by token-set Jaccard ≥ 0.15). A sentence scoring ≥ 0.50 shares at least half its words *in order* with one source sentence — that is a kept skeleton with words changed. ≥ 0.35 is a close paraphrase. As a control I ran the same alignment on Movement 5 (ch32–39) against its source chapters 12–14.

| Chapter | 6-word runs /10k | Sentences ≥8w | Skeleton ≥.50 | Close ≥.35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 12 | 181 | 2% | 10% |
| 41 | 17 | 234 | 1% | 14% |
| **42** | **131** | 203 | **27%** | **54%** |
| **43** | **182** | 194 | **32%** | **56%** |
| **44** | **179** | 201 | **23%** | **47%** |
| 45 | 104 | 238 | 16% | 36% |
| 46 | 74 | 255 | 13% | 30% |
| M5 control (eight chapters) | 31–104 | — | 2–13% (median 8%) | 13–26% |

**Verdict.** Chapters 42, 43 and 44 are a close paraphrase of source chapters 15 and 16 that keeps the source's sentence skeletons: roughly a quarter to a third of their sentences follow a source sentence word-order for word-order, and about half are close paraphrase — three to four times the clean-movement rate. Chapters 45 and 46 are mixed: new prose for most of their length, with specific scenes paraphrased at the same density as ch42–44. The author's report says every run was "recomposed whole, not synonym-swapped"; the alignment says otherwise for the spine scenes. The 8-word gate was passed by varying the words inside the sentences, which is exactly the failure the brief's 8-word tightening describes. Examples (manuscript first, source second):

- ch42: "It did not surprise him. It came the way true things sometimes come, as a shape at last coming clear out of something he had been watching from the side of his eye for a long time without ever turning to face it." / "It landed the way true things sometimes landed — not as a surprise so much as a shape finally resolving out of something he'd been looking at sideways for a year and a half without quite letting himself look at directly." The whole paragraph then runs clause for clause: reports read by somebody; questions written for somebody; the junior assessor; the watchers; the file never shut; a hand at the far end; credentials on its knee; honest "because honesty had become the correct procedure" / "procedurally convenient"; a face that "remembered him at fourteen."

It was the silence of people who had already, privately, done the arithmetic themselves and arrived at the same ugly number… two hundred students on one side of the scale, one on the other, and an honest man responsible for all of it… the way their cruelest mathematics could be performed entirely by decent people showing their work."

**What re-composition means here.** Not another pass over the same sentences. Each listed scene should be rebuilt from its event list with a different entry point, a different order of beats, or a different piece of physical business, so that the sentences are generated fresh rather than varied. Three concrete handles the author already owns: the provost's office can be played from Cael's count of the room (he counted crows and students on the way up; let him count the room — chairs, the aide's absence, Quenna's folder — and let Coss's speech arrive against that counting rather than in the source's order); the war council can open on Brom standing because there is no chair, and let the arithmetic be *said* by Lira or Brom in dialogue instead of narrated in the source's essay paragraph; the outline scene can be written as the page itself — what he writes, crosses out, and writes again — rather than as narrated reasoning about the page. The brief-quoted lines the packet asked for ("We cannot win this on the merits," "This is your enrollment, not mine to trade," "At fifteen," "Not a lawyer wearing your face," "I get to say it myself," "The argument needs the arguer," "It leaves them later. On whoever loses.") and every protected line stay exactly as they are; the sentences around them change.

## Repair brief

**Priority 1 — Re-compose the source-spine scenes of ch42–46.**
*Location:* ch42 scenes 3, 4, 6 (office; door and stair; family told); ch43 scenes 1, 4 (notice and case; war council); ch44 scenes 3, 6, with a lighter pass on 1 and 4; ch45 scene 4, with a lighter pass on 2 and 3; ch46 scene 5 and the opening of scene 2. They also carry the source's long essay paragraphs (paragraph medians 32–41.5 against a ceiling of 30) and two rewrite artefacts (ch45's doubled "no scars" sentence; ch42 scene 5 as a 248-word reflective orphan).
*Effect on the reader:* a register change from scene to summary across the movement's middle; the edition's own prose is audibly thinner here than in the chapters around it, and the edition's promise ("the prose is new") is not kept.
*Proposed scope:* rebuild each listed scene from its event list with a changed entry point, beat order or physical business (three handles given in §4); keep every protected and brief-quoted line verbatim; break paragraphs where the thought turns, by reading; remove the doubled sentence; absorb ch42 scene 5 into scene 4 or 6 and ch44 scene 3 into scene 2; add one break inside ch43's council. Re-run the 8-word overlap and, if the tool allows, a 6-word probe; the target is the clean-movement band, not zero.
*Strength to preserve:* every line the packet asked for already lands; Prynn's two scenes in ch45 (the wrong road; the digests) and ch44's declaration tree are the author's own and should not be touched.

**Priority 2 — Fix the Coss cutaway's clock and the three one-word canon slips.**
*Location:* ch41 lines 541–599 (the regional registry, "eight days later," "nineteen days," the Tuesday departure and Monday arrival); ch41 line 435 ("a thirteen-year-old girl"; "the oldest instrument in the world"); ch45 line 1321 ("a few hundred years").

Oona is fourteen at her Kindling by canon. The other two are soft leans toward dated or infrastructural secrets.
*Effect on the reader:* a reader counting days (and this book teaches its readers to count) finds the machine's best hand arriving before his own evidence; the age slip breaks the rule the first chapter taught.
*Proposed scope:* anchor the notation's arrival to the Kindling (sheet on the Monday post of week 18, entered within the week, on Coss's sheet by the Thursday or Friday of week 18), shorten the road to the two or three days the coach and hill allow, and keep the Monday arrival; reconcile or drop "nineteen days" against the M2 ledger (the planner should say where Coss was in weeks 6–15); "fourteen-year-old"; trim "in the world"; "generations" for "a few hundred years." Fifty words.
