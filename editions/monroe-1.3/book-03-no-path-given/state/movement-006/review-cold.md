# Movement 006 — cold read (chapters 40–46)

**Kind of read:** simulated cold read. One model (Claude Fable 5.1), fresh context, manuscript only. Read the wrapper and the compiled prompt (rubric + seven chapters) in declared order, every line, and nothing else in the repository: no canon, no plan, no notes, no drafting history. This is one reader's editorial judgment, not an audience measurement and not a sales signal.

**Coverage:** 7 files, chapters 40–46, complete as supplied. No editorial canon evidence was supplied, so anything below under "apparent inconsistency" is manuscript-internal and is not a verified canon violation.

**Orientation (arriving at chapter 40 mid-book):** Oriented within two pages. The chapters re-seat a newcomer on everything load-bearing without a recap: Cael, fifteen, on Greyvane's "unclassified observer track" under a borrowed category; Kindled at Denvash a year ago and given a word that is not a Path; the expulsion (Hesk's workshop, the courier, the bag on the bed); the Ardenmere circuit; a burned arm from a recent formal-yard match; the four at the scarred table (Lira, Brom, Karis, Cael); Oona, the other observer-track student; Quenna, Wray, Naveth, Prynn; and Coss, the Warden who held his file. The one thing I could not place and would want from earlier chapters: "the notice" that already used the words *contact-adjacent* and *single channel* before Cael had ever tested the spark (40, "That's what the notice said"; 41, "It's in the notice"). It reads as a deliberate carry-over rather than a gap, and Quenna's record in 46 picks up the same language, so I trust it is planted. "The word" written next to his name in Denvash is withheld throughout; that also reads as deliberate, and it works, but a reader who missed it earlier has nothing to hold.

## Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Immediately, at breakfast in 40: five terms counted on fingers, Brom adding "Buckets," and the cook's woodpile being called kindling. The experiment that follows is the best kind of fantasy-science scene: "Nothing glowed," then the ring of char and the thread of smoke "straight as a plumb line," and a cost that arrives *after*, cold down the arm, that nobody watching can see. Karis's "You haven't got fire. You've got a spark" is the sentence I would quote to someone to sell the book. Interest caught again at the bench in 41 (Denvash standing "in the passage in front of the real one"), and hardest at the gate in 42, where Coss is let in "because nothing about him was wrong."

**Where it slipped.** Two stretches. First, the back half of 44 and the front of 45, where the archive work has to be explained (enactment order, scars, four layers, the definitions clause). It is well disguised as fight-reading and never dull, but the thirteen-year-old lens in me skimmed the paragraph on how the drafting language changed "down the generations." Second, the registry section of 41: "eight days later" did not add up against Coss arriving on the Monday of the nineteenth week, and I stopped to count, which is the one thing a legal-thriller movement cannot afford (details below). A small snag in 45: the same sentence appears twice in a row ("That was why the schedule had no scars. / That was why the schedule carried no scars.").

**Which person matters.** Cael, and then Lira, who is the movement's spine: she takes "the part nobody's said" in 43, claps behind him in 44, and in 46 sets the rule "Three times. Not four." Coss is the most interesting figure in the movement precisely because he is decent; "he isn't worth burning anything on. The machine" is the right note and I believed Lira when she said it. Oona steals 41 outright. Brom's gauntlet in 45 is the movement's physical set piece and earns its length.

**Which event I expect next.** The hearing (first bell, first day of week twenty-two). Specifically: Coss standing up with the first draft "in that leaning hand"; the fourth file's eleven words read into the record; Karis finding something on the founding shelves that is not the argument but is the truth; and Hesk coming anyway despite "Please don't come." I also expect Coss to answer the seam not by defending the Compact's authority over Cael but by pointing at Greyvane's charter, which is the lever he actually named in 42; the manuscript has not yet let anyone on Cael's side say that out loud.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The movement ends a week and a half before the hearing with the argument drafted, a second reader at his shoulder, and the spark on the public record; that is exactly enough held back.

## Apparent inconsistencies (manuscript-internal; no canon supplied)

1. **Coss's arrival date cannot be reached from the registry section (41 → 42).** Oona is assessed on the Saturday of week 17. The notation sheet goes up "on the Monday post" (week 18). The registry section opens "eight days later" immediately after the Tuesday-of-week-18 paper session; read from there, the line is entered on the Wednesday of week 19, two days after Coss is already at the gate (42: "the Monday of the nineteenth week"). Read from Oona's Saturday instead, it is entered on a Sunday, and Coss still needs: a request "that night," approval "by the middle of the next morning," an aide "the next morning," departure "before dawn on the Tuesday," and six days to "reach the academy on the Monday following." No anchoring of "eight days later" closes that chain onto Monday of week 19. The rest of the movement's day-count (Wednesday council = day 1; Tuesday of week 20 = day 7; Friday = day 10; hearing in week 22) is internally consistent, so this is a single joint, not a systemic drift.
2. **Oona's age at Kindling.** 41: "a thirteen-year-old girl who had been afraid for seven weeks and two days." Elsewhere the text sets Kindling at fourteen (41: "their children came to fourteen on the road"; 42: "You were fourteen"; 41 digests: "a youth past the age of Kindling"). May be deliberate (assessment in the fourteenth year), but a reader will notice. Low-to-medium confidence that it is an error.
3. **Duplicated sentence (45).** "That was why the schedule had no scars." followed directly by "That was why the schedule carried no scars." One is a draft remnant.
4. **Which post (46).** The sitting uses "the oak post in its iron foot" fetched from section four. The spark post in the quiet room stands "in an iron bucket of sand" and by 44 carries seventeen rings. A reader briefly wonders whether Cael is burning the reading-post; one clause ("a clean post," or Hobb fetching "the quiet room's post") settles it.
5. **Repeated exposition (41 and 45).** The demonstration provision's origin ("written as a kindness... carters and drovers and pedlars") is narrated in full twice. Deliberate echo; the second instance could be cut to its new content (Cael's recognition that it was written for Oona) without loss.

Physical state (left arm burned, right hand grey after use, grey "to the second knuckle" after three on the record), power limits (contact or a hair; one channel; ~100 count; cost after), and knowledge boundaries (the aide fishing for "the observer made fire"; Coss's restricted-field superior) all held consistently across the seven chapters as far as the manuscript alone can show.

## Repair brief

**Priority 1 — Close the Coss timeline (41, registry section; 42, first line).**
- Location: 41, "At the regional registry office, eight days later..." through "...reach the academy on the Monday following"; 42, "the Monday of the nineteenth week."
- Observed issue: the sequence (Oona's Saturday assessment → Monday post → registry entry → Coss's request, approval, credential, aide → departure "before dawn on the Tuesday" → six-day journey) cannot deliver Coss to Greyvane's gate on Monday of week 19 under any reading of "eight days later."
- Effect on the reader: a genre reader counts, and this is the one movement where the author has invited counting (weeks, days, bells, counts of a hundred). A stall here costs trust at the exact moment Coss should feel inevitable.
- Proposed scope: one of two small fixes. (a) Keep the Monday-of-week-19 arrival and compress the chain: anchor the registry entry to Oona's Saturday explicitly ("Three days after the girl came back up the hill..."), approve and depart within two days, and shorten the journey ("the coach to the hill town and the road up from there" is two days, not six). (b) Keep the chain as written and move the arrival to the Monday of week 20, shifting the handful of later week labels by one (42 "nineteenth"; 44 log "week nineteen"; 45 "Tuesday of the twentieth week"; 46 log "Twentieth week"; the hearing notice "twenty-second"; Lira's "twenty weeks"). Either is a paragraph's work. Do not touch the day-count from the Wednesday council onward; it is sound.
- Strength to preserve: Coss's whole interior in that section, especially "He did not take any satisfaction in it. He noted it," the restricted field on the cover sheet, and the letter to his daughter. The procedural inevitability is the point; the dates only need to stop contradicting it.

**Priority 2 — Read-aloud and continuity sweep (45, 41, 46).**
- Location: 45, the twice-written "no scars" sentence; 41, "a thirteen-year-old girl" at a fourteen-year Kindling; 46, "the oak post in its iron foot" at the sitting; 46, "walked him to the stable for breakfast he did not want and ate."
- Observed issue: one duplicate line, one apparent age slip, one post that reads as the burnt one, one sentence whose final verb attaches to the wrong subject on first pass.
- Effect on the reader: each is a half-second stumble; together they cluster in the movement's two most quoted scenes (the discovery and the sixth sitting) where the prose otherwise earns being read aloud.
- Proposed scope: delete one of the two "no scars" sentences; decide Oona's age and make 41 agree with the Kindling-at-fourteen rule or make the rule say "the year they turn fourteen"; add a clause marking the sitting's post as a different post; recast the Lira sentence ("...and walked him to the stable, and he ate a breakfast he did not want"). Four line edits.
- Strength to preserve: "Nobody hits the ground." and the entire run from "He looked for the word they had written next to his name in Denvash." to "His word was not even among the rejected ones." Leave the rhythm of that discovery exactly as it is.

**Priority 3 — Let someone name the counter the text has already given Coss (43 council, or 45 Prynn exchange).**
- Location: 42, Coss: "a chartered academy that keeps an enrollment it knows to be invalid puts its charter up for review"; 43, Cael's "The whole challenge assumes the Compact has authority over me in the first place"; 45, Prynn: "That's the clever answer... I asked for the true one."
- Observed issue: Cael's seam argues that the Compact has no authority over an unclassified person. But Coss's stated lever in 42 is not Cael; it is Greyvane's charter, which the Compact plainly governs. Four careful readers (Cael, Prynn, Naveth, Karis) all accept the seam across two chapters, and none of them says the obvious thing: the Compact need not touch him at all, only the academy that holds him. An adult genre reader sees this by the end of 43 and spends 44–46 waiting for the text to see it too.
- Effect on the reader: if the hearing springs this as a surprise, it reads as the author noticing late rather than Coss being good at his job; if the hearing never raises it, the win feels unearned. Naming it early turns a hole into suspense.
- Proposed scope: one short exchange, not a new scene. Best home is Naveth in 43 after "the academy won't stand in it" ("He doesn't need authority over you. He has it over me."), or Prynn in 45 after "That's the clever answer," with Cael boxing the line as *not yet*. Two to four sentences. Do not resolve it here; let the hearing carry it.
- Strength to preserve: the architecture of the discovery (enactment order, scars, four layers, "shall mean") and Cael's reason for arguing it himself ("Every argument anybody's ever made about what I am was made over my head"). That speech is the movement's thesis and should not be diluted by the hedge; the hedge belongs in someone else's mouth.
