## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** The first page: Brom kneeling in pine needles with his hand open "like a man about to push a stuck door," and a boy whose body flinches from a knee that remembers being used as a drain. That is a concrete reason to care before any system is explained. It caught again at the intake room's third desk with a blank board ("the most accurate sign anyone had ever made about him"); at Brom's eleven minutes and his astonishment that nobody asked the question; at Lira signing without marking the box; at Naveth's shelf of brown ledgers and "Every student in this building is someone's clerical error"; at Wray changing one condition at a time until Brom has nothing to borrow; at Oona asking "Where's shattered?" in a room of thirteen-year-olds (the best single moment in the movement — the back-row reader stops breathing and so did I); at Prynn's terrible tea and the book left out; at the third pass of D1, where the strike lands, the fragment reaches, and "He chose. He chose nothing."; at Brom saying "Hobb" in his sleep.

**Where it slipped.** Chapter 3's tour — the bells recited, the lecture wing explained, the formal yard described — is information delivered at walking pace; the reader is being toured too. Chapter 5 carries seven scenes in 5,300 words (two lectures, a solo hour, a sign-in, a second lecture, a notice board, a notice), and although each is good, the chapter reads as a schedule. Chapter 8 opens its second week with a page of summary ("He learned the bells… He went to his lectures… He went to his supervised hours") in a movement whose brief asks for scenes. Lira's standings bout, the movement's third promised set piece, compresses its third and fourth exchanges into two paragraphs just when the reader has leaned forward. And across chapters 1–3 the sentences come so short and so evenly that a listener will hear the rhythm before the meaning.

**Which person matters.** Lira. Her arc is the emotional spine: the signature, the dot, the shoulders coming down an inch after the desk, the hit taken on purpose, and "I think I just let them both sit down. There's room on the bench." Brom's "I think I have a teacher" is the warmest line, and Hobb — a boy who can be furniture for an hour without minding — is the surprise. Cael is the camera and the ledger-keeper, and the movement is honest about that.

**Which event I expect next.** Edran's question coming back with paper attached; the second assessment; someone arriving who asks instead of decides; and, from the closing line of the packet, a registry index that has already matched a name. I expect Hobb to be thanked and Brom to ask his name badly.

**Would I continue.** Yes, without hesitation. The place is one I want to be in, which is what the brief asked this movement to achieve.

## 2. Two lenses

**A fluent thirteen-year-old.** Meaning lands on one pass everywhere; nothing requires rereading. The forms, provisions and tracks are a lot of paperwork for this reader, but the author keeps handing them physical objects — a blank board, a tag with a hole in it, a dot beside a name, a bell you learn by pitch — so the bureaucracy stays visible. Oona is this reader's way in, and her bluntness ("I hope you're not boring") is pitched exactly. The fights are trackable: who is where, what changed, what it cost. The reading level (FK 3.4) will feel young to a strong thirteen-year-old; the clipped cadence of chapters 1–3 may read as the book talking down.

**An adult genre reader.** The pleasure is institutional texture: a minor academy that prices clerical errors, a teacher who tells a class she might be wrong, a provision read for what it does not say. The ceiling argument and "panel speed" are the kind of system-thinking this genre rewards. This reader will notice the two-line sentence rhythm, the "he wrote it down that night" closers, and the "the way a hand goes back to a bruise" simile engine running hot in chapter 7, and will want Lira's bout to be as long as Brom's.

### 4.2 Findings

**F1 — Reveal pacing: Cael already holds the acquisition correlation (ch6, "Then he went through the binder").** The text reads: "And under all four, the thing that was not a fragment at all. How any of them had come to him. The fact that each had arrived after he had watched someone use their Path, closely, under real stakes." STATE_LEDGER (Cael — knowledge): *Believes: fragments "arrive" like weather. Does not know: that acquisition can be aimed.* BOOK_MAP §10 releases "observation is necessary but not sufficient; genuine engagement completes it" through Karis's hypothesis (M3), the nulls (M4) and the match (M5); the packet's M3 beat is "Cael's two days: weather or appetite." Here Cael states the observation-plus-stakes pattern as a fact in week one. It is never disclosed to anyone and never recorded, so this is **plan adherence / reveal pacing, not a canon disclosure** — but it pre-empts the discovery arc that two later movements are built on. Scope: keep "How any of them had come to him" and the "Never" decision; drop "under real stakes" and the causal "after he had watched someone use their Path, closely," in favour of what he actually knows (each has a source person; he does not know why; it feels like weather).

**F2 — Prynn's tenure (ch6, archive).** "Three times in thirty years." Canon anchor (BOOK_MAP §9.2, ch17): "Sixty years I've kept these shelves"; STATE_LEDGER: "sixty years with the shelves." Change thirty to sixty (or make the thirty refer to something else, e.g. since the provision was written).

Ch6 ("keeping his right side stiff… Right side still doesn't like leaning"), ch8 ("still moving carefully on his right side", "holding his right side") and the author report all say right.
