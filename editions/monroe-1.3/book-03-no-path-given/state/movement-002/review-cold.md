# Cold read — Book 3 "No Path Given", movement 2 (chapters 9–16)

**Kind of read:** simulated cold read. One model's read (Claude Fable 5.1), fresh context, manuscript only. No canon, plan, notes or drafting history were read. This is not an audience measurement and should not be used as one; it is one editorial opinion with located evidence.

**Coverage:** the eight chapter files supplied in declared order (chapter-09 through chapter-16), read complete. Only this coverage is claimed. The movement as supplied is complete; no chapter breaks off or is missing.

**Canon evidence:** none was supplied, so everything in the "Apparent inconsistencies" section is reported as apparent inconsistency inside the manuscript, not as a verified canon violation.

Locations below give chapter, an anchor phrase, and the line in the compiled prompt file (`prompt l.NNN`) for convenience.

## Orientation — arriving at chapter 9 mid-book

I am oriented within about four pages and never seriously lost. The manuscript hands a newcomer what they need by use rather than recap: Cael is an "observer" with a hollow-circle tag and no Path, enrolled at Greyvane under a demonstration provision, keeping a binder; Lira (re-certification dot, thrown out of Fenmark) and Brom (Iron Skin, Velmere, a mending side) are his two people; Edran holds the top of the standings; Quenna assesses him, Naveth is the provost, Prynn runs the archive, Wray teaches with Hobb as her "furniture"; Oona is a pre-Kindling child who has extracted a promise not to be lied to. The binder's four section heads in ch 12 (*Wind-adjacent. Lira.* etc., prompt l.910–916) tell me, without a lecture, that Cael's capabilities arrived as "notices" after particular people, which is enough to carry me.

Things I could not fully place, none of them blocking: the "afternoon at the north gate" where Edran asked a question (l.101); Hesk, Vell, Feryn and Reydan as names; the "refusal", the "stranger's note" and "Vell's sheaf" in the binder's back pocket (l.848); and the bracketed designation itself, which is never stated here (I infer *shattered* from l.1975). A reader who began at chapter 1 will have all of these. A reader starting here can still follow every scene.

## Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.**
- Immediately, at Lira's rumor market (ch 9, l.67–85). Three theories, one of which she invented on the stairs to test propagation, is a character and a world in half a page.
- Karis copying the standings board *from the bottom up* (l.89). The best character introduction in the movement: a trait shown as a method, before she has said a word.
- "The model is missing a category" (l.161) and the page of questions she will not ask, ending *If he says no, the finding is no. Record it and go home* (l.199–205). This is where I decided the movement had a spine.
- The archive exchange, "Why should I trust you?" / "You shouldn't. Not yet. Trust is a finding, not a premise" (ch 10, l.279–283).
- Clause four in Brom's hand, *He can say no to any one session without saying no to all of them. No reasons.* (ch 11, l.609), and Karis turning the declaration card around (l.647–654).
- "When you use it, does it feel borrowed?" (ch 12, l.768). The movement's central question, asked once, left alone.
- Coss's *Subject fourteen. Cooperative. Frightened, controlled it well.* (ch 13, l.1026) and the two true lines of the challenge that stop at "purpose" (l.1042–1056).
- Prynn: "You read *shall*." (ch 14, l.1308–1312). The family outside the Arbiter station (l.1272–1286).
- The whole Edran–Karis bout (ch 15, l.1550–1620): coins laid on the retreat, the lag on release, the crescent, the channels, the yield. Entirely trackable from the rail.
- Edran: "What I can't work out is what the board is for, if somebody can stand outside it" (ch 16, l.1849), and the petition pinned with brass tacks (l.1933–1949).

**Where it slipped.**
- Ch 9, the Karis retrospective (l.125–205). Roughly two thousand words of summary about a new point-of-view character — notebooks, a courier, a shield instructor, source-ranking, a copying house, a winter of killing hypotheses, the advisor, the parents — before she has a single scene in the present. Each paragraph is good; the block is long, and the forward thread (Edran watching Cael watch him) is dropped for it. The internal calendar also wobbles (see Apparent inconsistencies).
- Ch 11, the middle clauses (l.587–621). The negotiation earns its length overall, but the fifth-and-sixth stretch reads as a list being completed.
- Ch 14, the quiet week (l.1198–1246) and the routing code consulted twice with the same verdict (l.1354–1383). This is the only chapter where I noticed myself reading faster to get back to pressure.
- A register problem that accumulates rather than appearing in one place: Quenna, Naveth, Prynn, Karis, Lira, Brom, Hobb and the narrator all speak in polished aphorism ("Things with numbers on them get obeyed," "Ears are more honest," "The difference is patience," "Trust is a finding, not a premise," "Processes never get tired. People do"). Individually these are pleasures; across eight chapters the cast begins to sound like one wise voice. Brom (barley, four words, the steward) and Oona ("rude by accident") escape it; Lira escapes it less than she should, given she is the one person here who says things she has not finished deciding.

**Which person matters.** Karis, as the engine; Coss, as the weather coming up the road; Edran, who turns from antagonist into the most honest questioner in the building. Lira remains the emotional anchor, and the two best lines about Karis are hers ("She keeps asking properly, and then you've answered before you've decided to," l.1512; "That's why I'd like it not to be us," l.1649).

**Which event I expect next.** Cael telling Edran, in some public form, that there is no Path to hide — the movement ends on "I think I'll have to" (l.1989). Behind that, Coss arriving at the gate with a challenge packet built on *purpose* rather than wording, and the question of who sits above the ruled bar (*Field restricted*, l.956) and whether the unmatched routing code (l.1358) is that person's fingerprint. Also planted and waiting: Karis's unnamed paired partner at Ternhall (l.137, l.193, l.654), and the quarter-breath that her off hand and his read share (l.133, l.351, l.1745).

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. I would turn to chapter 17 to see what Cael says to Edran and whether he says it before Coss's coach arrives.

## Apparent inconsistencies and boundary checks (manuscript-internal; no canon supplied)

**Calendar (apparent inconsistency).** Ch 9: Marlowe's joke is "late winter" (l.143); Karis takes "three weeks" and "four months" (l.149), then "spent a winter killing the entries one at a time" (l.153), and the last ledger "arrived at the start of the summer" (l.159). A joke in late winter cannot be followed by four months of work and then a winter and then that summer. Ch 11 then calls it "the middle of last winter" (l.527). Ch 13 has Coss describing the Iron Rank Eight bout as "fought only a few weeks ago" (l.1002) and the carter saying "end of the season" (l.1008), while Karis had that ledger entry "at the start of the summer" (l.159) and Greyvane's term is in autumn with leaves yellowing (l.1759). Weeks versus months. One calendar needs to hold across ch 9, 11 and 13.

**Knowledge boundary (apparent error).** Ch 16, l.1745: Karis says, "I timed how far ahead of Hobb's strikes you moved, at the sittings, from what you told the panel and what I could see from the stool." The sittings are closed; ch 14 has her waiting in the passage outside the hall and refusing to "look through the keyhole" (l.1186). She has no stool at a sitting and no access to what he told the panel. The intended meaning (she timed the framework at the first session, the same framework he runs for the panel) is recoverable, but the sentence as written puts her somewhere she was not, at the exact moment the chapter is showcasing her rigor.

**Age arithmetic (minor).** Prynn's eyes are "seventy-odd years old" (l.1298) and she has certified copies "over sixty years" (l.1318), at an academy that is "sixty years" old (l.1198). That has her certifying archive copies from roughly age ten. Possibly intended as a joke about Prynn; reads as a slip.

**Physical state (consistent).** Brom's side: "nearly mended" (l.694), "fading yellow" (l.906), "Mended?" "Mostly." (l.900). Cael's left wrist swollen and wrapped the next day (l.1464, l.1474). Brom's left wrist purple at the pairing after Cael returned half the push into it (l.1450, l.1518; Brom's left palm was on Cael's forearm, l.1430). Cael's right shoulder locking (l.1454) matches the Compression cost "across the collarbones" elsewhere. All hold.

**Power limits (consistent as stated).** Karis's declaration (l.649): channel joins two standing points, up to eight paces, fails if either point fails. The bout respects this: she keeps every point holding before joining (l.1612). Her "never more than nine points at once" (l.1638) is Cael's count, not a stated limit; fine. Cael's Compression: a quarter caught at a fixed price, then half at double (l.1432, l.1462); he states the rule himself. Nothing here exceeds what the text has established.

**Reserved disclosures (flag for the owner, not an error).** Ch 16, l.1969–1973: Cael tells Oona, with "half the academy at his back" (l.1961), that the faculty is not hiding his Path because "There isn't one to hide." That is a public statement of the thing the petition is built on. The text treats it as a promise kept to Oona, and the chapter closes on him deciding to tell Edran, so this may be exactly the plan. If the plan reserves that disclosure for a later scene, this is the line that spends it early. I cannot tell from the manuscript which it is; I am reporting the location.

**Uncheckable from supplied text.** Ch 14, l.1170: "Last month there had been seven [words]. This month there were six." The first sitting is not in these chapters, so I cannot confirm the seven.

**Rank versus standing (not an error, but a question a genre reader asks).** Karis is Iron Rank Three (l.77); Edran is Iron Rank Two (l.1935) and sat above her on the board until the pairing. The text treats standings as bout-earned and rank as Arbiter-issued, which is coherent, but no sentence says so near the board, and a reader may briefly wonder why the higher rank started "second".

## Repair brief

Three priorities, in order. Keep the developed action; nothing below asks to shorten the bout.

### 1. Chapter 9 — the Karis retrospective, and the calendar that runs through it

- **Location:** ch 9, from "Karis Dellenmoor had kept two notebooks" (l.125) to "Record it and go home." (l.205); with the dependent lines at ch 11 l.527 and ch 13 l.1002.
- **Observed issue:** about two thousand words of summary narration about a new point-of-view character before she has a present-tense scene, during which the chapter's live thread (Edran openly charting Cael, l.99–121) is set down. Inside the block, the seasons do not add up (late winter → three weeks + four months → "a winter" → start of summer), and ch 11 and ch 13 each give a different anchor for the same events.
- **Effect on the reader:** the thirteen-year-old lens skims to "The model is missing a category"; the adult lens stops to do the arithmetic and loses trust in the timeline right where the book is asking it to trust a character whose whole identity is careful dating. A mid-book reader also feels the movement change books for a dozen pages.
- **Proposed scope:** tighten the research passage (l.141–163) by roughly a quarter — the copying house, the mother's cousin and the ink that changes colour can be one paragraph, not three — and choose one calendar, then conform the three anchor phrases (l.143/153/159, l.527, l.1002). Consider letting one small present-tense beat from Greyvane interrupt the retrospective at its midpoint so the live thread is not out of sight for the whole block. Do not cut the two-notebook premise, the off-hand entry (l.133), the advisor's lamp (l.179–181), or the mother's one question (l.185–191).
- **Strength to preserve:** the page of questions she will not ask, ending *Whether I may ask* and *If he says no, the finding is no. Record it and go home.* (l.199–205). That page is the movement's thesis and should stay exactly as written.

### 2. Chapter 14 — the quiet week and the routing code consulted twice

- **Location:** ch 14, "The days after the request arrived passed" (l.1198) through Karis's handwriting page (l.1246); and the two routing-code consultations, Naveth (l.1354–1368) and Prynn (l.1372–1383).
- **Observed issue:** the week of waiting is narrated as tension ("he was doing it the way a person in an unfamiliar house at night counts every sound", l.1200) rather than dramatised, and then three adults in a row tell Cael how to feel about it (Quenna, Karis, later Naveth). The routing code — a good plant — is examined by two experts who reach the same "probably nothing", and the "shoulders came down" beat is used at l.1190 and again at l.1222.
- **Effect on the reader:** this is the one chapter in eight where both lenses read faster to get back to pressure. The double consultation also spotlights the plant harder than a plant should be lit; a genre reader now knows the code matters, which takes some of the pleasure out of its eventual payoff.
- **Proposed scope:** keep one routing-code scene. Prynn's is the better one ("Keep the page. Don't build anything on it," l.1380), and Naveth's dismissal can become two lines reported inside it. Cut or compress one of the three reassurance conversations; Karis's *Handwriting, size of, under load* (l.1232) is the most original and should be the survivor. Remove the duplicate shoulders beat. Net effect is a chapter perhaps a tenth shorter with the same events.
- **Strength to preserve:** the family outside the Arbiter station (l.1272–1286) and the certified copy read aloud to Prynn, "You read *shall*" (l.1302–1326). These two scenes are the chapter's reason to exist; nothing in them should move.

### 3. Chapter 16 — the pen-stop explanation puts Karis somewhere she was not

- **Location:** ch 16, l.1745: "I timed how far ahead of Hobb's strikes you moved, at the sittings, from what you told the panel and what I could see from the stool."
- **Observed issue:** the sittings are closed to her (ch 14, l.1186), there is no stool at a sitting, and she has no access to what he told the panel. The intended sense — she timed the framework at the first session, which is the same framework he runs for the panel — is recoverable but not what the sentence says.
- **Effect on the reader:** the attentive reader catches it, and catches it at the precise moment the chapter is demonstrating that Karis only writes what she can see. One sentence undercuts the finding it is delivering.
- **Proposed scope:** one or two sentences. For example: she timed how far ahead of Hobb's strikes he moved at the first session, "when you ran it for me at the speed you run it for the panel", and today how far ahead of Brom's weight he called it. No other change to the scene.
- **Strength to preserve:** the pen that stops "so that I don't write down what I expect instead of what I see" (l.1745), clause five invoked as the reason she will not write *why* (l.1749), and the closing binder line *Her pen has started stopping. Not when I stop. When something fits.* (l.1805). That is the best single beat in the chapter.
