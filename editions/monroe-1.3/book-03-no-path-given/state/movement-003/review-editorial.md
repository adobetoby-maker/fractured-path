## 1. Unscored reader response

Interest caught at once: "He was the last one into assembly, and everybody in the room knew why" and Lira's two fingers held up "where only he could see them". The council behind Naveth's shut door is the best procedural scene the edition has produced — three adults with three different jobs, each refusing to lie, converting a trap into a test — and Quenna naming the losing case out loud ("the petition will stand on that page for as long as the page exists") makes the eight days matter before a punch is thrown. The second half of ch17, where Edran refuses to be told, turned the movement for me: the opponent becomes a person with a principle, and Oona's "It's not a words one" lands it.

Ch18 kept me with it through sport rather than dread (the tout, "That's the right answer"), and the seam discovery is proper detective work — the place "where the precision itself cost something". The Compression "drop" is a small, strange, good scene. Interest slipped slightly in the second Prynn visit (the record confirming what the first visit already said) and the sixth-night scene with Lira, which is lovely but static.

Ch19 is the movement's set piece and it earns its length: four exchanges, each with a different problem, a crowd that reacts in four different registers, a cutaway that reads one beat ahead so the reader is told what to look for and then sees it. The fourth exchange — Edran narrowing his own gap in the time it takes to walk back to a mark, and the gap coming up thin — is the kind of mid-fight adaptation the brief asked for. Wray's ruling is the right length. The three still points at the end are the right last image.

Where it slipped: tracking which forearm carried which structure. I lost the arms twice in exchange 2 and once in exchange 3, and the rule stated in exchange 4 ("Glass could not form on a forearm that still had glitter on it") is not the rule ch18 taught. The reader who wants to see the seam close can't quite, because the arm bookkeeping shifts under them. This is fixable without touching a single beat.

Ch20 gives the aftermath its due. "Whatever you are, it's real" / "I withdraw the petition" is placed and paced exactly; Naveth at the window ("the dullest paragraph I've ever wanted to read") is the movement's warmest scene; Gerda's "Posted. Answered. Withdrawn." is a small perfect thing. Ch21's hypothesis scene is excellent: Karis asking Lira's leave because clause five is hers, Lira keeping her finger on a page she has stopped reading, the three observables set out "hedged exactly as far as the evidence needed and not a word further", relief arriving before dread. Ch22's backward read of the binder and the Oona scene (frightened of looking at a thirteen-year-old, then relieved she has "nothing yet to take", then hating the relief) are brave and clean for the Reader Standard. Ch23's wall is the renewed covenant it needs to be — "It isn't generous. It's arithmetic" — and Hesk's letter paying off the ch2 letter ("I am glad of the third") is the sort of long-range callback that makes a series feel kept.

Who matters: Edran most, this movement — he gains dimension in every scene he is in. Then Karis, then Lira (the cutaway and the wall). What I expect next: Fenmark's file; Lira's two bouts; the first attempt to aim. Would I continue? Yes, without hesitation.

Where my attention flagged as a reader, independent of the metrics: the clipped two-to-eight-word narration between speeches in ch17, ch20 and ch23 — many paragraphs of "He sat down." / "Cael said nothing." / "She went in." — gives the quiet scenes a staccato texture that ch19 and ch22 do not have. The calendar also wobbles in ch21–23 (see §4): by the chapter's own sequence of Tuesdays and Thursdays, the Wednesday hypothesis cannot be in week eight, and Cael's log says it is.

### 4.2 Defects (verified against the manuscript and canon)

**D1 — "Three weeks ago" ×2 (ch18:151 Karis; ch19:173 Lira).** The Karis–Edran bout was fought on the first day of week 6 (ch15:97: "The pairing went up on the first morning of the sixth week, and by the fifth bell…"); the petition went up two or three days later (ch16); Karis speaks on the evening of the third day of the six (about a week after her bout); Lira speaks at the exhibition (eight days after the posting, about ten or eleven days after the bout). "Three weeks" is wrong by roughly two weeks both times. The packet's "Edran lost first place to Karis two weeks ago" was itself stale against M2's prose; the author appears to have followed the packet and rounded up. Fix: "a week ago" / "last week" (ch18); "ten days ago" or "not two weeks ago" (ch19).

**D2 — The ch21–23 calendar cannot be week eight as written.** Ch21 states the sequence: the chalk line on "the first Tuesday after the exhibition" (ch21:35); Quenna's post permission and the read-back on "the second Tuesday after the exhibition" (ch21:29, ch21:461 in prompt numbering); "She almost asked him something on the Thursday" after that (ch21:97, "Through the felt, twice more"); then "On Wednesday evening Brom gave back volume six" (ch21:165) and the hypothesis follows that same evening; ch22's Thursday (clause four) and Friday (lecture, rail, wall) follow; ch23 is the next day. The exhibition fell on the eighth day after a posting early in week 6, i.e. week 7, day 4 or 5. Counting forward, the second Tuesday after it is in week 9, the Thursday is week 9, and the Wednesday of the hypothesis is week 10 at the earliest. Brom's own margin ("DAYS 1–24", ch21:183; he began counting the day Wray wrote in the notebook, week 6 day 2) independently puts that Wednesday at about week 9 day 4. Against this, ch23:297 logs "*Week eight*"; ch23:211 says the petition papers came down "two days ago, after their week on the board" (which, counted from the withdrawal's posting on exhibition day, lands ch23 at the end of week 8); and BOOK_MAP §3 anchors "Karis's hypothesis; Cael's two days; the covenant on the wall" in week 8, with the null series binding from week 9 (B4 Ch9). So the chapter's internal week-count and its stated week disagree, and the internal count crosses an anchor. One of two repairs: (a) hold the anchor — make the read-back the first Tuesday after the exhibition, put the chalk line at the first session after the exhibition (the Tuesday morning after the Edran-rebuild scene is already "the next morning"), and move the *Not asked* beat into that Tuesday's hour (near its end, "Thank you. That's the hour."), so Wednesday (hypothesis), Thursday (clause four), Friday (wall) and Saturday (letter, calendar) all sit in week 8 — the "twice more through the felt" line compresses into the same session; or (b) accept week 9 for the hypothesis and change "Week eight", the "two days ago" board line, and the M4 packet's opening week. I recommend (a): it costs a few sentences and keeps B4's hard number undisturbed. Either way the planner must rule; the prose currently says both.

**D3 — Lira's reassessment "nine days off" (ch23:217, 231, 251) crosses the §3 anchor.** BOOK_MAP §3 places "Lira's formal reassessment and the Fenmark file" in weeks 8–9. Nine days from the last day of week 8 is week 10, day 2 (and later still under D2(b)). The packet says the review is "three weeks posted" (honored at ch23:213) but does not fix the gap. A one-word change ("five days off"; Gerda's "Six for me" / "Six days") keeps it inside week 9 and leaves M4 room to open with Fenmark's file. If the planner wants the reassessment in week 10, M4's packet must say so.

**D4 — Arm bookkeeping in the bout (ch19:57, 131, 135, 145, 217, 225, 281).** Exchange 1 opens with the shell on Edran's left forearm and the edge rising on his right "a breath behind it" — two structures standing at once. Exchange 2 opens the same way ("shell up on the left, edge rising on the right", :131), then two sentences later the second structure is "the edge on the left forearm now, the first shell burst and gone and the second already up out of its glitter" (:135) — so the second is on the left, and the right-hand edge from :131 has gone unaccounted — then "the third edge came up on the right" (:145), and Cael's eyes are on the bare left forearm (also Karis's chart, ch20). Exchange 3 has the shell on the left, the edge on the right "up out of the glitter of the shell" (:217), the second bursting and leaving "Edran's right forearm… bare" (:225) — so there the sequence runs left → right → right. Exchange 4 then explains the seam as "Glass could not form on a forearm that still had glitter on it. So Edran had stopped forming on the same forearm" (:281). That is not the rule ch18 taught (the third "had to be made from nothing, in the middle of everything", because the second was called in a hurry — a rule about attention, not about glitter on a forearm), and it does not fit exchanges 1–3, where structures already alternate arms. The reader cannot track the seam closing because the arms move under them, and the "so simple that Cael almost laughed" fix reads as something three years of mornings would have found if the rule were really about forearms. Repair scope: choose one convention (I suggest: the pre-formed shell on one arm; the second forms on the other arm as the first bursts; the third normally forms on the arm the second just left, after its glitter clears — that is the sliver; Edran's fix is to start the third on the free arm *before* the second bursts, with half his attention, hence thin) and apply it consistently to all four exchanges and to Karis's chart line; drop or rewrite the "glitter on a forearm" sentence at :281 so the explanation matches ch18 ("a structure called while the second still stood was a structure called with half a mind" already does the work). No beat, touch, ruling or line of speech needs to change.
