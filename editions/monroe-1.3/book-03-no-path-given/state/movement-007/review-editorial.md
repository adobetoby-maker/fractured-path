## 1. Reader response (unscored)

Interest caught at Karis's "A second reader who reports as she goes isn't a second reader. She's an echo with a delay" (ch47) and held through the yard: "Institutions re-use people" lands because the three on the wall have each met one of the four. It caught hardest in three places: Prynn losing on *unclassified* and then taking the one thing she could have ("One line, one box, one signature, at the door"); the deposition's "No." with nine dashes after it, told twice, once from the chair and once from the recording desk; and the Saturday count, when box seven goes out past Prynn's knees with the register in it and Karis's hand goes to her coat without her knowing.

It slipped in three places: the second half of Havel's coach ride (ch47, the Yorlan portrait is told rather than shown, and the two gate questions are better than the paragraph before them); the dating chain in Karis's cutaway (ch50, the weather log's double-dated line is a fine idea explained one sentence too many times); and the back-to-back form texts in Havel C (ch52: the return and the referral are both entered word for word, and the second is half the first again — necessary for P16, but the reader has to read a registry sheet twice).

The person who matters this movement is Prynn, and after her Karis. The event I expect next is Coss reading "No" into the first sitting in his own voice, and the register not coming back up the hill. I would continue.

**Thirteen-year-old reader.** The deposition and the Saturday count are gripping at any age: short questions, a boy who answers in one word, a woman on a stool in a doorway. Lira is the way in everywhere ("I don't want sorry. I want to know who"; "I'll still not hit anybody. I'd like that noted somewhere"). The long clause fight in ch48 will test patience — fifteen hundred words of statute read aloud — but it is rescued by Prynn's "Every amendment is a confession" and by a reader who has been taught what a scar is in M6. The Havel sections are the hardest for this reader: three blocks of administrative procedure whose point is that nothing happens. The "Not only me this time" entry earns them.

**Adult genre reader.** This is the series' long game paid in small coin: a documents war with a real duel in it, a found word kept unused for a reason the reader can follow, and a recording officer building something one dry line at a time. The Yorlan formula and the "panel notes it" business are the kind of procedural texture this reader comes for; so is Coss refusing the lending book because "it would be a cheat." The one place this reader will feel the plan showing is ch51's decline, which keeps the source's beat order under new words (§5).

## 3. Canon, state, limits and reserved disclosures — rulings on the flagged items

Each item below gives the ruling first, then the evidence.

**3.1 Ilsev's query goes down the hill by courier Wednesday evening and returns Saturday afternoon (ch49, ch52).** *Accept as a plan deviation; record it in the ledger; no repair.* Canon does not fix the timing. Source ch18 has the return "before the end of the hour"; the packet's cutaway is "arrival, the deposition from the recording desk, and the afternoon," which implies the same afternoon; B4 ch18 (l. 87 ff.) says she "had filed that referral in a room at a small academy in a cold month, in the second hour of a review she had conducted the way she conducted every review," which reads most naturally as the same session but can bear a multi-day review. Nothing in B6 ch16 dates the return. The author's reason (a hill academy cannot get a registry answer in an hour) is sound, and the three-block shape gives Havel his best beat (the courier "two days before Havel had expected him"; "It'll come back before we sit. Or it won't come back at all."). Two things for the ledger and M8: the referral goes down on Monday's courier (ch52), and the return reached the hill on Saturday, so by B4's "fourteen months" nothing has come back down. Flag for the bible pass, not for this author: B4's quoted referral carries "of the preceding year," which BOOK_MAP P16 omits and which makes no sense in a referral filed the same week; the edition's form is the right one.

**3.2 Havel and the designation by name in the Compact's routing papers (ch49).** *Accept; consistent with canon at every layer.* It is Ilsev, not Havel, who finds the name "in the priority field of the routing history of the records request of the autumn"; Havel reads it in her query because "entering a thing word for word meant reading it." That is the source's own placement (ch18: "in the routing history of a records request processed months before the challenge… sitting in the priority field") and B4 ch18's ("appearing in the priority field of subject file"). It is reconciled with this edition's M2 canon — Greyvane's transmittal shows only "a short string of letters and numbers broken by a slash," never "Level 4" — by locating the full field in the Compact's own routing history at her clearance, which Greyvane never saw. B2 ch15 has Havel reading the designation by name in the file's status header as a four-year officer, so his knowing the name is canon too; the edition keeps him from naming it in his own memory (ch47: "a designation that the manual reserved for something nobody could have mistaken the boy for"), which is the right restraint. His inference that Coss once asked is true in canon (BOOK_MAP §1: Coss's unanswered upward note) and is kept private and unproved. Nothing here reveals origin; the Book 6 plant is advanced exactly as §10 permits.

**3.3 Brom's page records the third exchange, not a loss (ch53).** *Correct; the source eve was wrong and the edition fixes it.* Source B2 ch11: "Bout to Brom… Four exchanges"; the third exchange is where "my read went to where he was and found the floor" has its canon in Vell's "Atypical movement, third exchange"; the log that night reads "Lost to Brom, four exchanges." Source B3 ch20's "because I lost that fight" contradicts Book 2; the edition's "I won that bout. Saying what happened in the third exchange doesn't make me look one bit better" is the canon-true form and keeps the protected line's "trying his best to beat it" honest. One slip inside the same speech: Brom says "Then the next day we sat on the floor and you told me about the window that wasn't a window." In B2 ch11 they sat on the floor the same night, immediately after the bout ("I want to know how you did that thing in the third exchange"; the window hypothesis is explained there, l. 211), and they had already been arguing over breakfast for two weeks. Line fix F2.

**3.4 UNBOUND copied from the margin of Vell's oldest circuit ledger (ch51, ch53).** *Correct.* B2 ch16: the earliest surviving volume, "roughly two-thirds of the way through," a marginal note; the edition's "A bout, a fighter whose name had gone, and a note in the margin beside it" and the log's "the margin of Vell's oldest ledger in Ardenmere" match. **3.5 The five packet lines re-composed to clear the 8-word gate.** *Correct procedure; owner's call on restoring any of them.* All five packet lines (Prynn's "Their code forgot to write the word…"; "you're the second source now; act like one"; "The remedy for a hole in the law is to write the law"; "Shelf them yourself. **3.6 The deposition's "No." and "four weeks" (ch49).** *Accept; both truthful, both consistent, both to be carried into M8.* "Four weeks" is exact: the Ember point was acquired on the Thursday of week 16 and shown at D6 on the Thursday of week 20 (STATE_LEDGER M5, M6). **3.8 Karis puts her conclusion copy into the binder herself (ch53).** *Character and terms inconsistency; line fix F5.* "I put it in the back of your binder this morning while you were at breakfast… I didn't look."
