# Cold read — Book 4, Movement 8 (Chapters 51–56)

**Kind of read:** simulated fresh-context cold read by Claude Fable (review seat). Manuscript only; no BOOK_MAP, STATE_LEDGER, packets, source chapters, author report or other books were opened. This is not a real audience measurement and I have no drafting history for this movement. The six chapters were read in order from `manuscript/chapter-51.md` through `chapter-56.md` and checked against the text compiled into `review-cold-prompt.md`; they are identical apart from trailing separators. Coverage claimed: these six files and nothing else.

Where I say "a listener" below I mean someone hearing the text read aloud once, without the page to go back to, because that is the test the wrapper asks for.

---

## Unscored reader response

Interest caught on the first line and did not let go through the final. "The hip woke before she did" is a whole situation in six words, and the two-stage landing is explained in the body before it is explained in rules, so by the time the adjudicating instructor reads the terms I already know what the bout is about: a four-minute man and a three-burst woman, each refusing to fight the other's number. The sealed sheets under the sand-glass are the best quiet idea in the movement and they pay three times (the yard, the ceremony, Gault's office).

Interest slipped twice. Once in the middle of calibration night (Chapter 53), where Karis's slate is doing load-bearing arithmetic that I could not quite reconstruct, and once in the last page of Chapter 55, where the homecoming happens twice and Karis is told "renewed" as news after she has already made a joke about it. Both are fixable seams, not failures of invention.

The person who matters is Cael, but Lira is the heart of the first half, and the movement is honest enough to let her be: her grin at the closed seam, "Third one someday", the thumbnail marks in the chair wax. Brom is the surprise; he is given the best single piece of advice in the book ("Like somebody with something in his pocket") and the best refusal to perform (the hard chair, "That was horrible").

The event I expect next: what the Archmarshal actually wrote. The movement counts his pen-strokes (one on the empty tier, two in the wing) and then lets him leave without looking at Cael. I also expect Lira's Arbiter evaluation to come back, and the Mire instructor's post-mortem on the notch. I would continue.

### Lens: a fluent thirteen-year-old

Would love the fight, especially Brom going straight at the hip and Lira landing on it on purpose, and the frame breaking with the clerk's brown shoes in the way. Would get "He didn't ask" at the end of Chapter 56, because the set-up (Brom: "put it down like a full plate") is simple enough to remember. Would drift during Ilsev's reading of the yard as a document and during the brackets on the slate, and would not track what sits second in the posted order. Would ask, at the end of Chapter 55, "Wait, didn't Karis already know?" Would count the elevens and start to giggle.

### Lens: an adult genre reader

Admires the restraint: the registrar writing the result at the foot of a sealed sheet and blotting it on his cuff; Gault's "That is what a real semester looks like"; the Archmarshal's three animals in similar coats. Notices that the movement's governing pleasure is procedure made dramatic, and that it keeps its promise in every room. Also notices the number pile-up, the duplicated arrival, and that two of the eleven questions are dispatched in a single sentence after nine have been staged in full.

## The four measured rooms

The wrapper asks three things of each room: are the rules clear to a listener, do the tallies add up on the page, are the speakers always clear.

### Room 1 — The final (Chapters 51–52)

**Rules.** Clear, and given in one sentence by the instructor: four exchanges, each on the glass, each to the cleaner touch or even; more touches after four wins; stoppage, yield or panel can end it. The two clocks are explained in the body of the chapter before the glass turns. The ladder's "a blow on a set guard is not clean" is stated the moment it matters. One rule is never stated and a listener will feel the gap: *what ends an exchange.* Exchange one ends on the instructor's hand going up at a non-scoring blow; two, three and four end on a floor. None ends on the glass. It works by inference, but "each on the table's glass" sets up an expectation the bout never uses.

**Tallies.** They add up. Even / Iron Skin / Wind / Wind gives "Two touches to one" as called. Bursts: none named in exchange one; first and second in exchange two ("Two gone"); third in exchange three ("Three, that's three"); fourth in exchange four. The registrar's "Ten bouts unbeaten" plus the final gives "Eleven bouts unbeaten." Two wobbles for the ear:

- "four minutes of iron and eleven seconds more had put his turn on the fifth count" (Chapter 52, exchange four) uses the length of exchange three (4:11 by Cael's count) as if it were Brom's whole clock, though Chapter 51 says both clocks "started the moment the two of them walked out" and exchange one ran "most of two minutes". Either the clock is cumulative hard contact, in which case the number is wrong, or it restarted, in which case the earlier sentence is. A listener who has been doing the sums, as the chapter invites, will stop.
- "touch" means both a single contact ("Wind, touch to the ribs") and the exchange award ("Two touches to one"). The instructor's reading makes it survivable, but the rib slap is a touch that does not count, and the page does not say why in those words.

**Speakers.** Clear throughout, with one small exception: in Chapter 51 after exchange two, "Two gone," she said / "Two gone." / "How much does she have?" The unattributed echo is Cael, but a listener may assign it to Karis and then lose the next line's speaker for a beat.

Observation to keep: the whole exchange-four sequence (eleven seconds, four reasons it was not the end) is the clearest piece of fight-writing in the movement and should not be touched.

### Room 2 — Calibration night (Chapter 53)

**Rules.** Karis's slate is the rulebook, and most of it is clear: a floor and a roof; "On the board, five free, or six"; "weight four with you clear at least five times in six, or weight three with you clear every time"; the plate "doesn't move by so much as a graduation"; ON THE NUMBER. Brom's rule (let it cost, visibly) and Lira's rule (read one person in the gaps) are both plain.

**Tallies.** Here is where I could not make the page add up. Karis "drew six lines" and names them: one board, two frame read-only, three combined, four plate, "the last two are the first two over again". Then: "This time the read stops being a trial of its own and gets carried inside the third and the cold runs. The fifth slot is new." So the read-only trial is removed, the two cold runs collapse into one slot, and a new fifth is added. That leaves board / ? / combined / plate / new fifth / cold run — five named items and an empty second slot. The next morning Gault says "The six stand as posted otherwise" and accepts the Ember "Second, then", which only makes sense if the posted second slot was already a known hole or a known trial. A listener cannot tell which, and so the cleverness of Cael choosing *where* to place his one surprise lands on a slot the page never showed him. The drift count (eleven by thumbnail, four caught, all in the gaps) is clean and lands well.

**Speakers.** One pronoun stumble: after Brom's line "Like at the post, before the delegation came," the next paragraph begins "The nearest chair-back took his grip." For a listener, "his" is Brom until the following sentence proves it is Cael.

Observation to keep: the scared-plate rehearsal ("Now give me a scared one") and Karis's discovery that the danger was under the floor, not over the roof. That is the turn of the whole night and it is written exactly right.

### Room 3 — The evaluation (Chapters 54–55)

**Rules.** Gault's three rules are given in his own words, the order change is entered aloud, the interval rule is stated the night before and used on the day. The chalk-dust tray is explained before it is read. Clear.

**Tallies.** Mostly clean, with a cluster of small misfires that together will make a careful listener distrust the arithmetic the room is built on:

- Board: eight runs, "Six free", seventh paid, eighth wide; Gault's note says "Seven runs within the baseline's spread", which matches. Good.
- Combined: eleven of twelve at weight four. Cael calls this "sitting on the floor of them as near as made no difference", but Karis's floor was five in six, which is ten of twelve. Eleven is a call above the floor. One word fixes it.
- Plate: four, low, both times. Exact. Good.
- Gault, after trial four: "This wing measures six things. Five of them have moved and one hasn't." At that moment four trials have run; the cold runs have not. The sentence is true by the end of the sitting (board, read, combined, cold board, cold read moved; plate did not) but it is said before the instruments have said it, which is precisely the thing Gault would not do.
- Fifth on the reserve frame: nine runs, eight clear, "The last two are the hip" (eighth late but clear, ninth lost). The dust agrees. Good.
- Glance tally after the fault: Gault, Mire, Ash, counsel, Havel, Ilsev are listed, then "seven people in that room had needed the second look". Six are named. The clerk is the seventh but is not in the list.
- Ledger, day twenty-two: "Drifts: two" and then, a few lines down, "Three hours under the Compact's four best chairs, and it never flickered." The same entry contradicts itself.
- Karis's joke: "four working legs and three working arms." On the page it is the other way round: Lira one leg, Brom one leg and one hand, Cael one leg and one shoulder, which is three legs and four arms.
- Brom's right hand is strapped to the second knuckle from the first line of Chapter 54, and nothing in Chapters 52–53 says how. He hit a thick book sixteen times and then threw from the wall all evening, so the reader can guess, but the page never says it.

**Speakers.** Clear. The Mire instructor's exit speech ("I'm not a magister. I'm the woman who'll be taking that frame apart instead of eating supper") is the best unlabelled voice in the movement.

**Structure.** The last page of Chapter 55 arrives home twice. First: the stair, Brom with the ice, "like the survivors of a coach accident", Karis counting legs and arms and naming Cael "a renewed enrollee", the tune joke. Then, a paragraph later: "In the common room Karis sat with a book on her knee, open at the page it had shown since the second bell. She shut it. 'Well?' He told her, and at *renewed* her eyes closed for a moment." Karis hears the result as news after she has already joked about it, and the page she has been staring at "since the second bell" contradicts her having just been the only one fit to carry a tray. The Ember payoff (the third word going under *a spark* and *a letting-go*, "That's the last one") lives in the second version and must survive; the first version carries the better comedy. A listener will hear a scene restart.

Observation to keep: the clerk's brown shoes as the fourth of four pieces, the quarter-burst paid into the hip, "Because the clerk was standing where I'd have landed," and the fourth chair's single look. Nothing in that run should be shortened.

### Room 4 — The interview (Chapter 56)

**Rules.** The clearest room of the four. Bracken reads the subsection and explains both halves; the counsel states the sitting's rules in six sentences (any question may be refused, "declined" and nothing more, half an hour, the house closes it). Five chairs in twelve by fourteen, counted. The book kept by the house's clerk, and why.

**Tallies.** Eleven questions announced; eleven delivered, with follow-ups kept distinguishable from new questions. The clerk's column reads twenty-two minutes after the ninth; "the remaining eight minutes are yours"; finished at twenty-nine. Adds up. The only cost is craft, not arithmetic: questions ten and eleven are summarised in one sentence after nine have been staged, and the fall-off is audible.

**Speakers.** Clear throughout. The Archmarshal's lines are unattributed by design and the design holds; the room is small enough that "the man" is never ambiguous. Brom's rehearsal as the Archmarshal ("be somebody nobody knows what he sounds like") is a gift to the ear because it teaches the listener the silence before the real one arrives.

Observation to keep: the sixth question, the brace, "Records of what? Ask it," and the chin dipping over a receipt. The prepared refusal is never spent and the page knows that is the point.

## Apparent inconsistencies (no canon supplied)

Reported as apparent inconsistencies within the supplied text, not verified canon violations.

- **Brom's clock.** Ch 51: both clocks "started the moment the two of them walked out"; Ch 51 end of exchange one: "His four minutes had begun"; Ch 52: "four minutes of iron and eleven seconds more" taken from exchange three alone. Three different start points for one clock.
- **Physical state.** Brom's strapped right hand (Ch 54 onward) has no on-page cause. Cael's hip, shoulder and headache are tracked precisely; the hand is not.
- **Power limits.** The landing beat is stated as never shortened by anyone (Ch 52, Ch 56); the quarter-burst (Ch 55) does not shorten the beat but refuses the burst halfway, and the ledger and the interview both deny it as a repeatable skill. Consistent on the page. The read/hold "one cup" rule is kept consistently from Ch 51 (let up on purpose) through Ch 53 (found still up) to Ch 54–55 (on its stub).
- **Knowledge boundaries.** Ilsev knows where Cael sits and does not look; the Archmarshal cites "a report on your file" for the notation habit; nobody in the interview room knows anything they were not shown. Consistent. Lira's paper reads "Rank Two" (Ch 51, Havel) while Fenmark wrote "Rank One" when she was fourteen; the certificate is two years old, so a reclassification is implied but never said, and a listener may pause.
- **Reserved disclosures.** Feryn's fragment stays off the plate with its reason given; the wing-paper slip is burned and Karis lays the question down; the sixth question is not followed. All handled on the page.
- **Internal contradiction.** Ch 55 ledger: "Drifts: two" against "it never flickered" in the same entry.

## Repair brief

Three priorities, in order. None of them touches the developed action; the final, the notch and the interview should be left at their present length.

### Priority 1 — The doubled homecoming (Chapter 55, from "They arranged themselves in the common room" to "That's the last one")

- **Observed issue:** the party arrives in the common room twice. In the first arrival Karis names Cael "a renewed enrollee" and makes the tray joke; in the second she is discovered alone with a book "since the second bell", asks "Well?", and closes her eyes at *renewed* as if hearing it for the first time. "And the column?" is also unglossed; a listener does not know it means the thread in the glass straw.
- **Effect on the reader:** a scene restart at the emotional landing of the whole evaluation; the Ember payoff, which is the quietest and best beat in the chapter, arrives inside a continuity error and is half-lost to it.
- **Proposed scope:** merge into one scene. Keep the stair, Brom's ice, the coach-accident tableau and the tune exchange; let Karis go for the tray and come back, or simply turn to Cael, and ask "And the glass? You said you'd tell me." Cut the second opening ("In the common room Karis sat with a book… 'Well?' He told her, and at *renewed*…"). Keep the third word under the two, the short line, and "That's the last one. I can feel that it is." Also reverse the legs-and-arms joke so it counts (three legs, four arms) or make Karis miscount on purpose and be corrected.
- **Strength to preserve:** the grey notebook with *a spark* and *a letting-go* already standing in it, and the line drawn under all three.

### Priority 2 — The six that do not count to six (Chapter 53, Karis's slate; Chapter 54, "Second, then"; Chapter 55, Gault's "five of six")

- **Observed issue:** Karis's semester order removes the read-only trial, folds the cold runs into one slot and adds a new fifth, which leaves a second slot that nothing occupies until Cael asks for the Ember to go there. Gault's "The six stand as posted otherwise" assumes a posted second slot the page never showed. Downstream, Gault announces "Five of them have moved and one hasn't" after four trials, and Cael calls 11/12 "the floor" when Karis's floor was five in six.
- **Effect on the reader:** the movement's central device is a boy landing on a number; when the listener cannot reconstruct the numbers, the device stops being suspense and becomes trust. The Ember placement should feel like a chess move into a known square and instead feels like a move into fog.
- **Proposed scope:** two or three sentences on the slate making the posted second slot explicit (for instance, the wing keeps a second slot "at the enrollee's placing", or keeps the read-only frame short and Cael asks to swap the Ember into it; either is fine so long as the page says it). Move Gault's "five of six" to the note at the end of the sitting, or let him say "four so far, and one of them hasn't", which is more like him. Change "sitting on the floor of them as near as made no difference" to "a call above the floor". Leave every figure on the day's trials as it is; they are consistent.
- **Strength to preserve:** Karis's floor-and-roof brackets, the scared-plate discovery that the danger was under the floor, ON THE NUMBER, and Gault's speech defending the flat plate line.

### Priority 3 — Numbers for the ear (across the movement; the glance tally and ledger in Chapter 55; the clock in Chapters 51–52; Brom's hand in Chapters 53–54)

- **Observed issue:** *eleven* carries roughly nineteen unrelated counts across six chapters. "Four minutes of iron and eleven seconds more" treats exchange three's duration as Brom's whole clock after Chapter 51 said both clocks started at the walk-out. The glance tally names six people and claims seven. The ledger says "Drifts: two" and "it never flickered" in one entry. Brom's strapped hand appears without a cause.
- **Effect on the reader:** this is a movement whose pleasure is that everything is counted; when the incidental counts collide, the listener stops believing the load-bearing ones, and the ledger's self-contradiction lands on the exact sentence that should be the day's verdict.
- **Proposed scope:** a numeral pass. Vary the incidental elevens (Ilsev's form, the porters' minutes, carrel, subsection, years of print, Karis's weeks, Gault's years, the seam-hunters) to other figures; keep the structural ones. Decide whether Brom's clock is cumulative or per-exchange and make Chapter 51's "both had started" and Chapter 52's "four minutes of iron" agree. Add the clerk to the glance list or change "seven" to "six". Change "it never flickered" to something the two logged drifts allow, such as "it never went". One clause in Chapter 53 after the book-and-fist rehearsal for Brom's knuckles.
- **Strength to preserve:** the glance tally as Cael's oldest instrument, "He didn't look twice," the clerk's brown shoes, and the exact figures on the day's six trials, which are right.
