## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Immediately: "The hip woke before she did." Lira doing sums on a bedpost, the hip that "has moved in and hung up its coat", is the right door into a chapter about two clocks. The untying of the batons ("Receipt") pays Movement 6's knot without a word of explanation. Bracken walking two sealed sheets out under the sand-glass before the yard fills is the best single invention of the movement, because the reader does not yet know why it matters and then, at the ceremony, watches a registrar write a result at the foot of a sheet in public and understands that "the seam was the proof." Exchanges 3 and 4 of the final (Lira walking Brom backward round the oak to buy a fifth count; landing on the bad hip on purpose so the fold carries her through a beat that cannot move) are the series' best-matched bout since Cael–Brom, as the packet asked. The slip burning in four seconds and Karis laying "a cloth over a birdcage" on her own question is a quiet, exact scene. The fault second in four pieces and "Because the clerk was standing where I'd have landed" lands. The interview's unasked twelfth question ("Records of what? Ask it.") is the movement's true climax and the author lets silence do it.

**Where it slipped.** Chapter 53's middle: Karis's floor-and-roof brackets are explained three times in slightly different words, and the rehearsal's four vocabularies (the corner/hold, the read on its stub, the gaze, the drift) arrive close together; a young reader has to work to keep them apart until Lira's "I don't mean the read. I mean *you*" finally separates them. Chapter 54's dawn crossing (fire-watch slate, watchers, arch) is a slow bridge between two strong scenes. In chapter 55 Gault announces "Five of them have moved and one hasn't" with two trials still to run, and the homecoming scene re-introduces Karis asking "Well?" after she has already called Cael "a renewed enrollee" — both stop the read.

**Which person matters.** Lira, by a distance, and then Bracken. The Archmarshal is held exactly where the map wants him: a man with nothing in his hands.

**What I expect next.** The two unseen lines in the notebook; the departure; what Seln does with a file that came back unmarked. I would continue.

## Repair brief

Three priorities, all small; none touches a fight, a protected line or a scene that works.

**Priority 1 — ch55, Gault's count before the instruments (l. 67).**
- *Observed issue:* after the plate (trial four) Gault says "This wing measures six things. Five of them have moved and one hasn't." Only four trials have been run; trial five (new, no baseline) and the cold run have not happened. The "five up, one flat" is the end-of-day result (the note, the Log, Lira's recap), not something Gault can know at that moment.
- *Effect on the reader:* the thirteen-year-old counts on her fingers and stops; the adult reads it as the author summarizing ahead of the scene, which breaks the evaluation's one rule — nothing is said before the instrument says it.
- *Proposed scope:* one sentence. Either make it present-tense and partial ("Four instruments so far. Three have moved and one hasn't.") or move the six-and-one arithmetic to the note at l. 179, where it is already true. Nothing else in the speech changes.
- *Strength to preserve:* the whole defence of the flat line — "a man who comes back after a single term better at every instrument I own is either a wonder or a man whose evaluator should go and look at his instruments" — and "That is what a real semester looks like."

**Priority 2 — ch55, Karis's second entrance (ll. 245–249), with ch53 l. 201 as a rider.**
- *Observed issue:* Karis is in the common room, names Cael "a renewed enrollee", makes the tray joke and goes for supper; the next paragraph opens "In the common room Karis sat with a book on her knee… She shut it. 'Well?'" and she reacts to *renewed* as news. Two drafts of the homecoming are stitched together. The rider: ch53's "Weeks ago, in hall three, Brom found it" for a slip eight days old.
- *Effect on the reader:* a stumble at the exact moment the chapter should be settling; it reads as if Karis had not been listening, which is the one thing Karis never does. The calendar slip makes an attentive reader re-check the dates the movement has otherwise kept perfectly.
- *Proposed scope:* delete the re-entry framing and let the column exchange follow the tray joke directly (Karis asks about the third word before she goes for supper, or on her return with the tray), keeping the grey-notebook paragraph intact; change "Weeks ago" to "Eight days ago". About six lines and one word.
- *Strength to preserve:* "You can't carry a tune"; the third word written under *a spark* and *a letting-go* and the short line drawn under all three — "That's the last one. I can feel that it is."

**Priority 3 — ch55, the order of the four pieces (ll. 91–103), for the protected M9 line.**
- *Observed issue:* the four pieces are given as sound (pawl, catch-and-drag, "and then a release"), then the read on the forearms, then the answer in the legs, then the shoes. BOOK_MAP §10 protects Vastin's later notebook entry *Frame fault, trial five. Correction preceded the audible release.* As the second is now ordered, Cael's correction follows the audible release, and Movement 9 would be quoting a note the page contradicts.
- *Effect on the reader:* none now; in Book 4's last movement and in Book 5's callback, a careful reader who re-reads ch55 finds the Archmarshal's one precise observation to be wrong, which undoes the point of him.
- *Proposed scope:* one clause moved. Let the read fire during the catch-and-drag ("Even on its stub the read… laid it on his skin a hair before the lock let go") and put "and then a release" after it, so the hips are already dropping when the release sounds. The four-piece structure, the key-in-a-cold-lock image and the clerk's brown shoes all stay. The Iron read is canonically a fraction early, so this is truer to the system as well as to the plant.
- *Strength to preserve:* "He did not finish the burst." and the whole exchange from "Enrollee. Are you hurt?" through "Because the clerk was standing where I'd have landed."; the eleven minutes on the rail and "He didn't look twice."
