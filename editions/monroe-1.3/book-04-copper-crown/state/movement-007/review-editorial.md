## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** The first sentence (ch45: Cael doing nothing, on purpose, before the lamp) is a hook built from a cost the reader already knows how to price. Breakfast lands warm and quick ("Nobody talks about hips."; "I don't know anything about feet."). The Vastin window is the best cutaway writing in the book so far: the brass watch checked against a better clock, the pink tape cut at the knot so it can be retied, the drawer of two thousand margins, the boy on the bucket correcting a figure nobody will check. Ch46's inch at nine feet, then the same inch from Havel's side with the card's "beyond ordinary courtesy", is a small, complete story. Ch47's three-line exchange on the floor ("Read's off." / "On purpose." / "Then it's the right kind of off.") and Ilsev's "Two of them were for sneezing" are the kind of lines a thirteen-year-old repeats. Ch48's receipt stamp seen upside down for one second, Lira's weighbeam, and the pen that moves only for paperwork give the movement its spine; the count of marks ("*Two.*" … "*Three*") turns a nine-hour sitting into something a reader can keep score of. Ch49's folder laid on the chair "where nobody was", then Karis's shoe finding his ankle bone, is the movement's best jolt and its best tenderness in the same page. Ch50's "a coincidence with good posture" and the page with one line on it close the arc's question exactly as the packet asked: asked once, pocketed.

**Where it slipped.** Three places, all structural rather than sentence-level. (1) The middle of Vastin's window (ch45, the three tapes and the panel's order) is about 900 words of a man explaining his reading method; it recovers at the three margins, but the younger reader has to be carried through it on trust. (2) Ch48's attestation stretch is deliberately slack, and the slack is where the drift happens, so it is load-bearing; the reader still feels it as slack. (3) Ch49 runs Ilsev's window (≈2,300 words) straight into Havel's (≈1,500) — about 3,800 words with the lead off the page, the longest such absence in the book. The two windows are each excellent; back to back, with Cael last seen going thin in ch48's final paragraph, a thirteen-year-old may lose hold of whose day this is before "At the second recess Cael did not leave his chair" gives it back.

**Which person matters.** Vastin, through his pen rather than his face; Karis, who writes one line and refuses to write a second; Lira, whose beam metaphor gives Cael the frame he needed; Havel, whose three lines break his own rule.

**Which event I expect next.** The final (four days) and the twentieth (five), and specifically whether there is an answer for drift in gaps before a panel that confers between trials; whether the pen moves for Cael at all; whether Ilsev's third referral comes back with anything but silence.

**Would I continue.** Yes, without hesitation.

### Two lenses

- **A fluent thirteen-year-old.** Will follow the law because Withrow makes it physical (a stump; a list with eleven names on it; a door cut into a wall); will follow the pen count because it is a count; will feel the folder on the chair. Risks: the Vastin tapes passage, the Ilsev return window's three-witness analysis (abstract, though very clear), and ch49's double window. Nothing is beyond this reader; some of it asks for patience.
- **An adult genre reader.** Will recognise this as the book's keynote movement: an antagonist who is only ever competent and decent, pressure made entirely of paper, and a protagonist whose only victory is that nothing happened. The pen-count device and the "ruler before the reading" idea are fresh in the genre. The one thing this reader may notice is that the movement's set piece is a sitting, and the owner's "more action" lives next door in M8 — by plan.

## 4. Apparent inconsistencies and line-level findings

Each is cited; where only the plan supports a claim I say so.

**A. ch45 (Vastin's red bundle) — "Two years of demonstration sittings, sheet on sheet" and "fourteen and then fifteen and growing out of his boots".** The red bundle is "everything tendered in" the Greyvane record, "signed in turn by two officers of the academy". The edition's Book 3 holds Cael at fifteen for the whole book (B3 STATE_LEDGER l.11, LOCKED; "Came to Greyvane at fifteen" l.402) and the hearing's demonstration record is fourteen sessions in one semester (B3 ch28–29; B4 STATE_LEDGER §2 "across fourteen sessions"). Two years of sittings, and a boy of fourteen producing them, contradicts that. **Canon-level, moderate-high confidence** (it would hold only if the bundle were meant to include Ardenmere circuit paper, which "two officers of the academy" rules out). Fix is two phrases: a semester's sittings, fourteen of them; "when the person producing them is fifteen and growing out of his boots". The observation Vastin makes — that the numbers agree too closely for a growing boy — survives intact and is better for being about fourteen sittings in one term.

**B. ch48 → ch49 — the clerks' row.** ch48 seats the young clerk "on the chair beside Cael" and the inky-knuckled clerk "on the next chair along" (and again "The inky-knuckled clerk on the next chair was writing" during the first thin drift). ch49, after the second recess: "the chairs on either side of Cael stood empty" and the inky-knuckled clerk is "two places along". If he sat next to Cael all morning, one of the chairs beside Cael is his. The folder beat does not need him two places off: with the row as [young][Cael][inky][cold], and two clerks gone, there is an empty chair on inky's own side ("nearer his hand") and he still reaches across Cael to the far one. **Staging contradiction, high confidence; one-sentence fix in ch49** (keep "the next chair"; make the empty chairs "the one on Cael's other side, and the one beyond the inky-knuckled clerk").

**C. ch45 — Brom's boxes, "one for every night between the semifinals and the final" / "You didn't miss one."** The M4 ruling fixed the nineteen at four a week (Karis rules four boxes a week), and M6 has round two at exercise seven on d172; ch50 confirms one exercise a night now. A nightly cadence since the semifinal is plausible (the last stretch before a final) but nothing on the page says it changed. **Plan adherence, not canon; moderate confidence.** One clause ("Rooke had put him on one a night since the semifinal" or Karis's reason for it) reconciles M4's rule with M7's boxes. Note also that Karis "had told nobody but Cael why she stopped" at the eighteenth — a plant that M7 does not pay; fine if M8 does.

**D. ch46 (Havel) — "on a cold bright morning when he had been a much younger man with a much worse coat."** The pump step was two years ago (C3 ruling: "two years"; the same window says "carried the puzzle for two years"). "A much younger man" pulls toward the source's four-year drift. **Wording; high confidence; fix "a much younger man" → "two years younger" or simply "with a much worse coat".**

**E. ch49 — Karis's foot.** She is "a few feet in front of his chair" at the table's end; her shoe reaches his ankle by sliding back "under her own chair as far as it would go". A chair's width would do it; "a few feet" strains it slightly. **Minor; optional.**

By-ear referents (audio-first edition).** (i) "two places along from the counsel" is used twice for the Archmarshal (ch48) and "two places along" once for the inky-knuckled clerk (ch49) — in the same room, the phrase now points at two men.
