## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately on two linked questions: what Gault will permanently write about Cael, and why Brom has spent twelve days watching instead of fighting. The first east-hall session then made the book's idea of progression unusually concrete: Brom's forty-three failures are not humiliation but the first exact attention he has received. His delight at finding “the wall” and discovering that “it has students” is the first major emotional hook.

The movement strengthens as its separate tracks rhyme. Lira can win without her wins changing her certificate; Karis can secure knowledge only by mastering filing distinctions; Cael can be present everywhere while remaining eligible nowhere. All three make the institution feel less like a generic academy and more like a machine whose honest procedures can still produce injustice. The best passages make administrative language carry the force another book might reserve for a weapon or prophecy.

My attention slipped in two places. First, chapter 9's rule that practice bouts last three exchanges conflicts on the page with Lira's four-exchange, 3–1 result. Second, chapter 10 folds the calendar backward after chapter 9 has moved beyond the thirty-fourth day; Lira's “eight and nil” can therefore look like a continuity reversal after the reader has already seen her at nine wins. The date evidence can reconcile it, but the reader has to stop and do that work. I also felt a smaller drag in chapter 12 while the low-line/high-line dilemma was restated across several nights before Lira named the third choice.

Cael matters most by the end because his defining strength—precise observation—is becoming morally complicated. He catches hidden variables in floors and failures in instruments, yet he can also build the answer he hopes to find. Brom is the movement's emotional center, and Lira is the person whose need to be properly seen gives Cael's assessment its deepest meaning.

I expect Seln to test the exact blind spot the movement has just taught us: Cael is wary of people who stare, while Seln's trade is not looking. I also expect Ephram's withheld judgment to turn the semester evaluation into a public standard well before the formal day arrives. I would continue without hesitation.

## Manuscript-only consistency check

- **Canon:** N/A for verification. No project canon was supplied. I found no manuscript-internal name, relationship, or institutional contradiction across the seven chapters, apart from the bout-rule issue below.
- **Physical state:** The movement carries costs reliably. Brom's left shoulder leads to ice and ongoing retraining in chapters 8 and 11. Cael's fifth and sixth Wind bursts produce the predicted hip bill in chapter 13; the closing passage shows him favoring the leg. The plate leaves his right arm numb and shoulder aching, and chapter 14 consistently keeps that arm on the table or close to his side. No unexplained physical reset stood out.
- **Power limits:** The stated Wind ceiling—four free bursts on sprung timber, the fifth billed, the sixth degraded—is demonstrated rather than contradicted. Lattice defeats the surface read because a held frame does not move weight; reading the builder is explicitly partial and fails against a master. Seln's Shadow Path manages attention rather than instruments or already-landed glances, and his behavior follows those limits. No quiet ability resolves a decisive problem without prior explanation.
- **Knowledge boundaries:** Cael infers that faculty silence is organized but deliberately does not confirm it. He does not know Seln's identity; the Seln cutaway gives that knowledge only to the reader. Seln avoids looking directly, while Cael records the resulting absence as a small anomaly rather than leaping to the truth. Ephram knows the public transcript and explicitly limits his judgment to it. These boundaries remain clean.
- **Reserved disclosures:** No reservation document was supplied, so compliance cannot be verified. Within the manuscript, the intended secrecy is consistent: the public baseline shows Wind and the surface read while Pressure, Compression, Ember, and Feryn's fragment remain withheld. The narration names those hidden capabilities to the reader, but the panel does not learn them.
- **Apparent internal inconsistency:** Chapter 9 says, “Bouts were short: three exchanges, each to a clean touch or called even,” but Lira's first practice bout contains four scored exchanges and ends 3–1. This reads as a rules contradiction unless the intended rule is first to three touches.
- **Apparent chronology ambiguity, not a verified contradiction:** Chapter 9 advances through Lira's ninth win, the thirty-fourth evening, Fiske, and “for a week” beyond that. Chapter 10 then reopens on day twenty-eight and reaches day thirty-two, when Lira reports eight wins. The dates permit an overlapping timeline, but chapter order initially encourages a linear reading, making the eight-win line look wrong until reconstructed.

## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Chapter 9, Crown-yard rules and Lira's first practice bout. **Observed issue:** The text specifies three exchanges, then stages four and scores the result 3–1. **Effect on the reader:** It momentarily makes a carefully built competitive system feel unreliable and forces the reader to guess whether the rule or the result is wrong. **Proposed scope:** Make a one-line rules correction—most naturally “first to three clean touches,” if that is the intended format—or adjust the bout to three exchanges without cutting its tactical development. **Strength to preserve:** Keep all four adaptations if possible; the opponent's learning and Lira's answer to each adjustment make the bout work.

2. **Location:** Transition from the end of chapter 9 to chapter 10, especially “Inside ten days she had fought nine and won nine” versus day thirty-two's “I'm eight and nil.” **Observed issue:** Chapter 10 rewinds into an overlapping strand without an explicit backward-time cue. **Effect on the reader:** The win count can register as a continuity error, and reconstructing dates interrupts the otherwise strong braid of the four characters' progress. **Proposed scope:** Add one brief temporal signpost at the opening of chapter 10 or before the day-twenty-eight scene; do not linearize or restructure the chapters. **Strength to preserve:** Preserve the braided structure and the way the same institutional weeks look different through Lira's ladder and Cael's provision.

3. **Location:** Chapter 12, from Karis's explanation of “laid against” through the fourth-evening common-room decision. **Observed issue:** The low-line/high-line dilemma is reiterated in narration across several nights before the group scene restates it and Lira supplies the third option. **Effect on the reader:** The assessment's urgency remains clear, but momentum softens just before the strongest set piece in the movement. **Proposed scope:** Compress repeated formulations locally—roughly a few paragraphs or sentences, not the scene—and reach the common-room debate sooner. **Strength to preserve:** Preserve the ethical distinction between concealment and lying, Brom's insight about being straight with Gault, and Lira's solution of fully showing only what the public record already contains.
