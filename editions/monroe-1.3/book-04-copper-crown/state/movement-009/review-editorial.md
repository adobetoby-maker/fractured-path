## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** Three places, in ascending order. (a) Brom repeating Rooke "word for word" and then explaining the man: "He adjusted, and he made sure somebody heard him do it… That's his apology. He sent it by carter." That is the movement's method stated in miniature — people say things so that they will be in somebody's book — and it lands before the reader knows it is the method. (b) The council in ch58: Karis reading a corridor timetable off a page her pencil has gone through, Lira saying "He named the range," Cael fetching the marbled book and reading two-and-two back at them. "Omission is free… Tonight he performed an act." I would have read straight on from there without stopping. (c) Vastin's three readings on the back of a bill — *No adverse finding* folded, *Subject warrants attention* torn, the comfortable one never put to paper — and the torn halves going into the lamp chimney. That is the best-built thing in the movement.

**Where it slipped.** Ch59's posting-house window is 4,400 words of a man reading a file; it is superb for an adult and it is the one stretch a thirteen-year-old might skim. The slippage is not in the prose (the pencil travelling toward the wall is a good clock) but in length against event. The second slip is factual: a reader who remembers ch48 — Cael at the wing's counter watching the delegation's list clerk bring the compilation home, pink tape, blue stamp dated the twelfth, "nothing anywhere said *see me*" — will be thrown by ch58, where the same case arrives by the afternoon paper ten days later and Seln discovers it unmarked after an "evaluation seat had held it for eleven days." Then ch59 has Vastin still holding those filings under pink tape on the road. See Finding 1.

**Which person matters.** Seln, without a single sentence of his own about why. The movement's discipline — the companions see him, the arrangement holds "on a stair landing with nobody near as well as it held anywhere," the recess roster with his name against every working day, the figure sixty feet along the wall — is honoured completely. Lira matters almost as much: the station from inside is physical and unsentimental, exactly as the packet asked ("I think I prefer the door"; the hand that shakes afterward, put in a pocket "so that nobody else would have to see it").

**What I expect next.** Book 5 opening on a recess with Seln at his counter, the Continental's charter pinned on the covered walk, Ephram and Cael on the oak with Rooke at the rail, and the clause in Karis's grey notebook doing nothing until it is ready.

**Would I continue?** Yes. The ending is earned: a stamp instead of a verdict, a belief said aloud and labelled as a belief, and a barge horn answered by somebody nobody can see.

## 2. Two lenses

**Thirteen-year-old reader.** Clean, kind, and clear at the sentence level (FK 4.5). The council, the station, the porter changing a word and moving a name, the Copper Crown retired on a shelf between a lamp and a pile of drafts "that must on no account be thrown away" — all of this reads straight through. The costs are legible: Lira's hip "had views on stopping dead"; Brom's strapped hand; Cael's three days of ice. The risk is pacing in ch59 (one long interior window) and in ch62's inventory (a page and a half of italic ledger). Both are short-lined and readable; a young reader will get them, but may read them faster than they deserve. Nothing here is above the reader's head except the ethics of gratitude, and those are explained by Brom with a post.

**Adult genre reader.** This is the stronger lens for M9. The two files going in opposite directions (Seln's quarterly up the river; Vastin's note down it), the three readings refused, the collector Vastin helped end at thirty-four, Karis's finding against herself ("wrong class of object"), the clause that "has the shape of every good thing that ever happened to him" — this is the consequence-and-ledger movement the packet asked for, and it does not pretend to be an action movement. The adult reader will notice the continuity conflict in Finding 1 and the first-month/second-month wobble (Finding 2), because this reader is exactly the kind who keeps the dates.
