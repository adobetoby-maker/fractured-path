## Unscored reader response

Interest caught first when Seln compared the reports he had filed and discovered that he could no longer tell their order: the apparent bureaucratic stillness became evidence that he had begun protecting Cael. The course offer then turned that hidden choice into a dangerous invitation. The strongest pull came in Chapters 32–34, from the records-hall intrusion through the three nights of watching. The progression from gallery, to stair, to the open archive door is patient, spatially legible, and increasingly tense. “The door stood open. A man was standing in it” is the movement’s cleanest turn.

Interest slipped slightly during Chapter 31’s extended discussion of whether the course is a trap. The characters’ different reasoning styles are enjoyable, but the scene revisits several distinctions Cael has already worked through in Chapter 30 before the new pressure—the unchanged coats and the written contingency plan—fully takes over. A manuscript-only entrant also has to hold several inherited terms at once: the pin, five fragments, the minute, rule two, the Compact, Path families, the assay provision, and the prior Greyvane cost. Most become clear through use, but the opening asks for trust before it supplies footing.

Cael matters most. His vigilance is both gift and wound, and the text makes his moral bookkeeping as consequential as his power gain. Seln matters nearly as much because his protection is expressed through omissions, positioning, and professional restraint rather than confession. Lira is the emotional safeguard: she sees the future danger in Cael’s choices and makes care concrete without becoming merely cautionary. Karis and Brom remain distinct, useful people with private habits rather than a generic support group.

I expect the next major event to be the semester evaluation, complicated by Cael’s need to present five known fragments while keeping the sixth ordinary and hidden. I also expect the unknown client seeking “the plans of the door” to press on the assay provision, and Lira’s final against Fiske to force the group’s attention outward just when Cael’s new fragment makes attention itself unstable.

I would continue. The movement delivers a complete local arc—invitation, surveillance, acquisition, cost, first training, and an emotional landing—while leaving three clean forward pressures: the evaluation, the unidentified private interest, and the uncontrolled rent of the Shadow fragment.

## Manuscript-only consistency checks

### Canon

No editorial canon evidence was supplied. I therefore cannot identify or clear any verified canon violation. All findings below are internal to Chapters 30–37, and anything involving earlier events is only an apparent manuscript relationship, not a canon judgment.

### Physical state and chronology

Lira’s hip and shoulder injuries carry consistently into gait, training limits, seating, and the later final countdown. Cael’s forearm recovery is recorded, amended, and then respected. The post-acquisition sequence also carries fatigue, eye pain, tremor, Wind misfire, deferral, recovery testing, and the three-clean-day restriction forward rather than resetting him.

There is one apparent arithmetic discrepancy in Cael’s sleep record. Chapter 32 states, “He slept for three hours after the last bell” before the first watch. Chapter 33 then states, “He slept for two hours” after that watch, but Lira later says, “You’ve had four hours in two nights.” Chapter 34 opens with Cael assessing “four hours of sleep in three nights,” while Chapter 35’s deferral says “seven hours’ sleep in four nights.” The explicit three-plus-two hours do not reconcile with the two four-hour summaries unless one span is being counted by an unstated convention. Because exhaustion affects his judgment and fragment cost, the figures should agree on the page.

### Power limits

Compression’s recovery is amended from two mornings to four; the sixth fragment is acquired only after the five conditions are checked; acquisition blanks several seconds of perception; the first attempts at deliberate use fail; the fragment shares an attention resource with the read; accidental idling creates social and physical risk; and the movement-masking half fails whenever Cael actively reads the crowd. ## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Chapters 32–35, from “He slept for three hours after the last bell” through “seven hours’ sleep in four nights.” **Observed issue:** The stated sleep totals do not reconcile internally: three hours plus two hours conflicts with the later “four hours” summaries. **Effect on the reader:** A concrete bookkeeping error weakens trust precisely where fatigue is being used as evidence for judgment, recovery, and power cost. **Proposed scope:** Make a surgical numerical/timeline correction across the three or four affected sentences only; do not alter the watches, their duration, or the recovery arc. **Strength to preserve:** The bodily accumulation of cold, exhaustion, tremor, and depleted attention makes the surveillance feel costly.

2. **Location:** Chapters 30–31, from Cael’s three-sheet reading of Seln’s course offer through the common-room trap/no-trap debate and the Log entry headed by “So: both.” **Observed issue:** Several successive passages restate closely related interpretations before the unchanged coats and signed contingency advance the problem; cold readers are also carrying multiple inherited terms at this point. **Effect on the reader:** The opening movement briefly feels more recursive than progressive, and a younger or manuscript-only reader may lose the hierarchy of danger even while understanding each individual argument. **Proposed scope:** Tighten only duplicated trap/no-trap reasoning and add, at most, one unobtrusive orienting phrase for the minute/rule-two framework. Preserve every character’s distinct contribution, especially Brom’s public-recruitment logic, Karis’s four roads, and Lira’s insistence on the contingency. **Strength to preserve:** The story’s signature pleasure is intelligent people making uncertainty explicit without flattening it into certainty.

3. **Location:** Chapter 36, from the tray/stair/clerk incidents through the ladder exercise and the first wash-house trials. **Observed issue:** Three related concepts arrive in quick succession—accidental presence suppression, ordinary attention redirected by a ladder, and deliberate suppression that fails when aimed. The distinctions are present, but a first-pass reader can momentarily treat the ladder’s zero count as proof that Cael successfully used the fragment. **Effect on the reader:** The otherwise excellent limitation may blur at the exact point the training problem is defined. **Proposed scope:** Add or sharpen one sentence at the transition into the wash-house trials clarifying that the ladder supplied a mundane model, not a successful activation; leave the experiments and failures intact. **Strength to preserve:** The insight that attention must go somewhere, followed by Brom’s “furniture” diagnosis, turns an abstract stealth power into an original learning problem.
