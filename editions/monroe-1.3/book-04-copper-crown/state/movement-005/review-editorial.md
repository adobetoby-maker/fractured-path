## Continuity, canon, and story findings

### Material findings

1. **Protected countdown and live chronology conflict — owner/coordinator ruling required.** In Chapter 37, the board says, “Sixteen days to the final, and the semester evaluation the day after it,” and the protected closing log says **“Semester evaluation in seventeen days.”** The book map protects the same log line with **“Semester evaluation in nine weeks,”** and the movement packet also leaves the evaluation nine weeks away. The manuscript's seventeen days is internally consistent with Chapter 30, where the Fiske bout's six-week promise already has “fewer” weeks remaining, and with the intervening operation and eighteen-day age of the Shadow fragment. Therefore this is not safely repairable by replacing one number: the protected wording conflicts with the established movement calendar. Everything else in the closing protected line is exact.

2. **An unapproved pending name is used as canon.** The packet says **“Gwen” is pending** and requires “the second-year beside him” until approval. The manuscript names Gwen on the Chapter 31 sign-up sheet and throughout Chapters 32–36. Her voice and comic function are successful, but the name currently exceeds the supplied naming authority.

3. **Brom's entry count is behind the binding movement state.** The packet enters with Brom **fourteen of nineteen**. Chapter 30 instead says, “Twelve boxes. Three weeks, four a week. He finished the twelfth last night.” Chapter 35 later reaches number nineteen. This does not damage the scene, but it breaks the explicit handoff state and changes the implied training calendar.

### Plan variance that is not established as a canon error

The packet calls the counter exchange Seln's first words in nine weeks; Chapter 30 says thirteen weeks and anchors the last exchange on Cael's fifty-first day. The serialized day count makes thirteen weeks plausible, so this reads as a packet-duration discrepancy rather than a prose continuity failure. Reconcile the planning record when the calendar ruling above is made; do not change the manuscript merely to obey the isolated “nine weeks” note.

## Repair brief

1. **Resolve the protected countdown against the live calendar.** **Location:** Chapter 37, the board paragraph (“Sixteen days to the final”) and closing inventory (“Semester evaluation in seventeen days”); compare the book-map protected “nine weeks,” the packet close, and Chapter 30's fewer-than-six-weeks line. **Observed issue:** the manuscript is internally coherent, but it violates exact protected wording because the protected wording and movement chronology cannot both be true. **Effect on the reader:** the immediate countdown is clear now, but later inspection/final timing will expose whichever authority is left unreconciled and weaken trust in the book's carefully measured calendar. **Proposed scope:** obtain one owner/coordinator ruling; then synchronize only the affected countdown/protected line and the corresponding planning record. If “nine weeks” remains exact, the earlier Fiske-to-birthday calendar needs restaging; do not make a one-line swap. **Strength to preserve:** the entire birthday inventory, “we built ready,” and the pressure of the approaching evaluation.

2. **Restore the authoritative movement handoff and naming gate.** **Location:** Chapter 30's “Twelve boxes”; Chapter 31 sign-up sheet and every “Gwen” use in Chapters 32–36. **Observed issue:** Brom enters at fourteen of nineteen, not twelve, and Gwen remains pending with an explicit role-placeholder instruction. **Effect on the reader:** neither error harms the local scenes, but both turn controlled continuity into accidental canon and create avoidable downstream cleanup. **Proposed scope:** reconcile Brom's count and the timing of number nineteen at four exercises per week; secure owner approval for Gwen or replace the name with the required role description until approved. **Strength to preserve:** Brom's steady four-per-week progress and every beat of the second-year's eye-drawing, pastry-aunt, dog-counting comic voice.

3. **Widen sentence architecture only in abstract explanatory passages.** **Location:** Chapter 30's file/audit rationale; Chapter 31's council and Log; Chapters 35–36's integration comparison, idle-state explanation, and working-ledger setup. **Observed issue:** mean sentence length passes the working range and long-sentence share is healthy, but SD 11.47 is far below ≈26, FRE 87.6 is far above 72.3, and FK 4.41 is below 6.8; meanwhile paragraph median 29 already sits at the accepted ceiling. **Effect on the reader:** the prose can sound syntactically younger than its ideas while still asking the listener to retain several layers of abstract reasoning. **Proposed scope:** sentence-by-sentence only: join adjacent clipped explanations that are one thought, allow a few more clearly subordinated sentences and precise multisyllabic terms, and cut true restatement rather than scene substance. Do not alter dialogue, protected wording, the fragment notice, scene breaks, or the three-night operation. **Strength to preserve:** the short landing beats, audio clarity, exact physical suspense, and warm simplicity of Chapter 37.
