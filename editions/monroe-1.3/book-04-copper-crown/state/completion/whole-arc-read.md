## Verdict

The book holds as one arc. Read end to end it does what the map asked of it: the mid-tier academy is a real place with a yard dug four feet into the ground so a thousand people can look down at two; the system is taught hard in the first third (the boards, the seeding half-sheet, the four-places rule, the sum that shuts twenty-three names out of their own ladder, the assay clause read with a finger under each word) and then surfaces only at its turns; the watcher is found by the one method the book has spent three books building, and the sixth fragment is priced on the page before it arrives and charges rent for the rest of the year; the institution holds still on purpose for three days and then for eleven; and the boy who was written down by everybody ends the year knowing that the one system that never asked what he is had already decided. The calendar chains from the third week of the Greyvane term to the last evening of the Halcenvane recess by the ledger's day-numbers without a break; every stated standing, tally and count agrees with the page (Lira's eleven unbeaten, Brom's nineteen four a week, 0 of 7 then 0 of 31, nineteen of nineteen, 91/63/19 on the brackets, 311 sheets, 54 of 60, 11 of 12 at the fourth weight, fifty-one minutes); every protected line is exact and lands where the map puts it; the record of five with two capabilities public is kept to the letter through the baseline, the evaluation and the interview; Seln never learns, Vastin never guesses, Ilsev never takes the next thought, and the three Architect plants stay at their altitude. The best writing in the book is the Seln study (ch15–18), the pin (ch27), the three nights (ch33–34), the final told with two clocks (ch51–52), the evaluation (ch54–55), the twelve-by-fourteen room (ch56), Vastin at the posting-house (ch59) and Lira's four seconds in a grain factor's counting room (ch60); the quiet ninth movement earns its quiet.

The weaknesses a straight read shows are not structural. They are, first, a repertoire of habits that recur so often a listener hears the author under the characters: the explanatory "the way a X does Y" simile 283 times in 60 chapters, "exactly" 208 times, the measured pause 194 times, and the number eleven, which has become a tic (228 hits in 53 chapters; carrel eleven, eleven weeks, eleven seconds, eleven questions and eleven days are canon, but eleven instruments, eleven swings, eleven feet, eleven minutes for the porters, eleven thumbnail marks, eleven crosses in a term and "Lira has done it eleven times" are the author reaching for the same number). Second, a soft stretch in M3 (ch15–18), where the counting study is told once in the shorthand and once more to the three, and a window-dense run in M7 (ch45, ch47, ch49, the last of them the longest chapter in the book at 6,809 words) in which Cael's own stakes pause for three cutaways. Third, a handful of continuity seams that a line pass closes: the ladder book holds "four hundred and twelve names" on day 28 when ch9 has the Copper column at sixty-five lines; Lira is "ten days on the top line" three days after she took it, with a hip that was back in hall one on a day that has not happened yet; the Crown yard floor is dressed stone in ch6 and oak in every bout fought on it; Seln has six hands in ch14 and four everywhere after; ch34 reads rule two aloud in words the minute never used; the Iron read and Brom's acquaintance are "three years" old in M2–M3 where the ledger's canon is two; and the baseline is "the first month" seventeen times against the forty-eighth day. Sixty-eight line fixes below; three cross-movement priorities for one same-author pass in two lanes. Nothing needs a rebuild.

## 3. Book-wide repetition — the worst ten

Counts are `grep -oiE` over all 62 chapters (hits / chapters). "Keep" names instances that earn their place.

1. **The explanatory simile "the way a X does Y"** — 283 / 60. The book's dominant tic, as in Books 2 and 3 (351 and 237). It glosses gestures the sentence has already made ("the way a man puts down a thing he means to come back to", "the way a carter lifts two fingers", "the way a cat comes to sit by you only when you have stopped calling it"). Keep the character-specific firsts: Karis "the way Vell had once taught him to read a ledger" (ch12), Fiske "the way a carpenter comes into a room where she is going to hang a door" (ch9), Bracken "the way other men signed forms" (B5 echo), the porter "as a man tells a thing he has told so often he could tell it asleep" (ch6). Target: two per chapter, none on a gesture already plain.
2. **The number eleven** — 228 / 53 (ch10 fourteen, ch31 twelve, ch53 eight; seven each in ch11, ch12, ch20, ch28, ch56). 4. **The measured pause** — "for a (long) moment / while / time" 194 / 58. Nearly every dialogue scene. Keep in the records sittings and the twelve-by-fourteen room, where length is the point.
