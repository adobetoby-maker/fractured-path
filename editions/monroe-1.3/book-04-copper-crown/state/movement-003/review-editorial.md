## Continuity, story, and canon findings

### 1. Verified canon contradiction: Copper ranks do not end at Rank Eight

**Location:** Ch21, the Fiske passage beginning `She was the best Copper fighter Cael had ever seen`, specifically: `Copper Rank Eight was as high as Copper went. There was no Rank Nine above it`.

**Evidence:** `UNIVERSE_BIBLE.md`, “The Tiers,” is LOCKED: every tier has Rank 1 through Rank 10, and Rank 10 is the advancement threshold. The book map and ledger establish Fiske as Copper Rank Eight, but neither makes Rank Eight the tier maximum.

**Finding:** This is a canon error, not merely plan drift. It also weakens the intended institutional critique: Fiske can still be trapped by an instrument that fails to recognize her, but the prose cannot erase two locked ranks to make that point. Reframe the ceiling as the limit of Fiske's paper, the bracket, or what Halcenvane has chosen to measure—not the limit of Copper itself.

### 2. Internal action/count contradiction in Lira–Nyle

**Location:** Ch20, third exchange, from `She committed. She went left and was gone` through `Three exchanges. Four bursts.`

**Evidence:** The first exchange explicitly uses one burst; the second explicitly brings the tally to two. The third then depicts a left burst, a right burst beginning as its landing beat releases, and an additional “short one.” That is five depicted bursts, although the narration says four. The movement brief and book map specify four bursts, and Ch21 correctly records four free on sprung timber with the fifth billed.

**Finding:** The choreography, tally, and stated cost cannot all be true. Because the passage makes Cael price each burst in his own hip, the error directly damages the progression accounting at the bout's emotional peak. Preserve the eleven-second win and Nyle's adaptations; remove or reclassify one movement so four actual bursts remain.

### 3. Chronology seam needs one explicit cue

**Location:** Ch19, from the day-76 wrong issue through the day-79 revised issue and the following four-day break from counting; Ch20 opening, `The brackets went up ... on the seventy-seventh morning`.

**Finding:** Chapter 20 intentionally returns to an event Ch19 has already passed. The day number makes the order recoverable, but no prose cue tells the listener that the narrative has rewound. In an audio-first book already carrying many day numbers, a brief “two days earlier” or equivalent orientation would prevent a false continuity alarm. This is a clarity seam, not a canon violation.

The movement delivers its planned arc: the watcher is found through bookkeeping, tested, kept in place by a contested group decision, charted from the outside, and then set beside a bracket that mismeasures Lira, Brom, Fiske, and Cael in different ways. Cause and effect are strong. Failed methods produce improved methods; the attention cost produces the late read; the late read produces the bruise; the four-day rest produces two explicit recovery instances; Brom's known seam produces Merrick's plan, and Brom's refusal to redirect produces Rooke's next correction.

Physical state is carried well. Cael's read is late rather than wrong; his ribs receive consequence and advice. Brom's jaw, arm, breathing, and later headache survive the bout. The Wind landing beat remains unchanged. The five confirmed fragments remain five, the Tide anomaly is not advanced, and nothing is deployed or acquired.

Knowledge boundaries hold. Seln does not learn or infer the integration mechanism; his window never declares a switch of loyalty; the locked case is untouched. The permitted Architect plant remains an unwritten thought at the end of Ch21 and does not add a maker, will, reset, or listening intelligence. No Ephram–Cael floor contact occurs.

## Overall editorial judgment

This is an engaging, unusually coherent investigation movement with excellent reciprocal friendship, purposeful humor, a memorable secondary POV, and action whose tactics grow directly out of prior learning. It should proceed to a focused repair, not a structural rewrite. The canon rank error and burst-count contradiction are concrete blockers.
