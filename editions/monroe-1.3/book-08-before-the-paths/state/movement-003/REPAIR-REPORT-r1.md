## C. Recomposed

1. **No number of places and no fifth place.** 17:25–41: Karis sorts the sheets by their sign-groups without counting on her fingers, and answers her promise from chapter 11 honestly: "I have checked it, and I am not going to give one, and I owe you the reason. What I counted that night were groups of signs. I let you think that I was counting places, and I thought so myself." She matches four to the hide by their measures and turns the other corners down: "I shall not call them places, and I should be glad if nobody else did." "More than four" is not said. Gone: "Five", "And one over", the fifth's measure, the differently filled square, "the same five places" (Seln: "the same places"), "Each of the five places" ("Each of the four places on the hide"), "five small marks" ("the small marks, the one in pencil and the four in ink"), Vastin's "There are five places" ("There are places in that country", 18:157), and "five sheets and an iron stake" ("four sheets", 19:277). The grade scene now ends on "I have four alike this morning, and I can make no more of four."
2. **No second calendar.** Cut from chapter 17: "the days run strangely", the broker's paper, the stations' year, the quarrel, the difference and Karis's margin. Vastin's account of the form stands and gains two sentences of procedure ("A clerk enters what the form has a column for. What it has no column for goes with the book."), which Karis applies to the missing remarks space. Chapter 18: Seln read the date "as a carter reads a load, whether he chooses to or not", and knew the day because he had written it; his two days of doubt are a date read once, by lamplight, over a shoulder, in another person's pencil; his certainty is the original, in daylight, a figure at a time, forward and back, and then laid against the line that he copied twice (18:9–25). Cael reads the date, half knows it "as a person seems to have met a face in a crowd", and lays the date that he knows as he knows his own name against it, a figure at a time, twice: "They were one date." (18:99–105). Gone: every "turn", "taking off", "the difference", "a child could have done it", and Seln's thought that the boy had heard the difference. No figure, no span, nothing owed to Vastin.
3. **Three tracked passages, beat order broken.**
   - 17:89 → 17:95: the gloss comes first and the gesture is another one (her pencil laid across the sheet's foot to keep the wind from its corner); the "beside, not on it" frame is gone.
   - 18:139 → 18:129–147: the fear now sits at the blank ("it frightened him more than the date had"); he puts the book back; the lifelong habit is set against it only after the row is tied and Lira has looked at him ("There was one line now that Hesk's book would never have. He would always know the place where it was not."). The "first thing… how easy" pair is gone.
   - 19:33 → 19:33–53: Oryn enters by the board at the foot of a bed and the column; the place is refused only afterward, when Brom asks "Where was it?": "I'll keep that. It's not shame… They keep the same sheet at the foot of every bed inside the Line." "It doesn't matter which; they're all one city" is gone. ("I was made in a city" is gone with it; "confirmed" replaces "made anything else".)
4. **The ruler line, verbatim, once (17:109):** `"No," said Karis. "A measurement is a fact about a ruler, not about the hand that held it."` "It was as a row of lamps—" and "I shall not finish that" stand.
5. **The four words.** Karis lays the ledger beside the sheet, shuts it, and says "I shall not swear to a hand on four words. I shall not say that it is his, and I shall not say that it is another's." The narration now says only that the words are in none of the three clerks' hands. Brom's line about forty steps and a stake is gone, and so is Vastin's "and is dead"; chapter 18's first sentence no longer calls them "a dead man's four words". Her gloss has no "made": "he knew what a device does. It does the same thing at every hour, for every person who comes to it, and it has an edge that you could rule." / "He does not say. He had room for four words, and he spent two of them on that."
6. **Clarity.** At the spring, Brom counts the three findings on his fingers at the three existing turns: "Six years, and nothing's moved." (17:61); "Their tools work in there. It's sigils that stop, and nothing else that they carried." (17:77); "One man wrote down what he thought. The year after, there's nowhere on the sheet to think." (17:139). Seln's fifth omission and Vastin's account of the form are each broken into shorter paragraphs where the question changes. Chapter 13's second paragraph has one anchoring sentence for the page (13:5): "It was one line out of an old register, copied by a man who had then walked three hundred miles to lay it on that table, and it sent whoever read it to a town."
7. **Oryn's lead-in (19:179–183).** Her own lines now end "I don't know yet, and I've a round in front of me."; she begins to push a sleeve up and draws it down; then the restored line. "I'll not do it tired, at a fork" is gone, so that nothing is said twice.
