## Ground for the landing (one picture)

The river runs north to south, a hundred yards across in the dry months and more now. Lowmarch is on the east bank. The cable hangs bank to bank. The scow hangs on it by two running blocks, each on a short strap to a post at the scow's rail: **the upstream strap** (north) and **the downstream strap** (south). The hauling rope goes from the scow's **bow** (her east end, coming home) to the **snatch-post**, an oak post at the head of the eastern landing with a block lashed to it, and turns there into the round shed, where the gelding walks the drum. In flood the cable bellies south, the scow rides south of the landing, and the rope leans on the snatch-post sideways. South of the snatch-post, two strides off, the old slip's **sill** lies under the mud: a baulk of squared oak that the scow sat on all cold term.

## Chapter 7 — The Mule's Chair (days 271–272) · Cael ≈5,000

## Chapter 8 — Both Hands (days 273–276; Vastin's window, 265–276) · Cael ≈2,700 · Vastin ≈2,400

1. **The square (273, first light).** The silent yard's pegs still stand in thawed turf. Lira takes the longest stave in the bundle, one that cannot be used one-handed. The healer's rule is off: "she's had her hands on all of me." Cael's ten are shut, by the yard's rule and his own. First exchange: her left hand comes a beat behind her right, so that everything from her left side arrives late; he goes in on the late side. One to him. Second: she lets go with the left and fights one-handed, as she has for a hundred and seventy days, and is too quick for him. One all. He tells her what she did.
2. **The arm (273).** Third: both hands on; she takes his stroke across the wood between them and the arm tells her, once: her fingers open; he checks; "Don't you stop"; she shuts the hand and goes through. Two–one to her. She slips on the wet turf and turns in the air to come down on the right side. Fourth: she tells him to come at the left; he does; two all. Fifth: hers. The tall man, on the bench with his pole, grounds it once. Oryn has seen it from the eave and says nothing.
3. **The river (275–276).** The last of the shelf goes on the 275th. Ephram's letter gets its line. On the 276th the flood: the water is over the lowest plank; whole trees; the ferryman looks at it for an hour and says "Tomorrow. Light, the first time." Brom and the money that will buy no rope tomorrow.
4. **Vastin (the 265th).** The last station on the east road: the ford with the wrecked ferry and its toll-board; the swept house; the register open at its ribbon and a line that stops at the fourth column; the sill between the house and the frame-house, where nobody puts out a hand for his paper. The brown coat; the stick; the knee; the flat on his back; the leaf in his coat. He steps over.
5. **Vastin (the road, to the 276th).** Three people tell him to go to the plank: a courier with half a hat-brim at a ford; an old carter at a halting-ground ("You're paper"); a boy on the western landing. The broker looks him over for a hand and finds no use for him at any price. He waits for the boat with what he carries. He has read the sheet for its bar and has never weighed its date.

## Chapter 9 — The First Boat (day 277, first light to forenoon) · Cael ≈5,000

1. **First light.** Half the meadow on the bank. Seln cuts the forty-seventh notch. The scow goes over light, with the ferryman at the sweep, and the river takes her across in a third of the time. On the far bank he loads people and waves the carts off: eight.
2. **The strap.** Coming home, a raft of shelf ice rides up on the scow's north rail. The upstream strap parts. She swings on the downstream block with her bow to the current's side; the hauling rope takes the river; in the shed the gelding is walked backward. The rope leans on the snatch-post, and the post begins to go. There is no second line to send: it is under a mountain.
3. **The post.** What he has and will not use, with the reasons: Pressure (narrow and deep; it would go through the ice and into nine people), Anchor (a held point costs him everything else, in full view of a bank of people who are going west, and he has held one for sixty counts once). The read, on the ground: the post's foot is going south a finger at each heave; the bank is slurry; the old sill is two strides south and worth sending a load to. Brom and one of the scow's blocking timbers. Lira and the brown mule on the second bar. Seln at the pawl. **Deployment: thirty.**
4. **The line.** On the scow a passenger in a brown coat lays down his stick and takes the tail of the parted strap from a carter who cannot hold it. He passes it behind his back, sets his boots against the rail-post's foot, and stands. He does not haul. He holds her upstream corner to the cable for as long as it takes to wind her home. Nothing on him changes colour; nobody on that boat declares anything. The ferryman has to tell him twice that he can let go.
5. **The landing.** Cael's near sight is gone. The passenger comes up the planks; he makes the short bow to the ferryman; he pays his copper. The stance; then the face. Seln's one word. The title, and the protected answer. Lira's face for men in grey. The ferryman and his rope. Brom: nothing on him; he held it with his back.
6. **The plank.** He goes to it, since three people told him to. He reads the oak from the left-hand post. Pike's question and the two protected answers. A copper and a half for bread, counted out. Teague, drawing a slip, reads the man's hands a step longer than a crew looks. Cael names an hour and a board.

## Chapter 10 — There Is No Key (day 277, noon) · Cael ≈5,000

1. **The doorway.** Seln has the door. The four protected lines, their first words to each other. The short bow, to the room. The board hears the town's name for the second time in nine days, from the other side. Nobody tells him why they have gone quiet. Karis asks one question about the paper's age and gets "tonight".
5. **Nothing to do.** He has said it. His hand goes to the place where a pen would be. Nobody gives him a task. The papers themselves are for the evening, in an order he chose on the road, and he says so.

## Chapter 11 — The Thing You Want to Ask (day 277, noon to evening) · Cael ≈5,000

1. **"Why us."** Lira. His answer: they showed him a thing once and asked him for nothing; and there is nobody inside the Line he could hand it to who is not standing on it.
2. **The knee.** Oryn has been watching how he sits. He says, before she can, that he has not come to be mended. Her protected line. She stays where she is.
3. **What follows him.** Seln asks. Nothing that can cross the Line: his old office wants a district to run in. Lira: six stood on a rim. His answer has "is not mine" in it. What he does not know and says he does not.
4. **The thing she wants to ask.** Cael remembers a sentence said to a window in a borrowed room, and keeps his own question. His protected line, to Karis. She asks why a man with forty years' standing carried it to a board and not to a council. The protected close, with one beat of mine where the map has its ellipsis.
5. **The ferry goes west (after noon).** Ephram's letter and a copper to the boy. The carts go over, two to a crossing. No cordage comes back. Vastin on the bench under the eave with his stick and nothing to carry; Brom will not let him lift a box; Cael does not invent an errand for him. The log's line: thirty.
6. **The back room: the records (evening).** The store behind the chimney, seven people and a lamp. His order, and why. The flat: a seat-courier's flat; six rows, a year to a row, each in its tape. The head of every abstract carries four signs and a number; Karis lays the dead man's ledger beside the first. The columns. The narrow one at the right, and the word at its foot. "More than four." She will not say how many more until she knows what a row counts. Under the sixth row, loose, the last sheet: the completion report, with five names and a town; it is how he knew where to walk. Held: it has no number in any register that he could find.

Lira asks whether he has ruled; Brom goes to rope the boxes so that nobody need say it. Seln lays his wand down. The log: the 277th; thirty. Overhead, a step.
