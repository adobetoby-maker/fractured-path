## Decisions made before drafting

1. **Deployment stays at thirty-two.** No fragment is used outward in this movement. The two inward readings are not counted, as the three before them were not. Anchor asks at each gate and at the iron door and is told no; Shadow lifts at each dusk and is told no; the read asks at the bridge and is told that it need not know. No fragment is acquired.
2. **The line between the order and the reading.** Oryn's order was `Write it down and stop touching it.` What he did three times was go straight at the place and bring the ten up to it one after another. That is the touching. On the 281st he lets the going-along take its own way and follows where it bends, the whole way round, and asks the place nothing. He says to Lira beforehand what he is about, and enters afterward that he chose the reading of the order that he wanted.
3. **The finding is about the instrument.** Tide feels what moves. Everything he has ever learned about the place is a fact about what goes round it. Where nothing moves the instrument does not say *empty*; it says nothing. The image is used once, in narration, in the packet's words. Coming out of the fourth reading his right hand is on the waypost's stone, and the going-along comes to that palm and has nowhere to go: the same nothing. It is not a use (nothing is asked, nothing is read, no person is touched) and it is not counted.
4. **Where the Line is.** The river is the Line at Treswick, and the sill is under the outer gate's arch. The hedged fields east of the bridge are worked from the town and its watch rides them to the nine-mile post. Seln lifts the road's silence at the first hedge and says what replaces it: outside, a declaration draws what hunts; inside, it draws a clerk. Nobody declares, on either side.
5. **Vastin sits** at the road's edge by the three-mile post, at dusk on the 281st, by lowering himself on his arms with the bound leg straight out. A man in that binding cannot get up again without an arm, and he knows it before he goes down. That is what it means.
6. **"I read your file …" is recomposed**, anchored to events and not to a number of years (BOOK_MAP §12 C2): from the week before he came up the bluff until the morning he left a seal in a tray.
7. **The why of the declining is mine**, built only on what the edition's Books 4–6 show: the three days on the bluff told him nothing; in the borrowed office he said that the refusal was the one sentence the seat still needed of him. The confession: that was true, and it was the reason he could give with his back straight. The other reason was fear. A second reading under his certificate would either be silent again (two silences on one file, for which an office writes a word) or would light (and then his three days and his forty years of reading were worth what a plate said). He could afford neither answer and kept his hand off the door. Nothing in it touches the date, the mechanism or the layer.
8. **What Cael gives back.** That the place a healer found in him was read again today, twice, and that the reading came back with nothing, because the instrument cannot read what does not move. He does not say what read it or how. Vastin does not ask. Nobody else holds it at that hour.
9. **The city (mine; flagged).** Three walls. The outer gate counts heads on a frame of beads and has a book that it opens only for those who come to trade under a name. Inside the second wall (slate roofs) Iron walks by right; inside the third (the old town) Bronze does. Everybody else goes through on a pass that states a purpose and a count, which the keepers tick with a pen. Keepers wear the gate's coat: undyed grey at the outer, dark blue at the second, pale buff at the third. The station is the one building that reads; everything else reads by paper, coat and habit.
10. **The inn with two doors (mine).** A street door with a book on a stand inside it, for those who lodge under a name; a yard door for carters and hands, who pay in front and are written nowhere. Two marks for the long loft over the stable for a week, with the pot at dusk: 138 → 136.
11. **Karis goes as the pass's bearer.** Cleon's pass under the provision names him and nobody else: the matter, its bearer, and hands not above four, all by count. The four are Cael, Brom, Lira and Vastin. Seln is on no paper and goes up by his own way. (Flagged: the plan names the four hands and does not say how Karis is entered.)
12. **Seln's route.** From the third gate, not the street ahead (which opens on the square) but the lane on the right hand under the wall, and down a stepped cut of five flights and twenty-two steps, a stride and a half wide, between blind walls three men high with no doors, to the registry's yard gate. Carts go the long way round by their own street, past a weigh-bar where a load is written against its owner. The cut's measures are BOOK_MAP §8's, so that Movement 5 finds the same place.
13. **Cleon's noon house** is a cook-house built against the foot of the second wall, with a counter inside and a hatch on its wood-yard. Karis goes to the counter alone. Cael stands in the yard with a bowl and hears it through the open yard door. Cleon never looks at him. The two names are kept out of one sentence.
14. **Oryn's cutaway uses no new hold name.** The first of her five gates and the second are unnamed. Oxhollow is the ford at the far end of the round; the man with the wrist is a carter of Oxhollow's whose wrist was strapped at Oxhollow's gate in the cold term, when no healer came. The birth is at the second gate, on the night of the 283rd, and the child is born alive at first light on the 284th. Nobody dies. She asks before every hand.
15. **Markers, not guesses**, wherever the plan quotes a line in part or leaves its speaker unpinned (listed at the foot).

## Ground (one picture)

## Chapter 20 — Wayposts (day 281; noon to mid-afternoon)

**Scene 1 — the foot of the rise; the first hedge; the nine-mile post.** Present: the six. Seln stops at the first hedge and lifts the silence, and says what a town puts in its place. The road fills: a dung cart, a boy with geese; nobody looks twice at people carrying boxes. At the post (a dressed stone with a figure cut in it) Lira sets her box down with her back to the town; Cael sees that she has not once lifted her chin toward the crown since the rise, and understands that she heard a term given at a fire and took it for her own. He does not thank her. *Changes:* the hold is off; Lira's discipline is on the page (packet: "Lira keeps the same discipline unasked"). *Fragments:* the read asks at the post what the stone weighs; told that it need not know.

**Scene 2 — nine miles to six.** Cael asks Karis what a historical-Path practitioner does. She gives what she has from a catalogue and no more: such a person reads how a made thing is built, and not what it says; she does not know which Path. He goes over what he is carrying toward a town with a station on its crown. He works out the line between the order and the reading (decision 2). He tells Lira, on the road, that he means to read himself at the next halt, and what he will and will not do; she says that he is to write down that she asked. *Changes:* Cael decides. *Protected:* Oryn's order, verbatim, recalled once: `Write it down and stop touching it.`

**Scene 3 — the six-mile post; the fourth reading.** Halt. Brom sees to his foot. Vastin stands. Cael lies down behind the hedge-bank with a palm on his breastbone and two fingers on his wrist. At ten the short reading gives him his own gait. Then the deep reading, and the ten named as currents, one at a time, with where each lives and what it costs to carry: Wind, the read, Ember, Pressure, Compression, Anchor, Blade, Shadow (known by what it takes away; shut), Storm, and the going-along itself. *Fragments:* Tide, on himself, the fourth time; not counted.

**Scene 4 — the reading round.** He lets the going-along bend where it bends and follows it the whole way round the place for the first time. He learns how far out the leaning begins, and that the way is worn. Then he sees what he has: every one of his facts is about what moves. The instrument has never told him that nothing is there. It has told him nothing. *Finding (packet):* the reading feels current; the still place has none; **"a cord on a stone"** (once, narration). Rising, his hand on the waypost: the same nothing under his palm (decision 3). He needs an instrument for what does not move, and he knows of two, and is afraid of both.

**Scene 5 — six miles toward three.** Lira asks what he found and is told that it will be in the log tonight, where she can read it. The town grows: he can count gate towers. Vastin, beside him, says that he saw a man go behind a hedge and come back the colour of paper, and that he does not ask. *Changes:* Cael has a finding that he has told nobody.

Cael beats: the hold as something he had stopped noticing; Lira's discipline seen; the line worked out; that he chose the reading he wanted; the instrument's limit; the fear named.

## Chapter 22 — The Grey Bridge (day 282; first light to dusk)

**Scene 1 — three miles; the bridge.** Karis stops on the bank. The bridge is older than the walls: five piers of a dark stone laid dry; the outer wall is pale, mortared, and built up against the bridge's end without being bonded into it. She says so, and then cannot say anything else for a while. They cross among carts. Cael keeps his eyes on the parapet. *Fragments:* the read asks what the bridge carries; told that it need not know.

**Scene 2 — the outer gate.** A sill of stone under the arch, with sockets for a bar. Keepers in undyed grey; a frame of beads; a book on a sloped desk, strapped shut. The keeper's thumb moves six beads and he asks for nothing. Seln has said why: the book is for those who want to trade under a name. Vastin goes through a gate without anybody standing up. *Fragments:* Anchor asks at the sill; told no.

**Scene 3 — the district as a mechanism.** The brokers' street: a tally board at every door, chalked with what is wanted, by the load and by the hand, and the colour of pass that goes with it. The porters' bench. The rope-walk; Brom prices sixty yards of four-strand and does not buy. The public trough, and a brown mule at it that is not theirs.

**Scene 4 — the two forges; the inn.** One forge with a bellows and a man sweating at it; one with no bellows, a white bed, and a woman in a dark blue coat standing by with her hands in her apron. Brom, verbatim: **"They shoe by the tier."** Up the street, the second gate and its coats. Then the inn with two doors. Two marks at the yard door: 136. Seven bowls.

**Scene 5 — cutaway, Oryn (the first gate; dusk of the 282nd).** A walled hold under the hill-foot. A carter of Oxhollow's is on the bench with a wrist that was strapped at Oxhollow's gate in the cold term and has knit crooked. "May I?" She reads it. It will have to be parted where it has joined and set again, and she will not do that tonight or without his word. He gives it in the morning. She spends more on it than she has spent since the cut, and thinks of a Gold by a window who had what he wanted on the first day; and of a set of ribs that she has walked away from unread.

Cael beats: entered as a bead; that a gate can count him and not file him.

- **Chapter 23 has six scenes.** Seln's report is given in two sittings, with the lantern burned down a finger's breadth between the four things and the difficulty. He did not go above the trough: he hired a frame for a copper and sat three hours on the porters' bench. Vastin's papers are "three sheets under one seal", in oilskin, beside Karis's receipt.
- **Chapter 24.** Seln goes up alone on the 283rd as a counted hand (a keg of lamp oil, on a buff card) to walk both ways to the yard; Lira stays in the loft, so that the 284th is her first sight of the town from its top. Nobody says that evening who is to carry.
- **Chapter 25.** Oryn reaches the second gate after dark on the third day; the bed is in a gate-house built on the wall's outer face, so that she goes through no gate. The matter card is rare enough that both upper keepers remark on it (neither asks anything).
- **Chapter 26.** On the cut's fourth flight the stick slips; Compression comes up into Cael's heels unasked and is told to wait; the load does not come. Not a use. The registry's lowest three courses, and the undercroft beyond the last pillar, are of the bridge's dark dry-laid stone. Cleon's name is on the page once, in the last scene, and nowhere in chapter 24.
- **Fragments, as written:** the read asked at the nine-mile post and on the bridge (told that it need not know). Anchor asked at the outer gate's sill, at the second gate twice, at the third gate and at the iron sill (told no each time). Shadow lifted at dusk on the 281st and the 283rd (told no). Compression rose on the stair (told to wait). Tide on himself, twice, on the 281st (not counted). Nothing was used outward. Deployment thirty-two.
