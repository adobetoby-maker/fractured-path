## C. Recomposed

1. **Why Vastin declined (ch21:123–161, 241).** The Ostrand reason is now stated by him as the reason and the cause, twice, and he says that he does not take it back: "five young people had laid a case on a table that hour, and shown me what the office I served did with honest readings; I would not lend it my name for one more." Kept: the two outcomes he set out, "I could afford neither answer", the night awake, and "a better man would have been kept awake by the first." The close of the speech is rebuilt: he wrote *declined* "on a form which has no line for why" (Book 6 ch49) "and I should have written it if there had been no such night"; but when the pen was down he found that he was glad, and not on Cael's account: relieved that he need never learn what the plate would say. "I had kept my hand off a door for a sound reason, and I was thankful of it for a poor one." Gone: that he declined in order not to learn, and that Cael was left a blank so that an old man might go on supposing that he could read.
   - **Cael's side:** "for the sake of a man's sleep" is gone; he had been given the true reason in a borrowed room and had never quite believed that it was all, and what lay folded inside it was a man's relief on his own account. The man is not made smaller: he did a right thing for the reason he gave, and then walked three hundred miles to say that he had also been glad of it for a wrong one, which nobody living could have found out.
   - **The exchange:** "It kept me off the plate." / "The refusal did, and I shall answer for the refusal, since it was honestly made. I shall not take credit for the gladness."
   - **The log (ch21:241):** "*V. sat. He told me the rest of why.*"
   - **Later mentions:** chapter 23's cutaway and chapter 24's "a man who had earned it" say nothing of the reason and needed no change.
2. **The two tracked passages in chapter 26: beat order broken.**
