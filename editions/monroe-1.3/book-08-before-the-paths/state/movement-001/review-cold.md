# Simulated cold-read review

This is a **fresh-context simulated cold read**, not a real audience measurement and not a self-review. I read the six supplied chapters in declared order, and only those chapters. No editorial canon evidence was supplied, so canon findings below are limited to internal manuscript evidence and apparent consistency. The movement reads as a complete winter-stay arc leading into a new westward objective, but four visible production placeholders mean the text is not yet reader-ready.

## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in the keeper refusing the mule and strengthened when Cael refuses Ember at the frozen trough. That small decision gives me a practical problem, a moral habit, and a dangerous power before I fully understand the world. The first real lock came in Chapter 1 when Lira reframed Wind as “a way of leaving.” From there, the movement repeatedly turns limitations into tactics: the shale-backs are moved by uncovering food rather than fighting them, Cael learns to enter on the pole's recovery, and the ice rescue makes a pace-and-a-half sensory range decisive without making it conveniently larger.

Interest slipped first in Chapter 1, from “He was nineteen, and he had no Path” through the explanation of the Line, ten fragments, three stopped places, the fenced fourth site, and the ruled map line. The ideas are interesting, but a cold reader receives several institutions, rules, past events, and mysteries before knowing the five people around Cael. A smaller second dip came in Chapter 5 during Karis's extended solitary reasoning after the cord and brass tests. Her refusal to force an inference is excellent characterization, but the argument restates the soundness of the instruments enough times that a fluent younger reader may feel the forward motion pause.

Lira matters most to me in this movement. Her injury never reduces her to a patient: she diagnoses Cael's bad footwork, teaches Teague's boy, controls the mule during the river rescue without seeing the river, refuses Cael's reading of her ankle, and finally chooses to ask Oryn to mend it. Cael remains a strong center because his defining action is often restraint—keeping a fragment shut, accepting “no,” or trusting another person's skill—but Lira supplies the clearest emotional payoff.

I expect the next major event to be the six-person daylight decision about going west into the Line, followed by the crossing and an attempt to enter Treswick's Deepstacks under registry scrutiny. I also expect Cael's measurement anomaly to become operationally dangerous rather than remain an intriguing laboratory result.

I would continue. The movement pays off its local promises—the fence, the roof, the ten fragment trials, the silent-yard lesson, the mule's fee, and Lira's healing—while converting the winter pause into a concrete, personally costly next direction. My willingness to continue is high despite the dense re-entry burden, but the visible bracketed production notes must be resolved first.

## Manuscript-only consistency check

### Canon

No canon evidence was supplied. I therefore found no **verified canon violations** and cannot certify names, dates, institutional history, or reserved truths against the wider project. The findings below are internal manuscript observations only.

### Physical state

Physical continuity is strong. Lira's left arm remains in the sling and her right ankle remains dependent on the oversized boot until Oryn treats them in Chapter 6; her work before that point is consistently adapted to one usable arm. Brom's right shoulder remains bound, and he sets posts, shingles, and rescue loads with his left. Cael's fragment costs persist: Compression stiffens the right forearm, the read degrades near sight, Storm delays perception, and Anchor thins the other fragments. Teague's wrist damage is established before the healer orders him to keep Force shut. Confidence: high.

### Power limits

The stated limits are repeatedly honored and made consequential. Wind locks Cael for one count and is therefore rejected on the river; Compression needs both heels and trustworthy ground, so it is unsuitable on ice; Pressure telegraphs, penetrates beyond its target, and returns from a curve; the read reaches only a pace and a half and can take Cael's near sight; Storm costs delayed perception; Anchor suppresses the rest of the kit and has a short reliable range. The ice rescue is especially clean because Cael explicitly inventories why the other nine fragments do not solve the problem. I found no internal power contradiction. Confidence: high.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully bounded. Karis distinguishes observation from inference, most explicitly in Chapter 5's “Cause: not found. No inference entered.” Cael does not use the read in the silent yard merely because no observer would notice, and he trusts Lira's river reading at Thornwater rather than quietly confirming it. Teague infers that Cael is refusing something but does not acquire knowledge he could not observe. Oryn postpones examining Cael until she is rested and knows what she is reading for. I found no apparent knowledge leak inside the manuscript. Confidence: high.

### Reserved disclosures

No list of reserved disclosures was supplied, so adherence cannot be verified. Internally, the largest mysteries remain reserved: the cause of Cael's measurement anomaly, the meaning of the four signs and stroke/bar mark, what the fourth site stops, and what the oldest Deepstacks contain. The four `[[TIER-B: ...]]` notes are production leakage rather than story revelation; they expose coordination instructions and, in two cases, alternative or partial protected dialogue. Confidence: high on the visible leakage, unavailable on canon reservation.

### Time, counts, and resources

The money is internally consistent: ninety-six marks become seventy-six after the twenty-mark cold stay, then one hundred sixteen after the forty-mark fence, and finally one hundred eighteen after five for the roof and three for the mule. Deployment rises from twenty-seven to twenty-eight at the calf and twenty-nine at the ice rescue; trials remain excluded as promised.

There is one apparent day-count mismatch in Seln's notches. Chapter 1, day 131, says the packet crossed “yesterday.” Chapter 4 gives twenty-eight notches on day 158, and Chapter 5 gives one hundred on day 230; both imply the first daily notch was cut on day 131. Chapter 3, however, gives eighteen notches on day 147, where the same convention yields seventeen. The initial notching moment is not explicitly staged, so this is an apparent internal inconsistency rather than a verified canon error. Confidence: medium-high.

## Overall judgment

This is a compelling quiet-to-danger movement with unusually strong causal continuity. Its chief success is that winter waiting becomes progression: work, measurement, consent, tactical learning, and relationship change all move while the larger clocks cannot. The two major action sequences should retain their present development and length. The manuscript's substantive weak point for a cold reader is early orientation density; its immediate production defect is the four unresolved placeholders.
