## Two plan-against-closed-text conflicts found before drafting (flagged; staged so that either ruling stands)

1. **Teague's spear.** The packet and BOOK_MAP call this person "the tall woman". The edition's Book 7 says "a tall man" in Cael's point of view (ch52) and "He was not a man of many words" in Teague's (ch51). I give this person no pronoun and neither word: "the tall one", "Teague's spear" (Cael), "the spear" (Teague). The weapon in the yard is "the pole".
2. **Whether Teague reads.** The plan says he does not read. Book 7 ch25 has him read a paper "from the first word to the seal". The packet's beat "he cannot read the sheet and does not need to" is staged as distance: Pike's cloth hangs in the lower inn and he is in the upper; he knows what it says.

## Chapter 1 — The Count Stopped Moving (days 131–133) · Cael ≈5,000

1. **The shed (131, later that morning).** The keeper will not have a mule under his eave; Cael walks Oryn's brown mule down to the ferryman's round shed, empty since the gelding went to the smith. Ice skins the shallows. The trough is frozen; Ember offers a point; he uses his heel. What he is and what the ten are, by what he refuses and what he opens: the read, on the new ice. The ferryman watches him use the shed and asks no fee. Nothing crosses until the thaw: the count has stopped.
2. **The back room (131, noon).** What a cold term is. Karis has the kit's case open and will not drive stake thirty-two. Cael says what he means to do with the term: the ten, by use; under three rules (out of sight of anybody who could carry it west; Shadow shut; nothing on a person unasked). Karis: trials are entered as trials. Seln will keep the door, and says what he is counting. Karis's first piece about where an answer lies.
3. **The shed (131, night).** Lira on the mounting block. The lantern lit with a point; the burn. Three bursts across the ring; one count locked each time; her correction: it is a way of leaving, so choose where to stand like a post. Shadow lifts with the dusk and is held. Seln's back in the doorway.
4. **Pike's plank (133).** The cold-term board: a roof, a well-house, a fence. The price: Pike's ledger speech; the protected rate line; marker for the one-word line; twenty counted out; seventy-six. The season line blank on purpose. Teague lifts his chin across the yard as his five load for the cold road. Brom takes the roof.
5. **The log (133, night).** The working inventory's first page. The cloth will not move before the thaw. The mule's fee "he has not asked for and will".

## Chapter 2 — Never Below Them (days 138–146) · Cael ≈5,200

Ground (one picture): a hillside falling south from a scarp to the hold's long house and byre at the bottom. The long fence runs east and west across the slope, half way up; the gate with the axe-head is at its west end. Teague's standing posts run east from the gate; then forty-one capped holes; then the old far side. The herd (seventeen, four of them calves) lies along the capped holes, under the scarp's lee. Lira sits on a hay sledge on a knoll at the west end, level with the fence, where she can see all of it.

1. **Out (138–139).** Two days on the cold road. The hold; the factor, who asks nothing but what they can do; the fence; the herd on the line; the byre underneath it.
2. **The trial and the plan (140, first light).** In the yard Karis reads out the derived page. The read is tried at three hundred and fifty yards, two hundred, sixty; Lira watches heads; nothing lifts. Entered as one morning's trial. Lira's reading of the herd: they are not frightened and not feeding; they go where there is bare rock to scrape. Clear a lane of rock east and uphill, from above.
3. **The lane (140).** Shovels. The line moves and no animal is seen to walk. Brom sets the lane by eye. Karis times it.
4. **The calf (140, afternoon).** A calf leaves the line for the cattle's salt and puts a foreleg through a capped hole. The carters' rule broken on purpose: Cael beside it, the read on the ground; Brom's rail from the uphill side; Seln with scraped moss held high and back; Lira calling the old cow's feet. Out. His near sight gone for an hour.
5. **Forty-one (141–146).** Brom reads the line of posts like a wall. Seln and two of Teague's posts; he tells only Brom; Brom re-sets them at dusk; Cael counts forty-three and does not write whose. The factor pays forty at the gate and asks no name. Home; the tenth line. Log: twenty-eight. Karis's second piece.

## Chapter 3 — Reach (days 147–150) · Cael ≈3,900 · Teague ≈1,700

1. **Teague's proposal (147).** No Paths; steel and bodies; mornings, in the meadow under the ash. His reason said plainly. Hands.
2. **The first bout (148).** Cael with a short stave; the tall one with a pole. Three touches to none. The read asks and is refused. The tall one never strikes the left ribs and never speaks.
3. **The bench (148).** Lira's reading of it: he goes back along the pole; his feet wait for a thing he is not using; go in as the pole goes home. He asks to read the ankle; she says no.
4. **Teague's window (the night of the 148th).** The upper inn. The old woman and his wrist; the boy and the hot cloth; the boy asks leave to learn the count from the girl on the bench; Teague grants it. The light in the ferryman's shed, which he does not go down to. The cloth he cannot see from there and knows.
5. **The shed (150, night).** Compression's frame with Brom and a sack; Pressure ("the Bronze from Ardenmere"); Blade and what a knife pays. Log.

## Chapter 4 — A Pace and a Half (days 158–161) · Cael ≈5,000

1. **The second bout (158).** The young one at the bench first, asking. Then Cael: in on the recovery; one touch; the tall one shortens the pole and takes three. The pole grounded once.
2. **The ice (161).** The river has been a road for a week. The three on the rope-house roof. A cart cuts the bend and goes through, eighty yards out. The ferryman's rule for his ice. Cael out first with the hauling rope's end, reading.
3. **The lip.** The cart hangs by its fore-axle; the carter in the water; Brom flat on his face with a fist in the man's belt; Seln at the mules. Anchor and Wind offer and are refused, with the reasons.
4. **The drum.** Lira leads the brown mule round the ferry's drum; the cart comes up a plate at a time; he walks backward before it, calling the ground. Out. The bill. The ferryman.
5. **The kit on the table (161, night).** Log: twenty-nine. Karis has the dead man's ledger open at its head line.

## Chapter 5 — A Hair (days 168–264) · Cael ≈3,000 · Karis ≈2,200

1. **The cord (168).** Karis teaches the kit in the inn yard. Forty-two, four times. Forty-one and a piece, three times. The protected rule, as a rule.
2. **The edges (168).** A gap scribed on the deal. A hair short; a hair long. Marker for her second saying. The log, verbatim.
3. **Karis's window (the night of the 170th).** The cord tried against the brass; the index; what she wants; the sentence that she will not write.
4. **The long dark (176 and after).** One Storm lane over the snow; Anchor on the door-bar. Two crews at the sack; Teague the better captain, entered.
5. **The thaw (230–264).** The river a river at the middle. Her sentence, in full, on the 263rd. The road opens.

## Chapter 6 — West, and Inside (days 264–270) · Cael ≈5,100

1. **The ford in thaw (265).** The sill under thigh-deep brown water. Lira reads it; the crossing.
2. **Thornwater (265, dusk; 266).** The bowman's two fingers. Oryn and her mule. The protected count and the release; Lira undoes the knot herself.
3. **The plank (266).** The protected ask; the ankle; marker for the thanks and its answer; the arm's discharge; Brom's boot.
