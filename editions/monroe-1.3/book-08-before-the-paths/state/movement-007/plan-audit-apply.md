#  2-12, 15-27, 58-63 (Vastin made to walk the whole road). The audit weighed the packet's "the road and the cut walked again on a bad knee" against Movement 3's ledger, but not against CLOSED Movement 6: after the alley (the 290th) the knee is twice its size; he is let down a ladder on a rope, sleeps in a stall, rides the handcart on the 291st-294th and takes a stair on his seat. Nineteen miles on the 295th is not available to that body. The author's answer stands: he rides a tail-board on the level at his own copper, gets down for every hill, walks the whole cut and stands on the leg there, and is put on the salt cart on the 298th by the field's first ruling. The packet's challenge is met by the hills and the cut.
#  39-44, 75-76 (the ages re-derived from a printed first-sheet date). A printed date would have to be subtracted from the present registry year, which is the one operation this book reserves for Cael's and Seln's unspoken sum. The author's corner figure stands as the device; the AUTHOR is to soften what it claims (a count of YEARS from the office's first sheet for the place, not proof of a sheet drawn in every year, and nothing about sheets kept back in a drawer).
#  48-51 (the Millrace count given to a "ford-keeper"). The woman with the net on the ford's stone is this edition's own LOCKED Book 7 (chapter 47: "Five off the ridge."); the author followed the edition, and no role is minted.
#  65-72 (the whole night exchange with Lira cut). Only "You went stiff" contradicts the page; the author re-grounds Lira's knowledge (see brief). The log, which the audit keeps and which Lira reads, already says that something unbidden came.
#  73 ("Twelve. And a mule." taken from Seln). Not protected speech; the plan pins the count only, and Seln's count here prepares his protected "Six. Six and no mule." at the book's end.
