**Review status:** Fresh-context simulated cold read, manuscript only. This is an editorial simulation, not a real audience measurement. I read the six supplied files—chapters 41–46—in their declared order and used no project canon.

**Completeness:** The supplied movement is not presentation-complete. Chapters 43–46 contain sixteen literal `[[TIER-B: … coordinator to supply protected text]]` blocks. ## Unscored reader response

Interest caught in chapter 41 when the skin was turned and the nine-line list appeared. The damaged fourth line gives the research a physical problem, Karis's refusal to convert a plausible word into a finding gives it intellectual stakes, and Cleon's choice to make a second copy gives it human stakes. From there, the strongest hook is not simply *what do the marks mean?* but *what will these exacting people permit themselves to claim?*

Interest slipped first during the long sequence of revisited road landmarks in chapter 42. The bridge, bank, fork-stone and spring all carry emotional accounting, but their similar backward-looking cadence briefly makes the journey feel like a succession of prior-book echoes. The much sharper break comes at the first Tier-B block in chapter 43. From that point onward, production notes repeatedly replace climactic lines—in the cut, at Teague's contract, during Karis's breakthrough, at Oryn's return and after the deep reading. Those interruptions make it impossible to experience several scene landings as written drama.

Vastin matters most in this movement. Cael remains the binding consciousness, but Vastin supplies the most affecting change: he counts his recovery without self-pity, accepts being useful without reclaiming authority, lifts the stick where his wall once stood, learns to sit without payment, and finally adopts another person's word—“Noted”—without turning it into an order. His arc makes the logistical travel feel consequential.

The next event I expect is a field test of the newly combined company before it reaches the oldest site: Teague's file and command, Karis's reading, Vastin's immobile Wall and Cael's twelve carried abilities have been assembled with explicit limits, while the rider behind them remains unresolved. Beyond that immediate pressure, I expect the eastward line to force Cael to decide whether “Still. Set.” is evidence he may share or a warning he must keep.

I would continue. The archive mystery, the practical progression system and the group's restrained care for one another are compelling. I would, however, want the protected-text gaps restored before treating this as a finished reading copy.

## Repair brief

1. **Location:** All sixteen Tier-B blocks: chapter 43 at “Here” and “Two words”; chapter 44 at the contract, the Wall reply and the second-cart reply; chapter 45 throughout the grade discovery, destination decision, Oryn reunion and medical examinations; chapter 46 at Lira's offered leaf. **Observed issue:** Editorial scaffolding replaces protected dialogue and sometimes leaves the speaker undecided. **Effect on the reader:** Climaxes break into production commentary, character ownership becomes uncertain, and the artifact cannot be read aloud or judged as finished prose. **Proposed scope:** Restore the approved protected text with final speaker assignments, remove every bracketed block, and recheck only the two surrounding paragraphs at each join for attribution, tense and cadence. **Strength to preserve:** Keep the spare dialogue and the restraint of the reactions around it; do not expand these landings into explanatory speeches.

2. **Location:** Chapter 45, from the three ruled boxes through “Two hundred and six,” the off-hide divider point and Cael's fork-stone log. **Observed issue:** A dense sequence moves from mark to grade, annual series number, age ordering, bearing, third tier and “four hundred years”; the last two claims exceed what this movement explicitly demonstrates unless they are clearly framed as Cael's inference from prior knowledge. **Effect on the reader:** The central revelation can feel like a conclusion delivered faster than the evidence, weakening trust in the otherwise rigorous discovery method. **Proposed scope:** Add or sharpen one or two evidentiary bridges, or explicitly label the unsupported parts of the log as Cael combining today's finding with knowledge already held. Do not broaden the explanation beyond that chain. **Strength to preserve:** Preserve Karis's refusal to guess, the tactile overlay of two copies, and the divider landing beyond the skin.

3. **Location:** High-load passages in chapters 41 and 46—especially chapter 41 from “The man had not sat down” through the two-copy work, and chapter 46 from “It was at about the three hundredth breath” through the withheld next word. **Observed issue:** Repeated generic referents (“the man,” “the thing,” “it”) occur while several people, powers and objects are simultaneously active. **Effect on the reader:** The intended mystery occasionally becomes grammatical uncertainty, especially by ear or for the younger-reader lens. **Proposed scope:** Make a narrow referent pass, replacing only the few ambiguous pronouns or generic nouns at decision points; use names or established power labels where doing so does not reveal more than Cael knows. **Strength to preserve:** Preserve the close, withheld point of view, the exact material vocabulary and the long/short cadence that makes the discoveries land.
