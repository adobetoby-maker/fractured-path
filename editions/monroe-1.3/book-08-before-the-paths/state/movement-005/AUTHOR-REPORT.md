## Owner flags

1. **The notice's typography and frame.** The protected text of fragment eleven names its source ("Vastin."), counts ("Eleventh.") and gives "Deployment: none", none of which the edition's earlier system blocks do (Book 7's reads `[unnamed]`, and Cael remarks there that it always does). I set it verbatim in a fenced block, as the edition sets system text, and framed it as the lines "put into the plain words in which he would have to write" them, so that nothing says the system itself has begun to name people. If it should be italic log text, or the frame cut, the wording is untouched either way.
2. **How the eleventh came.** It is not reached for (unlike Tide in Book 7). It arrives, as "nine of the ten" did, from standing within a stride of a Path used in earnest, in the minute when he is refusing the other ten. Its question (*This far?*) is mine. Cael notes that he stood a count of twenty by the wall at the cut and a minute and some here, and draws no cause.
3. **"I put the wall in front of it anyway."** The plan does not pin "it". I staged it as what Cael is, which Vastin could not read at four feet and cannot now (his "third head"). If the source's antecedent is otherwise (the healer's order, say), the three sentences before the line want re-composing; the knee and Oryn are his "first head", a few lines earlier.
4. **Seln's case and Book 6.** The map and the packet call the case "unopened" and its contents "unknown". This edition's Book 6 opened it once, on a table above Ostrand, for five people, and Book 7 says only that it has been "carried shut". I wrote *shut* and never *unopened*; no content is named; Seln keeps his Book 6 rule of never describing it, even to himself; Cael's narration says that he knows what is in it and that it does not go into a book.
5. **Who was in the yard on the 285th.** For Seln to answer the clerk he had to be within speech, though he kept off the 284th's count because a clerk might know his face. I put him on the hatch's own bench as a porter waiting for a return load, unseen by the five until he stood. What not lying cost him is that face.
6. **The clerk's difficulty.** The plan gives Vastin's reflex and Seln's answer but not what was at stake at the hatch. Mine: an honest clerk who would not write six boxes for three and was about to send up for the under-registrar. He asks one question in this movement, of the boxes.
7. **Cleon's week (from the 287th).** Minted so that the packet's "three days left of Cleon's week" on the 290th and §13's "seventh day" (the 293rd) are both true. Karis's own "Give me a week" was said to the crew on the 284th.
8. **The purse: 134.** The loft's first week ran out on the 288th. The map's cash track (136, then 130 on the 292nd after a four-mark meal) needs two marks spent between; I paid a second week on the 289th.
9. **The decipherment's method**, the board's figures (a ring for five, a peck for one), the counts **seven / twenty-three / eleven**, and **fourteen letters by the 290th**. The number-words are tested by the pattern of repeated letters, which is given in the story's own tongue as it is everywhere else (twelve, nine, seven; then ten, the number after ten, and four more). No word but a number is read; the bundle is not opened; Vastin's page is not shown and its title is not spoken. If Movement 6 needs her further on or further back, the figures in chapters 31 and 32 are the places.
