## Decisions made before drafting

1. **Deployment stays at thirty-two through the whole movement.** Nothing is used outward. The ten ask (in the yard, on the stair, in the alley) and are told no. The eleventh is acquired and not used: "Deployment: none."
2. **Why loads go up and down daily.** The form's line for hands is "to carry"; a hand with nothing on his back is a person, and a person is asked. Cleon keeps on his tables only what he is reading (the fifty-two first copies stay laid out below from the 284th). The second and third copies, Karis's working paper, the reserve, bread and water go down each morning and come up at the clerk's bell. So each day: card, two gates, lane, cut, yard, hatch, iron door; and back before the fourth bell.
3. **The Deepstacks' ground (mine).** Inside the iron door a stair of the dark stone, twenty-seven steps in three flights of nine with two landings. At its foot a cross-way; three galleries open off it side by side (east: the old town's ledgers; middle: the longest, shelved to the roof, a hundred and ten strides, with the old town's sorting tables down the middle of its far end and Cleon's desk and fixed lamp at the very end; west: the quarry's own, unshelved, tool-marked). The stair and cross-way are lined with the dark dry-laid stone; the galleries are cut in the bluff's pale bedded rock. A small bell on a wire at the stair's foot, pulled by the clerk from the undercroft when the yard is about to shut ("the clerk's bell").
4. **"A man is already reading."** Cleon lets them in, puts the lamp into the nearest free hand, and goes down ahead of them in the dark, which he does not need a light for. By the time the loads reach the middle gallery he is at his desk under his own fixed lamp, and he reads to the end of his page before he gets up. He has cleared three tables; thirty years of his own bundles stand on the floor beside them.
5. **Who holds the lamp ("the lamp trade").** Lira on the stair; Brom at the tables (his shadow, his breathing); Lira again (the slow arm; it moves); from the 285th, Cael, who can be a post. The rule: low, a hand to the left of the line. Cleon speaks to "Lamp", not to its holder; he takes no notice of who it is.

- **Chapter 31.** Karis's count is given on the 288th: seven kinds bear, twenty-three are carried, eleven are nothing. Letters: eight from the board (the 288th, forenoon), two more from the Ring and the Hall (ten, the 288th), three from the Stair and the Hall (thirteen, the 289th), one from the Hall (fourteen, the 290th). She covers a word that is not a number, does not ask for the bundle, and does not show the page. Seln's second sighting is a porter on the kerb opposite the cut's head with a horseman's boots. Vastin holds three tall books at Lira's word and qualifies it ("I cannot undertake to carry them").
- **Chapter 32.** Order going up: Vastin, Cael, Lira, Karis, Brom (so that Lira must pass Karis on the wet tread to reach the landing). Karis's gloves were in her pocket. The hood on the landing lifts two fingers and the lantern is raised for the wall itself; the cord takes the arm before the shutter opens. Compression rises as the old man falls and is told no: the catch is made with arms, a box and Brom's bound arm.
- **Chapter 33.** Seven stitches. Brom re-laths the knee with a slat from his own box lid. Cael's ruling goes no farther than that the stair is not climbed at dusk again; the next day's route is left to Movement 6.
- **Fragments, as written:** Anchor silent on the inner stair (ch27). The read asked in the yard at the dray (told no; ch29). In the alley all ten rose and were told no; Compression rose again at the fall and was told no (ch32). Shadow lifted at dusk on the 285th, the 286th and the 290th (told no). The eleventh asked three times (the step; the loft; the dark) and was told no each time. Nothing was used outward. Deployment thirty-two.
