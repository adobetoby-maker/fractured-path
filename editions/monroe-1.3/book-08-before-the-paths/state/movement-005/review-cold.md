This is a fresh-context **simulated cold read**, not a real audience measurement. I read only the seven supplied manuscript files, chapters 27–33, in order. I had no canon packet or prior chapters, so I can identify apparent inconsistencies but cannot verify canon violations. The two reader perspectives below are editorial lenses, not demographic research or a sales forecast.

## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in chapter 27 when Cleon puts the lamp into Lira's hand, descends twenty-seven steps without light, and leaves the group to infer the rules of his underground archive. The sequence turns clerical habits into suspense: a lamp's position, an honest entry in a green book, or the order of a name and rank can carry as much danger as a weapon. That feels specific and fresh. The first major emotional catch was Seln spending his anonymity to stop Vastin at the hatch in chapter 28; the first major physical catch was Brom lifting the dray without opening his Path in chapter 29.

Interest slipped in two places. First, the unresolved production placeholders repeatedly remove the exact line on which a scene turns: Karis's fear of being believed, Cleon's identification of the carvings as orders, and especially Cleon's end-of-chapter warning about documentation and repeatable construction. Second, the long middle stretch from Cleon's first inspection through Karis's letter-by-letter validation sometimes restates the same methodological virtue—look from the foot, infer only what bears weight, test a reading twice—after the reader has understood it. The process remains interesting, but some repetitions soften forward pressure before the ambush.

Cael matters most to me because his central struggle is both practical and moral: he possesses many ways to act, but repeatedly chooses restraint, accepts being treated as a tool, and learns that stillness can itself be a capability. Karis is close behind. Her refusal to let hope complete an unknown word makes intellectual integrity dramatic rather than decorative. Brom, Lira, Vastin, and Seln all earn attachment through distinct forms of competence and cost.

I expect the next event to combine the three-day deadline with the surveillance threat: Karis will try to complete enough of the alphabet to approach Cleon's taped bundle while the group changes its route or timing to avoid another controlled attack. I also expect Cael's new Wall-adjacent reading and his unpaid debt to Vastin to complicate that effort.

I would continue. The movement gives substantial local payoffs—the archive accepts the evidence, the writing is identified as orders, Karis begins to read the language, the ambush is survived, and Cael gains an eleventh capability—while leaving the attackers, the dark case, Cleon's bundle, and the remaining letters unresolved.
