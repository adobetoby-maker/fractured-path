## C. For you to recompose

1. **Trim the display of the year (ch52, the corner signs; ch53, the reduction).** The mechanism stands: the two corner signs are a year written by its first letters, in the old town's count, by somebody who reckoned so before there was an office to number it; and she has one other year in that count, at the head of a thing that she read aloud once. That is what earns the close's "as old as the record". But the page now works the comparison in front of the reader, and that is the one operation this book keeps for a sum that two people have done and never said. So:
   - **On the blanket (ch52):** she may recognise what the two signs are, and say that she has one other year in that count. She does NOT lay the two side by side in front of anybody, does not say "the same two letters" or "the same hundred", and does not say that she thinks she knows by how much. She says only that she will work it tonight, alone, and say a figure once. The thumbnail ring stands.
   - **In her night (ch53, scene 3):** she works it, and the page does not count with her. No "eleven", no row of candidates, no "fourth from the top". What may stand: that a year written by its first letters leaves a door open; that it is not shown to be the same year and is shown to be near it; that she will say it to the nearest hundred, and "or near it", once; and everything from "It came to her as she blotted the line" onward.
