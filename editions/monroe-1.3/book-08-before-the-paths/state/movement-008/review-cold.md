## Unscored reader response

My interest first caught in chapter 47 when Karis finds that her marker stone has been removed and the turf restored to make the ground agree with an official sheet. That turns a quiet return journey into a forensic problem. It caught much harder in chapter 48 at “Shale-backs. On a hill. Over the only water in three days.” The problem is concrete, the rules are quickly understandable, and the solution requires Cael, Lira, Brom, and Teague to contribute different kinds of judgment. The confrontation at the shelf in chapter 50 then converts the movement’s recurring ideas—counting, restraint, ground-reading, and the difference between a person and an office—into action.

Interest slipped in two places. The first is the opening third of chapter 47, where many remembered sites, day counts, unnamed roles, and prior incidents arrive before a cold reader can reliably sort the twelve travelers. The second is the long evidence assembly in chapter 52. Its discoveries are strong, but “the hide,” “the flat,” several classes of sheets, tapes, columns, seals, the brass, and the folded paper compete for working memory. The unresolved bracketed instructions cause a more basic break: they repeatedly turn story back into an editing session.

Cael matters most to me. His emerging competence is not merely an increase in force; it is an increase in judgment. He learns to read ground, holds Teague’s left for the promised three breaths, receives a dangerous new capability, refuses to fire it, recognizes where its cost would land, and keeps other people’s secrets even when disclosure might ease his own burden. Karis is nearly as important because she supplies the movement’s intellectual and ethical standard: evidence may point toward a wanted conclusion, but she will not create the missing line herself.

I expect the next event to be the approach through the square gap and entry into—or confrontation at—the oldest site on the line. I also expect Cael’s Force-adjacent capability and vulnerable ribs to become an immediate tactical problem, not merely a later progression note. I would continue. The world’s material specificity, the group’s quiet attachments, and the promise of the oldest site all pull strongly, but I would want the missing protected dialogue restored before treating this as a finished reading experience.

## Manuscript-only consistency checks

### Physical state

The visible injury and recovery chain is internally coherent across the supplied chapters. Vastin advances from twenty of thirty to twenty-six of thirty and refuses to use his wall; Teague’s recoil travels from wrist toward elbow and leaves the arm splinted; the bow’s previously good leg breaks, is knitted, boarded, and then bears weight only with a stick; Brom’s shoulder reaches its stated day; Cael’s collarbone bruise and old ribs affect later choices. I found no definite physical-state contradiction.

One apparent count ambiguity deserves a check rather than a canon-error label. Chapter 47 says “when eleven people stood,” later “nobody among the twelve,” and chapter 49 says “There were twelve on it now.” Chapter 48’s “There were eleven now” appears capable of meaning eleven companions in addition to Cael, but the inclusion rule changes without being explicit. Confidence that this is a real numerical contradiction is low; confidence that it can momentarily confuse a cold reader is high.

### Power limits

The movement establishes and honors useful limits. Sigils disappear at a fixed stride inside the iron and return at the same stride; the shale-backs make use of a Path too dangerous; Teague’s Force requires contact, alignment, bracing, and bodily recoil; Cael’s acquired analogue listens at close range, needs a line through his frame, and threatens his old ribs. Cael repeatedly declines available power, so solutions remain grounded in observation, positioning, consent, and teamwork. No definite limit violation appears within this coverage.

The distinction between a Path or sigil and Cael’s thirteen acquired things is inferable but not fully recoverable for a cold reader. His statement that “there was nothing in him that a line could take” can coexist with thirteen abilities because the log calls them “things that aren’t a Path,” but a brief local reminder would prevent this from reading as a contradiction.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully partitioned. Cael tells Lira and later Brom about Teague’s thirteenth but withholds it from Teague; Teague recognizes Cael’s restraint without learning what Cael took; Vastin knows the two seals but refuses to name them; Karis records the presence of the seals without guessing; Karis also refuses to bridge her crossing data to the scraped statement without evidence. I found no clear leak across those boundaries.

## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Every `[[TIER-B: ...]]` block in chapters 49–53, especially chapter 50’s crossing sequence, chapter 51’s arm warning, chapter 52’s seal exchange, and chapter 53’s final lamp scene. **Observed issue:** Protected dialogue is absent and coordinator instructions remain inside the manuscript; the final scene withholds the movement’s actual closing exchange. **Effect on the reader:** Immersion breaks, speaker intent becomes uncertain at tactical and thematic turning points, and the movement reads as unfinished. **Proposed scope:** Restore only the approved protected lines, assign speakers consistently with the surrounding staging, remove the bracketed notes, and recheck the immediate paragraph joins; do not rewrite the scenes around them. **Strength to preserve:** The spare dialogue, moral restraint, and escalation created by what characters choose not to declare.

2. **Location:** Chapter 47’s opening file inventory; chapter 48’s shale-back sequence; chapter 50 from “Brom walked” through the fall; chapter 51’s hut aftermath. **Observed issue:** Rapid switching among names and descriptors—“the boy,” “the tall man,” “the broad man,” “the bow,” “the old man,” and “the old woman”—makes identity tracking harder than the physical action itself, particularly because Cael is also young and Vastin is named only intermittently. **Effect on the reader:** A cold reader may attach an injury, choice, or relationship beat to the wrong person and lose some of the carefully carried consequences. **Proposed scope:** Add a small number of name-plus-role anchors at scene re-entry and before the fastest action, and make the eleven/twelve inclusion rule explicit once; avoid globally replacing the role language. **Strength to preserve:** The occupational, observational way Cael perceives people and the motif of individuals emerging from institutional roles.

3. **Location:** Chapter 52, from “She asked for Brom’s blanket” through the two slips under the oldest row’s knot. **Observed issue:** The evidence scene introduces or reactivates many similar physical referents—hide, flat, clean sheet, copied sheets, taped rows, brass, folded paper, ruled leaf, columns, and seals—while also asking the reader to follow orientation, chronology, and inference. **Effect on the reader:** The crucial distinction between what is measured, what is copied, what is withheld, and what remains only a hypothesis can blur, reducing the force of Karis’s rigor. **Proposed scope:** Add two or three compact orienting reminders at the moments when the blanket’s function changes: first reconstruction, then dating, then institutional suppression. Keep the evidence and conclusions unchanged. **Strength to preserve:** The unusual pleasure of watching disciplined inquiry become dramatic action, especially Karis’s refusal to draw a line the evidence has not earned.
