### 2. The evidence sequence is intellectually strong but audio-dense

Chapter 52, especially lines 117–221, asks the reader to track several physical documents and several meanings of “page” at once. The logical chain is sound: the old year is narrowed, the lamp behavior is separated from its cause, Karis moves her measured leaf away from the folded testimony, and Cael keeps a third page off the blanket. On paper, the spatial metaphor rewards attention. For a thirteen-year-old listening, “the hide,” “the clean sheet,” “six sheets,” “the notebook,” “the ruled leaf,” “a folded paper,” “two pages,” and “a third page” can momentarily blur object, document, and category.

The answer is not to simplify the discovery or collapse the two pages; their separation is the moral and epistemic center of the chapter. A very small orientation pass—reasserting which physical object sits at which end of the blanket at one or two transitions, and ensuring pronouns have unmistakable antecedents—would preserve the reasoning while lowering working-memory load.

### 3. Story and character architecture are working

Teague's field knowledge becomes Cael's bodily lesson; Karis's discipline stops inference from becoming fact; Vastin's history becomes useful without making him the solver; and the young one earns attachment through deeds. The best choice is Cael's refusal to test Force in chapter 53. It makes progression costly and ethical, preserves Teague's knowledge boundary, and converts a new ability into character rather than fireworks.
