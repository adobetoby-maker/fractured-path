# Movement 006 cold-read review

## Read status and coverage

This is a **fresh-context simulated cold read**, not a real audience measurement. I read only the seven supplied manuscript chapters, Chapters 34–40, in their declared order. I had no project canon. The thirteen-year-old and adult responses below are editorial lenses, not demographic research or a sales forecast.

The scores assess the manuscript exactly as supplied, including the visible `[TIER-B: …]` coordination blocks. Those blocks materially depress clarity and read-aloud scores because they replace several of the movement's most important lines with editorial instructions.

## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in Chapter 34's opening image: Vastin being lowered from the loft “like a sack of meal that nobody wished to bruise.” The handcart route then turns administration into pressure—the card, the head count, the weighing beam, and the clerk's decision all make ordinary passage feel dangerous without manufacturing a fight. Interest rose again when Cleon taught Karis how an instruction “leans,” when Cael acquired the twelfth fragment, and through the long night-follow in Chapter 36. The strongest pull is the archive sequence in Chapters 38–40: the opened bundle, the scraped name, the discipline of the two pages, and the yard fire turn a historical discovery into a personal reckoning.

Interest slipped in two places. First, the opening pages ask a cold reader to hold the cut, the crown, the card's “four,” the boxes, the plate, and several injuries before the movement supplies even a light reminder of who these people are and why the archive matters. The physical action remains readable, but the strategic meaning arrives late. Second—and far more seriously—the bracketed Tier-B blocks interrupt or replace the exact lines on which major turns depend. The acquisition of the twelfth, the leaf's text, Vastin's confession, Cael's declaration, and Lira's answer are presented partly as staging notes. A reader can infer the intended scene, but cannot experience its final dramatic rhythm.

The Oryn cutaway near the end of Chapter 37 briefly delays the archive opening. It nevertheless earns its place for me: the fourteen-year-old's Kindling provides a clean emotional counterimage to Cael's, keeps Oryn alive as an independent person, and sets the healer reunion in motion. A younger reader may feel the interruption more strongly because the unopened bundle is already waiting.

Cael matters most. His power is interesting, but his repeated choice not to use it—at Cleon's board, at the rope-walk, among the barrels, and at the barred gate—is what creates attachment. Lira is the person who makes his interior conclusions human and immediate. Cleon and Vastin also become more than functions: Cleon's deliberate refusal to look and Vastin's refusal to complete an unsupported conclusion are small, credible acts of moral change.

I expect the next movement to read the back of the leaf and its “list of parts,” establish the age or nature of the oldest site at the end of Karis's bearing, reunite the injured group with Oryn at the mill, and test whether the cut-boot watcher is already ahead of them on the eastern road.

I would continue. That answer is strong for the underlying story and provisional for the current artifact: the movement needs its coordination blocks converted into finished manuscript prose before it can deliver the climax it has built.

## Consistency and boundary check

### Canon

No editorial canon evidence was supplied, so I cannot verify canon violations, plan adherence, or reserved revelations against the project record. Nothing below should be treated as a canon finding.

### Physical state

The movement's visible physical continuity is strong. Vastin's swollen bound knee governs the handcart, the archive stairs, the stall, the mounting block, and his backward climb. Karis consistently protects the cut left hand until fatigue or concentration makes her use it. Brom's damaged arms restrict lifting while still allowing him to lean, pull, and use his wrists. Lira's slow arm appears in practical actions rather than disappearing. Cael's eye-cost begins after the twelfth's use, returns with later involuntary readings, and affects how far he holds the book from his face. I found no apparent contradiction within the supplied chapters.

### Power limits

The twelfth is unusually well bounded in Chapter 35's internal catalogue: it reads a made thing's structural “lean,” not meaning, rivers, or people; it costs the eyes; and it has no direct fight use. Later uses respect those limits—the rope-walk, dividers, and table all reveal structure, not semantic content. Its involuntary attention creates a new problem rather than free competence. Iron Wall and Shadow remain offered but undeployed in the pursuit, and the barred gate preserves the difference between what Cael could cross physically and what he chooses not to cross lawfully. No apparent limit breach is visible.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully partitioned. Cael tells Cleon only how this fragment came, without naming the other sources. Cleon's warning is kept out of Hesk's book and from Lira, which produces believable friction rather than accidental leakage. The leaf is first known to Karis, Cleon, Cael, and Vastin; the fair copy then carries its wording to Lira, Brom, and Seln. Oryn's cutaway does not grant her archive knowledge. Karis repeatedly separates the leaf's wording from inference through the “two pages” rule. Within the manuscript, these boundaries hold.

### Reserved disclosures and manuscript artifacts

Because no canon was supplied, I cannot judge whether the historical revelation occurs at the intended series point. I can judge the artifact itself: the visible Tier-B blocks expose BOOK_MAP/packet references, protected-text handling, staging alternatives, and coordinator instructions inside the reading copy. They also reveal intended interpretations before the finished dialogue earns them. This is not an apparent story contradiction; it is an unequivocal manuscript-completion defect.

## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Every `[TIER-B: …]` block in Chapters 35, 37, 38, 39, and 40, especially the twelfth's trigger, Cleon's warning, the full leaf, Vastin's fire confession, Cael's declaration, and Lira's answer. **Observed issue:** Coordination notes and elided protected text remain in place of finished manuscript dialogue. **Effect on the reader:** The drafting process becomes visible, crucial lines are read as instructions rather than lived speech, and the movement's progression and emotional climaxes cannot fully land. **Proposed scope:** Replace each block with its authorized final wording, then read the joins immediately before and after each replacement for pronoun, attribution, tense, and cadence only; do not restructure the surrounding scenes. **Strength to preserve:** The existing staging, silence, physical reactions, and sequence of who speaks are exceptionally strong.

2. **Location:** Chapter 34, from “We're done with the cut” through the card count, weighing beam, and archive hatch. **Observed issue:** A cold reader meets several inherited rules and nouns before receiving a compact statement of the day's objective and the roles attached to the card. **Effect on the reader:** The physical trip is clear, but the meaning of success or exposure must be reconstructed while new information continues to arrive; the younger-reader lens may attach a chapter late. **Proposed scope:** Add only a few unobtrusive orientation cues—who the card legally covers, what must reach the lower archive today, and why the carts' street is the chosen risk. Avoid a recap paragraph. **Strength to preserve:** The in-medias-res confidence and the conversion of bureaucracy into suspense.

3. **Location:** Chapter 37, transition from Cael's loft ruling (“The promise stands. Not the steps. Not the square.”) to Oryn at the fourth gate, then Chapter 38's return to the archive. **Observed issue:** The only POV cutaway begins just as the unopened archive bundle has become urgent, with little transition beyond the scene break. **Effect on the reader:** The thematic mirror is valuable, but a momentum-driven reader may experience it first as a delay or briefly need to reorient to person, place, and time. **Proposed scope:** Keep the Kindling scene and Oryn's decision intact; add a crisp temporal/location handoff and consider tightening only the approach-to-the-bench setup, not the ceremony or her realization. **Strength to preserve:** The humane contrast between a child whose Kindling is witnessed and Cael's unwitnessed one, plus the concrete promise that Oryn is coming to the mill.
