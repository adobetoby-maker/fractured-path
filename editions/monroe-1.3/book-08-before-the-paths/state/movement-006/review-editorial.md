## Reader Standard

The intended story passes the thirteen-year-old standard. There is no sexual content, profanity problem, graphic gore, or cruelty presented as admirable. Injury has consequence without anatomical indulgence. Moral goodness is active rather than decorative: Cael refuses invasive power use, tells Cleon the truth, honors Oryn's boundary, and does not turn uncertainty into a verdict; Lira requires accountability and then gives care; Brom repeatedly prices real bodily cost; Karis refuses to read into a hole.

The conceptual material is demanding but age-legible because abstractions are repeatedly attached to objects: a leaning row, a table leg, dividers, a covered cup, a hole in paper, and a fire basket. The main clarity risk for a thirteen-year-old is not vocabulary. It is the combination of indirect character labels (“the man,” “the old man,” “the small pale woman”) with dense institutional reasoning, especially in the chapter 38 chamber. That remains manageable in the finished prose but becomes impossible at the current marker interruptions. Resolve those rather than simplifying the ideas.
