## Decisions made before drafting

1. **Compass (flag).** The plan's §8 calls the pass's walls "west" (scree and shelf) and "east" (sheer). The bearing runs east through the notch, so on the page the scree-and-shelf wall is the **north** wall (the left hand, going in) and the sheer wall the **south**. Everything else in §8 is kept.
2. **The pass's ground (private staging).** A square gap in a ridge of rock, cut by old water; a bed of loose stones a hundred strides long and eight wide, with a shallow standing pool under the south wall just inside the west mouth (it is why Seln comes back wet to the ankle). South wall sheer, four times a man. North side: a steep bank of loose scree lying on a slab of bare rock "like a quilt on a table", up to a **shelf** two-thirds of the way up; above the shelf, rock, and the ridge. On the shelf: the **first stack** twenty strides in, a wall of loose stone held on the lip by a timber brace with one wedge, and a standing **dead pine** at the lip roped to the brace; the **second stack** sixty strides in, braced alike; between and behind them a hollow in the back wall where the team keeps its fire. At the east end (ninety strides in) a **stair** of broken rock goes from the bed up to the shelf. From the shelf's east end a cleft goes up to the ridge, and the team's own **path** runs along the ridge: it comes up from the western foothills half a mile north of the mouth and goes down again east of the gap. Whoever holds the shelf holds the path. The braces' timber is two cold terms grey: the rig was not built for these twelve.
3. **Seln's count:** nine, "and I'll not swear that it isn't ten"; three crossbows; the seven of the Span among them by a wet cough and an empty-handed man. Hooded; no faces.
4. **Why through.** Back gives the pass to the people who keep it. The path goes in file under three bows with no roof, and passes within a stave's length of the shelf. Five miles of ridge either hand is hands-and-knees rock, and there are two sticks and a mule. In the bed there can be a roof. **[[TIER-B: "It's through."]]** stands where the sum comes out (speaker not pinned).
5. **How the wall comes about (the honest account; mine).** Teague reads the rig as one stroke: bows wound; stacks kill a crew; a stack that has fallen is a heap. He wants it spent on nothing. He looks at the drawing, and not at the old man: he said at a bench that he would not ask a thing with a month on it. Cael does not ask either: it is not his knee, not his word and not his field. **Brom** says what a lean-to is (a roof that is leaned on a hill is carried by the hill). **Vastin asks leave of the person who set the term**: he does not break it; he asks that the entry of the 303rd be amended by the one entitled, for the length of a defile, and says that he will not do it otherwise. **Oryn** asks ("May I?"), reads the knee, and sets the price: he does it **sitting**, with the leg out straight, so that what comes down through him goes into the ground through his seat and not through her laths; it will take everything else that he has; he will not get up afterward; the month stands as counted. He has never stood a wall otherwise than standing and does not know that it can be done. "Then you'll find out." Nobody votes. **What it costs him:** an Archmarshal sits down in a stream-bed with his back to the mouth and holds a roof; each blow comes down through him; the fall of a whole stack comes down through him; he cannot move when a boy dies a stride outside what he is holding; he is spent to the stones when it is let go. The knee is not in it: read afterward, it has held.
6. **The slant (flag: a seated wall is mine).** He sits a yard inside the west mouth, facing east up the bed, the stiff leg out, and brings the stick's foot down behind his own hip. The line runs across the mouth from the south wall to the foot of the scree. The plane rises from it, leaning eastward over the bed, and its upper northern corner lies against the scree twenty-five strides in, at the height of three men: a lean-to with its low edge at the mouth behind them and its high edge against the scree wall ahead. Under it there is a wedge of shelter twenty-five strides long. It is seen by the dust that lies on it. What falls on it is turned and slides down it westward, out over the old man's head and into the mouth. The open end (the gable, three men high) faces east; bows that move east along the shelf can shoot in under it for the first eight or ten strides, so "behind it" also means deep under it.
7. **Positions as the wall goes up.** All twelve go in at the mouth in a knot along the scree's foot; the mule is left hobbled in a nook outside the mouth, with the three boxes on her (Karis keeps the flat, the hide and her notebook on her own back; Teague will not have a beast under a rig). Brom walks in front and then turns to lower the old man to the stones. At the gable end: Teague, with Cael at his left a stride back; the tall man; Teague's boy; the broad man, with his split bow lashed by the tall man with a thong ("It'll loose twice"); Seln at the scree's foot; Brom when he has done. In the middle: Oryn and the lean old woman. At the low end with the old man: Lira (Teague's order: nothing gets to the plane's foot) and Karis. So the girl's boots are twenty strides behind the boy.
8. **The doctrine, told once** (at the shale camp, over the drawn earth, to Teague's four): Cael, "Behind it. Noted?"; Teague's boy, "Noted," and the whole-head nod. **The tall man speaks to the six for the first time:** "And then?" Cael's true answer: he has drawn as far as the wall; past it there are three bows on a shelf and a stair at the far end, and he has no "and then"; the field's owner has. Teague: he will say when he has seen it.
9. **The seven volleys (Cael counts each; a span takes something over twenty).**
   - **One:** at the mouth, before anything is up. Brom has both hands under the old man's arms, lowering him; his skin is not grey. A bolt goes through the flesh of the left forearm. He finishes setting the man down.
   - **Two:** Vastin strikes; the plane stands; three bolts break on it over their heads. Dust.
   - **Three:** aimed through the plane at the seated man; broken. The team has learned that it is a roof. A hood goes to the first brace with a maul.
   - **Four, and the fall:** the wedge is struck out as the fourth volley is loosed. The first stack and the roped pine come down the scree, strike the slant, are turned, and go down the plane westward over the old man's head and out into the mouth, which they choke with a heap as high as a man and the pine across it like a bar. **The whole skin of the scree goes with it: the slab is bare.** It comes down through Vastin "like a house"; the plane stands. He gives his count: a hundred breaths, perhaps.
   - Seln, at the scree's foot, looks at bare rock and goes up it without a word. Teague sees him go: "Bow." The broad man is already limping to the gable's southern corner; he looses up at the lip to keep three bows looking at him.
   - **Five:** the bows have moved east along the shelf to shoot in under the high edge. The broad man is hit in the right shoulder at the slant's high edge and goes down with his head and arms outside it.
   - He looks back under the roof for his own, and his eyes find the boy. Nobody has given an order. The tall man's hand, two yards off, closes on air. **Teague's boy goes out a stride to drag him.**
   - **Six:** it comes while he has his hands under the man's arms, **a stride outside the plane.** Brief; no wound described. He sits down all at once, as he sat down in a hut at the Span, and does not get up. The broad man, one-armed, drags the boy back under the roof by the collar. **Oryn** asks "May I?" of a boy who cannot answer; the page says that it is the one time she has gone in without a word; her hands are on him and come off with nothing done. **Vastin cannot move**; he has watched it down the length of his own roof. **The broad man says the name, once:** narrated by its effect (Cael has eaten at one fire with that boy and never heard it; it is said as a man calls somebody in; nobody says it a second time); no sound of it is rendered.
   - Cael sees the hood with the maul go to the **second** brace, sixty strides in, above the bed that anybody making for the stair must cross. He tells Teague that he will see to the brace, and that nobody will hear it. Teague does not ask how. Brom, grey, walks out into the gable's mouth and stands there.
   - **Seven:** loosed at Brom ("That's three."). **Seln goes over the lip** as it is loosed, behind three empty bows. Teague, the tall man and Cael run the bed inside that span.
10. **The first use of Force (counted).** Cael leaves them at fifty-five strides and goes up the bare slab on his hands under the second stack; he opens nothing on the climb. On the shelf the wedge is half out and the stack has begun to lean; the hood has jumped back against the inner wall. Cael sets his right heel in a crack of the shelf and his palm on the brace's end-post, looks first for anybody along the line (nobody), lines himself as he did on the shale, and gives it the word. No light, no sound but the stone's. **The stack and its brace go a body's length eastward along the shelf, all of a piece, into the hollow where the team kept its fire, and come apart there against the back wall; not a stone goes over the lip.** Teague and the tall man are on the bed underneath when it goes. **The recoil comes back along the line and stops in the old three ribs, low on the left.** He is on his knees on the shelf, without breath. **Deployment: thirty-four.** Never at a person.
11. **The breaking.** Seln's weighted cord is on a bowman's arms; no bow is spanned. **Teague comes up the stair left-handed**, the dead arm on its slat. **The tall man comes up behind him with the spear reversed**, the ash butt foremost and the steel behind. The rig is gone and the shelf has four strangers on it. The team goes up the cleft to the ridge and away east along it. The tall man is within a pole's length of the last back, and lets the butt down. Teague stops at the cleft's foot. **None taken. Nobody strikes a person who is leaving. No face seen.** Teague finds a boy holding his left side and a heap in a hollow; he did not see it go, and does not ask.
12. **Win sentence (mine, to §8's sense):** Seln took the high ground from the team by going up the one slope that its own rig had swept bare for him; and an old man sitting in a stream-bed bought him the time to climb it.
13. **After.** Everybody goes out from under by the gable; Lira tells the old man that they are gone; he lets it go, and the dust that lay on nothing comes down in one sheet. **Vastin goes down** (over onto his side on the stones). **Karis sits with her bound hand on his shoulder.** **Teague kneels by his own dead:** "A wall keeps everyone behind it, and one of mine wasn't." (to Cael, who told the doctrine; without blame, and the harder for that). Oryn shuts Brom's forearm and the broad man's shoulder, each asked first. **The far mouth: [[TIER-B: the line "was not ink"]]** closes chapter 55.
14. **The burial (Teague's order; mine).** At the east mouth, on a flat of rock above the bed, in sight of the plain. The lean old woman washes the face with the boy's own cloth and folds the cloth into his hands. The tall man lays the bed of the cairn. The broad man sets the first stone with his one arm. Teague sets the last, left-handed. **Brom asks; Teague: "Carry."** The six carry stone from the bed to the three who build, and lay none. Vastin cannot carry; he is got to his feet by Karis and stands bareheaded for all of it ("He was a stride from it." / "You could not have moved it." / "No. That is the nature of the thing. I have never before seen what it costs somebody else."). No name is said. Teague begins a number and shuts his mouth on it.
15. **Seln's cutaway (two scenes): the ridge path with Lira, for the mule.** From above: the swept slab, the dead pine across the choked mouth, the old man's place under a film of dust. The shelf's leavings: nine bedding-places and a tenth unused, bolts with no thread, a maul, grey timber. His finding, given as a finding: at the Span they were told to hold and to see; here somebody had been told to stop a crew; between the two, seven people carried east what they saw at a fence. His own sum (he went over the lip one volley after the sixth; there was no way up before the fourth; it comes out the same each time). Lira: "He watched my boots. I was at the back." The mule is asleep on three legs with her nose in the bag, the only one of twelve who never knows what happened. The case is on his chest, shut; nothing about it altered; no decision. They bring her over by the path at dusk. He counts the fire as he comes down to it: eleven, and a mule.
16. **Dusk, the plain's edge.** Brom lays seven bowls; nobody lays the far side; he gets up and lays four there himself, and stands with a twelfth in his hand; the lean old woman takes it from him and puts it in her pack. The tall man brings the hot cloth for a captain's arm; Teague holds the arm out for it. Then **Oryn: "Ribs. May I?" / "Yes." / "That's a line," she said. "That's not yours." / "It's mine now."** He tells her: the thirteenth, the captain's, at the timbers, not told; first let go this morning, on a brace, at nobody. **No term tonight** (she will not set a thing in order to have it broken; ask her when he has stood where he is going).
17. **Oryn's cutaway (two scenes), the night of the 306th.** People have died on her round before she came and after she went; never with her hands on them. She asked a boy who could not answer and went in without it; her hands came off with nothing done. Two firsts. Six days past her cairn; three holds; she stayed for an edge. The ribs: a thirteenth current, a captain's, and a line that leaves a boy at three old breaks. What she knows of the place in him is what she said at a mill, and no more. **She has not heard the leaf and thinks of none. Nothing of the twelfth's words.** The lean old woman comes with the boy's second cloth wrung hot and lays it over Oryn's two hands; no word; the healers' fourth exchange.
18. **The instruments (last light of the 306th; before the turning, since eyes are wanted).** Cael breaks a hold of a hundred and thirty-eight days and says so: tomorrow somebody paces the oldest one, and he wants to know what his hands are worth on a cord before he stands by it. Karis lays the Lowmarch measure on the flat rock with the kit's cord from Vastin's case: one length and four knots and two strides, forty-two, between two stones (Karis, then Lira: forty-two). Cael: **forty-one**, three times: **a stride short**, where it was "forty-one and a piece" at Lowmarch. The edges, against her needle-pricks and the hair in her notebook: the Ternhall **a hair and a half** short in his hand, Hesk's **two** hairs long; true in hers. Karis enters it: cause not found; larger than on the 168th; no inference. **He draws no line.**
19. **The Arbiter.** He looks where it has been since he was fourteen, a hand's breadth from the left side of his face: dark, as on every day since a plate lit late; nothing in it to go quiet. Unchanged.
20. **The twelfth turned inward on purpose (not counted: a reading of himself, as the five with the tenth were not).** He goes over the warning as it was given, without its words being printed again. His leave: Oryn's own ruling at the mill (not on her plank; the road and the eyes are his), and he asks himself whether he is again reading somebody's words for the leave he wants, and answers that this time the words say it. He lets it lean. *Still.* *Set.* Then he does not stop the next: *Bones.* Something in it bears and something is carried, as on a floor. Then no more single words: what comes is a shape, and he has to find his own words for it: **a floor with nothing built on it; an instruction with no language** (it leans as a cut row leans, and there is not a sign on it). **[[TIER-B: "The same kind of thing, to that instrument, as the sites were to Cleon's."]]** He tells no one. The eyes pay for an hour.
21. **The log of the 306th:** my head lines (day; place; purse; Vastin twenty-seven; Oryn six days; the pass; eleven); **the S22 log, verbatim, in italics, once**; my count lines (the push: thirty-three and one, **thirty-four**; the inward look not counted; thirteen). **Lira and the book:** she sees him writing by feel with his eyes running and knows what bills them. He holds the book out and asks her not to read that leaf until after tomorrow. She puts her palm on the shut cover and pushes it back: she said that she would wait for the road, and she will not read ahead. So the finding is in a book that nobody has opened, and he has told no one.
22. **The 307th: the plain and the court.** Seven miles from the east mouth by mid-morning, eleven and a mule. The shale's steps lose their edges; for the last mile the rock is worn round and low and slopes in from every side, as a sill is hollowed. The court lies in the bottom of that: a floor of the grey, bare and level, a little over half a mile across, holding the light. Its edge is one sunk line, sharp as the day it was cut. **Eleven stumps** of the grey stand a stride inside the line, each no higher than a milking stool (the Fallow has eleven uprights; nobody says what that means). A band of cut courses, twenty strides deep, runs round inside the stumps; inside the band the grey is bare to the middle. Eight old peg-holes outside the rim, where an office sets its stations and draws them again.
23. **The pacing (two scenes; the arithmetic).** Round the outside of the line with the kit's cord: Lira carries the lead end **on the line** itself, heel and toe; Seln brings the tail up to her mark; Karis calls each length aloud; Cael keeps the tally in the book and does not touch the cord; Vastin stands at the first stump as the mark that the round must close on. **A hundred and fifty-eight lengths and nineteen strides round** (3,179 strides), closing on the old man's stick within a stride. By the round's rule (three and a seventh): **1,011 or 1,012 strides across — fifty lengths and twelve strides, "about fifty cords"; about eleven hundred and seven yards.** Half of it: **five hundred and six strides to the middle.** Trueness: a single length stretched straight between two points of the line stands off it at the middle knot by a span and two fingers, at each of eight places: **sharp and perfect.** Four hours; no water on that rock; Teague and the spear keep the rim.
24. **The cuts.** Karis on her knees outside the line, reading the first courses without crossing: one hand only; cut a finger deep where the Stair's were a nail deep; at the Stair she had three strata of cutting, and the lowest of them, the one under everything, is the only one here. **[[TIER-B: "The others are copies."]]** (speaker not pinned). **Both edges:** she lays the Ternhall brass through the four rings and asks the loan of Hesk's for the last two (the fourth ring and the point where they now stand, which she inks at last, having had a cord round it): the two edges make one line. **The bearing ends: no sixth** — no sheet, no sign-group, no run written from this place to another, and nothing on the plain eastward that Lira's eyes can find. Karis leaves her hand's breadth of pencil as it is and writes *no sixth found*. **The log is written standing** (mine: day, place, purse, twenty-eight, seven days, the round's figure, no sixth, thirty-four).
25. **The council.** Seln: the team's marks went north along the ridge's foot; no fire on the plain. Cael gives Teague **the same three sentences that he gave him behind a brow** (narrated, not reprinted) **and one more** (mine): "And if they're on that plain, they're let off it." Teague, one short: he heard it the first time; he would not have heard it yesterday at noon. Karis's reading, asked for: what four such places have done; she cannot say that the first does as its copies do; one at a time, a stride in and a stride out.
26. **At the line (dusk; two scenes).** By count. **Lira:** over and back, "Right." **Brom:** over; he puts his palm on a stump and prices it (he has put a wedge to that grey; what took this down was weather, and he has no number that long). **Karis:** "It's the first one." **Seln** gives the count (each of them at the one stride, and whole again at the one stride). **Vastin: [[TIER-B: his line, with its elision]]**. **Oryn** stands at the line as she stood at a white stone on the Fallow, with her hands held out over it; steps in one stride and back: **[[TIER-B: "Nothing," … "Everything, round it." … "Same as you."]]**; it goes unanswered and is not explained. **Teague** in one stride and back: **[[TIER-B: "My crew's stood at four of these. It's stood at enough." … "Field's mine. This isn't field."]]** He does not put his three over. He lifts two fingers; the tall man turns his spear the other way: **the turning begins.** Then Lira at Cael's shoulder: **[[TIER-B: the S23 close]]** ends chapter 58.
27. **The centre (chapter 59).** Five hundred and six strides, counted. The band of courses under his boots for twenty; then bare grey. **At a hundred strides** he kneels and turns the twelfth on the floor, on purpose (**counted: thirty-five**): the same reading as the night before, in the same order. The eyes pay; he walks the rest half blind, by count, on a floor that holds the light after the sky has let it go. At five hundred and six there is nothing to mark it. Nothing happens. The thirteen go round what they have always gone round — and he cannot find where it stops. **No edge. No seam.** Aloud, alone: **"Not the Quiet." "Not a fragment." "The same kind of made."** For everyone else a line is the last step of a stair that is not there; for him every floor in the world has been a step short, and here the foot arrives. He fit. It was not an accident. **He does not say, write or think the next sentence.** He counts the strides back: five hundred and six.
28. **The line again.** Oryn: "May I?" / "Yes." — standing, two hands, and nothing of his turned while hers are on him — "The same finding." Lira: "Well?" / "I'll tell you on the road," … "If there's road." He gives them the count instead: five hundred and six, in and out. **[[TIER-B: "Twenty-nine tomorrow," / "Thirty's the month,"]]** (speakers not pinned).
29. **Vastin's cutaway (two scenes): the court's rim at night.** The line he never asked about. The second time in his life that the thing beside his face went out, and came back to the grain. The boy who died a stride from his wall; he sat. What he is at dawn, when a contract ends at the end of its line: on nobody's count; he carries a tarred case that nobody has taken back, and he will walk where it goes until somebody says otherwise. **He does not ask, does not mean to ask, and nobody has spoken of it.** He knows that he is not told, and that it is six people's. No arithmetic of dates; the sentence about the apparatus is not finished in his head; the seals not named.
30. **The ledger and the letter (night of the 307th).** My head lines; **the S24 ledger, both parts, verbatim, in italics**; my lines: deployment (thirty-three; the brace, thirty-four; the floor at a hundred strides, thirty-five; himself not counted), purse 123 and nothing owed in money; two debts that are not money, to a captain and an old man, unpaid. **The letter to Hesk, verbatim, in italics, once**, with no setup for its fourth sentence.
31. **Dawn of the 308th.** Teague, a tall man, a broad man on a stick and a lean old woman: he takes the letter left-handed; **[[TIER-B: Teague's departure]]**; they go west, toward a gap with a cairn at its mouth; he does not say a number. Oryn, eight days past her cairn, takes the boxes off the mule: **[[TIER-B: Oryn's departure]]**; she goes north on the mule. The boxes go onto backs: Brom, Lira, Cael. The old man takes up the tarred case; nobody tells him to and nobody tells him not to. **Seln: "Six. Six and no mule. Nothing behind. Nothing beside."** / **"West," Cael said.** / **"West," said Brom, and picked up the box.** Then **the closing prose, verbatim, last.**

## Guards

- The young one is never named and no sound of his name is rendered; nobody in Teague's crew is named.
- No possessive before "Path" in narration. No season, month or weekday name. Feet, yards, miles, paces, strides, cords. No count of years since the Kindling; nineteen; no age for Vastin.
- *Still* / *set* and what follows them: told to nobody. The leaf: quoted by nobody; Oryn has not heard it. No line from the leaf to the lamps finding in any mouth or head. Never *terminal*, *circuit*, *infrastructure*; never *recurrence*, *primordial*, *source*, *tomb*; "the court" lowercase. "Four hundred years" only inside the closing prose. "The Architect" nowhere.
- The site does nothing and wants nothing; no maker's present will; the instruments' trend has no cause given. The third sentence is nowhere but in the closing prose's own words.
- Every reading has consent on the page; where it cannot be asked, the page says so.
- Vastin does not ask and is not asked; no vote; nothing of what comes after. Teague is not told. Seln's case is "shut", on his chest, contents unnamed, no count of years.
- No cutaway at the court by Oryn.

## Changes made while drafting

(Written after the draft. Where the list above and the chapters differ, the chapters stand.)

- **Chapter boundaries.** 54 ends with the roof up and the second flight on it; 55 holds flights three to seven, the push, the breaking, the release and the far mouth; 56 the burial, Seln's two scenes, the bowls and the ribs; 57 Oryn's two scenes, the instruments, the inward turn and the log; 58 the plain, the round, the cuts, the council and the line, ending on the S23 marker; 59 the centre, the line again and Vastin's two scenes; 60 the ledger, the letter, the two departures and the close.
- **Decision 7.** Brom is in the middle with Oryn (having the bolt's feathers broken off, asked first) when the broad man goes to the high edge; he walks out into the open end only before the seventh flight.
- **Decision 9–10 (timing).** Cael does not keep the captain's shoulder on the run. A heavy man with one arm lashed goes at a hard jog over loose stone and the tall man will not leave him; Cael outruns them, is under the second heap at the count of eight and at the lip at twenty, with the two of them under it as the frame begins to lean. Teague's distance from the roof's edge to the heap is "five and thirty strides". Seln calls the count ("Nine.") down from the lip, raising his voice for the first time.
- **Decision 11.** To reach the cleft the nine pass within a pole's length of Teague and the tall man, and the three bowmen pass the boy on his knees; nobody lifts a hand on either side. The tall man grounds the butt once when the last is by (his yard-sign for a touch taken).
- **Decision 14.** Vastin is got to his feet by Karis for the burial against what a healer said he had in him; he cannot lift a bowl that night (Lira feeds him).
- **Decision 16.** The ribs are read away from the fire, round a shoulder of the rock, so that neither an old man nor a captain's three can overhear whose line it is. Oryn's two scenes and the inward turn happen at that same place.
- **Decision 18.** The instruments are tried after dark, by Karis's lamp, with Karis and **Brom** (not Lira) laying the measure first.
- **Decision 24.** Karis's evidence for the first cut is a slip of the cutter's tool at the corner of one sign, which stands, cut on purpose and at full depth, on her sheets from the west pier and from the hall.
- **Decision 25.** The team's marks go **south** from the foot of their path (not north), which leaves Oryn's road north clear.
- **Decision 27.** On the walk in he thinks of the plate; the last four hundred and six strides are walked half blind toward the lowest smear of light in the east. Walking back he steers by the crews' fire.
- **Decision 30–31.** Added in chapter 60: Seln's one remark at the ledger (three reaches; he does not think the third the last); the tall man's second night with the cloth; the broad man's "Aye" to Vastin at parting; the tall man's grounded butt to Cael; Lira's hand on his sleeve in the night. The closing prose is led in by one paragraph of mine that sets it on the walk back of the night before, so that its "now, walking back out" is true where it stands.
- **Protected units.** "Ribs. May I?" was first drafted split across two speeches and is now one line. A second "It's mine now" in Oryn's head, a second "Sixth chair" and a second "By count" were written and taken out again, so that each stands once.
