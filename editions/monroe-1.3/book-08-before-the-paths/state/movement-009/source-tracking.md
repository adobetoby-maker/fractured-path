### 55:65–71 — the swept slope reveals Seln

The required event is only that the fall sweeps the scree clean and Seln climbs it. The manuscript keeps the source's more distinctive reveal in the same order: Cael looks at the altered slope, inventories its newly bare face, discovers Seln already on it, then locates him one-third of the way up and flat against the rock. The quilt/table image is new, but the stripped surface → sudden Seln reveal → one-third-up, body-flat sequence is not.

To break it without changing the event, reveal Seln first—through Teague's sightline, falling grit, or a hand against the sky—and let Cael infer afterward that the fall made his route. Alternatively, keep Cael's terrain-first observation but remove both the one-third measure and the flat-to-the-rock description, replacing them with a different progress marker or movement problem.

### 55:303 — the high-ground causal summary

The manuscript preserves the source sentence's complete causal chain and syntax: Seln takes/removes the enemy's high ground by climbing the slope their own rig swept bare, while the wall/old man in the stream-bed buys the time for the climb. Substituting “taken” for “removed,” “bare” for “clean,” and the wall's holder for the wall itself does not make the telling independent.

To break it, do not summarize the victory in that four-link order. Start with its cost—Vastin has spent himself to keep the roof up—then let Teague or Cael notice separately that the rig erased its owners' protection; Seln's possession of the shelf can land last as the consequence, not first as the thesis.
