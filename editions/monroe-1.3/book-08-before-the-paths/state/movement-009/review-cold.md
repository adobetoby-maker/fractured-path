## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in Seln's reconnaissance in Chapter 54. The map of the gap is legible, the two rigged stone heaps create a concrete problem, and Brom's lean-to idea converts an apparently impossible passage into a testable plan. The pull became strongest from the first volley through the avalanche and the climb in Chapter 55. That sequence keeps position, timing, power limits, and human choice visible at once. The boy's single stride outside the wall lands hard because Chapter 54 has just taught both him and the reader what “behind it” means.

Interest slipped in three places. First, the opening carries many unexplained backward references—mill, fence, stair, Line, Path, counts, injuries, and prior promises—before a cold reader can reliably distinguish present danger from remembered history. Second, Chapter 57's long sequence of Oryn's review, Cael's cord test, his private reading, and his written log is intellectually coherent but delays forward movement after the burial. Third, the circumference procedure in Chapter 58 is convincing but prolonged; the dramatic point becomes clear before all 158 lengths are completed. These are not dead passages, but their accumulation asks for more concentration than the action and grief scenes do.

Cael matters most because his need to catalogue everything is both competence and defense: he can state exactly what happened while withholding what it felt like. Vastin and Oryn also matter quickly. Vastin's request for medical permission makes the wall an ethical choice rather than a spectacle, while Oryn's insistence on consent gives her later decision to touch the dying boy real moral weight. Teague's unnamed boy becomes emotionally important with very little page time; the cloth, the loud “Noted,” and his need to be on the count make his death feel personal rather than functional.

I expect the next movement to make Cael explain the “foot arrived” experience to Lira on the road, to test whether Vastin remains with the six after the contract ends, and to show the faceless opponents adapting for a fourth encounter. I would continue. The combination of practical expedition detail, restrained affection, and a mystery that changes Cael's understanding of himself is compelling. I would, however, want the editorial placeholders resolved before treating the reading experience as final.

## Manuscript integrity and internal checks

### Coverage and completeness

All seven supplied chapters, 54–60, were read. The movement has a clear arc: approach and plan; passage and casualty; burial and reckoning; measurement and first crossing; Cael's central discovery; dispersal and westward continuation. It is nevertheless textually incomplete because eleven `TIER-B` blocks occupy important story positions, including the close of Chapter 55, the close of Chapter 58, and the departure exchanges in Chapter 60. Those gaps prevent a fully reliable judgment of cadence, attribution, and some emotional transitions.

### Canon

No canon evidence was supplied. I found no basis for declaring a verified canon contradiction. Any concern below is therefore an apparent manuscript issue only.

### Physical state and chronology

The visible injury and day counts are internally consistent across the supplied pages:

- Vastin is at twenty-seven of thirty when Oryn permits him to raise the wall seated in Chapter 54; he is at twenty-eight on the 307th day and “twenty-nine tomorrow” at the end of Chapter 59. The wall fully spends him, and his later need for the stick, box, and assistance follows that cost.
- Teague's right arm remains on its slat; his left-handed climbing, letter-taking, and departure are maintained.
- The broad bowman's boarded shin and later shoulder wound remain distinct from Brom's bolt through the left forearm. The similar body types and overlapping injuries can momentarily confuse a cold reader, but the states do not contradict one another.
- Cael's old rib damage receives the Force recoil in Chapter 55, is diagnosed as two recracked ribs in Chapter 56, and continues to limit carrying, sitting, breathing, and the 506-stride walk.
- Oryn is six days past her cairn on the 306th day, seven days past on the 307th, and leaves north on the 308th. The sequence holds.

### Power limits

The movement states and observes usable limits. Vastin's wall is fixed at its grounding line, has a safe and unsafe side, can be leaned, transmits load through its maker, and cannot move to cover the boy. Cael's Force travels on a deliberately aligned road, affects what lies on that road rather than to either side, and returns through him at the point where the borrowed pattern fails to continue. The quiet floors extinguish the others' Paths at a stride and restore them at a stride; Cael's abilities neither extinguish nor react. No decisive outcome is obtained by an unplanted exception.

### Knowledge boundaries and disclosures

Knowledge is handled carefully. Cael keeps the thirteen, the still place, and the two borrowed debts within the six-person confidence and his private ledger. Oryn identifies the thirteenth's owner through touch but does not carry the information to Teague. Seln consciously declines to uncover a surrendered opponent's face, preserving the promise of no deaths at the cost of a desired fact. Teague learns what Cael did to the rig but not the source of the power. These boundaries remain distinct.

The one apparent overreach is the final paragraph of Chapter 60. The movement has earned Cael's felt conclusions—he fit, the place was not the Quiet, and his still place was of the same kind of making. It has not yet demonstrated as fact that the place was “built to end him” or that Cael “was ... the pattern.” Presented in close perspective, those can be his emerging hypotheses; the paragraph's declarative narrative weight makes them sound more verified than the prior chapters allow, particularly after Cael repeatedly refuses to go past the evidence.

## Overall judgment

This is a compelling movement with a particularly strong two-chapter action-and-consequence spine. The gap sequence succeeds because every tactical fact has a human cost, and the aftermath refuses to convert death into motivation and move on. The court sequence is quieter but still progressive: measurement produces discovery, and discovery changes Cael's self-conception. The prose's repeated objects and phrases create excellent emotional continuity.

The movement is not ready for a clean reader test in its current form. The unresolved editorial blocks are the first obstacle. After they are supplied, the most valuable refinement would be a narrow clarity pass, not a shortening of the developed fight or a broad simplification of the voice.
