## Where we enter

It is the hundred and thirty-first day past the Line, the morning after the ferry's last run. Snow is on Lowmarch, the river is skinning with ice, and the ferryman is taking his ropes in. There are five at the lower inn. Lira's arm is slung and her ankle taped, by Oryn's order. Brom's shoulder is bound. Karis has fifty-two sheets, the kit and its ledger, and a map with four circles and a line through them. Seln has the case. Cael is nineteen, with ten fragments and a still place he does not touch. Hesk's straightedge is in his coat and there are 96 marks in hand. Teague's crew is across the yard at the upper inn.

## What this movement is for

Put the winter on the page and teach hard. This is the first third, so the formula wants progression vocabulary at its densest here. The reader should finish the movement knowing all ten fragments as costs and limits, and the kit as a tool. They should know that instruments read wrong in one pair of hands, and why Karis cannot let go of the index. The movement ends with six at a table and a word nobody has said yet: west.

Consequential turns and promises:
- **The winter priced** (133rd): Pike's four a month; "Eleventh"; seventy-six left. Then the board in winter: a roof, a well-house, a fence.
- **The fence at the axe-head hold** (~140th, ~3,000 for the set piece). This is two days out on the winter road. A shale-back herd shelters on the fence line's slope and must be moved without stampeding, with no Path that matters. Lira is benched in her sling and reads the herd for them. Brom reads the line of posts like a wall. Seln finds two of Teague's posts a hand off true and tells only Brom. The factor pays forty at the gate and asks no name: 116, and the tenth line, PAID.
- **The ice road** (161st, ~3,000). A carter's cart goes through the river ice. Cael, Brom and Seln haul it out without declaring. Cael's Iron read on the ice's load is the workhorse, taught as a practical skill.
- **The silent yard** (two or three bouts across the winter, ~1,500 each). Teague's crew and the five spar in the frozen meadow with steel and bodies, and no Paths. Cael loses to the tall woman's spear reach, then makes a visible correction. She never says a word to any of the five. Lira coaches from the bench, one-armed. The rival clock appears on the page. Teague is the better field captain.
- **Cael's working inventory** (~6,000, across private practice in the ferryman's shed or the meadow at night). Each of the ten has its cost: Wind's burst and the lock beat; Pressure ("the Bronze from Ardenmere" when spoken); the Iron read; Compression's frame; Ember, unused; Shadow's idle draw and the seal; Storm asleep; Anchor used both ways; Blade; Tide, "ask first", which he does not run on himself.
- **The anomaly as a scene** (168th). Karis has everyone pace the yard with the cord. It reads forty-two for everyone and "forty-one and a piece" for him, three times. The edges read a hair short and a hair long in his hands. Her ruler sentence is said twice, first as a rule and then not as one. Log it verbatim: "Claim: something. Evidence: a cord, two edges, a hair. Ruling: hold."
- **Karis's case grows in pieces** through the winter. She says the full sentence about west on the 263rd. The thaw comes, and the Thornwater road opens on the 264th.
- **Thornwater** (265th–266th). The ford in thaw. Oryn's yard. "Five months and twenty days." / "That's a season. Take it off." Oryn waits for Lira to undo the sling herself. "Will you mend it." / "Lie still": this is Lira's first ask since Norhold. The ankle is mended. "Thank you." / "Route's whole." The map on her table: "That's a line." Cael tells her about the cord: "Neither do you. Write that down too." She will not read him yet. She comes down to Lowmarch, unasked.
- **Six at Lowmarch** (268th): 118 marks. Karis makes her case, and **Treswick and the Deepstacks are spoken for the first time**, along with the scholars' provision and "a man there who is". Oryn's reservation about the station goes unsaid. **The closing log (270th), verbatim.**

**Physical challenge:** the shale-back fence and the ice road (set pieces), the silent-yard bouts (learning fights) and the Thornwater ford.
**Cutaways (~4,500):** Karis, ~2,500, on a winter night with the index: her want, and her refusal to theorize about the cord. Teague, ~2,000, at the upper inn: the young one asks to learn; the wrist; he cannot read the sheet and does not need to.
**Development:** about 14 Cael beats. Supporting: Lira 2–3, Karis 2–3, Brom 2, Seln 1–2, Oryn 2, Teague 1, Pike 1, the young one 1.

## What must remain true / withheld

- Cael is nineteen, with no birthday. He has ten fragments; the still place is not read or touched. **Oryn does not read Cael** (§13). Lira asks nobody for anything before Thornwater. **Seln teaches nobody.** The tall woman, the broad man and the older healer do not speak to the five. Teague's crew stays unnamed.
- There is no Iron Wall and no Vastin in this movement. Vastin is still inside the Compact, and nothing hints otherwise. No Force is shown in Cael's presence. No silent-yard bout drills a wall.
- Karis cannot read the index, and does not know that it is a grade. She does not name Cleon to anyone. "Treswick" is first said at the 268th table.
- The anomaly is deniable and gets no theory. Nobody says *who* or *why* about the pattern.
- **Invent no numeric ceilings for fragments.** Costs are qualitative.
- Reader Standard. Audio-first referents: with five people and Teague's four unnamed, attribute every line by action or name.

## What the author owns

How many chapters the winter takes, and which log entries become scenes. The shape of the herd problem and the ice rescue. The content of the silent-yard bouts and who wins each. The texture of the inns, the board room, the ferryman's shed, and the long dark. Letters gone west, with answers pending (none arrive in this movement). Humour: Pike's ledger speech; Brom's "It's a boot"; the mule's shed fee "he has not asked for and will".

## Where we leave pressure

Six at a table on the 270th, with the ice going next week and a map that runs east. Karis's answer lies west, inside walls, under a registry. Cael has spent five years staying out of registries. The kit reads everyone but him. He has written *west, and inside* and does not like the sentence.
