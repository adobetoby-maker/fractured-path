## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Immediately, and for an unusual reason: the coast coach's "long, unhurried look of a person checking a delivery against its invoice" (ch27 L17–L41). The movement opens on Cael being *read* by somebody who wants nothing, and the book's whole argument about measuring versus reading is in a nod. The corner game (ch27 L77–L252) is the best learning scene in the book so far: two honest failures, then the version that works, and the finding stated in one line, "I can read you building. I can't read you pricing." (L203). The arrival at Norhold (ch28 L125–L185) is paced like a held breath. Seln's joke (ch29 L109) and the table's silence after it is the warmest beat in five movements. Umber's eve (ch31 L3–L129) is a quiet, grown-up piece of writing about a man and a sentence. Brom's second-round bout (ch32 L19–L111) is the best fight of the movement: the door used against him, the planting, Rooke's four words, and "a moving man does not have to start moving".

**Where it slipped.** Three small places. The city-walk paragraph (ch32 L289) runs ~180 words and contains a dropped word ("There were and colour-sellers"), and it is the one paragraph a narrator would stumble on. The Compact-row description (ch32 L345) asks me to hold eight people and twelve chairs and does not reconcile with Vastin's seating plan in ch29. And the fifth exchange of Brom's bout (ch32 L93–L97) scores three Brom touches on the page and then calls it "Two to one" — I counted, because the book had just taught me to.

**Which person matters.** Lira, by a distance, in this movement: the corner game, the "I'd rather be beaten by somebody learning" line (ch27 L243), and the Ash-screener bout fought by ear. Brom second ("Rooke said walk"). Among the adults, Umber — the movement makes him a man before he is a ruling.

**What I expect next.** Lira against Zerin, and the ear-and-pricing lesson failing against somebody who "brought her room with her" (ch31 L451). The Rhagen Shield "survey" in a ring that is still bricked up. Somebody sitting in seat twelve.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. This is the strongest movement of the book so far for texture and warmth; its defects are all line-level.

## 5. Continuity and clarity findings (all line-level; none is a canon violation)

1. **Brom's fifth exchange scores three Brom touches and is called "Two to one" (ch32 L93–L97).** L93: the left-door point ("That was a point"), the hill fighter's sweep, then "Brom took another, plainly, through the middle, a breath before the bell." L95: a *further* Brom touch — the final left-door attempt, the wrist caught, "Brom's other fist came through over the top". L97: "Two to one. Nine to seven." That is 3–1 as written, which under the rule just taught would also have closed the exchange early. The bout's totals (9–7, 24/23) are consistent with 2–1; the prose has one touch too many. Effect: the one bout that exists to show the fifth exchange miscounts it, in a chapter that trains the reader to count.

2. **The Compact row's composition changes between ch29 and ch32.** Vastin's plan (ch29 L229): six observation seats, then the district officers, a liaison seat, two host-city seats, eleven plated and twelve held — which makes two district officers and no clerks. Cael's row (ch32 L345): Ilsev, Havel, "a district officer, two clerks, a liaison man, two officials of the host city" — eight people for eleven plated chairs, with two clerks the plan did not have and one district officer where the plan had two. A reader who has just read Vastin counting "One, two, three. Eleven." will try the same count on Cael's row and come up three short. (The ch29 line about the observation officers' *rotation* could absorb this in one clause.)

3. **The guesting-house geography contradicts itself (ch28 L255, L271, L241/L273).** Halcenvane has the third floor "with a hill house on the floor below and a southern house above"; then the hill house's clerk, having stopped at Halcenvane's door, "went on up the stair"; and Cael's room is twice "at the top of the house" though a house lodges above. Swap the two lodgers (hill house above, southern below) and L271 is right, but "the top of the house" still needs the attic or a different phrase.

4. **"The wool town's rule had walked all the way to Norhold" (ch32 L287).** *NO BOOK ON THE DEMONSTRATION BOUTS* first appeared at the **quarry town** (this edition's ch17 L245; the board-man's speech ch18 L157), not the wool town. One word.

5. **"There'll be thirteen more of her" (ch27 L39).** Fourteen houses less Halcenvane and less the coast house whose coach has just looked: twelve more. Lira is not a careless counter.

6. **Dropped word (ch32 L289):** "There were and colour-sellers with the fourteen houses' ribbons on long poles." A narrator will stop here. The paragraph is also ~180 words and the chapter's longest; a break before "And all through it" would help the ear.

7. **Rooke's "I told the squad about him at the start of the year, at the map table" (ch30 L141).** Umber is not mentioned anywhere in ch1–26 of this edition (grep). A reader cannot remember a briefing that was not on the page; "I told you at the map table there was a man in the west tower" or simply "I'll tell you again" would cover it.

8. **One banner or two? (ch28 L239 vs ch31 L183).** Rooke says the champion's-gate banner "hangs there from the end of the last cycle until somebody earns the right to take it down at the end of this one"; the procession's banner is carried in "alone" by the colour-guard. If it is the same cloth, it has been taken down for the morning; if a second, the page should say "the marching colour" or similar. Half a sentence. (Author flag 4 — the holder is unnamed, which keeps the M3 ruling.)

9. **Minor and optional.** "The tournament was still nine days off" (ch27 L293) reads as "the city is nine days off"; the opening is sixteen days off by the author's own calendar — "the city was still nine days off" is exact. Vastin's "a week before the opening" on a Fourth-day is eight days on the author's table; nobody will notice. The ring "fifty-odd feet, a little longer one way than the other" (ch30 L191) — 48 feet is not "fifty-odd"; "fifty feet or so one way and a little less the other" matches the planned 52 × 48 without stating it.

## Repair brief

**Priority 1 — Brom's fifth exchange (ch32 L93–L97).**
*Observed:* the prose scores three Brom touches (the left-door point; "took another, plainly, through the middle, a breath before the bell"; the final fist over the caught wrist) against one, then states "Two to one. Nine to seven."
*Effect on the reader:* the movement teaches the continental rule in ch30 and the reader applies it to this very bout; a 3–1 exchange would have closed early and made the bout 10–7, so the showcase fifth exchange miscounts itself.
*Proposed scope:* remove one Brom touch — cleanest is to cut "Then Brom took another, plainly, through the middle, a breath before the bell." so the sequence is door point, sweep, final fist = 2–1; or keep the middle drive and cut the final-fist paragraph's scoring sense. Totals and figures stay.
*Strength to preserve:* the final paragraph as written (the half-hand closing on the wrist, "slow and plain and impossible to stop") and the rail exchange that follows ("You kept it moving." / "Rooke said walk.").

**Priority 2 — the counting lines (five single-line fixes).**
*Observed:* (a) ch32 L345 the Compact row lists eight people for eleven plated chairs and does not match Vastin's plan (ch29 L229: six observation seats, two district officers, one liaison, two host); (b) ch28 L255/L271/L241/L273 the hill house is below yet its clerk goes "on up the stair", and Cael's room is "at the top of the house" with a house above; (c) ch32 L287 "The wool town's rule" should be the quarry town's (ch17 L245); (d) ch27 L39 "thirteen more of her" should be twelve; (e) ch32 L289 "There were and colour-sellers" (dropped word; and the paragraph would read better broken before "And all through it").
*Effect on the reader:* this book's reader counts because Cael counts; each slip is small and each costs a little trust in the narration's arithmetic, and (e) stops a narrator.
*Proposed scope:* one line each. For (a), either make Cael's list match the plan (two district officers, no clerks, and a clause that the other observation seats "changed on the rotation") or let Vastin's plan name clerks; for (b), put the hill house above and the southern house below, and change "the top of the house" to "the top of Halcenvane's floor" or move Cael to an attic room.
*Strength to preserve:* Cael's plate-by-plate reasoning (ch32 L353) and the closing Log, which should not be touched.

**Priority 3 — two clarity touches in the ceremony material.**
*Observed:* (a) ch31 L183 the banner is carried in alone while ch28 L239 has it hanging at the champion's gate "until somebody earns the right to take it down" — one cloth or two is unstated; (b) ch30 L141 Rooke cites a map-table briefing about Umber that is not on the page in M1.
*Effect on the reader:* a reader who liked the gate-banner image stops to ask whether it has been taken down; a reader who remembers the map room (ch5) looks for Umber there and does not find him.
*Proposed scope:* half a sentence in ch31 ("the colour-guard had brought it down from the gate at dawn" or "a second cloth, the marching colour, that the gate's never left"); in ch30, "I'll tell you about him once, here" or "I told you at the map table there was a man in the west tower" — a phrase.
*Strength to preserve:* "They had cheered the houses. They stood up for the cloth." and Rooke's whole Umber speech, which is one of the movement's best pieces of adult plain-speaking.

*Deferred to the owner, not repairs:* flag 1 (whether to protect the on-page Daeva log), flag 7 (whether Havel may read the grade), flag 8 (Umber's "forty years" beside Vastin's), and the M6 note that a four-exchange finish under the stated rule needs a margin over three.
