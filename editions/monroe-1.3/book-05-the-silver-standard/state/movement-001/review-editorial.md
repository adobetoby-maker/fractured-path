## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** The dawn session on the sprung oak (ch1): six bursts and no seventh, "cheaper tomorrow and dearer now", the drift arriving in the gap and Lira's "One". That is the first time the book shows a cost structure instead of naming one, and it is the best early page. Karis making Cael play the regional registrar on the bluff-road bench (ch2, "Find me the step") turns an exposition problem into a game and lands the protected sentence as a conclusion rather than a slogan. The learning fight (ch3) is the movement's best teaching: two weights, the back heel, and a read that reports the truth and is beaten by it anyway. Karis running up the residence stair with her hair out of its pins (ch4) is the movement's one pure jolt of joy. The mock exhibition (ch6) pays the whole movement: a boy who wins the bout and rates lower, and reads his own effect column as the price of what he chose not to show.

**Where it slipped.** Chapter 1 opens on a ledger. Twenty-nine lines of italic inventory before a person speaks is a cold start for a thirteen-year-old, even a good one; the dawn session that follows rescues it, but the order is backwards for a young reader. Chapter 4's middle (the four pages, Bracken's check, Withrow's order of operations) is three consecutive scenes of adults being correct at a table; it is well made and it is the longest stretch where nothing a reader can picture happens. The Vastin window (ch5) is slow in a way an adult reader will enjoy and a young reader will push through. The map room (ch5) teaches cleanly through Rooke's card, but a reader who does the sums will hit a wall in it (see §4.1 and the repair brief): marks "out of ten", "ten is par", and then a band "above thirty".

**Which person matters.** Karis, by a distance: the registrar game, the run, the torn strip, the note closed like a door on someone asleep. Then Ephram, who is suddenly a person ("I'd still rather have the bout"), and Rooke, who comes to the edge of his question and picks up an iron stand instead.

**Which event I expect next.** The wool town: Brom against the buried Copper Rank Four whose sheet he turned face down; what page Karis marked in Rooke's manual; and whether the first real panel gives Cael the twenty-three Rooke promised he'd "see again".

**Would I continue.** Yes, without hesitation. The movement ends on a road visible from a window and a figure the reader can already price.

## 2. Two lenses

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** Follows everything that happens; is slower on why it matters. The clause, the memorandum's missing plank, and the mirror paragraph are all explained inside scenes, which is right. The vocabulary is clean (FK 4.47). The handle is the joke they will quote. The broom on the hearthrug and "Oh, *come on*" are theirs. The things they will not get on one pass: why "the record keeps five of the six" matters (ch1 inventory), what a "fossil" provision is (ch4, explained, but at speed), and the band table in ch5 if they try to use it (they will try, because Rooke told them to, and the arithmetic will not close). They will feel the Vastin window as a pause and the four-page drafting as homework.

**Adult genre reader.** Enjoys that the movement's engine is paperwork and that the paperwork is dramatised as choices by named people with something to lose. Notices the craft: the fountain as the recess's clock; the mirror; Withrow refusing to look at Bracken while praising him; "That is how careful institutions surrender. In the schedule." Will notice the scale defect in the map room at once, and will notice that Cael's private pricing of Lira, Brom and Ephram (26 / 24 / 24) comes out exactly right the next night and that the text never remarks on it. Will find the inventories (ch1) dense but will read them as the series' signature and forgive them.

Neither lens is a sales forecast and I draw none.

### 4.2 Defects found (continuity, calendar, counts)

1. **The Standard's scale cannot close (ch5 ¶149, ¶175, ¶179; ch6 ¶226).** "Each one gives each fighter three marks … out of ten." "Ten is par. Ten on each of the three, thirty in all, is what a practitioner at Silver tier and fifth rank does on an ordinary good day." Then the table: "Above thirty: Gold. Twenty-eight to thirty: Silver." If ten is the most a judge can give, thirty is the ceiling of the instrument, "above thirty" is unreachable, and par (a Silver Rank Five) sits at the very top of the Silver band with no room for Silver Ranks Six to Ten or any Gold. Every worked figure in ch5 and ch6 is arithmetically correct (8/9/8.66 → 25.7 → 26; 8.33/7.33/8 → 23.66 → 24; 8/8.66/6.33 → 23.00; the two-thirds-of-a-point gap), and none of them is affected, because all of them sit below par. The defect is in the ceiling, not the method. See §9 for what later movements need.
2. **Bronze is missing from the band table (ch5 ¶179).** The bible's ladder is Copper, Iron, Bronze, Silver, Gold. Rooke's bands run Copper 12–17, Iron 18–21, "strong Iron to Silver-touched" 22–27, Silver 28–30, Gold above. The tournament's brackets are four (the source's ch2 and this book's map: Copper, Iron, Silver, Gold), so no Bronze *bracket* is correct; but a reader who has met Seln and "the Bronze from Ardenmere" will ask where Bronze went, and M2 fields two Bronze exhibition opponents, one of whom protests that he lost "to a boy rated below his own tier's floor" (source ch4). One aside from Rooke ("Bronze sits across the top of that band; the tournament doesn't bracket it") closes the hole without touching a figure.
3. **"Nine days" is thirteen on the page (ch5 ¶131).** The response went down "on the morning courier of the Sixth-day" (d12; ch4 ¶199). Hesk's reply comes "the fourth day of the silence" (d16), Lira's wall "the sixth evening" (d18), and the card comes up "on the Fifth-day" with Rooke's targets filling "the clean space that had waited there for a fortnight" (d25; ch5 ¶71, ¶107). Karis's "Nine days … between the response going down and the card coming up" is the map's figure (§6 "about nine days of silence"), not the prose's. Vastin's own "next nine days … On the tenth day" is internal to his window and fine.
4. **The memorandum's fourteen days lapse unremarked.** "Fourteen days. From yesterday's date." (ch3 ¶247, said on d8; the memorandum is dated d7) puts the deadline on d21. Bracken crosses the days off one a morning (ch4 ¶199). The card arrives d25. On the page, the fourteenth day came and went with no resubmission and nobody notices, which is either a hole or the best unspoken beat in the silence passage, depending on one sentence that is not there.
5. **"Two years" for Bracken (ch4 ¶101).** "That was the only time in two years Cael had known him ask for anything." Cael came up the bluff road at the start of Book 4; he has known Bracken a year and a recess (ch1 ¶261: "He had known that for a year"). "Two years" is right for Karis (ch2 ¶61, ch6 ¶238, ¶248) and wrong here.
6. **"Forty years before you were born" (ch4 ¶135).** Withrow: "their charter, written by their own board, forty years before you were born." The clause was written forty years ago (ch2 ¶85, ch4 ¶95); Cael is sixteen. Either "forty years ago, before you were born" or drop the number.
7. **The roster's "tenth line" (ch1 ¶207).** The list as given (five competitors, Rooke, Gault, two reserves, a wagoner, then Seln) makes Seln the eleventh line of eleven. Swap the wagoner and Seln in the sentence, or say "the last line".
8. **The charter's geography in the note scene (ch6 ¶248).** "The charter lay open in its oldest part, among the founding articles, near the place where the clause and the exhibition provision had both been written." Ch4 has put the clause in the sixth part (written forty years ago by an angry board) and the provision in the third schedule at the back; the founding articles are a third place, older than either. The sentence should stop claiming the clause and the provision were written among the founding articles. The same sentence's "in the years before the current classifications had words" pitches the stratum a shade above source altitude (it half-names the retired-word idea); "before the registry's last standardization" is the map's own phrase and keeps the plant where §11 puts it. The note's contents stay unknown, nothing is pursued, and the retired word is not used anywhere in the movement: those bounds hold.
9. **Three different "forty years" inside two chapters** (the clause's age, ch2 ¶85 and ch4 ¶95; the schedule "nobody has read in forty years", ch4 ¶37; the validation heading unused "for forty years", ch2 ¶175; the provision "forty years older than any of us", ch4 ¶67). Each is defensible on its own (one registry standardization about forty years back retired demonstrations and validation headings together; the scandal is a separate forty), but by ear a listener hears one number doing four jobs. Clarity, not canon.
10. **Attribution (ch3 ¶17).** Rooke's seeding speech and Lira's "Good … I'd rather start with the best of them" share one paragraph. In audio the speaker change is invisible. Split it.
11. **Earth idioms.** "does not turn on a sixpence" (ch6 ¶165) puts an English coin into a world that counts in marks; "like a hymn sheet" (ch2 ¶115) is the only churchly image in four books. Neither is in the gates' lists; both will catch an ear.

Nothing above is a reserved-truth breach, a power-limit breach or a knowledge-boundary breach.
