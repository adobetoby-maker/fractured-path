## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** Ch40's dawn is the right way in: a boy pacing a ring nobody told him not to, a foreman with a measuring rod, and "It's crowned" before anyone explains what a crown costs. The foreman's "We patch to the old line. Easier than deciding" is a plant a reader will not know is a plant. Rooke folding card four into his coat and tapping the pocket is the movement's best quiet beat and it is paid immediately by Lira on the stair ("It's called having a house"). In the bout, "Like throwing stones into a lake. The lake doesn't notice" is a thirteen-year-old's image for a Silver weave and it does all the teaching the chapter needs; the interval ("The clock?" / "There isn't one." / "He had not solved Shield. He had solved a clock.") is the book's midpoint turning on a single short exchange and it lands. Ch41 caught hardest at the bay: the guard "poured round the step" to fill a hole Cael is no longer in, and the uphill exit nobody takes. The captain's courtesy at the planking ("Nobody ever gives us eleven") and the protected finding read as the most dangerous polite sentence in the book, exactly as the narration says. Ch42's hired hall is the funniest scene in the movement (Ephram bowing to the pump; "and now! the blade descends!") and it earns the bout: the reader has already watched a sentence fail to find its listener before the lake man ever bows. Ch44 is the warmest chapter the edition has produced: Lira's sign on the table, Brom's "I look like I'm going to be here a while", Karis sitting on her hands, and Seln pulling out the end of the bench while five people make nothing of it. Ch46's third exchange is a structural idea rather than a spectacle: the floor the squad built out of roads and tolls is not beaten, it is "stepped over", and the reader understands the stormlane because the inspection taught the barrier as a wall first.

**Where it slipped.** Ch45 stacks three set-up scenes (Rooke's brief, Umber's cutaway, the inspection) between the draw and the trial, and the inventory before them is note-form by design; it is the only chapter where a young reader may feel the floor being laid rather than walked. Ch43's Vastin cutaway runs about 1,870 words against a 1,500 allowance and its middle (paper about paper; "He would read the figure. He would set his own two lines beside it") is the one passage where the adult register outpaces the younger lens. The light in ch42 moves the wrong way for west windows (§4.9), which a careful adult will notice in the exchange that depends on it. In ch46 the phrase "The category had waited three hundred years for a bout that needed it" is Umber's protected M8 line (§10 item 47) arriving a movement early in Cael's narration.

**Which person matters.** Rooke in ch40–41 (the planned loss; the pocket; "We're going to dinner anyway"), Karis in ch41 and ch44 (the two columns; "filling"; the first notebook), Seln at the blue door, and Daeva for one second. The movement belongs to Cael's midpoint, and the supporting beats are distributed rather than hoarded.

**What I expect next.** The empty frame staying empty, the filing with one word on it, and a floor rebuilt to hold her. I also expect to be told how Rhagen lost its semifinal (§3a).

**Would I continue.** Yes.

### (a) The team-trial rules minted here, and what M8 inherits

**Ruling: coherent with §8, #39 and #40; one line in the draw scene conflicts with §12 C14 and must be re-worded; approve the format with the inheritance list below.**

- **Against BOOK_MAP §8.** Every planned feature is on the page: the ninety-foot scenario floor; three platforms with "the south one costing double" (Lira: "That southern one costs double what it scores"; it takes two to hold and scores as one); the diagonal barrier with two joint gaps (Brom: "a third of the way along … forty feet past it"); sight-blocks (the timber stacks at both ends; Karis: "you can't see the middle of the barrier"); Lira's "fast north, slow south"; Brom in the second joint; Karis's ramps (anchored frames, consistent with M6's contact-only lattice); Ephram's calls (counting aloud behind the stacks); E3 the stormlane down the diagonal; E4 re-forming; Zerin declining an unpriced trade; Daeva crossing him once and stopping for one second. The geometry holds: a barrier from the north-west corner to the south-east corner puts a west-end gate in the southern triangle and an east-end gate in the northern one, as written.
- **Against #39.** Not applicable; the trial is not a bracket bout and no bracket bout is fought this movement. The two exhibitions are checked in §4.1 and are correct.
- **Against #40.** Consistent: the third place is the team trial ("the beaten pair would meet for third place on a later day"), and no individual third-place bout appears.
- **Against §12 C14 (defect).** Ch45 ¶ "Only four houses would field a squad … the standings had settled it the day the last bracket closed: the four houses highest on the count when the individual brackets were done." The edition has already committed the Iron, Silver and Gold finals to finals day (BOOK_MAP §6 "Finals day T24"; §12 C14; and on the page, ch38: "Houses brought their delegations to finals, and to the Gold, and on the last day they all came to everything"). Re-word to the count as it stood when the brackets reached their finals (one clause; see Repair brief, Priority 2). Nothing else in the trial depends on it.
- **The format as minted (Rooke, stated once; the referee paraphrases it at the presentation):** four exchanges by the timekeepers' glass; three platforms; a house alone on a platform scores quarter-minutes kept by the platform marshals (the objective ledger); the flags count touches (the engagement ledger); a touched fighter goes to his own gate and touches the post; the exchange goes to the house with more quarter-minutes; split exchanges go to the whole objective ledger. This is consistent with protected item 15 ("Recommend the trial sheet acquire a column": the marshals' sheet has a column per fighter, as Umber says) and with Umber's "rated on what a squad does together, against the Standard, on tables this office has used every cycle".
- **Rhagen's semifinal (the author's flag 3).** With Halcenvane third and the river academy fourth, the third-place trial against Rhagen (§2 item 34; §8) requires Rhagen, second on the count, to lose to the river academy off the page. This is coherent but not free: M8 owes the reader one or two sentences, and the cheapest honest explanation is already on the page in M7's own terms. The trial "is a game about ground, and the touches are how you take the other side's people off the ground for a while" (Rooke); Rhagen's doctrine is structure and coverage, built to win touches and hold, and a house that takes the touches while the other takes the platforms loses on the ledger. ### (b) The exhibition ring's build against M9's spec

**Ruling: yes, M8's "ring rebuilt at midnight" can carry it, provided M8 and M9 treat the Daeva ring as a new build to a new specification, not the same ring put back.**

M7's ring: fifty-two feet north to south and "a few feet less" across (consistent with M9's 52 × 48); planking chest-high on the posts with a rope coping; two gate bays a stride deep in newer, quicker stone; the whole floor crowned to the oak round by "about the thickness of his thumb", the same on every side, patched "to the old line" for two hundred years. M9's spec: a six-foot barrier with bull-nose coping; four gate breaks; four arrestor masts; "crown half an inch north to south"; reinforced stone over a through-bolted sub-frame. The two cannot be the same structure, and they do not need to be. Protected item 26 ("They're building the floor to hold her. Nobody asked what it would take to hold me.") says the floor is built, not re-erected, and the foreman's "Nobody's ever voted to change the shape … Easier than deciding" is a plant that pays when, for a Gold, somebody finally decides. M8's ring conversation ("floor numbers", §2 item 35) is the place to state the new figures as new. Three things M8/M9 must say or decide: (i) the barrier, gates and masts are a different build for a different fighter; (ii) the stone itself is re-laid over the sub-frame, so the old radial crown becomes the measured half-inch fall north to south (the "old line" is gone, and the foreman's patching sentence is the reason the city notices); (iii) the oak round's fate. The round is "the oldest piece of floor in the building" (M6) and the captain stood on it; I recommend it stays as an island in the new floor, since M8's "She owns the middle" and M9's starting marks want a middle the reader already knows. Flag for the ledger as M7 canon: the exhibition ring (first build) with the foreman's history of the crown.
