## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Early and quietly: the two grey coats on the ferry landing who do not follow because a printed card has made following obsolete (ch7, "A parcel, delivered"), then the credentials clerk's niece who paid her own allowance for the hearing transcript. By the validation table ("adjacent to it") the movement has found its subject, which is not fighting but measurement, and it keeps that subject in hand for seven chapters. The ganger's grin after four wasted drover's walks (ch9), Brom "renting" the hinge (ch8), the eleven-minute metronome of the rating table (ch11), the crane-beam's bar of light (ch12) and Seln lining up six figures and seeing a man setting them (ch13) are the five moments I would quote to someone deciding whether to read on.

**Where it slipped.** Three places. (a) The Karis–Lira semifinal (ch11) is the movement's most anticipated bout and the shortest; it resolves in three clean beats and I wanted one more turn. (b) The exhibition opponents converge on one observation — the ganger feels managed effort, the adjudicator writes "appears", the yard-master says "you took a fifth exchange you didn't need" — so by the third telling the reader is ahead of the page; the design wants this (three roads to one sum) but the yard-master's line lands as a repeat rather than a confirmation. (c) The figures are posted so often (roughly fifteen ratings chalked across the movement) that the late-middle chapters can read like a scoreboard; ch13's Seln window redeems it by making the scoreboard the plot.

**Which person matters.** Cael, by a distance, and then Lira: her band warning on the rail (ch10), given with two figures and underweighted, is the best-placed plant in the movement and pays in ch13 three ways. Seln's one scene is the movement's hinge. Karis is at her strongest when she is wrong (losing to Lira and "gaining two lines").

**What I expect next.** The variance program meeting a real grappler and costing more than its design (the packet promises exactly this); the man in the travelling coat at the coaching inn turning into M3's market; somebody other than a friend "making" him, as the adjudicator's ruling pointed out of the window.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The movement ends on a confident sentence the book has already told me is wrong, and I want to watch the bill arrive.

## 2. Two lenses

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** Every bout is trackable from the rope: the author names the terrain (slope, soft pine, pillar, light) before it is used, then uses it once, which is the right rhythm for this reader. The arithmetic of the Standard is taught three times and each time through something happening (a clerk striking marks; two readers at a table; a man copying a board), so it never becomes a lecture. The teaching density is high in ch11 and ch13; a young reader may skim Seln's statistics paragraph (ch13:69–83) but the spoken version ("Variance is cheap. Buy some.") carries it. Humour is character-shaped and warm (Brom on half work "in sympathy"; Gault's *profoundly*). Nothing here is above this reader, and nothing talks down.

**Adult genre reader.** The movement's best idea is page thirty-one: an instrument that cannot be leaned upward and so leans downward "and never knows that it's leaning", with the note that the office wrote in advance saying it doesn't count. That is a progression-fantasy premise of real quality, and the movement earns it by showing the instrument work first. This reader will notice the convergence of three observers on the same point and the compressed guild-champion bout (≈1,170 words against the map's 2,300 allowance), and will feel the Ember mechanics wobble in the Karis–Lira bout (§4 below). The engineered-scatter list and its offence ("a craftsman being told to file a joint badly") is the kind of interior cost this reader reads the genre for.

## 5. The author's flags, judged (not ruled)

**Flag 1 — the mill-town Iron split at the fifth rank, and Movement 4's confluence.** The author is right that the packet's two promises (Lira vs the guild champion; Ephram's first regional final) cannot both hold in one Iron draw under M1's foot-against-head rule with Lira at Rank One and Ephram at Rank Six. The split "as the charter allowed", triggered by the guild yards' journeymen, is a clean local solution and it gives the house three titles at one meet, which ch13's "we're rich" needs. What it implies downstream: `packets/MOVEMENT-004.md` has Lira winning "a regional title" against the Rank 7 builder, "Karis third; Ephram second", i.e. all three Halcenvane Irons placing in ONE confluence draw. That is consistent with the split being a host option that the confluence does not exercise — but then (a) under foot-against-head Lira meets the confluence's top seed in round one again, which will be the third time in three meets that her first bout is the hardest, and (b) Ephram "second" means Lira beat Ephram somewhere in that draw, which M4 should stage or at least name. Recommendation to the coordinator: rule the split a host option, record in the ledger which meets split and which do not, and let M4 decide whether the Rank 7 builder is the head seed (round one) or a final (then somebody above Rank 7 is the head). The guild champion at Rank Four (flag 4) follows from the split and is fine.

**Flag 2 — Lira's debut against the top seed (R7 Stone, source R2).** Honours M1's draw rule; dramatically it is the right choice for a debut chapter. The cost is the pattern noted above; worth spending once more at most.

**Flag 5 — the exhibition format: five exchanges at most, two touches ends it, short of two the one with more, level the figure.** Against BOOK_MAP: the Daeva plan (§8, M9) runs E1 courtesy pass, E2 Cael scores, E3 Daeva scores, E4 stoppage — one touch each, no second touch, stoppage in the fourth; the format fits.
