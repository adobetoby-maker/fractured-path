## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Immediately, and for the right reason: the circular on the cohort board is a page of numbers, and the chapter turns it into people inside twenty lines. Brom tapping his own line with a knuckle "to remind it he was there", then calling it "a man counting spoons", is the fastest way I have seen a book make a reader care about a standings table. Lira's "You're writing it yourself" and "It's worse *and* tidier. That's your whole trouble in three words" lands the movement's whole thesis before breakfast is over, and the chapter then goes and proves it on the floor with the slow-feet drill ("Like lying." / "To him?" / "To my feet."). After that the road to the quarry town, the milestone nobody names, and the arrival ("There." / "Nobody asked him what. They could all see it.") are as clean a chapter close as the book has had.

**Where it slipped.** Twice, both in ch17. The Seln cutaway is the best-written prose in the movement for an adult, and the slowest stretch for a young reader: three evenings of procurement held off the page by design, then a long night of reading paper for its rag stock and linen thread, then a chain of inference that leans on four Book 4 memories delivered as allusions ("the post locked", "the third-year, Jask", "unmarked file. one of three.", "one slip of wing paper"). A reader who holds Book 4 well will love it; a reader who holds it loosely gets a list. The second slip is smaller: the table scene that follows is excellent, but Lira's errand to the board-man is set up in ch17 and paid in ch18, and across that gap the reader is asked to hold Brom's semifinal, the Auremont pair, and the grey-wool woman all at once. It holds, but only just.

**Which person matters.** Lira. This is her movement: the milestone, five days of nobody saying the name, "Huh. It's small." / "No. You mean it's small.", the hand on the Fenmark boy's breastbone instead of the sweep, "They kept your name and threw out your foot", "There's your bill", and the quay steps with Zerin's sheet. The author gave her the arc's first large turn, as the packet asked, and it is the thing I would tell someone about.

**Which event I expect next.** The circular that says they are over the line (Rooke has told us twice he will not believe it a day before it comes up the road), and Rooke's promised talk about the floor. Underneath both: Auremont's junior scout has a page on Cael's resets that he cannot read, and the book has just told us that is the first time in three years anyone has worked a seam he had not walked.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## 2. Two lenses

**A fluent thirteen-year-old reader.** Follows the fights without trouble: the warmth exchange (she walks, he stands, the stone takes his heat), the doctrine's left-foot gap, the damp north flags and the heat-shadow, the lever and the latch. Laughs at Brom and the ice, Gault holding the coat "like a man holding somebody else's baby", and Lira's board-man. Will carry "Huh. It's small." and "You fought me. Not the paper." out of the book. Would skim the middle of the Seln cutaway (the paper, the boxes near water, "Offices insist. Shops make do." is the line they keep). May stumble on why Cael's bursts are "four free in a day" while Lira's are "three a bout, same as always" in the same conversation, and on which Stone is which (a Stone reserve at home, the home house's Stone in the bracket, and a Stone foreman in the exhibition).

**An adult genre reader.** Gets the most satisfying thing in the movement: a progression system seen from the outside, as a market, with the compilers, the handicappers and the scouts each reading the protagonist at a different speed, and the one woman who "keeps my pace". Recognises the page-three funnel as a real idea about stale intelligence and enjoys watching it collect twice. Admires the Seln cutaway's procedure (the first read for what it says, the second for how it was made; "Door, record, man, company") and the way the records-broker thread closes as commerce rather than conspiracy. Will notice the one format slip in Lira's final and the slightly selective audit (see §4). Will want Rooke's talk about the floor on the page in M4.
