## Verdict

The book holds as one arc and lands its ending. Read end to end it does what the map asked: the Standard is taught hard in the first third, three ways (Rooke's yellow card, the mock ring with Gault's spectacles, page thirty-one by a stove), so that by the mill town a reader can price a figure before the clerk chalks it, and from then on the instrument surfaces only at its turns; the qualifying season is a real season, four towns with four floors, each floor an opponent that files no challenge; the doctrine is made to work too well, is caught by a protest, a betting board, a clerk with a pencil and a grey-wool woman in a gallery, and is repaired on purpose with a programme that costs a shoulder; Norhold is a city with the roof off, and the three companions' floors (Zerin, Marek, Ivenne) are each a different problem fought to a different cost; "Silver is not a bigger Iron" is earned in a pocket of quick stone with a Silver's hand coming down; the found family fights together once, and loses to a diagonal nobody priced; the frame empties from below and fills from above with one word; and the match (ch54–56) is the best writing in the book, three exchanges of a bank being spent to the minute, a break in which the other instrument is heard working underneath the account, and a reach held to the three numbered rules of a year-old minute, in the time it takes to draw a breath, with the stumble, the mercy on a clock and the notice exactly where §10 puts them. The panel's night (ch57) earns "under" over "by" in twenty minutes on one word; the hour in the room full of clocks (ch58) is the book's quietest and most dangerous scene; the closing's eight claps that would not stop, the banner's four ties in their charter order and two clerks signing in front of eight thousand people say what the book has been saying since Bracken's sand; and the convening window stays inside its nine hundred words and its altitude. The calendar chains from the last morning of the recess to the seventeenth day of the road home without a break against #41. Every figure, score, tally and count agrees with the page and the ledger. Every protected line is present and where the map puts it. Seln is never told, Rooke never asks, Ephram is kept outside the door and knows it, Vastin never guesses, Daeva declines to, and the Architect plants sit at their altitude.

The weaknesses a straight read shows are not structural. First, the author has a repertoire, and it has shifted since Book 4 rather than thinned: the squared page (198 hits of "square/squared" in 51 chapters: "squared to the edge", "square to the table", "her small square hand"), "flat" (186), the superlative-ever formula ("the most careful thing anybody had ever done to him on a floor", 41 instances), "as he read/did/considered everything" (27), "Nobody said anything" (31) and "said nothing / did not say anything" (92), "for a moment" (120), the micro-timing coinage ("a fraction" 53, "half a beat" 36, "quarter-beat" 35), "to nobody in particular" (25), the ears going red (13, eleven of them Ephram's), and the number eleven again (68, a third of them reached for). Second, a shape that repeats until a reader can predict it: the exhibition bout with its five parts (walk the floor; find the one thing the opponent never priced; the handshake aphorism; the figure "at the proper interval"; the Log entry with the bill), run fifteen times, and at Norhold the back room with its rocking table and the pump, visited twelve times. Third, a soft stretch at the confluence (ch21–23: five exhibitions in two chapters, three of them teaching lessons the quarry town has already taught) and a flat one on the empty board (ch47). ## 4. Arc and pacing

**Where it sags.** The confluence, ch21–23. ## 10. Close to source — passages that read as source-shaped

Not a probe; a reader's note for the lanes, since M8 reads 11% and M9 15% close by the tool. Three stretches carry the source's beat order so plainly that a listener hears an outline under the prose: the panel's night told in the pluperfect (ch57:51–157, "It had begun with a handcart… Then his own clerks had read out…"), where every argument is reported rather than heard; Daeva's history (ch49:103–157, the garden, the registry page, the programme, "Specimen.", the coast exercise), which is a summary of the source's window with its beats in the source's order, however fine the sentences; and the convening (ch60:109–129), which is a window by design and stays inside it. Two smaller ones: the road sorting itself (ch28:83–121) and the aftermath list after the lane (ch56:105–117, the barrel, the ledgers face down, the tower stairs), both of which read as the source's catalogue rendered. None of them is a close-band problem the tool would flag at the sentence; all of them are places a same-author lane could put a scene where there is a report.
