## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Immediately: Rooke's two fingers on the seam in a stable door, then the courier who "did not know what he was carrying". The circular among the bread, Karis trying four sums, and Rooke's one breath is the best-staged quiet climax I have read in this edition; it earns the chapter title. The five filings at the end of ch20 (the captain's *I have never lost a wagon*; the barge-master's *I have read this river all my life. Let's see.*) are a perfect hook into the meet. In ch21 the salt store's roar, item two run honestly, and then the captain pitching camp — *breath, plant, pane* — gave me a mechanism I could follow and a fight I could score. Ch22's builder bout is the movement's purest piece of craft: a fighter who declines to live in another man's structures, told from the rope by people who can see it. The barge-master's current, the honest fall, and "What are you carrying?" / "I don't know yet." / "Then I hope it's cargo." is the line of the movement. Gault's straw-packed boots and "I have been mispriced by professionals, young man" is the warmest page in the book so far. Vastin's three nouns and "Somewhere above him, the grammar had changed" is a cutaway that justifies its length.

**Where it slipped.** Three places, none fatal. (1) In the bracket reporting on the first confluence day, the clock stops making sense: all four Iron quarterfinals are reported finished by noon, yet at dusk "the Iron quarterfinal [is] still running" and Lira watches the builder's "last bout of the day" in the late afternoon and says next morning she watched "three of them" — the day has more bouts in it than a sixteen draw allows (details in §3). (2) The barge-master's two touches arrive without an exchange break between them, so a reader who has learned the format from the five previous bouts (touch ends the exchange, walk the circle, "Exchange.") loses count. (3) The cold term (ch25–26) is honest, well-chosen work, but it is four training vignettes in a row and the thread tightens only when the seedings go up and Lira sits on the wall.

**Who matters.** Lira most (the builder; the frame; "Put down that I'm not glad tonight"), then Ephram (the final he nearly wins, then the trestles, then "North"), then Gault, whose page in the observation notebook is "the longest in the book now". Rooke's single breath carries more than any speech. Vastin matters as the man who notices the words and prices what noticing will cost.

**What I expect next.** The road and the city; Zerin's "middle" that Lira cannot build; the Shield doctrine Cael believes solved meeting something it does not fit; the boxes on Vastin's chair.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

### 3.2 Findings (continuity, format, boundary) — numbered, with severity

**F1 — The barge-master's second touch has no exchange between it and the first (format + clarity). ch23 l.111–127.** After "Touch. Halcenvane. One to one." Cael counts *Two of five* "standing in the landing beat"; the barge-master "stepped back … planted his back foot again, somewhere new, and started a fresh sweep"; Cael "burst once more, late in the exchange" and touches him: "Two to one. Bout to Halcenvane." No "Exchange." is called, no circle is walked, no "Even" — unlike every other bout in the movement, where a touch ends the exchange (ch21 l.265–295 is the model). The AUTHOR-REPORT scores it "2–1 in four"; the page reads as two touches inside one exchange. Effect: a reader who has learned the format loses the count exactly at the movement's most-quoted bout. Scope: ten to twenty words — the circle walked with the forearm against his side, the steward's call, the fourth exchange opening on the new line. Severity: medium (the fix is tiny; the defect sits on a protected-adjacent page).

**F2 — The first confluence day has more bouts in it than a sixteen draw allows (continuity). ch21 l.57–95, 313, 341, 349–355; ch22 l.9.** At noon on d108 Cael is told that Lira has won her first round "in the morning" and "then" her quarterfinal; that Ephram beat the eighth seed and then the top seed; that Karis "had won twice"; that the builder "had finished his quarterfinal a few minutes before" — all four Iron quarterfinals done. Yet: (a) l.313 Lira comes to the salt store "between her bouts" after noon, when she has none left that day; (b) l.341 at dusk "the Iron quarterfinal [is] still running in the lamplight"; (c) l.349 Lira spends "the end of the afternoon … watching the builder fight his last bout of the day"; (d) l.355 "Every boy who fought him today" and ch22 l.9 "I watched three of them yesterday" give the builder three bouts on a day with two rounds. Effect: the careful reader's clock breaks, and the builder's reputation is built on a bout that cannot exist. Scope: four or five line changes, not a rewrite — e.g. "after her bouts"; at dusk, the hall's lamps lit over tomorrow's chalked card and rows of empty benches (the point of the image is the empty benches, which survives); Lira watching the builder "from the back row until the hall emptied" or re-reading her own notes of his two bouts; "Both boys"; "I watched both of his yesterday, twice over in my head". Alternative (larger): move the quarterfinals to the afternoon and amend the noon report to "would fight" — more lines touched; not recommended. Severity: medium.

**F3 — Ember "on a meet record" contradicts the figure-only meet record (continuity). ch26 l.105 (Log: "it's on a meet record now as well") and l.129 (Seln: "and now a meet record from the confluence").** The M2 r1 ruling made the Bronze's "twenty feet" hearsay because *meet records are figure-only*, and this movement's own posting (ch23 l.183) shows five figures with composites and nothing else. Effect: small, but Seln in particular would not misname the paper. Scope: two phrases — "on a seat's report now as well" / "and now the confluence seat's report". Severity: low–medium (cheap; touches the public-count question in F9).

**F4 — Pressure "since the recess" (continuity with M2). ch26 l.101.** The M2 ledger records at the mill-town plate (d57) "Right shoulder: the plate's plain ache, one blow of the four-a-sitting cap, a day."
