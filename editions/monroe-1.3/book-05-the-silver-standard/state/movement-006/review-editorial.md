## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** Hesk's clock, "wrong about the hour … right about the minutes" (ch33, opening), is a good first paragraph: it tells a thirteen-year-old what kind of instrument Cael is before anyone explains it. The back-room study caught hardest at "There's no gather because there's no burst" and Lira trying to go without gathering and sitting down on the floor ("That's why she started at twelve"). "She's never behind" lands as a real discovery because the two previous evenings came up empty. In ch35 the half-beat when Zerin is behind and "her weight hung" is the movement's best single moment; the gallery's reaction ("bigger and messier than a cheer") is earned. In ch37 the choice to show the con first through Umber's eyes, and only then from the rail, is the strongest structural decision in the movement: the reader is told the cheap gate is a structure before Marek pays for it, so the throw reads as inevitability rather than trick. In ch38 the unfinished lattice is the right ending for Karis because the page has spent an exchange and a half proving she had nothing left.

**Where it slipped.** Ch34 runs five consecutive talk scenes (Karis's study, Rooke's sessions, the Rhagen warm-up, supper plus the mirror study, the eleventh page, then the stair). Each is good; stacked, the chapter is the movement's low point for pull, and the first-time reader may lose which night it is. The Umber cutaway's middle (mark and craft, "a ruler lying on a table") runs a shade long and abstract for the younger lens. Ch39 summarises the week three times: Rooke's standings speech, the paragraph at the ring ("He had watched three people he loved …"), and the closing Log, which restates the paragraph almost line for line. One of those three could carry the weight alone.

**Which person matters.** Karis. Her arc (the dead study, the burned plan, "I'll fight it", the raw contacts, the broken-off stroke, the honest loss the next day, the finding on the table) is the movement's spine, and Cael's own beat (safety and loneliness are one fact) is paid through her. Lira's want is the second pole. Brom is the most satisfying to watch and the least changed, which is right for Brom.

**What I expect next.** The Rhagen captain finding the edges of Cael, with the whole delegation at the rail and Lira charting one-armed. The Velmere letter and the summons will sit until the road home.

**Would I continue.** Yes.

## 3. Owner flags — rulings

### (a) The calendar: the deferral notice, T5–T10, and what M7 inherits

**On the page: clear and consistent.** Ch33's noon scene does the work in full: the notice text (category needs the main floor and the senior panel; both are taken by the semifinals and the Copper final; the ring goes up "when the brackets have cleared the main floor"; bouts heard "in the order of their filing"; the clerk's *deferred* against both houses); Brom's "You said four days, Rooke. On the third night. I heard you."; Rooke's "I did say it. The tower said it too. Now the tower says otherwise."; Seln's "The tower has changed its opinion." A reader who remembers M5 ch32 ("Four days to the first and seven to the second") is told plainly that the line is dead and why. The author's claim is borne out. The notice is also consistent with the rest of the movement: the main floor clears with the Copper final on T9 morning, the crews raise the ring on the night of T10, ch39 says "in a day or two", and "order of filing" puts Rhagen's captain first, which matches M5 (his name stands first on the frame). The one-day lag between the floor clearing and the crews starting is reasonable and unremarked. One small inheritance for M7: finals day's morning card (BOOK_MAP §12 C14) needs the main floor again, so the ring will have to come down and go back up; the source already has it "rebuilt at midnight" (§2 item 34), so this is canon, not a problem.

**What M7 must inherit (ruling):**
1. The ring is complete by dawn T11. The captain's bout cannot be earlier than T11.
2. The order captain → duelist → rest day → trial is fixed by protected wording, not by the packet: item 22 (the midpoint log, "I just beat Silver") precedes item 23 ("Seventeen. Six fragments. Tomorrow I find out if the second technicality holds"), and item 23's "Tomorrow" ties the rest day to the day before the trial. So the rest day cannot stay at T11. With the duelist "three days later" (§2 item 30), the earliest clean sequence is captain T11 or T12, duelist T14 or T15, rest day the day after the duelist, trial the day after that. The birthday's date is OWNER-pending anyway (§12 C7; #35), so moving the rest day's T-number costs nothing canonical; never set it beside a season word.
3. §6's later dates (trial T12, filing T15, convening T16, ring conversation and third place T18, finals T20, panel T20–21, meeting T22, closing T23) shift by three or four days as a block, or the coordinator compresses the gaps; either way the coordinator, not the M7 author, should re-date §6 before M7 drafts. §6's "eleven days before the trial" for Zerin's semifinal was already inconsistent with its own T12 trial and should be dropped.
4. Seln's "held for him for nine days" (M7 packet; not §10-protected) must be recounted to the actual days the chair stood empty before Vastin sits (T1 to the day he sits).
5. Rooke's "four days … seven" is superseded on the page and needs no further mention; M7 should not restate it.
6. Aside for the coordinator: the M7 packet's "Cael's two Silver bouts sold the second floor to standing room" names the wrong floor; the exhibitions are in the main floor's ring. Not an M6 matter.

### (b) "The fair-day veteran" = the M1/M2 buried fair-day Copper (she)

**Correct, and the only identity that works.** BOOK_MAP §2 item 28 says "the returning fair-day veteran" and §3A says "semifinal rematch". Only two Copper opponents fit "returning": the wool-town buried Copper R4 and the Norhold round-two hill-country Stone. The Stone was beaten by Brom in round two of a single-elimination draw (M5 ledger, T2, 9–7 in five) and cannot reappear in a semifinal. The buried Copper was profiled by Rooke in M1 as a fair-day fighter ("Rank Four on paper. Fights like a Seven. Buried."), is "she, about eighteen, Iron Skin; her lean coach in brown" in M2, and lost to Brom 2–1 in four at 23–22 (M2 ledger). Ch36's recollection matches M2 to the figure (the muttering gallery, two touches to one, twenty-three to twenty-two, the lean coach in brown, the sheep market). "Veteran" reads as a fair-day circuit veteran, which at eighteen is fair. Approved.

Two soft points, neither blocking: (i) ch36 "Her market house had come up through its own region on her back … and had brought no other entry to any bracket in the city." M4 ch20/ch23 teach qualification as whole-house standings (the region sends three; Halcenvane's two companions on the list are perennial qualifiers), so a one-fighter house qualifying strains the arithmetic the book taught; it is Karis's hearsay and can stand, but "its own region" should not be Halcenvane's (it is not, since M4's two other houses are old academies). (ii) ch36 "in the fourth glass" for the fourth exchange is a word the book has not used for an exchange; on audio it will land as a drinking vessel. Change to "exchange".

### (c) Format-driven departures under OWNER-DECISIONS #39

**Brom vs Marek: 1–3, 3–2, 3–1, 3–0 for 10–6 in four — approved.** §2 item 28 binds the final to "four exchanges", and under #39 a four-exchange finish needs a margin of four or more after the fourth. With E1 to Marek (the survey must win E1 for the con to mean anything), Brom must take E2–E4 and E4 must close 3–0 or 3–1 with the margin at four. The author's allocation is the lightest one that keeps every §8 beat: five stakes (three flagged, two too light), tolls in E2, the sixth gap found and declined in E3, the unchecked shoulder at the break, the con in E4. "E2 to Brom 3–2" is right because a toll is a clean return and the flags must give it. The arithmetic itself forcing Marek's commitment ("One more point to Brom, and the exchange would close at three, and the bout would close with it") is the best use the book has yet made of #39: the format becomes the villain's motive. **One defect inside it (must-fix, see §4.3):** E3's last point is written so that it reads as a paired toll, which would make E3 3–2 and the bout 7–7 after three, 10–7 after four, margin three, and a fifth exchange required. The page needs one clause making it plain that Marek's final gate touch in E3 does not flag.

**Karis vs Ivenne: 0–3, 3–1, 1–3, 3–1, 3–1 for 10–9 in five — approved.** §8 lists four phases, not four exchanges, and §2 item 29 gives no count. With the file winning E1 and "the stranger" (Ivenne reading cold) winning an exchange, no four-exchange finish is possible, and five is the honest shape: file / abandonment / stranger / invention against reading / the deep groove. Level at 9–9 with the exchange at 2–1 is a legitimate state under #39 and the broken-off stroke taking the tenth point closes the exchange at 3 and the bout at five. Approved.

### 4.3 Continuity defects found (must-fix, all line-level)

1. **ch35, E4 — the burst count.** Lira's rate is three (M3; Rooke's "I'll tell you when you can"). The page spends them: the survey burst (E1), the banked burst at the third corner (E2), "the last burst of her rate" to close the east-rope corner (E3), and Rooke folds three fingers at the E3 interval. E4 then spends "a half-burst she could not afford" for the diagonal, and after that "She spent her last burst, and then she spent the burst after the last, the one she did not have." That is two or three over the rate. Rooke at supper says "you spent a burst you hadn't got" (singular) and the ledger carries one over-rate burst, a week of half work. Cut "her last burst, and then she spent" so the sentence reads: she spent the burst after the last, the one she did not have. The half-burst can stay as part of the same overspend if the author prefers, but the page must not add up to more than one burst over three.
2. **ch37, E1 — "And the fifth went somewhere Cael had never charted at all."** In the passage by the kitchen door that morning Cael names four gaps he has charted, and the fourth is "the way Brom's back heel settled when he loaded a drive, on any floor that gave at all." The fifth stake *is* that gap, found in the ground Brom chose. The sentence contradicts the chapter's own ledger ("He found six. I knew about four."; the pivot and the breath are the two Cael did not know). Rewrite to something like: the fifth went to a gap Cael had charted only on the oak at home, where the whole floor gave; Marek found it in the one board Brom had chosen to stand on. Keep "He found the gap in the ground Brom had chosen himself", which is the point.
3. **ch37, E3 — the last point.** "He went instead to the oldest gate he had, the half-beat after Brom's drive … and paid for it as he had paid for it every time before. Brom's return took the third point." In E2 the tolls were paired flags (Marek scored, then Brom's return scored heavier); "as he had paid for it every time before" tells the reader this is the same transaction, which makes E3 3–2, the bout 7–7, and the fourth exchange 10–7 — margin three, fifth exchange required, and ch37's "no fighter on earth could draw level from four points down" and Umber's "a point from the end of everything" both fail. The call "Three to one" is right; the prose must make the touch unflagged: Marek's touch arrived a hair late, or glanced, and the panel gave only the return. One clause. This is the one defect that touches canon (the format as stated in M5).
4. **ch36, the stair — "I wrote it this afternoon. While you were all at Brom's bout."** Karis was at the rail for Brom's semifinal and speaks there three times ("She's been watching him"; "He's showing everything"; the market-house line). Brom's quarterfinal was "in the hour after noon" and nine minutes long. Change to "while you were at his quarterfinal" or "before his semifinal".
5. **ch36 / ch38 — pencil against ink.** Lira: "she wrote it on her hand … with my pencil, because she'd left hers on the table." Cael, the same night: "the ink on her palm, smudged now". Ch38: "the ink of last night's line … half of it cooked away by Karis's own fire". Ink is the better object (it cooks; it smudges; Lira writes on her own hand in ink in ch33). Change "my pencil" to "my pen".
6. **ch38, Ilsev — "the bout before that had gone five exchanges against a man the gallery-sheets called buried."** The five-exchange bout was Brom's round two on T2 (M5 ledger, 9–7). The bout before the semifinal was the quarterfinal against the valley Stone, three exchanges. Change to "a bout in the second round had gone five exchanges" or "earlier in the draw".
