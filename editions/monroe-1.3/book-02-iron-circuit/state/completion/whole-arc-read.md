## Verdict

The book holds as one arc. Read end to end it does what the map asked of it: a circuit put on the page as a world with its own ladder, keepers and accounts; four fights that each change the problem (Ulric, Brom, Keth, Reydan) and a fifth that ends on apprenticeship (Ulric again, Wind only, the rope); three fragments becoming four, each one priced on the page before it is trusted; and a boy who came down a hill with a bag leaving it with two people who have read the whole book and not turned back. The consent boundary minted in M5 is kept to the letter from ch32 to the road: every read of a named person after supper is asked for and granted, the knock is argued as the floor's, and the three places Cael goes in unasked (Brom at the table, the old man at the dray, the attempt on Keth he refuses) are each written down as his fault. Every running tally Cael states matches the page: the knock ratios from eight-in-twenty-two to sixteen-in-twenty, the Keth bout's nine knocked and five answered, the Reydan bout's fourteen, twelve and two, the six Wind bursts, the three redirects, the twelve full-force reps and the shoulder's three. The fragment count runs two, three, four with one anomaly, and session nine happens once, is laid beside the Reydan half-second and kept apart, and carries its *Note* line exactly. Every protected line is exact and lands where the map puts it; the record window states only what §9 allows; the Reader Standard is met; the calendar names no month and the road names no season. The two central contests (Brom, ch23–25; Keth, ch39–40), the thirteen-day fortnight (ch45–51) and the Reydan bout (ch52–53) are the best writing in the book, and the Havel–Coss thread is the quietest and most exact piece of institutional dread in the edition so far.

The weaknesses a straight read shows are not structural. They are, first, a repertoire of about ten similes and gesture-tags that recur so often that by the middle third a listener hears the author under the characters (the explanatory "the way a X does Y" family alone appears 351 times in 60 chapters; "as if" 251; "exactly" 168). Second, a soft stretch in M5 (ch33–35), where three successive chapters each carry an officer section or a record window while Cael's own stakes pause for the watcher. Third, a handful of continuity seams that a line pass closes: Dace's tenure is twelve years in M1–M2 and twenty in M7–M8; Keth was watched three months in M6 and four in M7; Reydan Kindles at eleven in ch44 against the locked fourteen; ch60 remembers the clerk's account being read once in Dace's back room when ch48 has it read three times at the kitchen table; ch58 remembers Cael telling Ulric what he watched when ch1 has him give nothing. Sixteen line fixes below; three cross-movement priorities for one same-author pass. Nothing needs a rebuild.

## 3. Book-wide repetition — the worst ten

Counts are `grep -oiE` over all 60 chapters (hits / chapters). "Keep" names instances that earn their place.

1. **The explanatory simile "the way a X does Y" / "the way you…" / "the way he…"** — 351 / 60. The book's dominant tic, denser than Book 3's 237. It glosses gestures the sentence has already made clear ("the way a man puts his hand on a table before he stands", "the way a dog waits at a door", "the way water goes downhill"). Keep the character-specific firsts: Brom's "the way his grandmother's builder had once taught him to walk a new floor" (ch16), Hesk's "the way Hesk checked a drawing before he cut metal" (ch6), Vell's "the way she said *begin*" (ch2). Target: two per chapter, none on a gesture already plain.
2. **"as if …" / "as though …"** — 251 / 59 and 45 / 25. Often stacked with (1) in the same sentence ("as if it owed him money" twice, ch2 and ch12; "as if it had done something he had not asked it to" five times).
3. **"exactly"** — 168 / 57. Keep where exactness is the subject (the straightedge, Vell's lines, Keth's straw's-width cut); thin everywhere else.
4. **The measured pause** — "for a long (time | moment | while)" 96 / 45 and "for a while" 70 / 42. Nearly every dialogue scene has one. Keep in the alcove after session nine and on the stone after Reydan, where length is the point.
5. **"did not look up / without looking up"** — 86 / 42. Vell's and Dace's signature (they answer to the book and the wall); it has leaked to Brom, the heavyset man, Keth, Quenna and the mending woman.
6. **"plainly" / "said it plainly"** — 57 / 39, and its cousin "thought about it honestly" — 13 / 12 (31 / 24 for "honestly"). Brom's register has become everybody's.
7. **Brom's two tags** — "the corner of his mouth went in" 13 / 11 and the neck going "red from the collar" 7 / 5 (plus "his big (patient) face" 6 / 6, "with the whole of his attention" 3). Keep the first mouth-corner (ch17) and the first red neck (ch28, over *made a friend*); after that one tag per scene.
8. **Measures by the hand** — "a hand's width / breadth" 27 / 18 and "a finger's width / a hair's breadth / the width of a straw" 33 / 27. Keep for the fan on the stone (ch7), Lira's notched stick, Keth's cut (ch39–40), Ulric's walking turn (ch58).
9. **"like a (man | woman) who…"** — 32 / 24 ("like a man who has been robbed in the street", "like a man checking a sum", "like a woman sitting down at the end of a long walk"). Keep two or three a movement.
10. **The lock formula** — "two *ands*" 29 / 16 and "bright stillness" 10 / 10, nearly always together ("two *ands* of bright stillness"). The count is a motif and belongs in every bout; "bright stillness" is a phrase and should appear once per bout at most.

Just under the ten: "like weather / for weather / as weather" 9 / 8 (the heavyset man's face and Vell's "I've no opinion on what it means" own it; Havel and Reydan borrow it); "with great (care | seriousness | dignity | suspicion)" 12 / 11; "nodded (once | slowly)" 19 / 16; "it was not (quite) a question" 7 / 6; "his mouth went thin" (Dace) 6 / 6; "went still" 12 / 10; "the old face" (Lira) 5 / 5; "You're grey" / "Go and eat" 14 across M6–M7 (a running joke that works twice); "plum" for a bruise 14 / 10 (a motif from ch9 that becomes Lira's word; thin the mid-book ones); "from the front" 27 / 22 (Brom's reading habit, which the book names as such every time he reads anything). None of these is fixed below, because every one of them needs a reading pass and not a find-and-replace; the small verbatim repeats are the first thing priority 1 should take.

## 6. Reader Standard — met

No oaths, none of the softer cousins; the harshest words in the book are "stupid" for ideas and feet and "like an idiot" once, in Cael's own head, about smiling in the dark (ch56). Violence costs and is felt (the knees, the forearm's crack, the straw's-width cut, Lira's hands, Reydan's cracked ribs) and is never lingered on; Vell's rule is said before every bout and kept, Keth and Maud both hold the finishing blow, and Dace says what he will do if a man forgets his hand. No romance, no innuendo: Lira leans on Brom's arm and pulls his ear, and the book lets that be what it is. Wrong is named as wrong by the people wronged and by the book: Brom's family's kindness "that had already decided what you were" (ch16, ch26); the Fenmark slate (ch12); the scouted Stone read his own past (ch31); the Force man told to his face that two years hung on a step that was not there (ch31); the line nobody signed (ch33); Coss's chosen silence, which the book makes him pay for in his own accounting (ch41). Cruelty is never rewarded; Reydan's grievance-keeping rival is the counter-example (ch54), and Reydan chooses the other way.
