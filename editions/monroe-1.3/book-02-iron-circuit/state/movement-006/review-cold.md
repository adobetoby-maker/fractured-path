## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in Chapter 37 with the inked figures—“nine and twenty-two”—and Brom’s discovery that Cael’s successful knocks correlate with a planted foot. The practical experiment gives the opening a live question before the terminology is fully clear. Interest deepened when Cael entered Keth’s children’s ring and deliberately refused to use an uninvited read. That choice made the ability matter morally, not just tactically. The cleanest emotional catch was the line Cael finally writes in the grey book: “*I like him.*” From there, the coming bout carries a cost beyond winning.

Interest slipped slightly during the repeated validation work in Chapter 38. The barge lad, Orvet’s woman, the alcove repetitions, and the written accounting are all useful, but the movement explains the join/knock relationship several times before the main-floor bout. The larger dip came after the Keth climax, in Chapter 41: the market-name and credit montage is thematically apt, but the turn to Coss and registry procedure temporarily suspends the more immediate question of who will challenge Cael next. Chapter 43 also states Lira’s final understanding in several successive forms after the bout has already made it clear.

If I must choose one person who matters most in this movement, it is Keth. Cael is the anchor and Lira becomes the closing emotional center, but Keth changes the stakes: he is kind, formidable, curious, and willing to lose honestly. His generosity makes Cael’s victory hurt in the right way. Bede then earns attention quickly because he answers Keth’s warning without becoming an antagonist.

I expect the next event to come from outside the familiar circuit: the grey-coated woman, the up-river keeper, or the registry will convert Cael’s growing reputation into direct contact or pressure. I also expect Cael’s next opponent to attack a “shape” he cannot train away, while Lira works to remove the old middle from her hands.

I would continue. The movement pays off the Keth preparation, gives the win a social consequence, refuses to let the win settle into uncomplicated triumph, and closes on a credible widening threat: “*I didn’t send it. It went.*”

## Reader lenses

### Fluent thirteen-year-old reader

The bouts, visible practice gains, loyal friendships, dry jokes, and clear win/loss consequences should carry this reader strongly. Keth’s children, the pear cordial, Bede’s notebook, and Maud’s kindness after beating Lira keep the story humane. The main risk is cognitive load: terms such as knock, gaze, lock, hip, Pressure, hooks, Path, assessed, and equivalent arrive as already-established vocabulary. Most become legible through action, but a reader entering at Chapter 37 may need to reread some early paragraphs. The Coss section is also more institutionally interesting than immediately exciting from this lens.

### Adult genre reader

This reader is likely to value the reciprocal design: surveillance, consent, public reputation, classification, credit, and institutional legitimacy all grow naturally out of the combat system. The movement treats progression as interpretation and self-knowledge, not simply increased force. Keth, Bede, Maud, Vell, Coss, and the stall-keepers each reveal a different consequence of being measured. The chief risk is thematic overconfirmation: once the bouts dramatize “habit versus shape” and “true local measure versus inert official card,” later reflection sometimes states those meanings again.

## Continuity and constraint check

### Canon

No editorial canon evidence was supplied. I found no contradiction that can be verified as a canon violation from these chapters alone. Plan adherence is likewise N/A because no plan was supplied.

### Physical state

Physical state is generally carried with precision: Cael’s band progresses from ache to bar to fire and leaves his eyes slow; the straw-width arm cut is washed, dressed, and later healing; the Bede bout carries the numb forearm, heavy thigh, ribs, shoulder, and lost breath through the result; Lira’s hands, heel, back, and breath remain consequential after Maud.

There is one apparent numerical mismatch. In Chapters 39–40, the narrated Keth bout appears to contain eight explicit knocks with four answers: six knocks/three answers in the second exchange, then two knocks/one answer in the fourth. Chapter 41’s Log records “*Pulse: nine knocked, five answered.*” The prose may intend one additional off-page success, but the surrounding accounting is presented as exact, so the missing event reads as a continuity slip rather than compression. Confidence: medium-high.

### Power limits

Limits hold convincingly. The knock works at close range only about half the time and cannot share a moment with the hip; smear is dangerous when Cael projects meaning onto it. The gaze narrows peripheral vision and produces escalating head pain. The Wind goes left, imposes a two-count lock, needs breath for a chain, and leaves visible shoulder tells. Pressure is deliberately withheld. Lira’s new continuous catch is costly, inconsistent, and defeated when old hand memory returns. No effortless escalation is apparent.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully partitioned. Cael distinguishes public observation from going “into” someone, asks the dock partner’s permission during practice, and honors Lira’s demand not to use even the knock during her bout. Bede can infer visible patterns but not the invisible knock. Keth sees enough to suspect Cael’s hip without learning its full operation. Dace observes the grey-coated woman’s habits but does not know her identity. Coss recognizes the file from registry knowledge that the circuit characters do not possess. No apparent knowledge leak stood out.

### Reserved disclosures

The movement preserves its larger mysteries. The grey-coated woman’s name and purpose remain withheld; the origin and authority behind Cael’s blank classification remain withheld; the higher registry floors do not answer Coss’s note; the outside keepers’ precise intentions remain unknown. These are active plants rather than vague omissions because each produces observable behavior and escalating consequence.

## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Chapters 39–41, Cael’s knocks during the Keth bout and the later Log entry. **Observed issue:** The narrated bout appears to show eight knocks/four answers, while the Log says nine/five. **Effect on the reader:** A reader trained to trust Cael’s exact accounting may pause to recount at the moment the text wants the emotional aftermath to land. **Proposed scope:** Add one clearly located answered knock to the bout or revise the Log totals; no restructuring is needed. **Strength to preserve:** The rigorous cost ledger and the crucial distinction between an answer, a smear, and Cael painting hope onto a smear.

2. **Location:** Chapter 41, the pivot from Cael’s changed market life to “The word came to Coss secondhand.” **Observed issue:** In a cold movement read, Coss arrives with a dense file-and-routing history before his relationship to Cael is fully re-established. **Effect on the reader:** The institutional stakes are intelligible, but the emotional line briefly cools while the reader reconstructs who Coss is and why his silence costs him. **Proposed scope:** Add one compact orienting clause at the start of the section tying Coss to the earlier monitored file or prior meeting; retain the full choice at the officers’ table. **Strength to preserve:** The quiet moral action of choosing not to claim Cael and the bureaucracy rendered as personal fear rather than exposition.

3. **Location:** Chapter 43, from Lira’s post-bout alcove reflection through Cael’s later synthesis before Hesk’s letter. **Observed issue:** The difference between the circuit’s honest measure and its inability to alter Lira’s official card is stated several times after the bout and Vell’s ruling have already dramatized it. **Effect on the reader:** The ending remains strong, but repeated interpretation slightly softens Lira’s decisive turn back to training and delays the courier hook. **Proposed scope:** Compress one layer of recap—preferably Cael’s restatement of Lira’s conclusion—while keeping Lira’s own realization, the lamp, Hesk’s coat image, and the final line intact. **Strength to preserve:** Lira’s agency in choosing truthful provisional status and the excellent closing escalation from a known face to a name traveling without consent.
