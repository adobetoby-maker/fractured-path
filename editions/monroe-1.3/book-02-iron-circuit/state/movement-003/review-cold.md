This is a **fresh-context simulated cold read**, not a real audience measurement and not demographic research. ## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in chapter 16 with Brom reading Vell’s ledger from the front. The act is concrete, reveals his standards, and turns a record book into a test of character. The next hook—“There’s a boy at Ardenmere the registry can’t sort”—joins the newcomer to Cael before they speak. Brom’s history then makes him more than an obstacle: the unlocked gate, the family’s kindly attempt to “correct” him, and the honest losses in the boat shed all explain why he values a low-rating ledger and refuses to feed Cael false observations.

Interest slipped most during the Coss cutaway in chapter 17 and, more mildly, across the repeated technical rehearsals in chapters 19–22. The Coss material has real institutional dread, especially the unsigned marker and the second blank log page, but on this seven-chapter cold read the grey slip, green slip, Havel, the prior pie, and the unknown prefixes arrive in a dense cluster and do not reconnect before the movement ends. In the training line, the same tactical conclusions—Pressure locked, Wind as insurance, three hard answers followed by a fourth low strike—are restated often enough that discovery sometimes becomes confirmation of what the reader already understands.

Cael is the person whose choice governs the movement, but Brom became the person I most wanted to know. His restraint is active rather than blank, and his refusal to falsify the column turns the coming fight into an ethical test as well as a tactical one. Lira supplies the strongest completed emotional arc: she refuses Cael’s page, discovers Wendel’s slow side herself, adapts inside the exchange, earns a provisional rise, and then corrects Cael’s claim that he must understand himself “on his own.”

The event I expect next is the main-floor bout. I expect Cael to draw down Brom’s “account,” put the fourth attack below the knee, and discover that either the gap or the coverage map means something slightly different under live pressure. I also expect Brom to do something new when he cannot place Cael on a “shelf.” Behind that immediate event, I expect the unsigned registry marker to return and Lira to receive her two confirming bouts.

I would continue. The movement earns that answer through character and method, not merely by withholding the announced fight. Lira’s win and Vell’s written correction provide substantial local payoff, while the Cael–Brom relationship makes the deferred bout feel worth waiting for. The reservation is that seven chapters of explicit countdown ending at “Four days” creates a sharper expectation debt than an ordinary cliffhanger.

## Continuity and boundary check

No editorial canon evidence was supplied, so I cannot verify a canon violation or a reserved-disclosure breach. The findings below are manuscript-internal observations only.

### Canon and continuity

No verified contradiction found. Within the supplied chapters, the tier vocabulary, bout history, countdown, and character relationships remain stable. Brom has eight recorded Ardenmere bouts when introduced, seven wins after an initial loss, and the movement consistently treats Vell’s Iron-equivalent line as deliberately conservative. Lira’s line moves from Copper-equivalent to provisional Iron-equivalent only after the Wendel bout, with two confirming bouts still required.

The Coss cutaway is an unresolved connection rather than a contradiction. Its unsigned handling marker, Havel’s green entry, and Coss’s memory of the removed grey slip point to an authority above his grade, but this movement gives no evidence with which to identify it.

### Physical state

Physical carryover is strong. Cael’s Wind use repeatedly costs breath and the line from left hip to inner knee; the failed third chained burst produces greying vision and knees, while the reactive burst against the Blade costs substantially more than the pre-read burst. Rest intervals and warnings accompany later testing. His Pressure use produces the hollow under the ribs, a thinning third strike, and a consciously untested fourth. Lira’s upper-arm injury from Wendel progresses from red during the bout to purple in chapter 22 and affects how she carries the arm. No unexplained recovery was apparent.

### Power limits

Most limits are explicit and honored: Cael’s Wind fan does not extend into the right-rear quarter; every burst imposes a lock; chaining requires stored breath; three Pressure-enhanced strikes are his working ceiling; Brom’s extra sense works only at close range; and Brom’s selective hardening has coverage and recovery constraints.

One apparent mechanics inconsistency needs clarification. In chapter 16 Brom learns to harden “at the instant” of contact, and in chapter 18 Cael observes Brom inhaling and settling before the hardening. Yet the initial latency model measures roughly one to one-and-a-half pulse beats after “a newcomer’s fist landed” before Brom’s answer, while the later reset model measures the interval after one hard answer before another can occur. Those may be three different events—anticipatory activation, visible redirection, and recovery—but Cael’s language sometimes treats them as the same “gap.” Because the chapter 22 plan relies on striking during that gap, this is an apparent internal clarity problem, not a verified canon error.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully sourced. Cael distinguishes records, hearsay, direct observation, and hypothesis; he even refuses to promote the accidental shin kick to evidence. Brom’s range is inferred from his positions before he confirms it. Lira discovers Wendel’s slow side herself and explicitly refuses Cael’s page. Coss does not claim the current marker matches the removed slip because he did not record the old code. Cael’s belief that Brom has not lied to the column exceeds measurement, but the manuscript labels it as a character judgment on which he knowingly stakes the plan.

### Reserved disclosures

No reserved-disclosure list was supplied. The movement reveals that Brom senses Cael as anomalous, but it does not explain what Cael is; the absent “shelf” deepens the question. The registry’s higher-level marker also remains unidentified. On manuscript evidence alone, both disclosures remain controlled rather than resolved.

## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Chapters 18, 20, and 22—from “A newcomer’s fist landed” and the pre-impact breath observation through the reset/account theory and the four-strike plan. **Observed issue:** Activation before contact, post-contact latency, and recovery before the next hardening are not consistently distinguished, although the decisive tactic depends on exactly which interval is vulnerable. **Effect on the reader:** A reader can follow Cael’s conclusion while remaining unsure whether the fourth strike exploits reaction time, depleted capacity, or physical reset; an adult action reader may question how a blow can be redirected a pulse beat after it lands. **Proposed scope:** Make a narrow terminology-and-causality repair across those passages: name the observable event Cael times, separate it from pre-impact hardening, and reserve “reset” or “account” for the between-answer recovery. Do not change the tactic or shorten the developed training. **Strength to preserve:** The stimulus/latency/response discovery, pulse correction, live dock-partner confirmation, and evidence-first feel of Cael’s method.

2. **Location:** Chapter 17, the Coss cutaway beginning “The return came up to Coss” and ending with the daughter’s map. **Observed issue:** The simulated cold reader receives Havel, a prior pie meeting, a grey slip, a green slip, an unknown prefix, standing instructions, and two blank log pages before the thread has a concrete point of contact with the movement’s present action. **Effect on the reader:** Institutional menace is strong, but short-term orientation drops and the countdown to Brom temporarily loses pressure; some readers may retain only “the registry is secretly watching Cael.” **Proposed scope:** Add one or two compact orientation cues identifying what Coss previously did in relation to Cael and how the new marker changes the status of that known file, or trim one layer of repeated procedural uncertainty. Keep the marker unexplained and do not disclose the higher authority. **Strength to preserve:** Coss’s morally compromised caution, the unsigned line, the empty-page motif, and the daughter’s blank map.

3. **Location:** Chapters 19–22, especially the kitchen-table analysis, Lira’s bench discussion in “Off Side,” the main-floor walk, and the final written plan. **Observed issue:** “Pressure locked,” “Wind as insurance,” the widening gap, and the low fourth strike are each restated several times after their evidentiary basis is already clear. **Effect on the reader:** Cael’s disciplined preparation remains convincing, but suspense plateaus because later passages sometimes summarize rather than complicate the plan; younger readers may experience the repetition as delay before the advertised bout. **Proposed scope:** Consolidate only duplicated conclusions, keeping every new test, counterargument, relationship beat, and the full Wendel fight. Let the chapter 22 plan be the definitive concise synthesis. **Strength to preserve:** The pleasure of rigorous preparation, Cael’s distinction between evidence and hope, and the ethical trust expressed by “He hasn’t lied to the column.”
