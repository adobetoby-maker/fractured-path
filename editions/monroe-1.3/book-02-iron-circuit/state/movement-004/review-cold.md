## Unscored reader response

My interest first caught on Cael discovering that nothing hurt, distrusting the fact, and reducing his safeguards to two lines. The sharper hook came when he looked at the Ironyard and thought, *I'm going to lose there tonight*, only to find that he felt hungry. That gives the long pre-bout preparation a concrete emotional shape. The coal-box egg, Lira's invented errands, the old man's appearance, and the many lamps then make the bout feel like a communal event rather than an isolated power test.

The strongest sustained passage is the main-floor fight in Chapters 24–25. Its pleasure is not whether Cael will spring a surprise victory; the manuscript has prepared me to believe he will lose. The pleasure is watching each intelligent plan fail for a more intelligent reason. The post came back, the dock partner was heavy, Brom has already met speed, and damaged legs cannot deliver a full Wind burst. Each correction changes the available fight rather than merely adding explanation. Cael's one plain hit matters more because it does not reverse the result. Brom sitting beside him instead of standing over him is the emotional payoff of the contest.

Interest slipped in two places. Chapter 23's procession of preparations, wagers, lamps, onlookers, and floor ritual is atmospheric and ultimately earned, but a movement-only reader has to retain many unnamed or lightly contextualized people before the central contest begins. More materially, the opening half of Chapter 25 replays a substantial portion of the bout just completed in Chapter 24. Brom's interpretation is valuable, but the repeated mechanics briefly reduce forward pull after the fight's best surprise.

Cael matters most to me because he is rigorous without being emotionally bloodless. He treats a loss as information, protects his rule when breaking it would be easier, gives Keth only what is his to give, and recognizes that sharing the Log is a personal choice rather than a tactical transaction. Brom becomes nearly as important because his competence includes restraint and because he can learn from Cael without being diminished by him. Lira supplies the movement's emotional pressure: her care is practical, her distrust is allowed to remain unresolved, and her admission that she minded the Log being shared is unusually honest.

I expect the next movement to put Cael on a main-season card while he tries to make the new read function around motion or multiple people. I also expect the unexplained third-exchange disappearance to return as a separate problem, and I expect the Cael–Brom friendship to test Lira's sense of what had belonged to the original partnership. I would continue without hesitation.

## Internal continuity and disclosure check

### Canon

No canon packet or project evidence was supplied, so there are no verified canon contradictions to report. The literal token `[SHATTERED]` in Chapter 26 appears to be unresolved or protected manuscript text, but without canon I cannot determine the intended in-world word. It is a production-level reader interruption, not a canon finding.

### Physical state

The movement consistently carries bodily consequences. Cael's right arm goes numb in the first exchange; the reactive Wind burst widens the hip line; Brom damages both knees in the second and finishes through them in the fourth; the knees swell that night; Cael descends stairs seated the next morning; he uses the measuring stick; on the fourth day he tests the market steps without it; and a week after the bout the knee remains explicitly “bad.” Forearm bruising, swollen hands, shoulder pain, and the new-read headache also continue into later scenes.

The one plausibility pressure point is the intensity of the fixed-cross work within a week of the bilateral knee injury, especially Chapter 28's full-force answers that turn Cael over the bad knee and throw him into a wall. The text mitigates this with negotiated stop terms, Cael's active checks, Brom's earlier caution, and the knee's continuing complaints. I read this as aggressive character choice rather than a clear state contradiction.

### Power limits

Limits are unusually well maintained. Cael refuses Pressure during the bout, later tests only a quarter-strength use, and receives the predicted returned cost in his shoulder. The Wind charges full price even when damaged legs provide only a third of the movement, so he shuts it. Brom's Iron Skin requires breath/read/contact logic, learns repeated attacks, and has a recovery window that his throws deliberately make inaccessible. The new Iron-adjacent read initially requires stillness, non-aiming attention, close range, a single strong target, and exclusivity from Cael's other fragments; it produces a disproportionate headache after only a few breaths.

The third-exchange disappearance is internally treated as an unknown event with no sensed activation, cost, handle, or repeatability. The later Iron-adjacent acquisition has a notice, a surface sensation, a perceptual effect, and a measurable attention cost. Nothing proves they are the same ability, but their proximity and shared relation to Brom's read invite that inference strongly enough that some cold readers may temporarily conflate them.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge moves through observation, disclosure, or explicit testing. Cael's pre-bout model is wrong exactly where bench observation cannot reveal Brom's adaptations. Brom knows Cael's public classification through talk and Vell's book, senses only what his read can detect, and sees the full Log only after Cael offers it. Cael honors Brom's privacy by giving Keth the general discovery about touch rather than Brom-specific technique. Lira learns about the new fragment and the shared Log directly from Cael. These boundaries are clear and dramatically productive.

### Reserved disclosures

Without editorial context I cannot verify what was reserved. Within the supplied manuscript, disclosure remains controlled: the cause of the third-exchange absence is withheld; Brom postpones part of the estate-wall history; the system behind Cael's fragments remains unnamed; Lira's private book is noticed but not opened; and the fragment's range in crowds or around unkindled people remains untested. I found no internal moment where a character reveals knowledge the movement has not shown them acquiring.
