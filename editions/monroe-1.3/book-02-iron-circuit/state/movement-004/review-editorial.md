The boarding-house egg, Lira's invented errands, twenty-six lamps, and the old yard-owner's double nod then make the bout feel like an event without replacing the personal question: can Cael obey his own rules when the plan fails? Interest slipped briefly at the opening of chapter 25. Chapter 24 has already rendered the first two exchanges and the disappearance in close detail; Brom's cutaway then summarizes the first two exchanges again before replaying the third from his side. The new information is excellent—especially the shelves, his body's reliance on the read, and his decision not to break what he cannot yet understand—but the approach to it repeats enough known action to delay the answer promised by the POV change. The later sparring sequence is long, but each morning changes the problem and the relationship, so it earns its space better.

Cael matters most, but Brom becomes the movement's emotional center: he wins without cruelty, offers equivalent information before asking for trust, accepts correction when he becomes overcareful, and teaches a capability whose logic runs against Cael's deepest habit. Lira remains essential because her care is neither effortless nor erased by the new friendship; “That was ours,” followed by her honest correction, gives her attachment and jealousy moral weight without making either shameful.

I expect the next movement to test Iron-adjacent in the noise of the main season, put strain on the new three-person relationship, and make the empty *Carrying* column matter beyond technique. I would continue without hesitation.
