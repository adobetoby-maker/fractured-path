## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** On the first page: Dace's hand flat on a man's chest, the count of 504 "and two on the slate shelf that I'll have down… by the ankles," and "You're the only one I don't have to count." The bout holds from the first knock. The forearm, the crack "like a mallet on a wet post," the cold of the stone coming up through his shirt, and then Lira's cutaway at the south rope with her hands white on the rope and the one thought — *if he doesn't get up I'll not be able to keep my hands on this rope* — is the emotional peak of chapter 52 and the best use of a cutaway in the movement. Chapter 53 is the best chapter: the three reads struck through as a list, the mill wheel on Fen Street, every burst landing "on the *three*," the fork ("He had perhaps the length of a heartbeat"), and the knees locked "like gate bars dropped into their brackets." Reydan counting himself on the stone "a clerk before you are a fighter" is the right way into him. After the bout, the lamp relit in the north window (56), Brom's "Does the form ask for your family?" (56), Hesk's half-sheet with "a great deal of white round it" and the seven pins (57), the Ulric bout won by a turn that "walked" (58), and Vell's "I'm not young, so don't be too long about it" (59) each caught me again.

**Where it slipped.** Chapter 55's "He knew, without anybody having told him…" paragraph is a list of six townsfolk secrets; it earns its last line but reads as inventory. Chapter 60's market row (pie, chestnut hand, slate, "Feed him," the betting man's line) is a run of small nods after the bigger farewells of 59 have already landed; an adult reader will feel the leavings run long (59–60 together are about 9,000 words after the Compression is confirmed). Two spots needed re-reading: Brom's "It's mine" at the notice (58), and the body map in the fourth exchange (bursts at the right side, the left shoulder turned "half his back").

**Which person matters.** Cael, then Lira (the rope; "I'm going"; the lamp), then Brom (the family form; the home he picked). Reydan is the movement's discovery as a secondary: a man who chooses to be "more interested than you were hurt."

**What I expected next.** After 54: that he would go, and that Brom would make it hard for himself. After 58: the road. The movement delivers both and ends where the packet asks, on the road, three abreast.

**Would I continue?** Yes. The hand-off to Book 3 ch1 is clean in fact (see §4) and the final line earns the next book.
