Interest catches immediately at chapter 1's opening error—“The feint went high, and Cael's eyes went with it”—because the mistake, the cost, and Cael's method arrive inside action. It deepens when the Ironyard proves to be more than an arena: Vell's calibration chains, Dace's erasable board, Stedd's earned standing, and the district's choreography during the sweep make the circuit feel like a functioning counter-institution rather than genre scenery.

Interest slips most in two places: the middle of chapter 2, where several explanations of ratings and recordkeeping arrive consecutively, and the long Power Log transcription in chapter 6. Both passages contain useful material, but they temporarily replace present pressure with organized explanation. The prose remains comprehensible; the issue is momentum, especially for a younger reader.

Cael matters because his hunger for accurate knowledge is inseparable from his ethics: he writes the bad evidence at the same size as the good and eventually refuses an unpriced experiment. Lira matters at least as much emotionally because she challenges, protects, jokes, withholds something of her own, and is allowed a life outside Cael's work. Vell is the movement's strongest supporting presence.

The next event I expect is the watched Blade opponent deliberately attacking the landing lock, forcing Cael to spend the new “perfect view” under real pressure. I also expect Lira's “step that doesn't stop” to become a parallel progression problem. I would continue.

## Continuity, canon, and protected wording

### Findings

1. **Verified count contradiction — Copper-formal wins.** `STATE_LEDGER.md` and `BOOK_MAP.md` place eleven defeated Copper-tier formals in the entry state before chapter 1. After Ulric, however, Vell says, “That's eleven Copper formals this year” (Ch. 1, line 111), and the Power Log later lists “eleven Copper formals; Ulric” (Ch. 6, line 63). As written, Ulric is both after the entry count and included within the same total. If the binding entry state remains eleven, the post-Ulric total must be twelve. Confidence: high.

2. **Verified internal/canon count contradiction — landing trials.** Chapter 5 depicts a successful burst to enter each numbered trial from one through ten. The ninth is explicitly followed by “Nine in an hour” (line 169), and the tenth then uses another burst (lines 199–215). Yet the aftermath says the discovery cost nine bursts (line 273), and Ch. 6 records “ten trials, nine bursts in one hour” plus depletion “after nine” (lines 77 and 85), matching `BOOK_MAP.md` §6.1. The scene currently contains ten activations but records nine. Confidence: high.

3. **Protected notice is exact at first appearance but altered when recopied.** The protected `FRAGMENT UPDATE` is reproduced exactly in Ch. 7, lines 148–150. Ch. 8, line 17 then presents the notice again under “REVISIONS” as `FRAGMENT UPDATE. [Wind-adjacent]. Duration revised: sustained. Integration: partial (unchanged).` This changes the protected em-dash format and adds “(unchanged).” Either reproduce the protected notice verbatim again or frame this unmistakably as Cael's paraphrase rather than copied notice text. Confidence: high.
