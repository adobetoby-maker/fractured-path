## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in Chapter 48's count of the room, and fully locked at: “Two hundred and six people, and not one of them was dishonest.” That sentence gives the movement its real antagonist. The threat is not a secretly corrupt official who can be exposed; it is a lawful process made of decent people, each doing a narrow job honestly. The movement keeps finding fresh physical forms for that idea—the hood, the public minute, the station door, Brom's guidance slip, and finally the clean ruling “given under the Compact's hand.”

Interest slipped most during the middle of Karis's closing in Chapter 49. The argument is intelligent and ultimately moving, but the sequence through discretion, precedent, known result, the scale analogy, the four indicators, and the fourteenth chapter asks the reader to retain a lot of procedural logic before the scene returns to the emotional fact that she must leave her best knowledge unspoken. The Havel opening in Chapter 51 creates a smaller pause: his perspective becomes worthwhile, but a cold reader needs a moment to remember who he is and why the copied return matters to him.

Cael matters most because the movement's decisive question becomes not whether he can win, but whether he will consent to being defined by the instrument that cannot read him. Karis is the standout presence in the first half. Her fifty-one-minute close gives her competence, fear, discipline, and agency at once; “I knew it was, and I needed to hear it lose” is an especially sharp statement of who she is.

The event I expect next is the four withdrawals at Bracken's counter, followed by the consequences of the public bout and the group's departure east before the evaluation notice can be enforced. I would continue without hesitation. The final “Good room” closes this movement emotionally while opening the next movement practically.

## Repair brief

### 1. Reduce one layer of repetition inside Karis's close

- **Location:** Chapter 49, from “That was an honest answer, and it is the whole of this case” through “The seat is asked to order a test in order to obtain a failure.”
- **Observed issue:** The argument establishes the same core fact through several consecutive forms: discretion, the tool's purpose, ninety-one precedents, the known-result scale, four proofs that the result is known, and the fourteenth chapter's intended use. Each element is defensible, but together they briefly outrun the emotional scene.
- **Effect on the reader:** A fluent younger reader may retain “the frame cannot read him” while losing which legal step Karis is presently proving; an adult reader may arrive at the conclusion before the speech does and begin waiting for the four-second silence.
- **Proposed scope:** Line-level compression only. Remove or merge one recap of the known-result proposition, or shorten the bridge between the four indicators and the fourteenth-chapter argument. Do not reduce the whole closing or remove its legal spine.
- **Strength to preserve:** Karis's exactness, the wool-scale analogy, Jent's honest agreement, and especially the silent omission of the true argument.

### 2. Trust a few institutional images to carry the theme without explanation

- **Location:** Chapter 50, after the girl leaves the north-road station; Chapter 51, Havel's reflections after pinning the return and tying his six sheets; Chapter 51, Seln's explanation after examining the ruling.
- **Observed issue:** The movement's central insight—that honest people can maintain a false system—is stated in several closely spaced reflective passages after the images have already made it legible.
- **Effect on the reader:** The repetition slows the approach to the ruling and slightly reduces the force of the best formulations by giving the reader less room to complete the thought independently.
- **Proposed scope:** A light sentence-level pass across these three locations, cutting only explanatory echoes that repeat an immediately preceding image. Preserve the station scene, Havel's public-versus-private-record decision, and Seln's identification of “the hand” intact as scenes.
- **Strength to preserve:** The refusal to invent a villain, the dignity of minor officials, and the physical record motifs—door, minute, seal, sheet, hand—that unify the movement.

### 3. Anchor Havel's point-of-view handoff one beat sooner

- **Location:** Chapter 51, opening paragraphs from “Havel had written the time” to his arrival in the copyists' loft.
- **Observed issue:** After four chapters closely anchored to Cael, the new viewpoint begins with a familiar-looking procedural action before re-establishing Havel's role as the junior records chair and his personal connection to the unexplained marks.
- **Effect on the reader:** A cold reader may spend the first page identifying the viewpoint instead of immediately feeling the significance of his seeing the return in writing.
- **Proposed scope:** Add or adjust a single orienting phrase in the opening paragraph that ties Havel to the records table/junior chair. No recap of his history is needed.
- **Strength to preserve:** The quietness of the handoff, the copyist's plain-language recognition that the sentence cannot make sense, and Havel's decision that a public record does not need a seventh private sheet.
