## What I decided where the packet is open

- **Why four days in Ostrand:** "call days". An old rule of the code: in the four days before a ruling a seat, or its evaluation seat, may recall a party upon an hour's notice, so the parties keep "within the bell" of the seat. The house takes the upper floor of the wool-factors' guesting-house in the lane behind the exchange. Brom is there for his panel in the same town; Lira by Rooke's leave ("She's drilled in worse"). Seln stays on the hill.
- **Ilsev's six minutes** fall on the fourth call day (day 200) as the evaluation seat's return, read with the presiding chair empty and covered. Bracken: it is a return, not a sitting; fourteen stands.
- **The ruling's shape (text composed by me inside the packet's elements):** lawful; compliant; the enrollment basis revoked, the revocation to take effect at the close of the academic year; re-evaluation within five weeks upon a date to be notified; chapter fourteen upon the result; *Given under the Compact's hand.* Read in two minutes forty by the exchange's clock.
- **"Five weeks became five days":** the code's notice for a compelled evaluation is five clear days. The district clerk reserved the frame-house for the first lawful date before the ruling was given (a prudent clerk books a frame). Bracken read the reservation on the district's board on the first call day; the counsel showed Withrow the arithmetic that evening: the notice will be served the morning after the ruling, for a day inside the year, while he is still her enrollee — she must then produce him with her own hand or refuse a seat. The only course that leaves the house clean and him out of the frame is that he withdraw first. She has known four days and has not said so, so that the going would be his and not her need.
- **Who wins the last bout:** Cael, two touches to one (E1 Ephram; E2 none; E3 Cael; E4 Cael). Ephram's right shoulder goes out on the fourth and is set on the oak.
- **"Both. Enter both.":** Rooke's clerk asks which book the bout goes in, the program's or the house's.
- **The boards ritual:** the yard's custom that after a bout on the oak the two walk to the porter's boards and the one who lost chalks it. There is no column for an unclassified enrollee; Ephram, left-handed, chalks a short rule above his own name at the head of the Iron column and leaves the space above it empty.
- **Protected lines given only in part** (the ruling paragraph; the "Still glad." speech; *Nine. Taken in earnest from a friend…*): the given words kept exact; the rest composed; flagged.
- **GAULT / VASTIN:** the minute's line verbatim. The prose says the certificate form's column is headed in the code's old words, that the protocol's certifying office is the Archmarshal's, and that the house's own evaluator of record sat on the fourth bench and had declined nothing.
- **Seln and "twice more":** nobody says the mechanism in his hearing. The reach is never named at the council; Lira says "The rule." and Cael "Tonight. On paper." The priced leaf is initialled by the four, not by Seln.

## Chapter 48 — Two Hundred and Six (~5,000)

1. **Day 187, the coach and the room.** The twelfth sitting, "upon the close". The long room full for the first time since the preliminary (a closing sells). Cael counts it because there is nothing else to count: a hundred and eighty-seven on the benches, nineteen there by office (the hood, the clerk, the five, the records table, Jent and his clerk, the five at the house's table, three door officers). Two hundred and six with himself. He goes down them as he once went down the instrument, looking for the one who is lying, and there is not one.
2. **Jent's ten minutes.** He concedes it all again in a list; states the petition in four sentences; says of Withrow, by her title, that the Chancellor's house has done nothing wrong and that he wants it in the minute in his own voice. The petition asks one question and the code answers what follows. He sits.
3. **The house's counsel**, twelve minutes on the remedy (no seat has ordered a practitioner into a frame over a provision that stands), and then she yields the merits and renews the motion. The hood: leave is given; the advocate of record heard at the next sitting, once, within the hour. Karis does not move. On the steps the counsel: "You have a week, and a room. Don't spend the week being grateful."
4. **The hill, the week.** Ephram on the Crown yard on the First-day at the second bell, running his six steps; he tells Cael he is keeping a thing until after the last Fifth-day ("I'll not ask a man anything while a seat has him"). Karis's thirty slips, read to the window, timed with Hesk's clock (it loses; add two).
5. **The east hall after the tenth bell.** The honest floor's last use: not a fight. Brom in Rooke's chair under a grey horse blanket as the seat; Lira as the petitioner ("No objection. I never have one. It's the whole of my method"); Seln holding the door; Cael with the clock. Lira lands on whichever foot Brom's blanket calls, between slips. Karis says the whole of it to a painted floor: sixty-four minutes. She cuts it on her knees. She asks Cael to say it is not ground, so that she can say it as if it were. He laughs for the first time in a month and knows what it is he would be sorry to lose.

## Chapter 49 — Fifty-One Minutes (~5,300)

1. **Day 194.** The thirteenth sitting. Karis at the house's table with the slips squared; she eats nothing; Bracken has the seal; Gault on the fourth bench with Lira, Brom, Seln and the assistant. Jent puts his spectacles on to listen, which he has never done.
2. **The closing, first part.** The list of what is agreed (seven things, five given by the petitioner). The question asked at the fourth sitting and its answer, read from the minute. A discretion is a tool with its purpose written on the handle: the preamble (the testing of instruments), the ninety-one, the seven about conduct, "novel" minuted.
3. **The scale.** An evaluation is a question whose answer is not known beforehand. The tournament office's entry; the advisory the petition quotes with approval; the evaluation seat's minuted scope; nobody in thirteen sittings has said the frame will read him. Chapter fourteen was written for the case where the registry tried and could not; not to be reached on purpose. The preamble tells the registry which to doubt when twenty-two officers and one instrument disagree.
4. **The slip she does not read.** One slip she turns face down and goes on. Cael knows what is on it. The cost of the truest argument unsaid.
5. **The end of it.** The true record exchanged for an empty one; the ferryman's daughter who was waited for; what the house asks. Fifty-one minutes by the exchange's clock; nine slips dropped on her feet, everything already conceded. Jent: "The registry does not dispute a syllable of it." — and why the petition stands anyway.
6. **The standing entries.** The clerk reads them; the certification line verbatim; four pens stop; Gault; an absence entered. The seat reserves; ruling on the last Fifth-day at the hour after noon; the parties to keep within the bell from the First-day.
7. **After.** The counsel on the steps: twenty-six years. Karis eats two of Brom's apples in the coach and sleeps. Cael finds he is not angry; it was not for the seat.

## Chapter 50 — Within the Bell (~5,600)

1. **Day 197.** The guesting-house; the rule of the call days; the coats take a window across the lane. That evening, through the parlour's glass door: the counsel's rule turning, Withrow very still, the code shut by her own hand. He does not know what was said.
2. **Day 198, the yard.** An old wool press in the yard behind. Brom under the platen with his forearms crossed; Lira at the capstan, and then bursting onto the bar to make the load arrive as a frame's springs do — "a little over". She lands on a moving bar on whichever foot she chooses. Their talk: the blue door of the district hall; "Ask me after the Fifth-day." Cael on the gallery sees that each of them has finished something, and that it is not his to decide for them.
3. **Karis's supplemental.** Three leaves upon the entry of the certificate's absence: four times a seat has proceeded without one, every time upon a frame, never a person. Receipted at the seat's window. Inert, and she knows.
4. **Day 199, the north road.** Within the bell as far as the toll-bar. A coat forty yards behind, openly. The district station: a low stone house, a frame-house behind, the sigil over the door, a bench. A girl of fourteen, her hair done that morning by somebody who took time over it; her mother. The district officer calls her in, kindly. Cael at the trough across the road. What he knows is in that building. The new thing asks at the door and is named. She comes out with her slip and says the word twice. The officer watches them go with plain goodwill. Nobody there is lying. He cannot know whether what was entered is what the plate showed, and neither will she. The decision the ruling will not make for him. The term's shortest entry.
5. **Day 200, the call.** An hour's notice at the second bell. The long room with eleven people in it and the presiding chair under its cover. Ilsev stands. The scope; the designation at the schedule's fourteenth line; both returns entire, slowly; her standing note; "The evaluation seat notes the contradiction…"; no finding. Six minutes. Jent: no observation; he was never told its office either. Karis's pencil transcript and her one sentence on the steps.

## Chapter 51 — The Hand (~5,500)

1. **Havel's window (~2,000).** He timed the six minutes. The minute carried up to the copyists' loft; a copyist who thinks a sentence must be wrong ("Copy it as it stands"); the fair copy pinned in the porch, read by one boy. At the inn by the bridge: the six sheets; what was read today is in the minute now and does not want a sheet; no seventh. He numbers them one to six for the first time and ties them. The transfer request, written on the form's own lines: relief upon the proceedings' close; the district records room that is a man short. He does not write the other reason. He learns nothing.
2. **Day 201, the second bell.** Cael and Bracken at the top of the market street, where both doors can be seen. Brom and Lira go in at the blue door; out within the hour. "I held it." "It lit." They do not tell you; they write.
3. **The ruling.** The fourteenth sitting, "for ruling". Two minutes forty. The five heads. *Given under the Compact's hand.* The hood goes out by its own door. He had thought the floor would go; what he feels is a corridor ending.
4. **The steps.** Jent to Withrow ("Chancellor"), to Karis, to Cael. Withrow at the coach: "you did not lose badly".
5. **The records hall, evening.** Bracken's board line. Seln reads the hand under the counter lamp: true stock, true mark, a seal on the schedule, a seat's own clerk — the first paper all year with nothing behind it. Brom squares the copy-sets by weight (thirty-four for the houses that wrote, and the faculties'); one is a leaf short. Lira watches from the stair.

## Chapter 52 — What Leaving Costs (~5,900)

1. **The reading room after the tenth bell.** Bare table; the case in Seln's post satchel by his chair; the lamp in the window as high as ever. Cael's column: four names, his own line blank. Karis's three slips: *Frame. Fight. Leave.*
2. **The frame, priced by Cael.** The result known; nullification, suspension with no date, part nine; the fences already walked; inside the instrument for good.
3. **The fight, argued by Karis at full strength** — the appeal that lies on new matter, and the newest matter in two hundred years — and lost out loud, by her own verdict: "You don't file a grievance with the instrument that forged the record. You leave its jurisdiction."
4. **Bracken's knock.** The district's finding by the evening bag: Iron, found; to be signed before the registrar. And the guidance note, a printed slip issued by rule. Bracken has issued it himself, and thought it a kindness. Brom reads it. "How many boxes is your research?" / "Three." / "Then I'm carrying three boxes." Bracken weeps, briefly, on a stair. He asks when, not why.
5. **Lira's whole bill**, from the carter's book. "Turns out it doesn't live here."
6. **Karis's forfeiture in bibliography order.** *Annex. Archive privileges. Assistant. Budget. Greyvane. Research standing. Tier: retained.* The request redirected to an instrument-maker at Denvash; the letter for the assistant.
7. **Seln's one sentence.** Cael's own line, written at last.
8. **Ephram.** Brom fetches him. Told the departure, nothing else. The back of the third sheet. He asks for one bout, all of it, or he will put his lath down. He waits on the landing with his back to the door. "The rule." / "Tonight. On paper." Five hands, Seln's last.

## Chapter 53 — The Sunward Mark (~6,000)

1. **Withrow, alone.** Her lamp; the folder open at the eighth day; the right-hand column still empty. The four days she knew: the reserved frame, the five days' notice, the counsel's advice she would not pass on. "Five weeks became five days." The other column: what the clerk will write. "The record will say you left. I'll spend the rest of my tenure making the record say why." She opens the door herself.
2. **The price.** At the sill, by Hesk's clock. The ninth, if it comes: what it buys, what it spends, and the one cost that cannot be paid. Brom's initials (the finding and the envelope both square on his table, unsigned, unopened); Karis's. Lira's door: nobody. The leaf goes under it.
3. **Lira's window (~1,500).** The east hall alone; the key; the red book she does not open; she lands on both feet on a floor painted for her. Her trunk, the three piles, the steward's sheet. The leaf under her door: she reads the price, initials it, and writes one line in capitals. From her window, a lantern on the Crown yard: Rooke at the first board with the chalk.
4. **Morning to the fourth bell.** The board as chalked. The bluff and the town come; the lodge book; two coats sign as callers. The Crown yard counted one last time.
5. **The first exchange.** Ephram takes the north chalk, which at that hour faces the sun. The threes and the short beat. Cael is in the band before he has chosen it. Touch: the captain's.
6. **The second.** Ephram grounds the lath and will not fight a ninety-ninth. The coat falls. The Norhold rates and past them: the read at depth, the Wind, the push at load under a planted heel, a lane — which a Blade reads as a line. The bill; ten counts; no touch; no flag. "There it is. Three years. Again."

## Chapter 54 — Good Room (~5,600)

1. **The third exchange: the reach, in earnest.** Three edges on the guarding forearm with the give open; an edge is not a blow and the cup cannot hold it. Directed, open-eyed, costed the night before. It comes together like a rule laid along a line already drawn. He does not pick it up. The touch is the old read's: inside the short beat, a palm on the chest.
2. **The fourth: the commitment engine turned.** A Blade cannot take a commitment back. Cael is early to the place where the cut must end, with the give shut and the load sent to the floor as Brom sends it. The touch — and the shoulder. An unpriced cost.
3. **On the oak.** The healer, Brom's hand. Rooke: two touches to one. The notice, read standing. Rooke's clerk: "Both. Enter both." Gault's nod. Who cries.
4. **The yard empties.** Withrow last out. Lira's sentence to the Current girl.
5. **The boards.** Ephram, bound, left-handed. The rule above his own name. "Still glad."
6. **Not told.** The log: *Nine. Taken in earnest from a friend…* and the bills. The first pull: a lintel's edge. Anchor left in the kit, and why.
7. **The empty yard.** The four sheets on Bracken's counter for morning. "Good room."
