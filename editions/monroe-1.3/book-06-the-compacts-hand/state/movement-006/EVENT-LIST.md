## Chapter 35 — Three Routes (~5,200)

1. **Day 88, morning.** Cael wakes at the sill; the new thing asks nothing; he names his door anyway. Infirmary: the healer (spare grey woman) cuts Lira's strapping; she stands; she leaves the crutch in the corner "for somebody who needs it". She walks the corridor as if the floor might have been waxed. "What's your line?" "The fourth." "I'm the third. I'll be slow on the stair. Don't help me." Their naming of the infirmary door.
2. **The board and the wing.** The floor schedule on the refectory door: five lines on the Fourth-day after supper, a quarter apart, among a hundred ordinary lines. Ephram reading the board for his own name; "Ribs?" "Better." "The chart's got a hole in it. I don't like holes." Cael at the wing counter for the excused slip's countersign: the shelf above the copying table is empty, a clean oblong in the dust. Seln at his column: "Mm." Neither says anything.
3. **The evening; three routes.** Nobody planned the routes. Seln's rule: come the way you always come. So Karis comes from the refectory by the main stair, as always; Brom by the iron stair in the stacks, because he likes the long way round; Lira by the main stair, slowly; Cael by the main stair at his quarter; Seln up the bindery stair from the yard behind, as the wing's man does with returned volumes. Three ways onto the library's third floor, then the one tight top stair. Cael's walk: the court in the cold, the fountain, the library's lower hall under its green lamps, two readers and the night porter; he names the street door and the stair door; he counts who sees him (Seln's habit, caught). Up the four flights with the ribs.
4. **The room.** Karis has left her usual lamp in the window, turned as high as every night, so the valley sees what it always sees; they sit by a second lamp, shaded, on the table's far end. The table is bare to the cloth for the first time anyone remembers — every stack moved to the annex floor. Nobody in the window seat. The assistant sent home at the sixth bell. Brom's kettle on its shelf, not boiling. Lira on the bench, the ankle out straight, honest about it. Waiting talk: the stair, the ankle, Brom's sling off tomorrow. The landing clock? No — the lecture-range bell. Seln's single knock. He looks at the room in his order. He has the case under his coat.
5. **Four movements.** The brass key from his waistcoat. He lays the contents out in four movements, the way a man lays out tools: (1) forty pairs, each a copied Kindling record with a reclassification order clipped to its face by a brass paper clip, set in four rows of ten; (2) a bundle of field reports in the wing's pink tape; (3) the memoranda, printed, two stacks, the left one yellower; (4) a single leaf in a sleeve of folded paper, alone at the table's end. He steps back and puts his hands flat on the wood and says nothing. The objects stay on the table a long time; nobody speaks; Cael has no clock and does not count; he notices the lamp's wick and Lira's hands and Brom not reaching for the kettle.
6. **The pairs.** Karis: "May I?" Seln: "That is why they are on the table." Karis reads a station's Kindling record aloud (station, the keeper's reading: a Current, first rank, with a note at the margin — *reads into Mire*); Brom reads the order clipped to it (*Per corrective classification at Kindling evaluation: enter Mire, Rank One. Margin reading not to be entered. Observe nine weeks following entry.*). Then the slip at the back: *Observed nine weeks. Nothing to report.* Three pairs read. Cael sees every keeper's reading has something at its margin and every order takes it away (he notices; he says nothing — it is Karis's to find). Lira: "Nine weeks of nothing." Seln: that is his sentence; he has written it every week for fifteen years. Cael's realization: the same sentence, true and empty, used to keep him and used to bury them.
7. **The river-town father.** Behind the twenty-third pair, a letter folded small: Lira asks to read it. A river-town father, polite, who writes seldom: the keeper told them at the frame one thing, the certificate came back another; he does not complain; he wants to know which to tell his daughter. Then the district clerk's answer, four lines (copied by Seln as it went up): the entry is the registry's and he cannot alter it; the station's sheet carries the keeper's reading and it is not what was entered; he has asked his office why and has had no answer and does not expect one; he is sorry, and he would tell her both. Silence. Brom: "He told the truth." Karis: "And sent it up. And it came here." Cael: the honest people are in the drawer too. End.

## Chapter 36 — The Leaf in Its Sleeve (~5,300)

1. **The field reports.** Karis unties the tape. Twenty-three reports, evaluators in the field, each about a practitioner whose shown work runs ahead of the file; each read and returned with the same stamp: *Read against file. Enter at file.*; several carry an evaluator's later line, *Continue observation.* She goes through all; "Down. Every one. None up." Brom asks whether anyone was ever read higher than the file and kept higher. None. Cael thinks of his own band, a smaller him entered every Fifth-day on purpose, and of the difference: he chose it.
2. **The memoranda.** Printed circulars. Karis turns the stack over and starts at the bottom: nineteen issues, the same operative words, *corrective classification at Kindling evaluation*, reissued; type changing, paper changing, the registry's seal device changing, the officers' signatures changing; the words never. Brom: "That's a form." Karis on what a form is: a thing done so often that nobody should have to think about it. Lira: the first one must have been an argument. Karis: "The first one is the one I want. Not tonight."
3. **The leaf.** Seln slides it from its sleeve himself; the one original he took (the printed ones were spares nobody counted; everything else is his copying hand). Karis puts on her cotton gloves. She reads it aloud, slowly, a short paragraph: the *advisory corrections function* is appended to the reading; it does not alter the reading; it advises what is entered; it is to be maintained. She stops. She will not say what she thinks. "Not until I've got it in order. I don't guess past paper." She writes one word on a slip and turns it over.
4. **The finding, once.** Seln says it, once, plainly, standing: the registry corrects them, at Kindling, downward, by a form, for as long as it has had paper; that is what he carried. Nobody repeats it. Brom: "How many?" Seln: "I stopped estimating. Estimating felt like filing." How he came by it: his fourth posting, twelve years ago, a records-room press in an old building, found while looking for a subject's travel endorsements; copying by night for weeks; the spare printed issues; the one leaf taken because a copy of it would be worthless. And a year later, in a green-distempered corridor of a district station, waiting for a file, he heard a keeper tell a mother her girl read strong into a second family, and a week after saw the certificate in the out-tray with one family on it. That is when the paper turned into children.
5. **Pleasant.** Lira: two years of "Mm", the cup turned, being pleasant to the whole hill with that over his head; what did being pleasant cost. Seln: nothing anybody would notice; that was the price. Karis asks the craft question: how he knew paper "correct in every particular" was wrong. "My mother's ledger was always right." She kept a grain factor's books at a river quay; he copied them as a boy; every page had a mistake on it, struck through, initialled, the right figure beside it. Right paper made by people has corrections on it. This has never once been wrong in two hundred years.
6. **Keys.** What happens to it now. Karis asks for five nights to index. Seln agrees the case stays here: locked, on her shelf between two of her document boxes, looking like one of them; the room's key on her cord, the case's in his waistcoat. "Two keys. Neither of us opens it alone." The assistant never in the room after the sixth bell. People begin to stand. Cael: "Seln. Will you sit down. It's my turn to put something on the table." The three know what he means; Lira sits back down; Brom puts the kettle on the flame again.

## Chapter 37 — Two Files (~5,000)

1. **The mechanism, end to end.** Cael tells it in order, as the Log would: the Kindling where the station's instrument went dark and the keeper had no word; the first time, on a floor with Lira, not knowing; the notices; *-adjacent*; what each costs; the rule written after; the eight sources in order (Lira; the Bronze from Ardenmere; Brom; Reydan; Karis, with consent, in writing; one he holds back; Daeva; the woman on the Ostrand road). The covenant: three rules, then *costed*. The anomaly in three words. Seln listens with his hands flat, does not turn his cup, does not interrupt once. Cael realizes he has never told it from the beginning to anybody; each of the three learned it in a piece.
2. **Four questions.** Seln's trade asks only about exposure: Who knows? (Three. Now four.) Where is it written? (Hesk's book, in my hand; the old volume; both never out of my reach; now the satchel.) Can an instrument read it off you? (No frame has read anything off me but the band; the station went dark at my Kindling and has never lit since.) Does it ever slip where somebody could see? (The covenant keeps it to fights somebody else begins; the costs show as bruises; Lira counts.) Seln: "You may say it to me twice more in your life." Not a third.
3. **The one held back.** The stair: the third hour, the archive, the man in the doorway with the bag, Seln reading the form to him in the light; Cael in the half-landing's corner below, counting the rule on his fingers; the quiet; the notice — Shadow-adjacent, Bronze. Never used anywhere by anybody's count. Rent from waking every day, two years, near enough. Drifts in the gaps. Then Norhold, the eve: sealed for a debt — because of who it came from; if used, somebody sorting would trace it to a counter in the wing. In the last corner, against the barrier, it offered; he did not turn his head. Lira: "We all held the coat." Seln is very still. Then: "You kept my file too, then."
4. **Grounds revised.** The seal: Seln does not release it and does not take it. He sets terms. It stays sealed while his name is on any roll of the service; after that it is Cael's and he is not to ask. Cael writes the revision in Hesk's book in front of him: the debt closed; the seal kept on new grounds, his. Seln reads it upside down and says "Mm," and turns his cup a quarter turn. The kettle boils while nobody is watching.
5. **The stair down; the log.** Lira names the stair; Brom, without the sling tomorrow, goes down first. At the sill: what Cael writes and what he does not. *The case opened. What was in it does not go in a book. It is in a room with two keys.* The Shadow line rewritten. From the window, Seln crossing the frosted court with nothing under his coat.

## Chapter 38 — Thresholds (~5,400)

1. **Day 89, the counter.** The hill looks exactly the same, which is the worst of it. A first-year at the wing counter handing in the house's copy of her station certificate for her file; Seln takes it, "Mm", blots it, files it. Cael at the counter for nothing in particular watches the certificate go into the drawer and decides, on purpose, that he will not read every paper on the hill as a wound; he cannot carry that and walk the year. The wing three bout list without him (excused). Brom's sling off; he flexes the hand and goes to the frames to watch, not lift.
2. **The question.** North wall, dusk, the coats changing on the bell. Lira asks it first, as she asked it in the dairy: why are we still here; why are we answering their seat; we could be four days east by the Seventh-day. Brom: both roads; the boxes. Cael says nothing for a while, then that the room was never the system and now he knows what the system is, and it does not make leaving cheaper. Karis, arriving: "Don't say it again till I've priced it. Give me two days."
3. **Day 90, dawn, the east hall.** Lira crosses the program floor in front of Rooke at the rail, walking. She thinks about every step and Rooke sees it. "Not yet." She nods. On the way out she says to Cael that she knew it before he said it; she wanted him to say it.
4. **Day 91, Bracken.** Bracken brings the Greyvane leaves up the library stair himself (the budget's first spend, arrived before the recess as promised); weighs them in his palm out of habit. Karis fills the three blank lines under *Greyvane: the founders' hands*. She asks to borrow the house's enrollment book, the old volume, "for a citation". Bracken: "I don't ask what for. I ask when it comes back." "Tomorrow." "Then it's lent."
5. **The clean door.** That night in the reading room (the five). Karis has the code's withdrawal sections and the enrollment book open side by side. Pending a ruling, an enrollee who leaves is absent from the jurisdiction; the coastal default's words; the forty days from the fourth sitting's close; *fled* on his file, a mark on the house's charter for harbouring a flight, and a note on every enrolled associate's file — four files. But a ruled matter is not a pending matter: after the ruling, and before any revocation takes effect, an enrollee may withdraw, and the code calls it *resigned in good standing*; the enrollment book has it once, a generation back, a fourth-year who withdrew in the week after a ruling against him, entered in those words. One door, and it opens one day only in the whole year, and only if they lose. Lira: "So we don't run. We walk." Karis: "Perfectly. Every sitting, every form, every bout, until the ruling." Brom: "Corridor." PROVISIONAL mechanism — flag.
6. **Day 92 night, the honest floor, perception only (~1,800).** The east hall on Lira's key; shutters; two lanterns; Lira on the bench by the rail, honest about the ankle; Karis with chalk and a notebook; Brom in the doorway. The rule written first on the leaf in Cael's hand and initialled: perception only; nothing laid; every *There?* named; Brom counts aloud if anything fixes. (a) What the lattice-sense reads in an ordinary room: he stands in the middle and lets it look without asking anything back — the hall as rests: frame feet, the lantern's hook, the bench's legs, the bar on the gallery stair; lines between them like the room's own scaffold; Lira's two feet, one carrying more than she says (she is caught, and laughs). It marks where a foot is about to come down, a breath early, no more. He understands the woman's craft from the inside and does not like how easy it would be. (b) Brom holding the door: the great door ajar with Brom's right hand on its edge — the sense does not ask about it; Brom takes the hand away — *There?* at once; hand back — quiet. A door with a hand on it is already held. Brom: "So I'm a doorstop." (c) Karis's ignitions as thresholds: a chalk line across the boards between two frames, and at each end a pin-head of fire sitting on the wood, made with her left hand, counted aloud; to the sense two fixed points and a line between them are a doorway, and it asks about the chalk the way it asks about doors. She walks the pair outward a foot at a time; it asks clear to about ten feet, faint at twelve, nothing at fourteen — *for now*. Eight ignitions, left hand, small; she writes *Not an instance.* (d) The almost: at the last, the chalk threshold at eleven feet wants finishing and something starts to go out of him the way it went in the doorway; Brom says "One—"; Cael names it hard aloud (chalk; Karis drew it; nothing comes through it but air) and it stops. Brom does not say two. The bill: a dull ache behind the eyes; Lira on her palm, *bill*. Nothing deployed. On the way out Lira names the great door for him before he has to.

## Chapter 39 — Two Hundred Years (~5,600)

1. **The second night (day 93).** The five; the assistant gone; the lamp in the window as always. Karis's index: four folded slips, numbered, and a fifth face down. She says: four findings and one limit; she will not go past the cache, and if anybody asks her to she will stop.
2. **Finding one: the mechanism, reach not strength.** The forty pairs sorted by what the margin said, not by station: no keeper's reading in forty was struck for being too strong; several of the forty were weak. What the orders remove is always a second family, a margin, a "reads into" — how far a reading goes, not how much. Strength is left alone; strength they build a ladder for — Daeva was read at thirteen, in a garden court, and nobody corrected a line of her. The function corrects reach. Then the flag: when the reading returns nothing — a station that goes dark — there is nothing to advise, and the function does not stop; it fails safe; it marks the file, and the mark has no office behind it because no office made it. Ilsev sent it up twice and something answered. "She just never knew what was answering." Karis proposes, in eleven words: "Ilsev and Havel are never told. It would only cost them." Agreed by everyone; Cael counts the words.
3. **Finding two: the strata.** Dating: by paper, type, seal and vocabulary, and by the Greyvane leaves' seals of known years, the memoranda run two centuries and the leaf is the oldest thing in the case — the start of the policy, not before it. The leaf calls the function appended to the reading; Karis's image: a millwright doesn't make the river; he cuts a race beside it and keeps it clear; the leaf is a millwright's note. Under the policy, older than it, is the reading itself, and the oldest memoranda are about clearing the old custom out of its way: the older word, once — the assessor's provisional designation UNBOUND for work across many families (the B2 margin; the B3 directive; her own voicing at Halcenvane; she builds on it and does not discover it) — handled in three memoranda in order: close the door (assessors may no longer designate by what a person shows), remove the exit (the provisional custom, *pending further study*, struck), delete the name (the word discontinued, re-entered under flag Shattered). Then the limit, verbatim, and the middle of the story, verbatim. Lira: "They *found* it… It isn't a long time compared to whatever's under it." Nobody names a maker; nobody tries.
4. **Finding three: the anomaly checked.** Karis went through all of it for anything that touches session nine — a reading without contact, a family with no practitioner near, anything Tide. Nothing. Cael writes it in the book at the table: *Not this either. Still open.*
5. **Finding four: the stratum note paid.** Karis turns over the front cover's note from the first night of the year (*they're trying to move him back inside the instrument*) and reads it, and answers its question mark: a re-evaluation is an evaluation; under standard protocol it is the delivery; if the station reads him this time, the advice is entered; if it goes dark again, chapter fourteen; both doors open into the same room. Seln: that is why the road has gone quiet — why spend a team when the seat will deliver him at the year's close. Seln: "A conspiracy can be exposed. A convenience cannot." Karis: it can't be filed — every office it would be filed with runs on it. Cael: "It isn't broken. It's maintained." (His new pole, said aloud, not written.)
6. **The stair.** Lira and Cael going down; the naming; she stops on the landing: what it is for, then, if it can't be filed. "It's not evidence. It's a map."

## Chapter 40 — The Instrument (~6,800)

1. **Day 94, the proposal.** Cael says it: one man outside this room is owed the cache — the one who warned them in a room where nothing was written down, and who is still asked to sign the one paper the seat needs. Worst versions said in turn (Brom: he walks it up the stair because honest men do that with paper, and the stair eats it and Seln with it; Karis: he verifies it — he's an evaluator — the frame is theirs and the verification calls the cache a forgery, and it's minuted; Lira: he believes it and it breaks him and he goes home and does nothing, and we spent a good man for a bad night's sleep; Seln: he is already theirs and Norhold was a knock dressed as a kindness; Cael: he reads it and asks about me, and somebody in this room answers). Karis's rule: "He gets the cache. Nothing about Cael." Said aloud by each of the five, Cael last but one; Seln last. Five hands; Seln's goes up last, after a long time.
2. **The arrangement.** Seln reads the wing's posted station closures: the river stations shut for the Archmarshal's yearly inspection, the Ostrand station on the coming Seventh-day. Compact officers lodge at the inn on the wharf. The note: no name, no seal, an address and an hour, and one line Vastin alone can read — *Neither office's. Nothing written.* Seln leaves it with the inn's boy in the ordinary post of the house. The key: a records administrator on circuit for six weeks; the building's keeper lends his room by the morning to anybody with a reason and a deposit; Seln's reason is "a reading".
3. **Day 98, down.** Five people going to town on a Seventh-day by five ways; the coats log a market day. The office: verbatim description; a table, six chairs brought up from the keeper's store and none matching, bare shelves with the clean shapes of ledgers in the dust, a cold stove, pale squares on the wall where frames hung, a window over the roofs to the wool exchange's cupola four streets off. The landing clock on the stair outside, the building's, not anyone's. Seln at the window. "Front stair," he says — Vastin eleven minutes early by the landing clock, coming in from the street by the front, and up, single steps, not hurried.
4. **The reading.** Vastin in plain grey, nothing in his hands. Bows to the room. Karis lays the cache out in the order it was opened and sits down. He reads all of it: the pairs (the father's letter twice), the field reports (he stops at *Continue observation.*), the memoranda, the leaf last, with Karis's gloves offered and accepted. More than an hour. The left hand on the table opening and closing. He looks at Cael once, for about as long as it takes to read a line, and away. Nobody says anything about Cael.
5. **The silence and the line.** He sets the leaf in its sleeve. The longest silence (the page lets it run: the landing clock, the stove ticking cold, Brom's breathing, a cart in the street). Then: "I knew the system was imperfect. I didn't know it was designed to be."
6. **Refusals.** "You have not asked me for anything. I will tell you what I will not do, so that you don't wonder." Verify — every instrument he could verify it with runs on the thing it indicts; it would be theater. Report — to report it he must swear to the office that made it; an oath to a forger. Certify — when the seat asks him to certify the protocol for the re-evaluation at Halcenvane, and it will, he will decline; "It is the only sentence I have left that they still need." Nine thousand evaluations, something over, in forty years; he does not know how many were entered as he wrote them. Lira: "Are you with us?" "No. I am not with anyone." He stands, thanks the room — not any of them — and goes down by the front stair. Seln, at the window, watching him into the street: "He was not managing us." Cael: "He didn't join us. I'd have trusted him less if he had."
7. **Vastin's window (~2,000).** Days later, his room high in the Compact's house: the pigeons, the crooked press, the three trays. The junior's copy in *held*, the pinned note *Second frame reading: within tolerance.*, *Continue observation.* He reads it again and sees what the junior saw, and what he now knows the junior saw. Verification, gone through as he would make a witness go back: a third frame (the river station's frame and the mender's shim — a frame can be made to stop telling); a fresh reading (it runs on the thing it indicts); the station's own sheet (a request from his office with her name on it would go up his stair and be entered, and then she would be a file); the junior (he would make him a witness to something he could not unknow). Any of it would be theater, and some of it would have a victim. He files nothing. He does not write — not the letter-book, not a slip, not a candle. The clerk at the sixth bell: "Will you be getting home tonight, sir?" "Yes." He puts the copy back in *held* and goes home at the ordinary hour, which is the hardest thing he does that week.
