## Review status and verdict

This is an informed editorial review by a separate review seat, not a manuscript-only blind read and not a real human audience test. Coverage is the complete supplied movement, chapters 35–40, in order, with the compiled canon evidence, formula, measured metrics, and supplied overlap report.

**Verdict: close with two line-level fixes, then targeted recheck.** The movement delivers the book's central revelation with unusual patience and moral force. ## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in chapter 35's first line, because the new Anchor-adjacent perception has stopped asking and Cael does not trust the silence. It tightened when the case came off the shelf and fully caught at the river-town father's letter: that letter turns institutional harm from a theory into one child asking her father what she is. The strongest emotional material belongs to Seln—especially Lira asking what it cost him to remain pleasant, Cael revealing the Shadow fragment, and Seln realizing, “You kept my file too, then.” Vastin's refusal to join the circle is the right payoff because it preserves both his integrity and his independence.

I expect the next movement to test whether the group can keep walking the corridor perfectly after learning it was built to contain them. I would continue without hesitation.

## Continuity, canon, and story audit

### Findings requiring repair

1. **Chapter 38, line 173, clean-door calculation.** Karis says the entry occurs “forty days after the close of the last sitting he failed to attend,” then immediately uses the fourth sitting's close on day 75 to reach day 115. Cael attended that sitting. The supplied canon fixes the count at forty days after the fourth sitting's close if he leaves while the matter is pending (BOOK_MAP §4e #9; Movement 4 ledger). The current grammar instead makes a missed sitting the trigger. This is a localized explanation error, not a flaw in the clean-door mechanism itself.

2. **Chapter 40, line 279, borrowed-room headcount.** Vastin remembers “Five young people and a man at a window.” The hosts were four young people—Cael, Lira, Brom, and Karis—and Seln at the window. The next paragraph correctly enumerates those four plus Seln. This should read “Four young people and a man at a window.”

- **Plan adherence:** The case arrives in ordered objects; Karis supplies four findings and a limit; the clean door is found; Anchor practice stays non-deploying; the borrowed office and three refusals land; the final Vastin window pays the planted northern file. ## Repair brief

1. **Location:** Chapter 38, line 173, from “A respondent who leaves…” through the day-115 calculation. **Observed issue:** the prose says the forty-day clock follows “the last sitting he failed to attend,” but calculates from day 75, a sitting Cael attended; this conflicts with the supplied clean-door canon. **Effect on the reader:** the movement's essential reason for staying becomes logically unstable on first read, especially for the thirteen-year-old lens. **Proposed scope:** repair only the trigger sentence so it states the canon mechanism unambiguously while preserving day 75, day 115, the *fled* entry, four marked files, and the post-ruling good-standing door. **Strength to preserve:** Karis's three-part proof and Lira's “we don't run, we walk” payoff.

2. **Location:** Chapter 40, line 279, “Five young people and a man at a window.” **Observed issue:** there are four young people plus Seln; the following paragraph's enumeration confirms the correct count. **Effect on the reader:** the error jars against a book whose characters and prose treat counting as evidence. **Proposed scope:** one-word correction from “Five” to “Four,” with no surrounding revision. **Strength to preserve:** Vastin's deliberate refusal to name or describe any of them, especially Seln.
