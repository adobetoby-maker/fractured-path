## Chapter 41 — The Empty Hill (~4,900)

1. **Day 98, dusk.** Five ways back up from the town. The term's last carts go down the bluff road with trunks; the ferry low in the water with students. Cael on the north wall at the third buttress; the coats count trunks, not him. The recess slips at the wing counter: remaining in residence, a reason each, four enrollees (Lira the program; Brom the frames; Karis her standing; Cael writes *the proceedings*; Seln, staff, reads it and says "Mm"). Log at the sill: no word of the case; *A man read something in a borrowed room and thanked the room. Six now. That's the whole of it.* Hesk's clock losing its minutes.
2. **Day 99, the first morning.** The emptied hill as a place: one refectory table lit; forty enrollees left of four hundred and thirty; the porter's boy gone home; Bracken at his counter anyway. No wing three for fourteen days — nobody writing a figure for him — and he does not know what to do with his hands. Brom and Ephram at the frames (Ephram stayed; "Somebody has to keep the frames honest"). Brom's hand on his pocket.
3. **Day 101, Hesk's letter.** Read on the sill. Hesk at his altitude: the satchel (he answers Cael's thanks by telling him how to replace a strap and never the buckle); a ship's chronometer in for cleaning that gains, the opposite fault to his own clock; the widow's dog now asleep on his step "which I have decided is worse"; the stove; nothing of history. Ends *— H.* Cael answers at once: the hill empty, the weather, nothing he cannot write.
4. **Day 102, the argument.** The reading room. Karis has laid out the bones of a closing under the hearing provision's second paragraph, "if leave is given". Lira asks what it is for now. Karis: the record. Brom: what knowing costs him (he cannot look at *outcome anticipated* without seeing a form). Seln: fifteen years of true reports; "You get used to it. You should not." Cael says it aloud for the first time: he has stopped believing the text is ground — and he will attend every sitting anyway, because leaving early would hand the machine its confession (the coastal default quoting a man's own letter). Karis is hurt — it is her whole discipline. She decides: she will write it as if it were ground; that is her cost. Lira: she'll climb as if it were a ladder. No vote; nothing decided.
5. **Day 105, the silence read.** The lodge book's road column with Seln on the Seventh-day (the circuit of M3): the coats on the bell, older men; the post without a knock; nobody at the gate since the cut. Seln: they've stood down because the corridor is on schedule. Log quotes the packet line: *why spend a team when the seat will deliver him at the year's close.* Seln writes his weekly return at the copying table — true and empty (*Subject remained in residence for the house's recess…*) — and files the duplicate.

## Chapter 42 — Forty Paces (~4,800)

1. **Day 106, dawn.** Lira asks to cross the east hall again. She crosses talking to the Stone about his red book, not looking at her feet. Rooke: "Now." She is back on the closed floor, first drills; she lands on the left every time and doesn't notice; Cael notices and says nothing.
2. **Day 107, the orchard.** Seln teaches road-craft in daylight in the lower orchard and the meadow below the kitchen gardens, where anybody could see. Why openly: a thing done in the open goes in a log as what it is. **Water at forty paces** (the stream: close enough to fetch in the dark, far enough that its noise doesn't cover a foot; beasts come to water; damp). **The open approach** (walk up to a place on the track, in the open, hands seen, at an hour people expect callers; never from cover — the lodge-man's cottage as the practice house). **Two watches of four** hours, two to a watch, the fifth sleeps through. Lira argues for three watches; Seln: three watches means nobody sleeps. Karis asks about paper in wet; Brom about loads. Nobody says what it's for.
3. **Day 109, night road.** The farm track down the back of the bluff to the river meadows, after the tenth bell, by the lodge book (out by the orchard gate, in again). Seln sets them to walk a quarter mile without being counted by him; he sits somewhere they cannot see. Halts every two hundred yards: Brom counts by hand on shoulders. The Anchor sense asks at the meadow gate; named. The quiet thing offers in the dark — the one thing that would make him uncountable — and is held; Seln can't know. At the third halt Brom counts four: Seln has stepped off; Brom goes back without a word and finds him at the hedge. "How did you know?" "I check." Seln's count afterward: he heard Lira twice, Cael once, Karis's papers, Brom never.
4. **Day 110.** Rooke's board: the struck bout re-posted — Lira v the Current girl, rated, the Sixth-day, program floor. Lira asks Cael to stand at the door ("Not the gallery. The door. Where I can't see you."). The evening before: the north wall; she talks about everything except the bout.

## Chapter 43 — The Glide (~5,000)

1. **Day 111, the return bout (~2,100).** East hall at dawn, two lamps; the program clerk with the red book; the Stone with the flags; Rooke's rule restated once (four exchanges, a flag or none, three bursts, level goes to the figure). The Current girl, Iron Rank Four, glides "like water over a weir". E1: Lira's four-count; on the fourth she checks the landing and lands left — a beat late; the glide takes the flag. E2: she goes on the second count, flat, clean — her flag. E3: the Current changes to glide-and-step; Lira reads it, bursts, lands left again on the wrong line; no flag. E4: Lira goes early; the Current is already past; flag. Two to one. Two bursts spent of three. Rooke: "Four landings. All on the left. Your foot's been mended a fortnight; your head hasn't." The correction: land on the right on purpose, every drill, until she stops asking. The Current girl apologises; Lira: "Don't you dare."
2. **Lira's window (~2,500).** That night in the east hall alone on her key, one lantern. She lands on the right twenty times. The ankle doesn't hurt; it's the asking. The book: small, oilcloth-backed, bought from the chandler by the bridge on the Seventh-day of Ostrand — fighters, floors, houses, burst budgets, what the Stone said, what the bargeman said, in pencil — begun in the dairy week, kept for "a road with nobody's seal on it". Four losses on her sheet now. The ladder; the sheet that will go to the advancement evaluation. Fenmark: the one choice somebody made for her. The map. She decides nothing about leaving; she decides she will land on the right, and write the bout down tonight with the loss in it as it was.
3. **Day 113, the term opens (~400).** Carts up the road; trunks; the coats count up what they counted down. Log: the corridor resumes.

## Chapter 45 — The Lake House (~4,900)

1. **Day 125, Brom's schedule (~1,100).** Bracken brings it to the frames: the panel will read the record as it stands, the title, the house's attendance, and a reading in a sigil frame under load. Brom prepares beside Ephram: holds a measured load in the long frame, Iron Skin up, the frame's needle; Ephram works his sets and says nothing. The envelope still in his pocket; he touches it once.
2. **Day 128, the lake house (~1,600).** The lake house sends its Blade (Iron Rank Five, a lath) for the exhibition struck in M5; the Crown yard's sprung oak; panel of three; five exchanges at most, two touches ends it. Lira lands on the right on purpose in E1 and it holds; E2 his touch (the lath's commitment); E3 clean; E4 hers. Two to one.
3. **Day 129, Rooke's board (~500).** The registry's Silver advancement evaluation posted: the district seat, the first sitting after the year's close. Lira reads it. "That's after the ruling." Rooke: "It is." Nothing else.
4. **Day 129, the night spar (~1,700), the night of the slate.** East hall after the tenth bell, just the two. She asks for the rate he never shows — not a test: a fight he means to win. He writes nothing first; she says "Don't." They fight: Wind past six on boards, the push at fighting load, the give, the spark, one lane laid first and the read given back; she takes one exchange off him; the rest are his. Ten counts slow after the lane; she sees it. She decides aloud she will say nothing about it to anyone — not Brom, not Karis, not Ephram, not the book — "It's mine." He doesn't write the figures.
