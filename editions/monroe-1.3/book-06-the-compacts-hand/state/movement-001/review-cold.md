## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**
- Ch 1, the pre-dawn suite run. Each power named, sourced ("Lira's burst", "Brom's read", "the thing he had taken from the Bronze at Ardenmere"), costed ("the hip took the fifth and kept the receipt"), and then the lane, the wait for the pull, and "Nothing caught." That is the real opening and it works.
- Ch 3, Ephram's chart: "Never past twenty-seven … and then once, at Norhold, it left the room, and nobody had a number for where it went." Best single scene for showing the band without explaining it.
- Ch 4, "*There* you are," and the lane/read discovery: "I was looking at the road." The limit is learned on a floor, then solved on the same floor, with a quantified cost (ten counts slow, gone at eleven). This is the movement's clearest progression beat.
- Ch 5, the escort: "A thing going down the road travelled like freight. This was a thing coming up the road, and it was travelling like a prisoner."
- Ch 6, the counsel reading backward, and paragraph seven: the Compact quoting the honest man's own sentence as its warrant.
- Ch 7, "You are the frame," Withrow's "morning after," the four cups, and Lira's "What if the people didn't fail this time?"

**Where it slipped.**
- Ch 1 opening journal: eleven proper names in ~20 lines (Hesk, Bracken, Lira, Rooke, Brom, Velmere, Karis, Seln, Ephram, Vastin, Daeva, Norhold) before anything happens. Useful roll-call for a returning reader; a wall for a new one.
- Ch 2, the Karis reading-room scene: fine character work (the lock, the line in the accounts, "a reader who wants to keep her room starts reading a little more kindly") but it is the third consecutive quiet observational scene and the chapter has no event in it until Seln's one sentence at the counter.
- Ch 6, first half: Withrow's underlining, then Cael's word-count, then the counsel's backward read, then four rulings, before the first real turn (paragraph seven). Three procedural beats in a row at one table.

**Which person matters.** Karis drives the night and the answer; Lira owns the last question; Withrow's cracked registry voice is the movement's emotional thermometer. Cael is the observer and the object; his agency this movement is the honest floor (which he is invited to) and the count (which he volunteers). That is clearly intended ("I'm the frame"), but it leaves him deciding very little across seven chapters.

**What I expect next.** Karis's four-day draft and the twenty-second day; Seln's unsigned index appearing on her table; the coats at the foot of the road finally turning their heads; Brom's year-end panel; Lira's two points; the "note for the cold term" on the provisions section; and whatever Vastin meant by "not official channels much longer." I also expect the people not to fail this time, because the text has told me so twice.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## 2. Orientation (requested special judgement)

**Reader who has just finished the previous book.** Oriented quickly and well. The closed-interval journal in ch 1 is close to ideal for this: every thread gets a one-line status (Lira on Rooke's floor and silent about it; Brom's summons buttoned in with the Velmere letter; Karis's lock; Seln at the middle of the bench; Ephram captain; the coats changed; Vastin's promised sitting; the storm's flag). The suite run then re-teaches each power by use rather than by summary. The only snag for this reader is the arithmetic of the inventory (see Priority 1).

**First-time reader.** Can follow the spine: an unclassifiable boy ("[SHATTERED]" is finally on the page in ch 5) enrolled by an old provision, hiding his real rate on his home floor, who was seen at full strength at a tournament, and whom the Compact now challenges by a route that never touches the clause. The specifics that remain opaque to a new reader through all seven chapters:
- **Hesk.** The book, the steel pen, the clock an hour fast, "he knew what Hesk would say about leaving things out" — but who Hesk is, and why the journal is his, is never given even one clause.
- **"The Log"** (ch 3, "He wore it himself, in front of the Log, most nights") — capitalised, unexplained, apparently a prior name for the notebook.
- **Why the band persists.** Ch 1 says the full rate is now on paper in the tournament's own hand, "and paper did not forget," and then that on the bluff he still fights under it. Ch 3 calls it "the price of a door he was keeping shut." The new reader never learns what door. Returning readers presumably know; new readers will ask why he hides what the whole continent has already seen.
- "The stair," "the quiet thing," and the debt to Seln are correctly withheld as mystery and are fine as such; the debt is at least placed (ch 4: "a quiet man who sat at the middle of the bench").

Verdict on orientation: returning reader, strong; first-time reader, followable with effort, and two or three one-clause glosses would remove most of the effort without costing the returning reader anything.

## 4. Canon, physical state, power limits, knowledge boundaries, reserved disclosures

No canon evidence was supplied. Everything below is *apparent* inconsistency within the supplied text, or plan-dependent and therefore unknowable here.

**Apparent inconsistencies (text vs text):**

1. **The inventory count.** Ch 1 journal: "What I carry, at the year's open: seven, and the one I don't count." Ch 1 suite run enumerates Wind, read, push, give, spark (counted one to five), then "the sixth" is the sealed quiet thing, then "That left the last. Daeva's storm" — seven items including the sealed one. Later in ch 1: "Before Norhold the registry had held five of his seven" — but the storm came *at* Norhold, so before Norhold the text's own list gives six. Either there is an eighth thing never named in this movement, or the phrasing is off. A cold reader cannot resolve it. Medium confidence that this is a wording slip rather than a plant.

2. **"Settled" vs ten counts slow.** Ch 1: after the lane he waits for the pull, "Nothing caught," the Wind comes "Only the Wind," and he writes "Nothing pulls … Settled." Ch 4: after the second lane run, "For ten counts after the lane, everything answered him a beat behind his asking." The text distinguishes *pull* (wrong thing answers) from *slow* (right thing answers late), which is defensible, but nothing in ch 4 names the distinction or says why ch 1's run showed no delay (he "turned round, slowly" — perhaps more than ten counts passed, but that is my inference, not the page's). A reader may conclude "Settled" was premature and the narrative did not notice.

3. **Push count in the journal.** Ch 1 narration: four pushes on the post, then after the lane "one push, flat." Ch 1 journal: "the push, four, flat at the last." Readable as four real plus one flat, but it does not match cleanly.

4. **"Five people on the floor."** Ch 2: five people are announced; the Stone is called "the fifth man" after Rooke and two third-years (that is four); then Lira is revealed "on the line in front of him." Either the clerk at the trestle is one of the five and Lira is a sixth, or the count is off by one. Minor.

5. **Recess length.** Ch 1: "two months" since the ferry, program opened "the first morning of the recess." Ch 2: "The whole hall had been rebuilt in twelve weeks." Twelve weeks is longer than the two months the text has allowed. Minor.

6. **"Given under the Compact's hand" on two leaves.** Ch 5: Withrow reads it aloud from the first leaf. Ch 6: it is "the attestation" on the *last* leaf, "a line he had not heard read aloud … because Withrow had stopped at the first leaf." Ch 7 end: it is again "at the foot of the page" of the first leaf. Consistent if the first leaf is a cover summary that repeats the attestation formula, but the ch 6 sentence reads as though the phrase were new there. Low priority; one word ("again") would settle it.

**Timeline check (passes).** Day 1 First-day convocation; day 2 east hall; day 3 counter and coats; day 4 docket; day 5 Fifth-day bout (wing signs on Fifth-days — consistent); day 6 night floor; day 7 Seventh-day rest, Bracken's glass; day 8 First-day served. Instrument dated day 1, served day 8 = seven days, matches Bracken. Fourteen days from service = day 22, matches. Bracken's "thirteen years" is consistent across chs 3, 4, 6, 7.

**Physical state (passes).** Right-side rib ch 3 → pressed on the right side ch 4 → not mentioned after (bruise forecast "yellow by Seventh-day"). Hip bill ch 4 → "hip reminding him at every landing" ch 5 (day 8). Lira's left arm "close out of habit" ch 2 → "the left one came round as easily as the right" ch 4 (she is warmed up and deliberately rolling it; acceptable). Shoulder "fifth counted" ch 4, not revisited.

**Power limits.** Lane: fifteen feet, a third deep, ears, dust — identical in ch 1 and ch 4. Give: a cup, four in a row, fifth "goes somewhere" — new information, consistent with ch 1's "until somebody hit it too hard or too often." Spark: two contacts in public, house rule — consistent ch 1 / ch 3. Read: body *or* air, not both at Gold — new, stated as something he already wrote at Norhold ("*at Gold it will do one or the other and not both*"), so it is a reminder not a contradiction. The quiet thing is never used; the seal is honoured.

**Knowledge boundaries.** Cael knows Withrow's twenty registry years and her "nine hundred" signatures (ch 5–6): the first is presented as common house knowledge, the second she says aloud. Cael knows the counsel's eleven-year retainer and nameless condition (ch 6) though he "had never seen her before": the narration does not say who told him. Low concern. Cael "had never once seen written down" the Watch line (ch 6) — plan-dependent; cannot check.

**Reserved disclosures.** Vastin's three-sentence advisory is quoted whole in ch 6 ("You told us at Norhold there were three sentences … none of us had ever seen them"). "Suppression-Advisory Watch, Priority Level 4" is cited and deliberately not argued from. The procurator's name is withheld from the reader (Bracken says it is unknown; the name itself is never printed). Whether any of these should have stayed reserved is plan adherence, not a canon finding, and I cannot judge it cold.

## 5. Lesser notes (not in the repair brief)

- The closing prolepsis, ch 7: "He would learn, before the year was out, what it was standing on." This is the only step outside Cael's horizon in the movement. Lira's question, Brom's "Corridor," and the lamps going out already carry the irony; the line tells the reader what to feel. Consider cutting to "Tonight it stood, in his own sure hand, and looked like the truth, because it was the truth as far as he could see." Judgment call; not must-fix.
- Simile template "the way a man / the way you …" appears twenty-plus times across the movement. It is the voice and should not be purged, but the density is high enough to be audible in chs 1, 3 and 5. Thinning three or four would be enough.
- Ch 2's rating-system paragraph ("Five judges marked a bout in three columns…") is necessary and correct but is delivered as a block; it would read more easily split between Cael's narration and one line of Lira's.
- Ch 6, Cael's word-count: "*They don't expect to prove anything. They expect to be agreed with.*" is excellent and should stay. The four rulings that follow are the softest stretch of the night; the ferryman's daughter is the part worth keeping whole.

## Repair brief

Three priorities, in order. Each keeps the developed action intact; nothing here shortens a bout.

### Priority 1 — Ch 1 orientation: the inventory arithmetic and two one-clause glosses

- **Location.** Ch 1, journal ("*What I carry, at the year's open: seven, and the one I don't count*"), the suite run's numbering ("Five." … "did not hurry the sixth" … "That left the last"), and the band paragraph ("Before Norhold the registry had held five of his seven"). Also "Hesk's book" (first paragraph) and ch 3 "in front of the Log."
- **Observed issue.** The three statements of how many things Cael carries do not add up on the page (seven-plus-one; six-plus-sealed; five-of-seven before the storm existed). Hesk and "the Log" are referred to as known and never placed, even by a clause.
- **Effect on the reader.** A returning reader stops to recount and loses the pre-dawn mood exactly where the movement is earning it; a first-time reader concludes the book assumes a prior book and reads the rest of ch 1 at arm's length.
- **Proposed scope.** Make the count resolve in one of the three places (or make the uncounted one explicitly the quiet thing and adjust "five of his seven" to match). Add a single clause placing Hesk (who he is to Cael, why the book is his). Either gloss "the Log" or replace it with "the notebook." Two to four sentences total.
- **Strength to preserve.** The suite run's structure (named, sourced, costed, numbered) and the beat "Nothing caught." Do not add exposition inside the run itself.

### Priority 2 — Ch 4: reconcile "ten counts slow" with ch 1's "Settled"

- **Location.** Ch 4, after the second lane run: "For ten counts after the lane, everything answered him a beat behind his asking. At eleven it was gone," through Lira's palm ledger ("After: behind the eyes, ten counts slow, gone at eleven").
- **Observed issue.** Ch 1 declares the flag down because the suite answers true after the lane. Ch 4 shows the suite answering late after the lane. The text treats *pull* and *slow* as different things but never says so, and never accounts for why ch 1's run showed no delay.
- **Effect on the reader.** The careful reader suspects the movement has contradicted its own opening finding without noticing; the less careful one half-registers that "Settled" was wrong and loses a little trust in Cael's ledger, which is the instrument the whole movement asks us to trust.
- **Proposed scope.** One or two lines in Cael's head or in dialogue drawing the distinction (nothing *else* answers; everything answers *late*), and one clause explaining the ch 1 difference (he stood and waited before calling the Wind; here he called it inside the count). Lira's "Which thing, then writing" framing is the natural place. No change to the lane runs themselves.
- **Strength to preserve.** The full honest-floor sequence: the give as a cup, the push, "I was looking at the road," lane-first-read-second, and Lira reading the costs off her hand. All of it is the movement's best progression writing.

### Priority 3 — Ch 6 first half: three procedural beats before the first turn

- **Location.** Ch 6 from "Cael counted the instrument before anybody read a clause of it aloud" through the end of the four rulings ("'This one was written by somebody who read all five and decided not to write a sixth.'"), i.e. the count, the backward read, and the rulings, before Karis's first finding and paragraph seven.
- **Observed issue.** After Withrow's underlining (which is strong and should stay), the table runs three consecutive procedure scenes with no turn: Cael counts, counsel explains backward reading, four old rulings are summarised. The first thing that changes the room is paragraph seven, roughly two-thirds into the chapter.
- **Effect on the reader.** The thirteen-year-old lens drifts here; the adult lens stays but feels the night being inventoried rather than lived. Because ch 7 is also a table chapter, the sag compounds.
- **Proposed scope.** Tighten rather than cut. Keep the count (and its conclusion "They expect to be agreed with"), keep the counsel's "The end of a paper is what the writer wants," keep the ferryman's daughter whole. Compress the second, third and fourth rulings to a sentence or two, and consider moving the counsel's "In this paper the recital runs to four sentences" to sit directly before Karis's first finding so the three beats land as one rising line into paragraph seven. Target perhaps a tenth off the stretch, not more.
- **Strength to preserve.** Paragraph seven and everything after it; the Withrow underlining scene; Bracken's "Which is information."

---

**Verdict:** Ship-quality movement with three located repairs. It is a quiet opening by design, and the design holds: the inciting instrument lands in ch 5 on a runway the first four chapters have made load-bearing. A reader coming straight from the previous book is oriented within two pages; a first-time reader can follow the spine and will tolerate the opacity if Priority 1's glosses are added. Nothing in the movement reads as a canon violation from the manuscript alone; the six apparent inconsistencies are all wording-level and none affects the plot.
