## Chapter 28 — Where the Mark Sat (~5,100)

1. **Seln, day 77, before the first bell** (cutaway). Way in: the records hall dark except Bracken's counter lamp; Seln waits for Bracken to finish winding the counter clock. One question: where did the mark sit when you held it to the light? Bracken had laid his ivory rule on the sheet at the lodge window, because he lays a rule on everything; a sixteenth of an inch low against the south mill's registry stock. Seln says thank you and nothing else. Bracken does not ask why. Seln knows what a sixteenth means: a mould re-laid after a wire went; everything off it is a second run. (His own sample-sheets of registry stock in the back of a manual — the habit of a man who copies things.)
2. **Seln, same day.** The favours, counted: four owed to him in fifteen years, never spent. Two letters in the service hand at the copying table, each a single question: to the man who keeps the south mill's mould room (the run); west to a correspondent in the registry's western stores (the lots drawn off that run). The price of a favour is that it tells somebody you wanted to know. He walks down the bluff road on the rest day with a clerk's satchel; the coats see a clerk with letters; the drop behind a chandler's counter near the bridge; the bag that goes faster than the post. On the way back up he counts the coats and lets them count him.
3. **Cael, day 79, his room after supper.** The rain has started. The notice for the fifth sitting (documentary; the enrollee in person; the maker of the copies to attend) is pinned over his sill. Lira on the bed with her boots off rolls the left shoulder: "Clean." The rope bruise faded. Cael asks the three (Lira, Brom, Karis — Seln is not of this) to price a reach before the fact. Lira's question from ch27 is still unanswered; Cael's answer is to write what he'd pay if somebody else began it. The covenant at the front of the book (three lines; the Norhold line under it with four initials). "At the weight it will come at": Seln said the best tool somebody owns — call it Silver.
4. **Same scene, the price itself.** Karis scales from Storm (eleven weeks at Gold; at Silver her curve says about nine). Cael lists: the first minute after may be the wrong thing answering, as at Norhold; somebody has to stand over him for that minute — Brom: "Then that's me." The band cannot be held for a week; wing three must be excused; the lane and the read unsafe. Blanks left visible where her reach, rate and price would go (Seln's rule). Cael's honesty: writing it down is admitting he'd want it. Lira writes the last line in capitals. Four initials. The rain on the glass. Log line.

## Chapter 29 — The Catechism (~5,200)

1. **Seln, day 80–81** (cutaway). The mould-room answer on day 80 (in the man's own spelling): the registry mould re-laid this year; the mark a sixteenth low ever since; the second run. Day 81, the post-room sort (he still does it for the porter's shoulder), the service bag's envelope with the western answer, four lines: the second run's lots in the stores ledger, thirteen, each against an office; a fourteenth drawn above the ruled lines, and in the office column a mark where a word should be. He reads it twice and burns it in the sort-room stove, which is what the four lines were written to expect.
2. **Seln, the four grades.** He names them in the order the woman who taught them named them, in a room over a chandler's on a quay, his first year: the record (paper in a file); the instrument (a proceeding, in daylight); the knock (paper from an office nobody can place, at the door — it measures the door); the hand (no paper, no face, a road where no house stands). The fourth grade does not knock. A fourteenth lot is what the third grade looks like from the inside of a ledger. The fourth comes next.
3. **Seln, the catechism.** She taught that too. He says it once, aloud, in the wing with nobody there, all four lines, as he said it every first morning of a posting. Then line by line it loses: what he carries has done nothing for twelve years and is worth exactly that; a wall round the case is a wall round nothing the boy can use; the keeper invisible — the boy's people stand in plain sight in a court; the witness a location — he would rather be the place the boy can find; and the last line, which he had always believed, he sees he is about to break: he believes the boy. He has counted him for a year and believes him. Nobody has ever protected a man by believing him; he is going to try. Quiet. The largest thing that has happened to him since the green corridor, and the wing clock does not change its note.
4. **Seln, the case carried.** Down from the shelf above the copying table; across the court in the rain at dusk; twenty-three people passed, counted (porters, a fire-watch boy, first-years, a Magister with an umbrella, the lodge-man's boy); nobody looks at a clerk with a case. Up the residence stair to the boy's door. He sets it on the sill. "I'm going to open this. Not tonight." Cael: "You're sure." Seln: "No. That's how you'll know it's true." (Seln does not say what is in it. Cael does not ask.)
5. **Seln, the annex chosen and the cipher.** Not here; not the wing. A room with a lock and a name on the card that is neither of theirs; a reason for people to go up one at a time. Cael: the reading room, Bracken's card, the brass lock. "The researcher's room." Not before the sitting: you don't carry a new thing into a room with Jent in it. The cipher: the wing's floor schedule for the coming week, which Seln copies fair for the boards; five bookings on the First-day, five floors, five hours a quarter apart, each in a name; read your own line, the hour is your arrival. Cael counts six lines in the First-day column. "Five. The sixth is the captain, and he wants the floor."
6. **Seln, the null report.** That night at the copying table: the weekly return on the subject, in the old pattern, the old phrases, true and empty; the duplicate into the thickest bundle. It is not the last; he will file one every week until the year's close. The case back on its shelf.
7. **Cael, day 81 evening.** The river: the landing ferry laid up, the bridge-ward's chain across Ostrand bridge (the water at the third ring on the pier). Bracken at his glass. The driver's sum: up the near bank two hours to the upper ferry at the narrows, which runs in any water; over; the far bank's river road east two hours into the city. Withrow's rule since the gate: no enrollee off the hill alone. The counsel already in her rooms by the wharf. The seat does not adjourn for weather and the house has never asked it to. The five will go. Lira: "Then we go the long way." Log.

## Chapter 30 — The Upper Ferry (~5,300)

1. **Havel, day 80–81, Ostrand** (cutaway). A clerk from the district seat at the inn by the bridge on the Third-day with a letter and a pencil rubbing: the Halcenvane registrar's inquiry, sent across "for the registers" because it concerns the proceedings and the district's records room is short. His thumb aches. Three checks over two days, each in its own room: the district's impression book (wafers pasted in by issue); the registry's printed schedule of seals of office (the engraved plates, this year's revision); the die number in the rim's beading against the strong-room copy of the cutters' issue ledger. The facilities office is on the register of offices — constituted this year by an instrument filed under seal. The seal on that instrument is the rubbing's seal. In none of the three. He drafts the usual reply: the office constituted on such a date by instrument filed under seal; inquiries to the issuing authority. The form has no line for what he found. At the inn: the five sheets, the sixth written — *a seal absent from every registry of seals* — fact only, by the rule of the sheets. He sees the first sheet's mark with no office and the sixth's seal with no register side by side and makes nothing of it. He stops; there will be no seventh, because a note of what the registers could not answer is only worth keeping while the registers answer everything else. He goes to bed with the river loud under the window.
2. **Cael, day 82, before light.** The coach down the bluff road in the dark; the coats at the road's foot see it turn west; the near-bank road in mud; Brom with the record cases on his knees; Karis rehearsing the attestation under her breath; Lira asleep against the window; Seln on the bench with his eyes shut. Wind getting up, the rain gone east.
3. **The upper ferry.** First light at the narrows: a chain ferry between rock banks; the river loud and brown and narrow and deep. The ferryman (unnamed): crosses in any water because the narrows has no width to throw him; talks to the chain. "Road's good your side. Cut's dry. Only stretch that ever is." Chalk. On the far landing Seln gets down: he will walk the river path below the road and meet them at the milestone past the cut; a man on foot on a bank is a fisherman. Nobody asks. Cael notes it.
4. **The cut.** The road climbs from the landing up a chalk spur; a cutting 440 yards long, the north bank twenty feet, the south bank lower and, at the middle, undercut by old floods into a hollow under the lip; twenty-four feet of cambered road with a ditch on the north side; at the east end a stream comes down a gully and goes under the road by a culvert with a stone parapet, and beside it the milestone. A skein of geese over, high. Dry chalk, white dust. A carter's cart across the east end by the culvert, a wheel off, nobody at it. The driver stops forty yards short.
5. **Fourteen anchors.** Cael gets down "to look at a wheel" and puts his palm on the road: the read goes out. The ground is carrying weight in places where nothing stands — fourteen, counted twice: on the milestone, on the culvert parapet, in both banks, across the road behind them at the west mouth. Not Ternhall's anchors, which drink heat into a floor; not the Lattice Path's strung lines; these hold. A frame, closed at both mouths, open in one channel twelve feet wide along the south bank past the undercut to the culvert. Beyond the culvert, in the gully, a closed wagon with a barred back. He stands up and says it: "Fourteen." Then the team comes: six in road grey, hooded, scarves to the eyes; the lead at the cart; four down the north bank on ropes and out of the gully; and one who walks out of the undercut's shadow and stops in the middle of the road on the one patch of level crown, three-quarters turned. No half beat. Her. No paper. The lead's one sentence: the one wanted walks east to the culvert, nobody else is wanted, stay on the box. Hobbles and a hood at his belt; no blades. Cael: "Custody. Not assault." The five (four; Seln is not there) take their places: Brom into the twelve feet; Lira at the coach's tail; Karis at the near horse.

## Chapter 31 — Four Exchanges (~5,300)

1. **The perimeter and the twelve feet (Cael's eye, in glimpses).** The lead counts; the four close on the count to make a ring round the coach. Lira breaks the timing: each burst puts her where a man's next count lands, before he arrives — one, two, three; the fourth lands her on the ditch's lip on the camber and the ankle goes over; she keeps her feet a breath and goes down. Brom in the twelve feet does not move; the two heavier ones come at him in turn: the first contact he takes square; the second comes shoulder-first into his left shoulder, and the Iron Skin holds and the joint under it does not quite. He stays where he is. Karis at the coach walks into a course that has the near horse's legs fixed and the driver's reins fixed on the box; she puts heat into it where it meets the point; it frays like rope over a flame; the next; the fourth instance of her right hand, blistering, and she does not stop.
2. **Exchange one — subtraction.** Cael goes for the woman: she is the key. She does not strike. Every line he takes, the place his next foot wants is already fixed — he comes down short, his burst dies against air that will not move. She takes away directions one at a time. The quiet thing stirs and offers to hide him; he does not look at it.
3. **Exchange two — accumulation.** Nothing she lays comes up again. He tries the push at combat load against a point: it is pushing a wall that pushes him; four pushes, the fourth the shoulder's word; spent. He tries to make her re-lay: a feint wide so that she sets a point out of reach and must move it — re-laying costs her a half-second; she does it twice and then stops biting. The space round him is smaller after every exchange and she has not taken one step.
4. **Exchange three — the camber.** He lays a lane low along the road to get past her at speed; the lane takes the read (lay the lane, then give the read back — no time to give it back); blind to the points, he rides the lane into one at full pace eight feet on: the right ribs. He goes down rolling across the crown and down the camber; the gravel takes the skin of his left forearm from wrist to elbow. Ten counts slow, the right thing answering late, *bill, not flag*; she lays through all ten. The spark into a point, once: it frays a thread, as Karis's did, and the point holds.
5. **Exchange four — three points from finished.** On his knees on the camber he gives the read back and counts what is round him: a cage of points close in, and the root of it — the first point she laid this exchange, against the front of his own coat, which he has been carrying without knowing. Three more and it closes; a body inside a closed cage is held. The covenant in the time of a breath: not made by them; begun by her, in earnest, awake; priced on the Second-day at Silver weight with four initials. He reaches, eyes open, mid-fight. Not taking: holding still in front of four exchanges of her craft until it comes together. It comes. The lattice appears to him as lines — every course and point in the cut, threaded through each other back to her; and the one against his chest is the first stitch. He lets it go from inside. The cage unthreads; the frame's fourteen go slack one after another down the cut like a rope paying out; the horse's legs come free; the driver's reins.
6. **Four heartbeats.** She feels her morning pay out through her hands. Two fingers lifted; the team breaks off — up the ropes, into the gully, gone — in four heartbeats. She is last. She gives him one second of a look over the scarf: not anger; a craftsman seeing her own technique done backwards. Then she goes up the north bank without the half beat.

## Chapter 32 — Road Dust (~5,300)

1. **The bank.** Cael sits on the south bank's lip above the undercut. The notice has been waiting behind his eyes since the reach; he reads it now (verbatim, in the edition's block). He counts.
2. **The bills.** Brom straps Lira's ankle with the coach's spare trace-leather and his own neckcloth; Lira says the count of her bursts (four) before anyone asks; Brom's left arm hangs; his hand goes to the left pocket — the sealed letter still there; Karis wraps her right hand in a wet cloth from the culvert and writes *fourth* in her notebook with her left; the driver walks his horses up and down and talks to them. Cael sees every bill was paid for him and none by him. Seln comes up out of the culvert with an iron pin in his hand: the wagon's linchpin. Nobody saw him all morning. He drops the pin in the ditch.
3. **The log, on the bank.** Hesk's book from his coat, pencil: *Eight. Taken from someone the system sent namelessly, to hold me namelessly…* — a hand and no face — *Write it down anyway.* (Packet fragments kept whole; the connecting clauses mine.)
4. **The coach to Ostrand.** Two hours; the first fixity proposal arrives a mile short of the city at the toll-post's bar — a courteous offer, felt and refused. The city; the wool exchange's side door; the coach in the yard; **four minutes** by Hesk's clock. Lira wants it on the seat's record; Brom asks who could do anything with it; Seln says the driver will tell the stables; Karis: no faces, no names, no paper, five witnesses who are the respondent's own and a coachman in the house's pay, on the morning the seat asked for him in person — "It reads as manufactured theater." Cael agrees last. They go in.
5. **The documentary sitting.** The side-door officer's log; the house's counsel at the door (first mention) looks once at the five and asks only whether anybody needs sending for; no. The five chairs under their grey covers. Lot seventeen. The records in the house's boards read in by contents leaf; Seln attests the copies ("I made them. They are true."); the enrollee attests the figures ("They are mine."); the advocate of record signs left-handed. Havel writes the times and does not look. Jent, at the close, crossing behind their table, courteously: "Roads are poor this month." Cael reads him and finds nothing, as always — counsel, not face.
6. **The room.** Sitting in the long room with chalk on his knees Cael understands it: everything he trusted (text, provisions, attestations, a hood that initials where the clerk points) is one road the system keeps, and it kept the other road that morning through a cut with fourteen anchors in it. The room was never the system. The first pole dies quietly; he does not write a second one.
7. **Home.** The water under the second ring; the bridge open at dusk; the coats at the landing look at Lira's ankle. Withrow in her office hears it standing and writes one line in her own book, not the house's: until somebody gives her a better place for it. Log: *The room was never the system.*

## Chapter 33 — Fixity (~5,300)

1. **Day 83 morning: the postponement.** Seln's fair copy of the week's floor schedule on the boards with the five First-day bookings gone and five on the Fourth-day after next instead, and *Hesk-ward: excused (ribs)* against wing three. Nobody but five people reads it as anything.
2. **The cascade's grammar.** Fixity: the new thing offers, courteously, to fix a point — against a door jamb, a bedpost, a cup on its saucer, the crown of a stair, Brom's shoulder — about forty offers an hour, counted by Karis with a tally on squared paper; each one must be declined; declining costs a little; the offers come fastest at thresholds. Brom's rota: two hours each, Brom, Lira (on the crutch), Karis; somebody in the room always; written on the back of a schedule and nailed to the door.
3. **Storm resettles.** Mid-morning the storm lifts its head for forty minutes: ears closing and opening; the dust in the sill's corner running; the wrong weight in the chest; it settles. After noon a quarter hour more. Lira counts; *bill, not flag*, she says, and then, honestly, that she doesn't know which.
4. **The doorway, twelve seconds.** Afternoon: Lira comes up on the crutch for her shift; at the threshold the doorway fixes — a point against both jambs across the opening; she walks into air that will not give; the crutch's foot skids; Brom counts aloud to twelve; Cael has not asked for it and cannot let it go from outside it; at twelve it lets go on its own. Nobody says anything for a while. This was not priced.
5. **Brom's forge.** Night, Brom's shift. Cael: he measured it wrong; the doorway wasn't on the leaf. Brom tells the forge: his father's forge at the back of the estate; you measure the stock cold, on the floor, with a rule; once it's in the fire the fire does what fire does; nobody blames the rule for the fire. "You've measured. That's the part that's yours." (Never "a farrier's son".)
6. **Day 84, naming.** Toward the second day's morning, a proposal at the window latch; tired, he names the thing instead of refusing it — *latch; it opens* — and the offer goes quiet as if answered. He tries it on the jamb: *door*. On Brom: *Brom*. It works: the new thing stops asking about anything he has named. The idle state is naming, cheap and constant, every threshold, for the rest of his life; doorways never quite stop wanting. Karis's curve: forty, thirty-six, thirty, twenty-two… two days against her nine weeks; each new thing has come in faster than the last; she writes *for now* under the line and draws a box round it. The district seat's answer comes up the hill and says exactly what Karis said a week ago. Log, the price beside the priced: what was priced, what was paid, what was not on the leaf (the doorway).

## Chapter 34 — A Good Room (~5,100)

1. **Day 87 morning.** Rooke at the program board strikes Lira's three bouts with one line each. Cael's ribs on the stair. The eleventh of Sowing is said once (Lira's), never beside a season word; he is seventeen until it and eighteen from it.
2. **The annex, evening.** Karis's long table with every paper turned face-down, squared; the assistant brings up a cake from the kitchen and goes; Brom's spirit kettle; Lira on the window seat with the strapped foot on a stool; Karis's hand unwrapped today, the fingers a shade paler.
3. **Ephram.** He shuts grievances early for the first time in two years and comes; at the table he sees the face-down stacks, reads that they are face-down, and turns his chair to the window with his back to them, deliberately, without a word. His tally: ninety-one entries since the qualifying year, and a gap this week; he isn't asking about any of them; he wanted Cael to know somebody has been paying attention. Who sings: Brom, low, a Velmere harvest song all the way through, nobody joining; Ephram laughs at the last verse.
4. **Seln.** He comes last, stands in the door, looks at the room — the lock, the card, the two windows, the landing — and says, "It is a good room. I have been in a great many." Sits at the middle of the bench. Turns his cup a quarter-turn (the first time since the gate).
5. **Hesk.** Bracken's pouch brought a parcel at noon; Cael opens it here: a satchel of brown leather, a brass buckle Hesk filed himself, a pocket cut to the exact size of Hesk's book. The note (verbatim, Tier A). Cael reads it twice and puts the book in the pocket; it fits.
6. **Lira's three bouts.** When the cake is cut she says them aloud, one by one, the dates and the opponents, as losses, and then "There. Said."
7. **Night, the sill.** Inventory in Hesk's book: eight, the eighth "the hand with no face"; the anomaly under its standing words, *Still open. Still real. Patience.* The satchel on the sill, cold coming through the glass. Seln at the door at the tenth bell, saying only that tomorrow stands. Last log line: *Tomorrow, Seln's annex. Hesk says mind the shape. I think tomorrow is the shape.*

## Guardrails for drafting

- Seln never thinks of a sealed thing, Shadow or the stair; he knows the boy only by counting; the case's content is never stated; he does not open it.
- Seln does not see the reach (he is at the wagon). Nobody outside Lira, Brom and Karis is told the mechanism.
- Havel learns nothing; no inference past his rule; six sheets, the last.
- The woman: unnamed, scarf to the eyes, known by placement; never "he". The team: no faces, no names, no paper. No third move afterwards.
- Anchor: the unbinding (deliberate) and the doorway (uncommanded, twelve seconds). Nothing else. No hold.
- The fragment's reach, rate and price stay blank on the page except what the notice says.
- No season or month words; the birthday named once as the eleventh of Sowing; feet and yards.
- Reader Standard: no oaths; injuries without gore.
