## Ch14 — Vastin (cutaway, ~5k; his POV only; no age; "forty years"; "the Archmarshal")

1. Morning, day 41. The high room in the Compact's house: the too-steep stair, the porter and his paper bag in the paved court, the long cabinet of shallow drawers, the seal-press he screwed on crooked his first morning nineteen years ago, three trays (in, held, done). The left hand stiff — it used to object only to mornings; now it objects at both ends of the day. In the in-tray, between a frame-maintenance return from a river station and a leave request from a district clerk: a bundle with a printed distribution slip — *for the evaluator's file, per standing distribution*. Information, not a question. Every paper about this boy for three years came to him asking; this one tells. Inside: the instrument, the house's answer (its index only), the seat's notice, and the first sitting's minute (five evaluation chairs, "to be called"). The sitting has already happened. He learns of the proceedings after they have begun.
2. He reads the dull items first (his rule: interesting things last, so his judgment on the dull ones stays honest). Then the instrument at his own pace. Paragraph seven: his own three sentences, quoted whole and "adopted". He feels it as a signature he never wrote. He knows the corridor's architecture because he has walked two people down it: chapter fourteen triggered twice by findings of his, both correct. A clerk who sold sponsorship seals; an assessor paid to alter a station entry. The second man's daughter wrote to him months after, asking if her father's name would ever come off the list. He answered: no. He looks at the court for exactly as long as he allows, and stops.
3. He takes the boy's file from his own drawer, not from memory. His portion: nine leaves. The clerk's ruled column: eleven questions, numbered; the minutes down the margin; the boy's one question back (what is being evaluated, him or the provision). The twelfth: what the boy's private records say. He left it because asking would have been collecting, not evaluating, and he made a promise in a rainy corridor long ago. Still right. He notices that his rightness leaves the proceedings nothing to use except his three sentences, which they have already used.
4. He drafts a petition for standing: the senior evaluator who personally assessed the subject asks leave to submit a finding. Two provisions, both old; the older one from before the standardizing, which uses *shall receive*. He cites both; a petition on one leg deserves to fall. He sets out his history from memory: four supplementary findings in forty years, four granted, each received and weighed — one decisive against the petitioning district's own case, and the district wrote to thank him. He predicts: standing within days, a finding entered at the next sitting.
5. The clerk at the dispatch ledger hesitates at the routing column; Vastin supplies the route ("Registry seat, Ostrand. Proceedings. Institutional."). The clerk has never sent paper from this desk *toward* a proceeding. Vastin notes the hesitation and what it says.
6. Day 43: a junior evaluator from the northern district brings a hard file — a station's Kindling entry against a young practitioner whose shown work outruns it. They go through it for an hour; inside tolerance; growth faster than the station guessed; *continue observation*. The junior leaves comforted. Vastin keeps the copy on the blotter after, reads one line too many times, puts it in "held", takes it out, puts it back. No word for it; it is not yet a thing. [plant; unnamed]
7. Day 45, the fourth morning: the clerk's tread on the stair is wrong — single steps from the bottom, no pause at the door. Seat courier, not the post. Impressed seal, not inked (a grade of reply). [P] *The proceedings' evaluation seat is fully constituted. Supplementary findings are not contemplated by the schedule.* He reads it standing, sits, reads it twice more; on the third he hears it as a sentence he could have written — he has written hundreds of its cousins, closing doors cleanly so the person outside has nothing to hold. Good craft; among his best. Now aimed at him. No provision cited. It cost its sender nothing. "Fully constituted" — a seat he was not asked to sit on was full before he asked. He puts the sheet in *held* — no; in the boy's file.
8. He writes the finding anyway that evening, in his own hand, the addressee line left empty. The building's lamp rule (the floor's lamps out at the sixth bell). The clerk's one sentence in nineteen years that is not about the work — his own question (I'll give him: whether the Archmarshal would like the window shut, the pigeons are loud — no: a question that touches the man: "Will you be going home tonight, sir?"). Candle fetched and entered on the consumables under Vastin's name. The finding: the standard protocol cannot measure this subject; its silence is the instrument's limit, not the subject's fault; a re-evaluation whose result is known in advance is a procedure dressed as an evaluation; the honest instrument available is the demonstration record. The hardest page: the instruments are not at fault — the same sentence he has defended all his life for frames, now for a boy. Last paragraph: the schedule's purpose unknown to him; an evaluation that starts from its conclusion evaluates the evaluator. Nine pages. Into his own drawer in the cabinet of shallow drawers, under a heading he never needed before: [P] *Pending a proceeding worth sending it to.*

## Ch15 — Sideways (day 44)

Log: they're buying the question, not an answer; that market outlives the ruling.

She heard at noon in Ostrand, from seat clerks talking at the inn by the wharfs: the Archmarshal petitioned for standing to submit a finding; refused; two sentences; no provision cited. She tells Withrow in her office; Withrow has Cael sent for to hear it. Withrow asks for the sentence twice. Then: she has written that sentence; every registry officer of her grade has; she taught it to new clerks as the courteous way to refuse — saying no without pretending to weigh. She now has to think about what she taught. To Cael: tell the table; tell them I said *choosing* again.
3. Cael's arithmetic on the stair: the refusal was written in Ostrand this morning and the man it refuses is still waiting for it on a road somewhere; the house has heard first. [own beat]
4. The annex: Karis already has the two provisions open (only two exist; one older than the standardizing). She reads them aloud before anyone talks. The older says *shall receive*. Her count of their use: a handful of times in the record, granted every time. "He cited the right law. They answered without citing any." Not a ruling — a schedule declining to contain one.
5. The counsel, gloves on, at the door: she will not have them think the Archmarshal is theirs; he is an officer of the Compact refused by an office of the Compact. What she gives them: in twenty-six years she has been refused often and refused without a citation only a few times, and each time the office had already decided and would not write down why. Write the citation down when you get one. [P] "Its absence is a finding."
6. Supper, the residence table: nobody says much. Seln, at the bench's middle, cup turned: [P] "They benched their best instrument because he measures true." Brom looks at him a long moment. On the stair after, unasked (rare): Brom says Seln meant the Archmarshal — and himself too: fifteen years, the best they had, set on a shelf by his own true reports. Cael writes it down; so does Karis (she was on the stair above).
7. Log: Vastin warned the channels would change; they changed on him too. The proceedings didn't dispute his finding — they declined to read it. The only two credentialed opinions ever made about me, both set aside. They are learning to send people I can't read and to put away the ones I can.
