## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately on “The porter was late with the pigeons.” The ordinary irregularity is a clean warning that something institutional has shifted, and Vastin’s realization that his own exact language has been turned against him gives the opening chapter a complete emotional and moral arc. The strongest hook is not whether the law is technically valid; it is whether conscientious people can do anything once procedure has been designed to absorb conscientious objections.

Interest slipped briefly in the middle of chapter 15’s pamphlet calculation and again across the successive sorting, archive-query, and letter scenes in chapters 16–19. Each scene contributes a new fact, but several produce it through a similar rhythm: paper arrives, a careful person reads it exactly, the group names what it means, and Cael records the conclusion. The accumulation is purposeful and ultimately pays off in the five beans, but the sameness of delivery slightly reduces surprise before that payoff.

Cael matters most because his governing belief is visibly changing. He begins by treating the threat as something he can measure, document, and personally pay for; the movement forces him to see costs arriving first for Lira, Brom, Karis, the house, and Hesk. Lira is the strongest attachment point beside him. Her “That doesn’t make it fair. / No. It makes it mine” turns an imposed loss of daylight into earned tactical knowledge without pretending the loss was good. Vastin also matters immediately despite occupying only the opening chapter: his unused finding is a convincing act of integrity precisely because it does not rescue anyone.

I expect the remaining sittings to order the standard re-evaluation while the pre-restriction inventory closes around Cael’s relationships. I also expect Ilsev, Jent, and perhaps Vastin to constrain how the machine acts without being able to stop it, and I expect Cael’s private, unclassified capability to become strategically incompatible with continued concealment. I would continue. The movement ends on a genuine change in comprehension—Cael is “counting doors”—rather than merely postponing a verdict.

## Overall judgment

This is a compelling movement with a clear dramatic shape: exclusion of an honest evaluator, widening collateral surveillance, discovery of the pre-verdict machinery, a private truthful measurement, and a public ruling that validates everything while advancing the threat. Its best achievement is making paperwork suspenseful without pretending paperwork is action in disguise. The records matter because every record changes who can act, what can be denied, and which relationship will pay.

The main risk is cumulative sameness. Many characters share a professional devotion to exactness, and many scenes reach their turn through a document being read, correctly interpreted, and restated in a sharpened aphorism. Those repetitions are thematic, but a light differentiation of scene rhythm would make the five-bean and “Both” payoffs strike even harder. The physical sequences should remain at their present developed length; they are the movement’s clearest relief from—and embodiment of—the procedural pressure.

## Repair brief

### 1. Rebalance the middle accumulation without removing evidence

- **Location:** Chapter 15, the pamphlet-market calculation, through chapter 16’s post baskets, observer log, fee-office query, and cost column.
- **Observed issue:** Several consecutive scenes use the same progression: a paper or ledger appears, a careful reader extracts its implication, and Cael records a concise conclusion. Each fact is useful, but the repeated delivery rhythm briefly flattens escalation.
- **Effect on the reader:** A younger reader may feel the story pausing for proof; an adult reader may anticipate the rhetorical turn before the characters reach it, reducing the surprise of Seln’s beans.
- **Proposed scope:** Make a light scene-level cadence pass only. Compress duplicated procedural setup or let one implication remain unglossed until the bean scene; do not remove the gallery log, fee-office query, Brom letter, or cost column, because all four are needed for the later pattern.
- **Strength to preserve:** The concrete administrative objects and the principle that the threat becomes frightening through lawful, individually ordinary acts.

### 2. Give the chapter 20 argument one more human navigation marker

- **Location:** Chapter 20, from Jent’s four numbered pieces through the counsel’s TEXT / SHELF / PREAMBLE / NAMES response and the noon ruling.
- **Observed issue:** The logic is sound, but dense clusters of institutional terms make the local question—what exactly has just been conceded, and what remains dangerous—harder to retain on one pass, especially for the thirteen-year-old lens.
- **Effect on the reader:** The reader can understand the final paradox while losing some of the tactical pleasure of how each advocate constructed it.
- **Proposed scope:** Add or sharpen two brief Cael-level orientation beats at major transitions, phrased in consequences rather than definitions. Preserve the full hearing and its four-part architecture; this needs clearer handrails, not a shorter argument.
- **Strength to preserve:** Jent’s voluntary withdrawal of a false overreach, the steel-rule choreography, and the devastating two-paragraph ruling.

### 3. Correct the precision glitches locally

- **Location:** Chapter 15, “three other towns” versus Brom’s “other two towns”; chapter 20, the sentence ending “*Valid: held. To proceed: held. Both.*.”
- **Observed issue:** The market arithmetic appears to drop one town, and the italicized quotation is followed by a second full stop.
- **Effect on the reader:** Both are small, but this movement teaches the reader to trust exact counts, exact wording, and exact records, so tiny accounting and punctuation errors attract disproportionate notice.
- **Proposed scope:** Resolve the intended number of towns with a one-phrase correction and delete the redundant terminal punctuation. Recheck only the nearby calculation and sentence, not the surrounding scenes.
- **Strength to preserve:** The manuscript’s pleasure in verifiable figures and the clipped finality of “Both.”
