# M2 working event list (author's own notes; drafted from this and BOOK_MAP, not from the source page)

Calendar (Halcenvane weekdays; day 1 = First-day): day 8 First-day served; procedure night into day 9. Day 9 Second-day … day 12 Fifth-day … day 14 Seventh-day … day 19 Fifth-day … day 22 First-day = the answer's last day. Weather: heat going off; one night of rain (day 17); dust. No season, month or English weekday words. Feet/yards.

Ch8 Keel (days 9–12)
- Day 9, small hours → morning. Cael wakes late; the copy on the sill. Breakfast: Karis already gone up. The lumber room across the reading-room landing is being cleared by two porters on Bracken's order — a table for the assistant, shelves for the code. The house starts calling the pair of rooms "the annex" by noon (Ephram's word, to the cohort).
- On Karis's table: a bound sheaf with no note — every paragraph of the instrument against every provision it leans on, in a copying hand. Karis knows whose. "Thank him." "He'd hate that." She uses it.
- Her rules for four days: Brom carries the code volumes and the press's rulings up from the records hall twice a day (and back at night, because the press must be locked). Lira takes over the second-years' meal rota sheet from Ephram and turns it into a door rota for the records-hall steps, where the intake has started to gather. Cael reads every draft.
- The keel: Greyvane's ruling and transcript laid first, before anything; she explains by Hesk's fiddle? (No — by a boat on a slipway: you lay the keel, then ribs.) The address: she writes the answer's heading to the same address the instrument used — *Caelen Hesk-ward* — because you answer a paper in its own words. Cael watches his registry name go down in her hand.
- Day 10: ribs. Three routes to the clause's survival laid side by side (her own finding from Greyvane, the house counsel's reconstruction, Prynn's certified copy and her note). Each could stand alone; none is asked to. Brom up the stair at noon with volume two on his shoulder like a sack of meal.
- Day 10 evening: she hands Cael one leaf out of order: the renewals table (the grant at Greyvane; the first renewal at Halcenvane before the inspection delegation; the mill-town renewal). Three rows. "Read that first. Tonight." No reason given.
- Day 11 (third day): Cael at the window with the table and the old volume of the Log (full, in the drawer). The first renewal row carries the panel's day. He logged the panel's day, and nine days later the day Gault sealed the minute, after the inspection's officer countersigned it. The enrollment book took the panel's day; the sealed minute went up the road; only his log has both. "Nine days." She asks for the log, reads both entries, takes the table out and rebuilds it in front of him with the sealing days and a note — an hour she doesn't have. He: a note would have done. She: the petitioner's clerk finds it, one line, and the seat stops believing the other leaves. "There is no such thing as a small error in a document about a person." Then: she gave him the table early, out of order, because he was in both rooms — the one witness who can't be replaced. He realizes she let him find it; she had found it herself on the first morning (her pencil dot in the margin, faint).
- Day 12 Fifth-day: wing three, a short bout at the band (a Mire, Iron R4; figure twenty-four and two-thirds; the rib gone). Ephram's pencil. One paragraph of cost: the band as a daily expense, priced in a week when the house is spending its whole credit on his name.
- Day 12 night: bones. Karis comes to supper and says one word: "Bones." Brom passes the beans. Lira: "And the rest?" "Seven days."

Ch9 Eleven Pounds (days 13–19)
- Day 13: the integrity section. Karis writes forty pages in one night (the reading room lamp; the oil book again, the reading room this time in Bracken's hand). Morning: every recorded invocation of the integrity provisions, ninety-one; eighty-four against frames, stations, assessors, panels, records; seven touching a person, and all seven about something a person did, never what he was. Number sixty-something is Bracken's own fourth-year filing on the Ostrand frame. She shows him his own name on the list at the records-hall counter. Bracken reads his own old sentence, says nothing, then: "I spelled 'casing' wrong." She: "I corrected it in the citation. Silently." He: "Don't." He wants his error in the record as it was. She restores it with *(sic)*. Small warm beat.
- The house's counsel up the hill with her rule; reads the integrity section backward; finds one thing (a word — "never" where "not once in the record" is what can be proved); Karis takes it.
- Karis on the forty pages: the best thing she has written; she wants that minuted; she also knows it is worth exactly what a record is worth. Cael: "Both entered." Karis notices he has the counsel's habit; he says somebody has to keep the house's minute.
- She hands him the last page first. The habit since the road.
- Days 15–17: Bracken reads every leaf over two nights, initialling each, lamps in the oil book. Rain on day 17, the first of the year. Weighing at the counter on day 18: Bracken's grocer's scale from the press? — the post-room scale, brass weights; eleven pounds four ounces, written in his private ledger.
- Withrow at the counter: the registry's ordinary post would carry it; she declines. Under seal, under escort. "They sent theirs at a walk so the hill would watch it come. We'll send ours at a walk so the town watches it arrive." The house's escort: the head groom and his son on the two greys, Bracken's senior clerk in the middle with the case strapped across the saddle — her own hill's people, in the house's colours.
- Day 19, Fifth-day, three days inside the time: it goes down the switchbacks. Cael watches from the covered walk. The coats at the road's foot watch it go by and do not turn their heads to follow it. Cael's log: the best document anyone has ever written about him; he believes her; he believes the corridor; both in one hand.
- Day 19 evening: Lira at the north wall with two mugs. Wants Brom's word explained (Brom has said it at the frames, under his breath). Cael gives it in small pieces: six slips; one-way doors; chapter fourteen; Withrow's ferry. Lira: Fenmark — the examination hall, three examiners, a slate; every line on her form true; the room wrong. She had no word for both. "Corridor" is the word, four years near enough looking. She got out by walking into Vell's yard. "There's usually a yard out the back." She leaves him the mug. Log: she's counting exits already; the rest of us learned in libraries.

Ch10 Per Seal (days 23–25)
- Day 23: the seat's notice comes up in the noon bag. Text (my own): proceedings convened at the registry seat at Ostrand; sittings on the seat's Fifth-days upon the schedule of the academic year; the procedural conference on the coming Fourth-day; the first sitting on the fifth Fifth-day of the year; the evaluation seat to be empanelled from the district's senior evaluators; the procurator of record for the petitioner, Jent; the presiding seat: per seal.
- Jent: the name on the instrument's last leaf; Bracken still doesn't know it. Bracken on "per seal": a seat may sit by its seal and not its holder for proceedings the registry designates institutional; he has read the section, never seen it used, in thirteen years. Cael: so a seal rules on me. Bracken: whoever holds it that morning; the record shows only the seal.
- Karis: "They've filed the face out of it." (Cael says it? — Cael writes it; Karis reads it upside down and says it aloud, approving.) Log: every man who ever decided about him had a face he could read. This one has declined, by procedure, to have one.
- Day 25, Fourth-day, first light: the coach. Withrow's carriage ahead. In the coach: the five and the house's counsel. Lira with the drill-book unopened; Brom forearms on knees; Karis with the summary. Seln with his own copy of the cross-index on his knee, saying nothing. At the ferry Karis warns: he'll concede the whole answer in the first minute; it will feel like winning; it isn't.
- Ostrand: across the bridge (the ferry for the coach), up the market street; the wool exchange — four steps, the plinth, lanolin, lot numbers cut in the old selling floor; a new side door being hung by a joiner for the sittings. The conference in a district office room at the back: table, sigil, a window down the market street to the wharfs.
- Jent already there, rising. (Mine:) a modest man in a brown coat with mended cuffs, a travelling inkwell he unscrews and screws shut, spectacles on a cord. Greets the counsel by name, Withrow by title, Karis as "Advocate". The schedule confirmed. Exhibit lists across: the house's runs to a hundred and seventy-three items; he initials receipt without reading past the first page; his own runs to nine. Cael reads the nine upside down: every one a published text — none to prove a fact, all to be cited.
- Jent touches the answer with two fingers; read it twice; "the best-documented enrollment I've reviewed in thirty years. Genuinely." To Karis. Karis thanks him (she never thanks for compliments) — because she sees what comes next.
- Cael's four measures on him: hands (still, square on the leaf), breath (even), weight in the chair (the read laid lightly along the boards — settled, never shifting), the half-beat before a costly answer (none). Four nulls.
- Jent: none of it is in dispute; concedes the documentation and the provision; the petition turns on integrity and remedy, matters of discretion; he will argue them on the schedule. Offers to put the whole answer in the record, not the summary: it is better law than the summary. Rises: "Chancellor. Counsel. Advocate." To Cael: "Hesk-ward." Leaves on time.
- Coach back: the counsel to the window — not there to argue; to conduct; she has never lost to a man who didn't want to win, and isn't sure she has met one. Seln, eyes shut: "You've met hundreds. They're called clerks." — the counter joke. Lira laughs.
- Log that night: Coss wanted; Vastin wanted to measure; Umber wanted a true mark; every one had an appetite and the appetite was where you got in. This one has none to read. "He's a hand. Somebody else is the arm."

Ch11 Choosing (days 25–28)
- Day 25 evening: Withrow's office. Door shut. "Sit." No reassurance. The ledger, two columns, registry register. The respondent's standing under the same seal; an adverse finding enters the charter file. What fills the column (mine): third on the continent; the program's charter; Karis's line in the accounts; the intake bigger than any in years; the house's name in other people's books.
- She brings the charter file from the case under the framed charter: buff folder, the house's life in leaves; opens it toward him (not a thing she must do). The inspections, two old adverse notes before her time each with remedy and clearance; and the new entry dated the eighth day, remedy column blank. The entry stays whatever the ruling; the best outcome is the blank column one day saying *sustained*. She shows the leaf because he doesn't believe descriptions.
- The other column: she read the Greyvane transcript as a registry officer looking for the seam and found none; she has read this paper and the corridor and the law is on his side on every point that can be argued, and Karis is right about the doors. "I am choosing to keep believing that matters. Make a note of the word *choosing*." He does, in her section of the notebook. His note: not a legal position; a person deciding what she'll have been.
- "Go and train." (Mine:) "And stand up straight in the coach. The town watches the coach."
- Days 26–27: the year's geometry, from the boards and Bracken's counter: sittings on the seat's Fifth-days; Bracken counts eleven if the year runs honest; the ruling before the year's close. Lira's program: the first meet off the hill in six weeks (the confluence card). Brom's review: the last Fifth-day of the year at the district seat. Karis lays the two dockets side by side: the same day. "The registry isn't ironic. It's tidy. That's worse." The re-evaluation: *date to be set upon the seat's ruling* — a blank with a hinge.
- Day 26 (Fifth-day) wing three, one line: the band, a Blade, twenty-two and a third.
- Day 27 dusk, north wall: he places the coats. Older because the junior work — learning the hill — is done. What's left is the grade that waits. Log: "They're waiting for a status change." The paper will make one.
- Day 27 Sixth-day night: the honest floor, Lira's ask: she has a trial in three days against the Force third-year and she wants a floor that moves under her. Cael lays lanes across her path for her to cross on the four-count; Brom stands at the far end as the body. Each lane costs Cael ten counts slow. She learns to cross leaning air on the empty third count. Third lane: Cael's ten counts stretch (he loses count; Lira counts for him: twelve). She stops it. Writes *twelve on the third*. She takes away: cross on the fourth, not the third. Brom: "He'll laugh at you." Lira: "Let him."

Ch12 Admit (days 30–33)
- Day 30 (Second-day), second floor? No — the east hall, the program's own floor. Rooke's trial rule stated once: four exchanges; the rating table gives each exchange a flag or none; most flags takes it; level, the table's figure decides. The Stone at the rating table with a card; Rooke's clerk with the red book. Gallery full — first rated bout under the house's own seal on that floor. Cael at the rail with Brom and Karis; Ephram on the stair; the assistant.
- The third-year: big, cheerful, laughs; a builder — sets Force into the floor in courses, a settle in the knees before each; each course a band of boards that move; he walks behind them.
- E1: she gives it away on purpose — runs straight, eats a course, is shoved off her line, flag to him; she's counting: his settle comes on his own fourth count, every course. Gallery groans. Rooke writes nothing.
- E2: the four-count; the lie on her second count makes him set his course where she'll be; the empty third; on the fourth she's through, over boards that moved a beat ago; touch on his shoulder; flag hers. Gallery noise. Rooke writes one word.
- E3: he adapts — sets courses faster, two-count; floor everywhere moving; she spends bursts, gets caught on a course, recovers off a frame-post; long exchange; she takes it at the east end with the frames a pace behind her heel, by doing nothing on the third count for a beat longer than he can believe. Flag hers. Bursts: four.
- E4: he builds a wall of courses and stands behind it; she needs a fifth burst to clear the last band; she spends it knowing. Clean; she closes him at range — palm to sternum on empty floor. Flag hers. Three to one.
- The Stone's card: execution ten, effect ten, control seven: twenty-seven. Rooke reads the seven aloud: the fifth burst, and the fourth.
- Rooke's invoice at the rail, standing: five against the book's three; her whole day's budget on boards in one bout; landing leg, hip; two days at half work, no frames. Cheap today; dear on a road with three bouts in four days.
- The third-year at the rail with his kit: how long has she had the four-count? Since the second week; at speed since the sixth. Why not open with it? To learn whether his course sets on his count every time or only when he leads. "Every time." He takes it away to fix.
- Lira to Cael at the rail, collar dark, tapes off: the old pride; "I'm better now. I can admit I want it." Karis on the walk back: she used *admit*; enter it in the permanent section of the notebook, not the working pages. Cael writes it at the rail where Lira can see; she lets him.
- Brom's envelopes, the same week. First: at Bracken's counter, the district seat's confirmation of the docket — the panel constituted, the season's record entered — and at the foot *outcome anticipated: advancement, per title*. Bracken reads *anticipated* aloud: a district never writes that; somebody decided ink was cheaper than pretending. Brom folds it into the left inside pocket.
- Second: the evening post, Velmere's wax. Up to his room. Two days on the small table beside the docket, squared together; the lamp moved off them. Lira reports it at supper as logistics; she went past the door twice a day (the stair), not in. "I'd like that noted. Not on paper." Her rule: when he opens it, nobody asks. Karis: she wasn't going to ask; she'd sit where he could tell her; learned it from him.
- Third evening he opens it alone. He comes down to supper. "The face of a man being offered his whole ledger back." Cael files it, does not ask. Margin note: the number and the family in one fortnight; Brom has said "Noted" to neither.

Ch13 The Hand (days 31–35)
- Day 31: Karis spends the budget for the first time: a request to Greyvane's archive for certified copies of the founding compilation's oldest signatures, under the house's seal with a budget line; Bracken enters the line; Prynn's people slow and exact; before the midyear recess. Karis writes the date in her front matter and leaves space under it.
- The assistant's fourth error of the month at the annex table: Karis hands her the volume — "Find it." Twenty minutes, jaw set. "Yes." Pink. Cael's log: everything the house gave her honestly she spends honestly.
- Four evenings (days 29–32): Ephram and Brom at the old striking frames on the second floor, side by side, a hair apart. Wordless. Ephram stops to watch Brom's reset; Brom stops to watch Ephram's entry line. Cael at the rail watches; Lira comes once and watches with him; nobody speaks. (Keep the whole scene without a spoken word.)
- Day 32 night: Karis convenes the annex, the four, one lamp: what tomorrow is — a form filled in by people in a room. Seat receives both; rules on nothing; confirms the schedule. "Watch the seal."
- Day 33 Fifth-day, the first sitting. Coach at the foot of the market street at the half-bell. Thirty-one people on the four steps (counted from the coach window): correspondents with folding boards, Ostrand's paper-readers, two guild officers. The side door with its new hinges; the officer with a ruled board (mine: young, a heavy cold, wipes his nose with a folded handkerchief between names, apologizes to nobody). Reads the list: Chancellor; counsel; documentary support; institutional attendance (Brom, Lira); records support (Seln). Then: "The enrollee of record." "Present." Name for the log? The counsel: enter him as the instrument has him. Name column: *Caelen Hesk-ward*. Standing column: *the enrollee of record, unclassified*. Eleven seconds by Cael's count. He files them beside Pellin's brass plate.
- The selling floor: lot numbers cut in the boards; sigil on the end wall; presiding seat on a low platform; evaluation seat (empty, chairs covered); records table below the platform: a senior records officer and, on rotation behind him, a junior Cael has seen at Ardenmere, Greyvane and the bluff. Parties' tables. Forty benches full.
- The seal: a registrar in senior grey with the seat's hood drawn forward; Cael reads hands and posture for forty minutes: nothing managed because nothing to manage. The clerk reads the seal's designation into the minute; no name.
- Seln at the gallery's end, where the sightlines meet.
- HAVEL CUTAWAY (~1.5k): his rotation sheet: records table, junior, seat two; the duties in the rotation manual's order: the minute's times, exhibits' receipt, the door log cross-checked. The five sheets between the second and third manuals in his kit (left at the inn; he never brings them to a sitting). His orders on the marker (stand down; not his clearance). He writes the times: petition entered, nine minutes; answer entered, twenty-three. He sees the seal's impression on the minute — the seat's own, ordinary, the one on ten thousand minutes. He does not look at the respondent's table, because the card says ordinary courtesy and no more, and because at Greyvane he looked twice. Learns nothing; at the inn that night he takes the sheets out, reads all five, puts them back, writes nothing. A sixth would need a fact; he has none.
- Back in Cael: Withrow rises with the seal and inclines her head to the registry's measured depth. Coach: "I have bowed to that seat from the other side of it more times than I can count. Today I bowed from this side. Note that I bowed anyway."
- Coach council: the counsel (immaculate; the seat received both as if they weighed the same); Karis (they do, to a seal); Brom (Havel didn't look at you — he did at Greyvane, twice; I counted); Lira (then he's stopped learning too); Seln, eyes shut: three grades of officer in one room — district at the senior chair, monitoring on rotation, seat-grade under the hood — and none of them is a grade that decides. "The office has sat in rooms like that. From the other chair."
- Log: Greyvane had Coss's face, the inspection Vastin's, this a seal. "I keep looking for the person and finding the hand."
- Day 35, the month's close at the window, the docket dates on the sill (Lira's meet; Brom's review; the next sitting; the blank). Entry: everyone holding something the system finally gave them honestly — Lira's ladder, Brom's number, Karis's shelf. "Whatever this challenge costs me, it should not cost them." Karis at his shoulder with a draft, reads it (she doesn't do that; he lets her). "That is not how any of us are built." He writes the parenthesis verbatim. Reads it back to her. She: afraid of it, good; be afraid of it accurately — dates, a column, a total — so that on the day it starts costing them, you'll know it was that day. That's what a notebook is for.
