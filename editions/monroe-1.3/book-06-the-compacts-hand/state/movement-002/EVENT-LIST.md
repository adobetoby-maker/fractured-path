Ch8 Keel (days 9–12)
- Day 9, small hours → morning. Cael wakes late; the copy on the sill. Breakfast: Karis already gone up. The lumber room across the reading-room landing is being cleared by two porters on Bracken's order — a table for the assistant, shelves for the code. The house starts calling the pair of rooms "the annex" by noon (Ephram's word, to the cohort).
- On Karis's table: a bound sheaf with no note — every paragraph of the instrument against every provision it leans on, in a copying hand. Karis knows whose. "Thank him." "He'd hate that." She uses it.
- Her rules for four days: Brom carries the code volumes and the press's rulings up from the records hall twice a day (and back at night, because the press must be locked). Lira takes over the second-years' meal rota sheet from Ephram and turns it into a door rota for the records-hall steps, where the intake has started to gather. Cael reads every draft.
- The keel: Greyvane's ruling and transcript laid first, before anything; she explains by Hesk's fiddle? (No — by a boat on a slipway: you lay the keel, then ribs.) The address: she writes the answer's heading to the same address the instrument used — *Caelen Hesk-ward* — because you answer a paper in its own words. Cael watches his registry name go down in her hand.
- Day 10: ribs. Three routes to the clause's survival laid side by side (her own finding from Greyvane, the house counsel's reconstruction, Prynn's certified copy and her note). Each could stand alone; none is asked to. Brom up the stair at noon with volume two on his shoulder like a sack of meal.
- Day 10 evening: she hands Cael one leaf out of order: the renewals table (the grant at Greyvane; the first renewal at Halcenvane, the semester evaluation under the inspection; the mill-town renewal). Three rows. "Tonight. First." No reason given.
- That night: Cael remembers the evaluation room by its east wall — four chairs: the delegation's counsel with her ribbons, Ilsev with a pencil, Havel with a board, the Archmarshal with nothing in his hands; three hours of instruments; Gault's note; the clerk's stamp at the trestle, house copy stamped then and there. Then Bracken at the counter: the delegation's counsel had asked that the registry's own copy go up under the delegation's countersignature. Nine days later Gault called him in to initial that file copy, come back sealed for the purpose, before it went up again in the noon bag; the house kept only Bracken's book line. The old Log: the long entry for the evaluation day, and a two-line entry nine days on.
- Day 11: Cael brings the old Log up. Karis's dot: the house book dates the first renewal by the trestle; the petition's schedule two (which lists the file's standing entries) dates it by the file copy's seal, nine days later. Her old table had the house date only. She rebuilds in silence (an hour she hasn't got): two dates per row, trestle and seal, and a line of sources. He thinks a footnote would have done; she reads it on his face. She lays schedule two beside the old table: "Be the seat. Which of us is careless?" — then the protected line. Then why him: everyone else in that room is a name on a paper now; he's the one witness who can't be replaced, and witnesses see more when they don't know what they're looking for. He realizes she used him as a source, like a volume, and doesn't mind.
- Day 12 Fifth-day: wing three, a short bout at the band (a Mire, Iron R4; figure twenty-four and two-thirds; the rib gone). Ephram's pencil. One paragraph of cost: the band as a daily expense, priced in a week when the house is spending its whole credit on his name.
- Day 12 night: bones. Karis comes to supper and says one word: "Bones." Brom passes the beans. Lira: "And the rest?" "Seven days, with Bracken and the scales inside them."

Ch9 Eleven Pounds (days 13–19)
- Day 13: the integrity section. Karis writes forty pages in one night (the reading room lamp; the oil book again, the reading room this time in Bracken's hand). Morning: every recorded invocation of the integrity provisions, ninety-one; eighty-four against frames, stations, assessors, panels, records; seven touching a person, and all seven about something a person did, never what he was. Number sixty-something is Bracken's own fourth-year filing on the Ostrand frame. She shows him his own name on the list at the records-hall counter. Bracken reads his own old sentence, says nothing, then: "I spelled 'casing' wrong." She: "I corrected it in the citation. Silently." He: "Don't." He wants his error in the record as it was. She restores it with *(sic)*. Small warm beat.
- The house's counsel up the hill with her rule; reads the integrity section backward; finds one thing (a word — "never" where "not once in the record" is what can be proved); Karis takes it.
- After the counsel leaves, Karis doesn't speak; she writes two lines on a slip (the best thing she's written; worth what a record's worth) and pushes it across for his notebook, so it goes down without being asked for. He copies it. When he looks up she has turned the forty pages over so the last leaf is on top for him, and is at the window.
- Days 15–17: Bracken reads every leaf over two nights, initialling each, lamps in the oil book. Rain on day 17, the first of the year. Weighing at the counter on day 18: Bracken's grocer's scale from the press? — the post-room scale, brass weights; eleven pounds four ounces, written in his private ledger.
- Withrow comes into the post room while the weights come off. Bracken offers the noon bag. She names riders instead: they were served by three men at a walk; the house answers by three. The head groom and his son on the greys, Bracken's senior clerk in the middle with the wallet across his saddle. Bracken: three days inside the time. Withrow: she'd like them to notice that too.
- Day 19, Fifth-day, three days inside the time: it goes down the switchbacks. Cael watches from the covered walk. The coats at the road's foot watch it go by and do not turn their heads to follow it. Cael's log: the best document anyone has ever written about him; he believes her; he believes the corridor; both in one hand.
- Day 19 evening: Lira along the top of the wall with bread. Brom is wearing out a word at the frames. Cael tells it backward (the morning after first, then chapter fourteen, then the slips). Lira asks whether the corridor has an outside. Then Fenmark: the slate, the examiner wiping it with his sleeve so the form would be clean; every word on the form correct and the room wrong; she had only "angry", which is all one size. Then the road: a year of guild doors (six asked, two walked to), and then Ardenmere, and a noise like a chain on a boiler, and Vell's yard — nobody sent her, it was round the back of things. "There's usually a yard out the back." One line of log, no gloss.

Ch10 Per Seal (days 23–25)
- Day 23: Cael comes to the counter late; Karis is already reading the notice upside down and Bracken's finger is resting on its last line. Order on the page: the last line first (*per seal*), then the name (Jent — the last leaf's name, still nobody Bracken knows), then the schedule (sittings on the seat's Fifth-days; conference day 25; first sitting the sixth Fifth-day; evaluators to be called). Karis, who has the second volume nearly by heart now, explains the section; Bracken only confirms with his counter years. Cael doesn't ask who will rule; Bracken answers it anyway: he'll want to know who, so will Bracken, and the minute will tell neither. Karis: very well done; she hates it.
- Log: "They've filed the face out of it." The one roll-call of faces (Pellin, Coss, Vastin, the provost, Umber), each read across a room. No hand image here.
- Day 25: open at the bridge, the coach stuck behind a barrel cart; Karis tells a Greyvane story (an advocate who gave her a point at a sitting; she was pleased all evening and lost the next morning). "When he agrees with us, don't nod." Lira: where should we look? At what he keeps. Then who is in the coach: Seln with his copy of the sheaf that the wing's register says doesn't exist; Withrow and the counsel ahead in her carriage.
- The wool exchange: lanolin; a joiner hanging a new side door in a newly cut wall, an officer watching the hinge. The counting room up a short stair.
- Conference, entered by an object: the screw-lid inkwell being screwed down before the man is looked at; then his size (not big; brown coat, mended cuffs, spectacles on a cord). Greetings: the counsel by a name Cael doesn't catch, Withrow as Chancellor, Karis as "Advocate" — Karis's pen stops; Cael thinks it fits better than any title the house has given her. The clerk's dull schedule. Jent weighs the house's list in one hand, reads its index leaf, initials. His single leaf: the code extracts first, then the rest; every item already in Seln's sheaf's right-hand column; none can be cross-examined; all to be cited. The compliment (protected) and one line on the renewals table only. Karis says "Thank you" at once.
- The read first: Cael lets it run under the table to Jent's weight in his chair — settled like a sack, unmoved. Then hands, breath, the half-beat (none). Four nothings.
- Jent: he won't trouble the seat with the clause or the papers; the house is right about both. He'll trouble it with the registry's discretion: whether there's an integrity question, and what's to be done. Asks that the whole answer go in. Leaves: "Chancellor. Counsel. Advocate." / "Hesk-ward."
- Coach back: Lira asks the counsel what she makes of him; the counsel kept waiting for him to want something. Seln, eyes shut: one behind every counter; "They're called clerks"; she's always had the good chair. Lira laughs. Karis explains her thanks: she wanted it on the minute before he gave everything away.
- Log: the read told him the man's weight to the ounce and nothing else. "He's a hand. Somebody else is the arm." Short.

Ch11 Choosing (days 25–28)
- Day 25 evening: Withrow's office. She is at the case under the charter with the key on her chain when he comes in; hands him the buff folder and sits, says nothing until he's read. He reads it from the back (the counsel's habit): the newest leaf first (the eighth day's entry, #44 address, remedy column empty), then backward through the placings, the inspections, two old notes before her time each with remedy and clearance, to the charter's grant. "Ruled by the same machine."
- Withrow: the next inspector will read it as he just did, in the other direction, and will meet the empty column last; a finding against the house's judgment would make that inspection a long one. She takes papers out of her drawer one at a time and lays them on the folder: the program's charter, Karis's budget line, the intake roll, the delegation's placing. Not sorry; showing him the bill.
- The other column: when the house first asked her to take him she sent for the Greyvane transcript and hunted it for the trick, as she used to hunt appeals; didn't find one; she's a good hunter. She has hunted this paper the same way: it's honest law, and so is theirs, and Karis's doors are real. Two true things; she chooses which to stand on, aloud. "Make a note of the word *choosing*." His note: underlined twice, as she underlined *discretion* in her pocket code, once ruled and once not. Last: sit up straight in the coach; the town watches the coach.
- Days 26–27, entered by the blank: Cael draws the year on a notebook page — a line, the Fifth-days ticked — and finds one date that will be written by somebody he'll never see (the re-evaluation, fixed by the seat in its ruling). Then Karis's two-docket slip: Brom's sitting and the ruling the same morning. Somebody will call it spite; it's a clerk who likes his Fifth-days full; spite gets tired. She files the slip in the back of her notebook behind the six. Bracken: "A dozen if the year runs honest. More." Board line *The house will attend.* Wing three, a Blade, 22⅓, one line.
- Day 27 dusk, wall: the incoming coats arrive before the bell for the first time — men who are early for something. Call back to his third-evening line (men with no questions are sent at the end of something) — he hadn't known what the something was. Now: "They're waiting for a status change." The paper will make one.
- Day 27 night, honest floor: Lira wants a floor that moves. The Force's Path in the edition: a contact shove — through his front foot into the boards within a stride, landing a beat after the stamp and holding a count or two; the recoil comes back into his knees, and the settle is him taking it. Cael lays lanes across her line as moving boards; Brom at the end as the body; Karis on the stair counts from the moment each lane is laid, as she did on the first night. Ten; ten; third: slow through twelve, clean at thirteen. Lira stops it. Her lesson: lie with the foot you don't care about; go on the fourth. "Three weeks ago" (the first honest floor; Brom's line); "since the morning it settled".

Ch12 Admit (days 30–33)
- Day 30, east hall, gallery full. Rooke's rule once: four exchanges; a flag or none; most flags takes it; three bursts for the program's entrant, and past three the table still gives the exchange and the card pays for each one; level after four, the table rates both fighters and the higher figure takes it.
- The Force: a contact shove through his front foot — the boards within a stride jump a beat after his stamp and hold a count or two; the recoil comes up into his knees; the settle is him taking it, on his own count. Cael watches knees and listens to boards from the rail (no read at range).
- E1: Lira runs straight (burst one), lands inside his stride; he stamps; the boards jump under her landing; his palm, and the shove that comes after it, sits her down. Red. Bought on purpose: she's counting his settle.
- E2: four-count; the lie draws his stamp to where her burst would land; empty third; his settle on his own fourth; she goes on hers off the honest back foot (burst two). Blue. Karis: two bursts, two exchanges, one flag each.
- E3: he stamps fast, left-centre-right as he comes, a fan a stride ahead; she gets caught on jumping boards and scrambles out on her feet (no burst); walked to the frames; she stands the empty count with one foot on moving wood; his settle; burst three along the frames past him; blue. Karis: three, her whole bout.
- E4: he stands and stamps a ring a stride each side, renewing one as the last dies; no count to go on; she spends four (across the near boards) and five (off moving wood into the still middle); palm to the breastbone. Blue. Three to one.
- The Stone's card 10/10/7 = 27; Rooke: the seven is four and five; past three she rents the bout.
- Rooke kneels and presses her landing leg himself; names the bill by touch (hip down the outside); two days at half work, no frames, no bursts until he believes her; the meet is fought more than once a day.
- The Force at the stair: he'll fight to no count by the month's end; then "Did you give me the first?" "Yes."
- Lira at the rail watching the Stone fold his card: the man came from the river to teach her; "I'm better now. I can admit I want it." Karis on the court: *admit* goes in the back pages, where things don't get crossed out. Lira sees the pen from across the court and keeps walking. No log.
- Day 31, refectory: Bracken reads the district confirmation aloud (the panel convened under the fourth day's docket, confirmed; *outcome anticipated: advancement, per title*); he'll write to the district about that word: shake the man's hand, have him moved. Pocket.
- Velmere: from the court at night Cael sees Brom's window lit low and wrong — the lamp moved to the far wall. On the stair Lira: squared on the docket, unopened; the door doesn't latch and she didn't push it. Her rule: nobody asks; tell Karis. Karis leaves the reading room door open every evening (action). Day 33 (the third evening) he comes down late: the face (protected). Cael looks once and then at his bread. Nothing inferred about the contents.

Ch13 The Hand (days 35–42)
- Day 35: Karis's first spend at Bracken's counter: Greyvane, Prynn, certified copies of the founders' signature leaves; before the midyear recess. She writes the request's number on the back board of her notebook by the slips and rules three lines under it — "for what they tell me, if they tell me anything." Careful as with a key.
- The assistant: "Find it." Twenty minutes; the proviso (*upon a party's motion only*); "Yes." No log.
- Frames: entered by the lantern seen burning on the second floor after the eighth bell; four evenings; wordless; the look at the end; Lira once beside him.
- Day 39, the annex: the drawn floor; "a form"; Karis asks what to watch — Lira says Jent, Brom says the gallery — "The seal."
- Day 40: thirty-one on the steps counted from the coach; the side door's blacked hinges; the officer with the cold; the door log, eleven seconds, Pellin's plate.
- The room from the side door, which opens at the records table's end: the records table first (two officers; the junior he knows); the covered evaluation chairs; the platform and the hood; the benches; lot numbers last, seventeen under his foot. Cael counts the room three times and each time it lacks a face.
- The hood: hands that keep time with the procedure a beat after it, like a second clock. Seln on the back bench that sees every door.
- Havel: rotation sheet; the junior chair laid by the manual; door log checked; the times; the ordinary seal; looks: two at Greyvane from the recorder's desk, two on the oak at the bluff — four; he won't give himself a fifth; the five sheets at the inn; no sixth.
- Withrow bows to the measured depth. At her carriage door, to Cael: same depth from this side; she checked.
- Coach: Brom first (the junior never looked; at Greyvane from the recorder's desk, twice — "I counted, from the gallery"); Lira: done looking, or told to be; the counsel and Karis on how the seat took the lists (tense right); Seln, eyes shut, three grades. At the residence door, to Cael alone: "The office knows rooms like that. It used to sit at the other table."
- Log: forty minutes, a hood, hands; "I keep looking for the person and finding the hand."
- Day 42: the four slips; the entry; Karis comes up reading her draft aloud and stops mid-sentence — the room goes quiet and he knows she's reading his page; he leaves the book open. Her line; the parenthesis; he reads it to her; "be afraid of it accurately"; the ruled column, four names.
