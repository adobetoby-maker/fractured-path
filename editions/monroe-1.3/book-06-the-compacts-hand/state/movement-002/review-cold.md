# Cold review: Book 6 "The Compact's Hand", Movement 2 (chapters 8–13)

## Read type and coverage

- Type: fresh-context simulated cold read. I have no drafting history for this text. I opened only the wrapper and the compiled review prompt, which contains the six chapters. I opened no canon, plan or prior-book files.
- Coverage: chapters 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13, each read in full and in the declared order. The supplied movement looks complete: chapter 13 ends on a closing beat for the month.
- No editorial canon evidence was supplied. Everything under "Canon / state check" below is an apparent inconsistency seen from inside the text, not a verified canon violation.

## Verdict

**Continue. Revise at line level; no structural rework needed.** The movement does something hard on purpose. It makes a faceless, procedural antagonist frightening because nothing visibly happens, and it mostly succeeds. Chapter 12's trial is the action peak, and it is well built. Three repairs matter: an arithmetic error in the burst count inside that trial, a recurring "Log coda / please minute this" reflex that tells the reader what scenes have already shown, and a short cluster of referent and knowledge slips at the first sitting.

## Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.**
- The untitled sheaf pushed under the door at the third bell (ch. 8). I knew who left it before Karis said so, and I liked being trusted to know.
- The pencil dot in the margin of the renewals table, and "Nine days" (ch. 8). It is the best small mystery in the movement and gets the cleanest payoff.
- Bracken weighing the answer on the parcel beam, with his private oilcloth ledger of what every answer weighed (ch. 9).
- "The presiding seat: per seal" (ch. 10). One line of grey type turns the stakes over.
- Jent's "four nothings" (ch. 10). Reading faces has been the protagonist's weapon, and the movement takes it away.
- The new door cut in the old wall, and the officer with a cold writing "unclassified" into the door log (ch. 10 / ch. 13).
- Lira's trial in full (ch. 12), especially the flag she gives away on purpose and the empty third count.
- The Havel interlude (ch. 13). For the first time the reader knows more than Cael, and the clean seal sits against five private sheets.

**Where it slipped.**
- The logistics run early in chapter 8: the rules, the rota rewrite, who is on which steps. It is readable but slow, and the material is administrative before the keel image gives it a spine.
- In chapter 11, "The year's shape laid itself out over the next three days." This is a calendar recitation of dates. The blank-slip image rescues it at the end.
- The first two scenes of chapter 13 (the budget request, the assistant's mistake). Both are good small scenes but they sit in a row before the sitting, and the pressure flattens.
- Section-ending Log entries, repeatedly (see Repair brief, priority 2). Several restate what the scene just landed.

**Which person matters.** Karis, by a distance: the keel, the renewals rebuild, ninety-one, "It's very well done ... I hate it," and "That is not how any of us are built." Lira is second, through Fenmark's wiped slate, the trial and *admit*. Seln gains a great deal with very few lines, and the "clerks" joke is the warmest moment in the coach.

**What I expect next.**
- The evaluation seat gets empanelled, and Jent argues the remedy (a frame or station evaluation, the blank slip).
- The founders'-hands copies arrive from Greyvane before the midyear recess and show something about who "the hand" is.
- Lira's "yard out the back" becomes the non-corridor exit.
- Brom has to answer the Velmere letter. The Ephram–Brom frame-watching ends in a real bout.
- Havel's sixth sheet finally gets its fact.
- The week-by-week cost column fills in.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation, as an adult genre reader. A fluent thirteen-year-old would probably continue too, because of the trial and the faceless-seal hook, but would skim parts of chapters 8, 9 and 11.

## Canon / state check (apparent only; no canon supplied)

**1. Burst arithmetic, ch. 12 (internal contradiction). High confidence.**
- The page shows six bursts:
  - Exchange 1: "burst one off the left hip ... The burst carried her twenty feet."
  - Exchange 2: "She burst off the back foot."
  - Exchange 3: "she had to burst to get out of it", then "Burst three. Off the right foot."
  - Exchange 4: "Burst four", "Burst five."
- The text calls exchange 3's escape "Her second burst of the bout", but it is her third on the page.
- Karis's "One burst. Two exchanges. One flag each." is ambiguous. Read as "one left", it is right; read as "one used", it is wrong.
- Rooke's "Five. Against three." and the Control seven both depend on the count.

**2. Referent / POV slip, ch. 13. High confidence.**
- "Jent was at the petitioner's table ... He took the room's count, which was the first thing he did anywhere."
- The next paragraph makes clear that the counting viewpoint is Cael's, but grammatically "He" is Jent.
- "each time one thing failed to be there" is also vague about what was missing.

**3. Knowledge boundary, ch. 13. Medium confidence.**
- Lira says, "Then he's stopped learning too ... Like the coats."
- Cael's conclusion that the coats have stopped learning (ch. 11) is shown only as a private realization written on the coping.
- No scene shows him telling Lira. It may have happened off the page, but as written she quotes a thought she has not heard.

**4. Renewals table source logic, ch. 8. Medium-low confidence.**
- Cael remembers Gault sealing the minute in front of him and initialling "the receipt of the sealed copy".
- Two lines later, "The house had no copy of it. The registry did."
- Karis then says "the inspection's report gave the renewal a different one in a list at the back", which means the house did hold a second date.
- The claim that only the Log shows both dates side by side survives, but the copy question will make a careful reader stop.

**5. Lira's rank and score. Low confidence; check against canon.**
- She is "six ranks above Lira's one", that is, Iron Rank One, yet her figures run to "twenty-seven at her very best last year, the top of what the board called strong Iron."
- This may be correct under the world's rules (tier versus rank versus score), but cold it reads like a mismatch.

**6. Range and discretion of the read, ch. 12 vs ch. 10. Low-medium confidence; power-limit check.**
- In ch. 10, sending the read through the floor is something "he would never have let it go in a ring with a judge watching".
- In ch. 12 he feels the Force's course "go into the floor from forty feet away" from a gallery rail, in a packed hall with a rating table below.
- If the read is passive at that range, fine. If it needs contact through his feet, the gallery position is a problem. Verify against canon.

**7. Reserved disclosure, Velmere letter, ch. 12. Plan-adherence question, not a canon error.**
- The narration says "Nobody at that table ever learned what it said".
- It then all but states the contents with confidence: "everything he was ever priced out of ... with a place at the foot for his own hand, and very possibly a date".

**8. Word muddle, ch. 12. Medium confidence.**

## Repair brief

**Priority 1: fix the burst count in Lira's trial.**
- *Location:* ch. 12. These lines: "burst one off the left hip"; "She burst off the back foot"; Karis's "One burst. Two exchanges. One flag each."; "she had to burst to get out of it. Her second burst of the bout."; "Burst three"; "Three bursts ... Her whole bout."; "Rooke's rule was three bursts. She had spent three."; "Burst four" / "Burst five"; Rooke's "Five. Against three."
- *Observed issue:* six bursts on the page, five in the narration and the scoring.
- *Effect on the reader:* the book trains its reader to count. Cael counts everything and Karis keeps a running tally aloud. A counting reader catches the error at the climax of the movement's only full fight, and the cost mechanism (Control seven, the leg, Rooke's lesson) loses force.
- *Proposed scope:* line-level only, total five. For example, make exchange 3's escape a non-burst (a slide or a long ugly step out of the course), make Karis's line explicit ("One burst left."), and make "Burst three" the frames burst. Keep every exchange.
- *Strength to preserve:* the full four-exchange structure, with the flag bought in exchange 1, the empty third count, the Force adapting twice, and the well of courses. Do not shorten the fight.

**Priority 2: thin the "Log coda / please minute it" reflex.**
- *Location:* section endings across the movement, especially:
  - ch. 9, the second paragraph of the Lira-on-the-wall entry ("She's started walking the walls...")
  - ch. 10, the first Log entry's roll-call of Pellin, Coss, Vastin, the provost and Umber, repeated in the second entry
  - ch. 12, "The word was want" entry
  - ch. 13, the "At Greyvane there was Coss ... Vastin ... Umber" entry, the third time the roll-call appears
- *Related dialogue tic:* Karis's "I'd like that minuted" and "I'd like that noted too", Withrow's "Make a note" and "Note that I bowed anyway", and Lira's "I want it entered".
- *Observed issue:*
  - Nearly every section closes with Cael writing up what the scene has just shown, often explaining its meaning.
  - Four different characters now speak in the same "enter it in the record" idiom.
- *Effect on the reader:* the subtext gets told after it was shown, which the adult lens notices. The thirteen-year-old lens feels the pace stall at each scene end, and distinct voices blur into one register.
- *Proposed scope:*
  - Cut or compress three or four codas where the scene already landed. Keep the line that adds something new: "He's a hand. Somebody else is the arm." and "I keep looking for the person and finding the hand."
  - Keep the antagonist roll-call in one place only.
  - Let one or two characters express the "record this" impulse in their own idiom, or by action alone.
- *Strength to preserve:* the Log as a running instrument, especially "Four nothings", the lane figures 10 / 10 / 12, and the closing exchange "That is not how any of us are built" / "be afraid of it accurately", which ends on the ruled column.

**Priority 3: repair the referent and knowledge slips at the first sitting.**
- *Location:* ch. 13.
  - "He took the room's count, which was the first thing he did anywhere. ... each time one thing failed to be there."
  - Lira's "Like the coats."
  - The counsel's "came to the same size" and Karis's "They are."
  - The backfill paragraph "She had ridden down in her carriage. She had sent it home empty..." placed after Withrow has already spoken in the coach.
- *Observed issue:*
  - The counting act reads as Jent's, and the missing "one thing" is not named.
  - Lira quotes a conclusion Cael reached alone in ch. 11.
  - There is a tense mismatch in a two-line exchange.
  - The coach paragraph is out of order.
- *Effect on the reader:* the sitting is the movement's long-promised set piece, and readers stumble on who is doing the looking at the exact moment the faceless seal is meant to land. Lira's line breaks the knowledge chain the book is otherwise careful with.
- *Proposed scope:* four line edits.
  - Name Cael as the one counting, and name the absent thing ("a face").
  - Either give the coats line to Cael or plant one sentence earlier in which he tells the others.
  - Align the tense: "come to the same size" / "They do."
  - Move the carriage sentence before Withrow speaks.
- *Strength to preserve:* the officer with a cold and the eleven seconds; the hands that do only what procedure asks; the Havel interlude with its clean seal and no sixth sheet; Brom's "I counted"; and Seln's "It used to sit at the other table."
