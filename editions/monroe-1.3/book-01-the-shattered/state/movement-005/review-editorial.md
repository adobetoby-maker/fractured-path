## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.** Chapter 28 at the desk: the red case number that is not there, "It was not a case. It was some papers in a case" (ch28 L47), and the register of codes with the right prefix and no entry (L57–67). Halvern's gutter measured in knots. Torvin on the doorstep, and "Tell him about the oats." Chapter 29's recognition is the best single beat in the movement: Torvin says the whole name at the table, and Cael realises he watched the man step off the cart and let him go "like weather" because it was a good morning; the Log line *Write everybody down. Especially on the good days* (ch29 L85) is the kind of self-correction this series runs on. Lira's string paying off chapter 27. Lira's bout, start to finish. "Under his floor" (ch30 L241). The reading room's false seams and the real one, read aloud four times "the way you check a column of figures by adding it from the bottom." Lira striking the bracing clause: "You've written his argument for him." The meeting, and "It's not the floor. It's the leg." Coss shimming the desk with the fallen paper (ch33 L189–193) is a perfect, wordless cutaway ending.

**Where it slipped.** Chapter 28's second-day walk (L217–231) tours a district the reader already knows through Cael (the porter, the fish-pan Ember, the mender's doorframe, the fish stall, the yard-lane prices); it earns the one line it needs ("circuit" filed with dice and dogs) at some cost in pages, and it lands directly after the Dessa high with Cael off the page for 4,700 words. Chapter 29 then stacks three listing scenes in a row (supper recount, the Log entries, Hesk's letter and the three-line margin list) before Lira's string lets air in. Chapter 30 reopens on Coss restating, almost verbatim, a lesson chapter 28 already taught (don't push a district's shut face: ch28 L185 and ch30 L17). Chapter 33 reprises chapter 32's close (who would notice; the arithmetic) once more than it needs to before Vell.

**Which person matters.** Lira, by a distance: she chooses her own bout, wins it her own way, asks for a report and not a ruling, edits the objection, and says "I'm choosing it. That's different from having to, and I'd like it noticed." Coss second, as a new kind of adult in the book — correct rather than kind, and honest about the difference. Torvin's wife in one line ("If you go anywhere, you tell me first. I'll want to give you something to carry").

**Which event I expect next.** The six weeks' bouts, with Vell deciding; the over-commit on Lira's right side being named; Renn II; a senior evaluator on the merchant road; and whoever the polite "circuit kid" man was, now that the Compact's own file plainly does not know about the circuit.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The movement does exactly what the packet says it is for: the reader stands inside the institution long enough to feel how wrong the file is, then watches a fourteen-year-old beat the procedure by reading.

**Thirteen-year-old lens.** The bout is fully trackable because every term it uses (the dip, the gate-turn, the skipped middle, the step, the huff) was defined in earlier chapters and is re-cued here before it is used. The statute chapter is hard reading by design, and the book helps: Cael lists what each Part is *for*, the two false seams are short, and the find is framed as a load figure read aloud. A young reader will follow the logic and will feel the shim. The one place this reader may stall is ch29's run of lists; the question "why doesn't he just go and ask the man?" is answered by Hesk's letter, which is good.

**Adult genre reader lens.** The Coss cutaway is restrained competence writing of a high order; the thin-file mystery is planted without a wink. The two-men question is withheld properly (Cael writes an inference in the back of the Log and refuses to let it "put on a fact's coat"). The one genre reflex worth watching is repetition of explanation: Coss's doorstep philosophy twice, Brenna's shield described twice in the book in nearly the same words (ch15 L169 / ch29 L299).

## 4. Rulings on the items the author flagged

**(1) The Coss cutaway at ~6,900 words (24% of the movement; map ~6k).** *Earned in content, over in weight, and the overrun sits where it costs most.* Every element the packet lists is on the page and good: attention over brilliance (the deskmate anecdote), what the file should hold and does not, the register, Halvern and the lent pin, Torvin's minimum, the district as a town his training cannot read, "circuit" sliding past, the first retrieval clause for a child, the risky note, the coda. The brief allows ~6k as "the book's longest cutaway" and says it may be two passages; a whole chapter from Coss is at the edge of "brief cutaways, never alternating chapters" (BOOK_MAP §5) but was planned at this size. The ~900 over is spent on two things the reader does not need: the second-day survey of a district the reader already has through Cael (ch28 L217–231), and a doorstep lesson taught twice (ch28 L185; ch30 L17). Book-level, Coss has now used ~6.9k of ~11k, leaving ~4.1k for the M8 and M9 passages the map sizes at ~5k; flag to the coordinator. Ruling: trim 500–800 words from the walk and the doubled lesson (Repair priority 3); keep everything else.

**(2) Lira's bout (~2,300 + ~500 aftermath vs 1,500–2,000) and Brenna as the opponent.** *Earned; do not shorten.* The rubric's own test is a changing problem per exchange, adaptation, cost and earned length: first (sideways, two fingers on the shine, the foot stays), second (the dip is gone and she is caught going in anyway), third (the skipped middle, three times), fourth (anger brings the dip back; the over-commit; the head), fifth (two empty touches, the step, the gate caught and sent on). Both fighters adapt. Cost is on the page. The aftermath is the point of the bout ("You didn't lean." / "Not any more." / "Somebody who watches feet"), and it pays ch15 L35–43 (the sleeve, the left foot) and ch18 L333. Brenna is a source canon name, allowed by the packet; her record against Lira (ch15 L311 "I've fought her twice… when I went in on it she did exactly what she did to you") is honoured exactly. Brenna training out her dip is the right consequence of ch15's fifth exchange ("She doesn't know she does it" — she knows now) and gives the circuit a memory. The one defect in the bout is a continuity number, not its length: ch29 L325 and L371 say Brenna fixed the dip "in the three weeks since" Cael's bout; that bout was day 15 (STATE_LEDGER M3) and this is day 53 — five and a half weeks. Fix the number (Repair priority 2).

**(3) The Movement 6 plant — the over-commit in the fourth exchange.** *Works; well placed; carry it to the ledger.* Ch29 L375–377 shows it plainly ("as if the first half of her had arrived and the second half had not been told to stop") and ch30 L101 shows him keeping it out of her report ("once was not a pattern"). A reader will register both. BOOK_MAP §3b gives M6 "Cael teaches back: he names a habit she did not know she had" — this is the habit, and it is a second one (the left foot was M3). One note for M6: he has withheld evidence from a report she asked for as evidence; when he names it, the withholding should cost him something with her, or the "I don't want a ruling" pact reads as cover.

**(4) New canon.** All acceptable; none contradicts the Bible or the map. Recommend approval of: the post's three-part layout (front counter, the upstairs office with the plank partition not reaching the ceiling — ready for M8's "through the thin wall", the reading room three steps down on the river side); the sealed-file procedure (handed sealed in Denvash, seal broken before the station clerk, who signs — this also fixes the source's "in Denvash" seam, as the packet asked); the register of authorization codes (the Book 6 plant, done without a gloss); the statute chain (Part Two under-sixteen rule and its note; Part Three countersignature; Part Four s.12 note 3 → Register statute 19 → Second Appendix; the index loop at 40) — the numbers are small inventions, internally consistent across ch31, ch32 and the letter in ch33; the physical detail for Halvern (sandy, soft, ~31, ink on the writing hand), Halden (thin, grey, a pencil behind each ear, nineteen years) and Coss (forty-one, thin face, close-cut hair greying at the sides, thumb to the bridge of the nose, a private working log, a daughter fourteen at midsummer); Lira's circuit rating Cu 3, which is exactly what ch10 L297 promised ("Higher than my registry rank. Lower than I think it should be") against her registry Copper R2; Lira's lodging (the roof room, the dye-shed sisters, the hoopless barrel) and the tin of "weeks of door", which sets up M6's own room without contradicting M2's "unspecified". One ledger note: the author's "three weeks" for Brenna's retraining must become about five before the ledger carries it.

**(5) Vell learns "Hesk-ward" on the page (ch33 L127–137).** *Correct, and done in character.* He gives it as part of making himself expensive; she keeps it "where I keep the things that are mine" and out of the book "until the yard's seen it", which is her own custom (M3/M4) exactly. The coordinator's M2 note is satisfied before Darrow's ledger line. ✓

**(6) Audio: Halvern and Halden.** *Enough for this movement.* The two never share a scene or a POV; Halvern is named only inside Coss's passages (12 uses, all ch28); Halden only inside Cael's (ch31 ×6, ch32 ×1); Cael calls the counter man "the clerk" throughout ch32. Their bodies and rooms are opposites (soft and sandy at a counter upstairs; thin and grey at a table below), and "Halden" is spoken aloud once as his whole greeting, which fixes it by ear. Residual risk is three chapters apart, not inside a chapter. Guard for M8: if Cael ever has to name the counter clerk, use "the permits clerk" rather than "Halvern".
