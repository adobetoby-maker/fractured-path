reports, EDITION_BRIEF, BOOK_MAP (all), the two "Coordinator rulings" blocks in STATE_LEDGER,

## Verdict

The book holds as one book. Read straight through, the calendar never slips against the M9 day canon
(vouch day 8, assessed day 97, Ilsev day 141, bout day 200): every dated reference I could test — the
letter lags, "sixteen days", "seven weeks", "five weeks gone", "thirteen bouts in my book", "a week ago
Monday", the Log's entry numbers against Vell's bout count, the instance count from the honest four to
the eighth, the untold-to-Hesk count from three to five to nothing — comes out right. Every protected line
is on the page and first appears where §7 maps it; three of them are broken only by an attribution or a
comma. The knowledge boundaries hold: nothing that only a cutaway shows (the register and the code, the
lent pin, *Noted*, *Atypical*, Hesk's cut palm, the senior flag) ever reaches Cael's page, and the Reader
Standard is clean end to end. The plants are paid or carried exactly as BOOK_MAP §8 and the M9 ledger say.
What a straight read catches that the movement passes could not is texture: a dozen gestures, similes and
signature lines that each movement used once as texture now stack — the "the way …" simile tail runs at
about twenty per ten thousand words across the whole book (the one source habit §9 names), "Don't thank
me" is said ten times by six people, Vell turns her tea a quarter turn by its handle five times, Lira's
"morning brightness" comes up seven times, and Coss borrows Cael's "stone in a boot" — and two stretches
where the method shows: the Kestrel-and-keys fortnight in ch41–44, where four consecutive back-page
entries end on the same note, and ch48's Part Six reading, which rhymes beat for beat with ch31. Neither is
a structural fault; both are a same-author pass. The season question resolves cleanly (winter runs from
about day 85 to about day 135; day 97 is winter and day 141 is spring, as ch55 and ch60 already have it)
and leaves only two lines to change. Thirty-two line fixes below, three priorities after them.

## 3. Book-wide repetition — the worst ten

Counts are whole-book occurrences (grep, case-insensitive), rate per 10,000 words where it matters.

   someone's room" (ch1), "the way a man ending a conversation" (ch57). Keep the ones that are the book's

## 4. Arc and pacing

- **Shape.** The thirds land where §4 puts them. M1–M2 (loss, arrival, Lira, Renn) read fast and warm; M3
  (the three losses, the plant, the dark Arbiter) is the book's first real engine and the Log's columns are
  the best structural invention in it; M4–M6 are the richest stretch — the pin, Dessa I, Coss's thin file,
  the reading room, the six weeks, the notice — and nothing in them sags; M7's Feryn (ch45–47) is the midpoint
  set piece the map asked for and earns its 3,000 words; M8's quiet week is quiet on purpose and does not
  feel slack because Dessa II and the old man's lesson move the method; M9 drives from the board to the
  closing log line without a wasted chapter.
- **Where it sags.** ch41–44 s1: the Kestrel two mornings, the keys at the mender's, the courtyard nothing,
  the half-heartbeat in the heels, *Inconclusive*. Each scene is honest and each ends on the same note
  ("Find out. Don't guess." / "One morning isn't three months." / "I'm writing it down so that I remember
  I couldn't." / *Inconclusive*), so the fortnight reads as one beat repeated four times before the telling
  in ch42 s4 releases it. ch43 is where the rhythm meter also flags it (mean 11.65, ≥40 share 1.6%).
- **Where it rhymes too closely.** ch48 s5 (Part Six) repeats ch31's method — the three books, "begin at the
  beginning", the false seams, the aloud readings, the clean copy — and then ch49 repeats ch32's desk. The
  rhyme is intended (the seam opens both ways; Halden's "I said I'd find you the next book"), and Ilsev's
  "Section one says" is a better scene than Coss's, but the reader knows the shape from the first page of
  ch48 and the chapter spends its surprise on confirming it.
- **Where it rushes.** Nowhere seriously. ch59 (the ledger by name, Feryn's goodbye, the bread, the proud
  letter, the stranger) carries five beats in 3,700 words and all five land; ch60 is right-sized.
- **Plants paid or carried** (BOOK_MAP §8, M9 ledger): Book 3 sparring plant ✔ ch18 (plus Renn ch12 and Dessa
  ch25 as support); the mechanism uncertain — watching vs contact, Kestrel inconclusive ✔ ch41–47; Book 6
  disproportions — the sweep ✔ ch21, the untraceable authorization ✔ ch28 (cutaway only), Ilsev's flag ✔ ch49,
  the senior slip ✔ ch60 — nobody explains any of them ✔; "Pre-Compact Era Holdovers" ✔ ch4, 31, no gloss;
  Hesk's name ✔ never given, "practice-related incident" voiced only as doubt ✔ ch4, 6, 33; Coss's rank ✔
  "field agent" only; Lira's past ✔; the stranger ✔ unidentified, not Compact, "allowed", sex unstated; Feryn's
  open account ✔ ch47, 59; Darrow's autumn ✔ ch59 and Feryn's coat ✔ ch59 (between-books, for Book 2's map);
  the tollhouse half-mark ✔ ch53 untouched; the polite "circuit kid" man ✔ ch19 → 29 → 33 → 49, never
  explained; Hesk's guild review ✔ "remain on the book. Open." ch36; Alis "until the spring" ✔; Hesk's palm ✔
  unknown to Cael; Dessa's "Then we'll talk" ✔ ch51 "Now we've talked"; Renn's second look ✔ ch35; the old
  man's lesson ✔ ch52; the information seller ✔ ch22; "ask Hesk about Tuesdays" ✔ ch2; Red Cap's W ✔ ch57–59;

Clean. No oath or softer cousin anywhere (grep for damn/hell/bastard/bloody/God: zero hits; the source's
"Damned if I know" does not carry). Violence costs every time and is never gore (the one "crack" in ch58

is chaperoned by the widow's "No visitors after the lamps" in every scene, and the ch58 embrace is "round
the shoulders and nowhere near the ribs". Wrong is named as wrong by the people who do it: Cael's
withholding (Lira ch37 "You *ruled*", ch54 "Telling's when it's done", ch60 "You're doing it again"; Cael

in his working life that he could not have written down and then stood behind", ch60); Red Cap's half-mark
("that was stealing… it was business", ch53 — left as a joke about a boy, which is right for thirteen).
Cruelty is never rewarded; every winner helps the loser up.
