# Movement 004 — Cold read (chapters 21–27)

**Kind of read:** simulated cold read. One model (Claude Fable 5.1), fresh context, manuscript only: the seven chapter files as compiled in `review-cold-prompt.md`, no canon, no notes, no drafting history. This is one reader's editorial judgment, not an audience measurement and not a sales signal. Where I flag an inconsistency it is *apparent* inconsistency within these seven chapters; I have no canon evidence to call anything a verified violation.

**Coverage:** 7 of 7 supplied files read in declared order (21 The Pin; 22 The District's One New Variable; 23 At the Month; 24 Four Pages; 25 Spending and Banking; 26 The Honest Sort; 27 Unrated, Over Dessa). The movement reads as complete: it opens on a disturbance (the pin), closes on the arrival that answers it (the man off the west cart), and the central bout is fully resolved in between.

**Orientation, arriving mid-book:** oriented within two pages. By the end of the first scene of 21 I know: Cael, fourteen, thirty-six days into Ardenmere after something at "Weaver's Row"/"Kindling" where a brass plate failed to light and left him with a word in square brackets on his card; the Compact (hand-and-balance pin) is the authority he is hiding from; Torvin's is his lodging; Hesk is the guardian he writes to in Denvash; Lira is the friend who trains him; the Cinder House yard is a fight circuit with a ledger kept by Vell; he has lost to Renn, Brenna, Amrit and Petra. The Log's front/back split and the *h* hearsay mark are explained in use. Things I did not understand and did not need to: "Garrik says the arm's still running clean"; "Tuesdays and the file"; what exactly the bracketed word says. Those read as earned earlier context, not as gaps.

## Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Immediately: the market "handed a cup filled to the brim and told not to spill it," and the nut boy looking at his pointer for the first time in a month. That is a better way to introduce a threat than showing the threat. The old man's "Wonder what changed," and Cael walking down the lane finding only one answer. The woman in the shawl's three sentences, which are a genuinely good payoff and reprice the whole chapter. Vell's "Atypical" and the twelve strokes inside her back cover. The second exchange of the Dessa bout, where he sees the tell early and *banks it* against every muscle in his legs. The fourth exchange: "It was a wall deciding to arrive," then "What happened next, he did not decide." The ch 26 ledger confession, where a careful sentence is written, admired, and struck through because the column it stands on is short two figures. The last page: a tired man with a case that is lighter than he expected, going up the steps to the post, and Cael not writing him down.

**Where it slipped.** Three places, in order of weight. (1) After the three sentences in 22, the single most dangerous fact in the movement — the carriers' clerk told the Compact that Torvin's sends letters west to Denvash — is never weighed by anyone. Cael and Lira do not discuss it on the page; Cael's only reaction is "I don't know yet what for." Then in 27 he posts another letter to Denvash with the same clerk, who says "Torvin's, isn't it," and Cael says "Yes" with no flicker. For a boy whose whole character is mapping edges, that silence reads as an authorial choice the reader is not let in on. (2) The Vell section of 23 (ledger, "Atypical," the card rule) is the richest material in the movement for me and the slowest stretch for a younger reader; the card-naming rule is explained twice (Vell's evening; Cael at the board in 27). (3) The bout is told in 25 and then re-told in 26 (Log, exchanges 1–3 nearly beat for beat), and in 27 (to the brothers; to Hesk; in Lira's report; by the old man). Lira's report and the old man's note each add something new and should stay. The Log recap of exchanges 1–3 and the brothers' retelling mostly do not.

**Which person matters.** Cael, obviously, but the relationship that carries the movement is Cael–Lira: the reports with no ruling, "borrowed" set down and not pushed, "you'll be the one I tell first." Vell is the second pole: the one adult who has seen the thing and refused to name it. Dessa earns her chapter and a half. The man off the west cart matters most of all for what comes next.

**Which event I expect next.** The man with the thin case is the second man Cael predicted in 21 ("another man to stand somewhere quiet and watch who moved"). I expect him at Torvin's door asking by name, probably within a day of Hesk's reply arriving (day 51–52, which the text has already counted for us). Behind that: Renn back at the turn of the month; the old man finally teaching; Dessa in the benches; a fifth instance.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The ending is the right kind of quiet.

## Repair brief

**Priority 1 — Make the tell read one way on one pass.**
- *Location:* Ch 24, second Sunday, paragraph beginning "It was so small that he did not trust it the first time," specifically the sentence "Tonight, at the end of six exchanges, it came off the ground and down again a half beat before the frame had finished forming above it," and the preceding "It came off early."
- *Observed issue:* "came off the ground and down again ... before the frame had finished forming" says the foot lands before the frame is complete (early). The next sentence, the Log line ("The frame forms before the foot"), Lira's quiz in 25 and the bout itself all say the foot lands *after* the frame (late). The paragraph contradicts itself at the one mechanical fact the whole bout is built on.
- *Effect on the reader:* A re-read for the adult; a wrong mental model for the thirteen-year-old carried into ch 25, where "the back foot ... came down a half beat behind the rest of her" then seems to reverse what they learned.
- *Proposed scope:* One or two sentences. Keep "It came off early" (the lift) and recast the landing: the foot lifts early, the frame finishes forming, and the foot comes down late beneath it; the "half beat" is the gap between frame-set and foot-set. Punctuation and word order only; no new material.
- *Strength to preserve:* The discovery itself — "He shut his eyes and opened them and went back over it, the way you go back over a figure you have just added" — and the two-line Log entry with its small-hand caveat. Those are exactly right.

**Priority 2 — Let the three sentences cost something on the page.**
- *Location:* Ch 22, end ("*Paid nearly four days at Torvin's. Three sentences. Worth it. I don't know yet what for.*"); the ch 23 cooper's-roof scene ("A pin and a letter and a woman in a shawl"); Ch 27, the carriers' hut ("Torvin's," the clerk said, looking up. "Isn't it.").
- *Observed issue:* Cael learns the Compact now knows Torvin's sends letters west to Denvash and that the clerk gave it up. He never weighs whether to keep writing, re-route, warn Hesk, or move. Lira, who said "We'll map it," is never shown hearing the result. In 27 he hands another Denvash letter to the same clerk and confirms "Torvin's" without a thought on the page, while explicitly recalling the shawl woman's words about that clerk.
- *Effect on the reader:* The most methodical character in the book appears not to have thought about the one fact that most endangers him and Hesk. The adult reader suspects an oversight rather than a choice; the younger reader loses the thread's pressure for three chapters.
- *Proposed scope:* Small. One beat in 22 or on the roof in 23 where he and Lira name the choice (keep writing, because the letters are the one thing he will not give the Compact, and because stopping would itself tell Hesk something; or re-route via another house; either is in character) and he decides. Then in 27 a single sentence at the hut that shows him choosing again, eyes open, before "Torvin's, isn't it." This sharpens, rather than softens, the man off the west cart.
- *Strength to preserve:* The shawl woman's three sentences and the refusal to add a fourth; the dormant quiet of 24 ("a thing you could not watch was harder to put down"); the ending's "He did not write the man down." None of that should move.

**Priority 3 — Trim the bout's re-tellings in 26–27 where they add nothing new.**
- *Location:* Ch 26, the Log write-up of exchanges 1–3 (from "The first exchange took four lines" through "the sweep going through the empty place"); Ch 27, the brothers' retelling ("They wanted it told, and they wanted it told twice...").
- *Observed issue:* After the full bout in 25, exchanges 1–3 are replayed beat for beat in the Log (26), summarised to the brothers (27), summarised to Hesk (27), reported by Lira (27) and analysed by the old man (27). Lira's report ("then you were going backward, and then you weren't") and the old man's long-side note each add new information and new voice; the Log's exchange 1–3 entries and the brothers' passage do not.
- *Effect on the reader:* The thirteen-year-old feels two chapters of aftermath for one chapter of fight and starts skimming exactly where the movement's most important writing (the four-instance confession) begins. The adult notices the Log recap re-tracing 25 in near-identical order.
- *Proposed scope:* Compress, do not cut. In 26 keep the "ideas that go beyond Dessa" paragraph, the pride paragraph ("every move had a reason, and every reason was his"), and the uncolumned fourth exchange; reduce exchanges 1–3 to the claim/ruling lines only. In 27 keep the brothers' one new fact (left or right) and the wheezing man coming down to supper; trim the "told twice" summary. Do not touch the bout in 25, Lira's report, the old man, Dessa's post-bout exchange, or Vell's second line; the owner's developed action stays at its full length.
- *Strength to preserve:* The struck-through sentence and the numbered four; the step scene with "borrowed"; the kitchen's warmth and Cael's unguarded laugh. The aftermath's *emotional* length is right; only its mechanical replay is long.
