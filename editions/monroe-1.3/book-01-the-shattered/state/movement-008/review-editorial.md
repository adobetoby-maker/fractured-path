**Seat and context.** This is a fresh-context editorial review by Claude Fable 5.1, standing in for the usual Sol seat (out of usage). ## 1. Reader response (unscored)

Interest caught at once at the salt crock: a long white letter with a seal "the colour of dried blood", against a bowl of soup, on a day that had been "the same size as everything in the day before it." It held through the reading room, where the law is made physical (pages never cut, a bone knife, "the people who start in the middle are always wrong"), and peaked twice: at the kitchen table when "section four stood up off the page" and Cael sees his own autumn objection is the first half of someone else's argument; and on Dessa's mark when she puts her own palm on the dirt and the yard makes "the sound people make when they see a thing they did not know they were going to see."

Interest slipped once, briefly, in the first half of the errand-boys scene (ch52), which is pleasant and goes nowhere until the pie; and it thinned in ch48–50 where the four-step argument is told at the table, rehearsed on the step, delivered at the desk, retold on the roof and written to Hesk. Each telling is summarised rather than repeated, so it does not stall, but an adult reader feels the argument's weight more than its wit by the fifth pass.

The person who matters is Lira, and specifically the tin: "I'd rather not know and be here" is the movement's best line because it is her decision, made about her own fear, not his. Second is Coss, from the pin going into his inside pocket to the pie he does not eat.

What I expect next: Hesk's letter arriving; Coss's offer sitting unanswered; Vell's book finally carrying a name; and a boy who has just decided that quiet "isn't going to get me any further" being made loud. I would keep reading.

## 2. Two lenses

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** The legal scenes work for this reader because they are staged as a fight: a woman who "waited like somebody who had shut every door in a house," a boy who has been told "don't hope at her." "Section one says" is a thing a thirteen-year-old can carry out of the book. Dessa II is the chapter they will reread. The Coss scene lands through objects (the brown coat, the pie) rather than through "policy," and Lira's "It's only people" closes it at the right size. Nothing is above this reader; nothing is beneath them.

**Adult genre reader.** Appreciates the craft doctrine paid off on schedule (Doss's "once too often," the old man's "Now they'll plan for you," the clock-regular tell) and the way the win is earned by a change of method rather than a fragment. Notices that the Part Six logic is restated more often than it needs to be, that the narration runs to very long sentences in the reading room and the barges, and that three time-stamps in the text do not add up (section 5). Will enjoy Sella and Torvin; will find the errand boys the one scene the movement could have lived without.

## 5. Canon, state, knowledge boundaries, reserved disclosures

**5.1 Knowledge boundary — the sweep's authorization code (ch49, ch50).** Ch49: "There was an authorization code that was not in Coss's book." Ch50, Lira's second finger: "The code on the sweep that wasn't in Coss's little black book. He said so. Not in words, but he said so." The register and the missing code exist only in Coss's POV (ch28, ch30, ch33). On the page Coss told Cael exactly this (ch32): "I don't know… That's true. It's also all I'm going to say about it. Don't ask me again." STATE_LEDGER after M5 lists "the untraceable code" under what Cael does not know. Cael has never seen the black book. This is a plan/ledger adherence finding, not a universe-canon error, but it matters: the Log's discipline is the book's engine, and here Cael counts as evidence a thing he was never shown. Lira's hedge ("not in words") does not cover Cael's flat narration. Line fix in §9 (keeps the "one thing or three" count at three).

**5.2 Calendar arithmetic (three places, plus one).** Objection: day 55. Ilsev: day 141 (author's own calendar). That is twelve weeks, not seven: ch48 "seven weeks ago, at the desk that rocked" and ch49 "in the reading room below, seven weeks ago" are both wrong. Ch51 "sat across a desk from an assessor five days ago" is wrong by the same calendar (Ilsev Thursday day 141, the market Tuesday day 153: twelve days). Ch53 "she had put down a tin in her pocket a week ago on a wall" — the trust talk is day 158, the roof day 178: three weeks. Also ch48 "The six weeks had run out on the forty-second day. That had been five weeks ago" — on day 139 it is six weeks (ch47 said five on day 133, correctly). Line fixes in §9.

**5.3 Path contradiction — the Fenrow Tide girl (ch50).** "a girl from Fenrow with a Tide Path who put out his heat with a slap of water like a bucket emptied off a roof." The universe bible defines Tide as flow architecture — current, perception and redirection of internal energy, contact range, healing as its civil face, combat use a costly stall. Book 1 itself establishes it that way: ch01, Alis Trent, "Tide Path. Copper, Rank 1," a healer's trade. A Tide Copper throwing water at an Ember is a different Path. The author's report lists "A Tide girl from Fenrow" as new canon; it should not be approved. Line fix in §9 (drops the Path name).

**5.4 Reserved truths — kept.** The flag is unexplained by everyone: Ilsev "draws no conclusion," Coss "can't see who's walking," Lira "footprints and not their feet." No maker, no system intelligence, nothing non-human is theorised; "above the Compact's ordinary chain" is as far as anyone goes. Coss's rank stays "field agent." Hesk thinks about "a file, thirty-two years ago, opened in a room he had stood in at twenty-two" and "stopped there… did not go on" — exactly source ch3, no more. Lira's past: nothing before the academy. The old yard-owner: unnamed, no Path shown, past unasked ("He did not ask the old man where he had learned it"). The market stranger does not appear. No fragment is used in a bout; Dessa II is logged "No instance." "What are you?" is not asked (the carter asks "What Path have you got, then?").

**5.5 Protected wording.** `*He doesn't want me gone. Someone else wants me gone. He's delivering the message.*` — exact, ch53, first appearance, produced on the page by Coss and Lira before it is written. `*Being seen is not the same as being safe. The edition correctly kept the source's Ilsev farewell ("But it's not nothing") and the source ch18 log line ("That's not nothing either") off the page.

## 6. Rulings on the flagged items

**6.1 Length 33,300 against ~28,000 (+19%).** Earned. The brief sets 300,000 as "about" and BOOK_MAP caps a movement at 42,000; the book projects to ~315k, inside tolerance. Nothing here is padding: Dessa II is a real contest with two problems; the quiet week's beats are each a packet ask (Unclassified, apricots, errand boys, Sella and Torvin, the yard-owner, Lira on trust, Pressure alone); Coss needs both the cutaway (where the "view" comes from) and the table (the spine). If the coordinator nonetheless wants words back, the only two places that would give them without loss are the first half of the errand-boys scene (~300 words of ticket-sorting before the pie) and the paragraph "She had grown it for him…" in ch51 (~120 words that re-explain what exchange two already showed). I do not recommend either cut.

**6.2 Dessa II (~3,300 against 1,500–2,000).** Ruled good; keep the developed action. The allowance is a ceiling for a real problem and this bout has two: a defender who has rebuilt her own beaten tell as a trap (paying Doss's "once too often" and the old man's "Now they'll plan for you" exactly), and Cael's own tell read back at him (paying the nod thread from M6 and the week of failing to train it out). The win is by a change of method — deciding everything on the mark so there is nothing left to nod at — not by a fragment, and the text says so in the Log ("No instance… Everything in it was mine"). Costs are carried: the left hip through the old man's lesson and three weeks of healing, the eye through the market. Dessa learns too ("Next time I'll watch your mark"), which spends her 2–3 BOOK_MAP beats well and sets a real problem for any later meeting. One craft note only: the clock is explained in exchange two ("It was the one she used only to take back ground… she had given him this ground on purpose") and again on the mark ("She had grown it for him…"); the second can be shorter. Not a repair.

**6.3 Hesk writes the "being seen" letter on the page in ch53.** Consistent with BOOK_MAP §7 ("first appears in the letter — not earlier"): the protected sentence's first appearance in the edition is inside the letter, in Hesk's hand, and no one has said it or paraphrased it earlier (the source's two early echoes were deliberately kept out). The packet asked for exactly this. Two obligations fall on Movement 9: (a) Cael opens and reads the letter; M9 quotes it only as Cael reads it, and nobody says the line before he opens it; (b) because the reader now knows the answer a week before Cael, M9 should play his reading for what it does to him, not for the information. One further pointer M9 must pay: this letter ends "I'm proud of you past anything I've the words for, and I'll say so properly when I've found them," which points at spine 24's "I'm proud of you" letter after Darrow. That is elegant if M9's letter is visibly the "properly," and a dangling promise if it is not.

**6.4 Coss never sees a flag; learns of the ring from Ilsev's report; Cael never names Vell.** Ruled correct and better than the source. BOOK_MAP §5 lets Coss know Ilsev's flag "from her report" and "by M8 that the boy fights in the circuit," and the edition routes both through one honest answer Cael gives Ilsev about his arm ("I'd rather not say." / "It isn't material."). "I don't know anything about a flag" is handled on the page as a true sentence that stops at the edge of the floor — he has read her paragraph nine times and says so — which is the right size for a Book 6 plant. The senior-level flag stays for M9. The section head's "view… Several people hold it" and the query that came back "Noted" are good disproportion: pressure without a source.

**6.5 Cutaways.** Coss ~1,520 (under the packet's ~2.5k): earned, because the section-head exchange inside it is where the whole "policy, not law" turn comes from, and without it the table scene would be a man asserting things. His running total (~7,950 of ~11,000) leaves ~3,050 for M9's flag, which is enough. Hesk ~1,160: earned, and the right size. The protected line needs an origin and only the workshop can supply it; the loose spring is the argument, not decoration. The overrun was M6's, not this movement's; with no Hesk cutaway planned for M9, his book share lands a little over ~9k and should be accepted. Cael ~92%: on plan.

**6.6 State.** Confirmed as the author reports (§5.6). Nothing in the movement advances the fragments beyond BOOK_MAP's M8 row.

**6.7 New canon.** Approve: Part Six's content (five sections; the s.1 definition of primary classification "including any entry made in the Supplementary Register" sits cleanly on M5's statute chain and is the hinge of the book's law); Ilsev's detail; Coss's section head and the "Noted" query (unexplained, as they must be); the form of the old yard-owner's lessons (coin on the post, "push me," one finger, getting up in pieces — no Path, no past); how Pressure looks when Cael uses it (a full breath to gather, the trunk still in his own shadow, water flattened, a bucket moved a finger, both arms leaden for most of an hour — matches BOOK_MAP M8 "slow, visible in his silhouette, a leaden arm," and stays felt-and-roughly-logged for Book 2); Hesk's timepiece; Sella as the boots woman; the decide-on-the-mark technique as a one-use answer. ## 8. Source distance — spot-check of two retold scenes

Mechanical: 0 unprotected shared runs at the 8-word gate; the coordinator's 6-word probe at ~12 runs per 10k is the clean baseline. I read source ch18 and ch20 in full and set them beside ch49 (Ilsev) and ch53 (Coss at the table, the roof, the Log).

I would not touch this scene for source distance.

**Coss (ch53 vs source ch20): new prose, with a handful of source-shaped sentences.** The scene is re-entered through the walk, the brown coat and the pie, and everything that makes it land is invented (the pin into the inside pocket, the dropped stitch, "I can't see who's walking," "whoever gets sent for things that have stopped being files," the look back from the corner). But about eight sentences follow a source sentence in order with the words varied: Coss's opening speech ("Legal review will take months. Ilsev told you that, and she was right. What she couldn't tell you, because it isn't hers to know…" ← "Months, like Ilsev told you. But I want you to understand something she may not have said as directly, because it's not really her job to say it"); "It ends the file's active status" (five words verbatim); "Coss went still. It was not a large stillness" ← "Coss went still — not dramatically, just a small stillness"; "a true sentence that answered exactly the question asked, and stopped there" ← "the kind of honesty that answered exactly the question asked and nothing more"; and the Log's "I decided it on a roof in less time than it takes to cross the market, and I trust it more for that" ← "I decided that faster than I expected to, and I trust the decision more for how fast it arrived." In the ~60-sentence table scene that is roughly one sentence in eight: well under Book 3 M6's 23–32%, and nowhere near a defect, but it is the one place in the movement where the source's sentence shape shows through. Recompositions offered in §9; the author may prefer their own.

Verdict: the event-list method is working. The retold scenes are new prose with the packet's scripted beats; the invented material is entirely clean.
