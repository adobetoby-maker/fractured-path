## 1. Unscored reader response

**Am I oriented, arriving at chapter 41 mid-book?** Mostly yes, and faster than I expected. Within the first two pages I had: a boy (Cael, fourteen) at a boarding house (Torvin's) practising a borrowed move at night; a Log with a "back section" for things he cannot explain; a teacher (Lira, Wind Path) and a dawn yard ("the grey half"); a yard-keeper (Vell) with a book and a circle; and a strange notice that was "written in the dark" behind his breastbone. The chapter re-grounds those without ever stopping to explain. What I was *not* oriented on and simply carried as open tokens: Hesk (a mentor in Denvash with a workshop and a kitchen table), Kindling and the Arbiter (some declaration ceremony that failed him), Pellin's office and "the word", "the six weeks" and "the forty-second day", "the man with the pin", "the summons", "the Compact", "Coss", "Weaver's Row". A long roster of past opponents (Renn, Brenna, Petra, Dellin, Dessa, Talis, Corbin, Sarel, the cook) is name-dropped in lists (lines 175, 829). I never lost the thread because of them, but the lists are the one place a mid-book reader feels the weight of what they missed. The bracketed token `[SHATTERED]` (lines 955, 1617) reads to a cold reader as a redaction placeholder rather than a word a character says; I understood that it is the boy's tier and that it frightens people, and that is enough to work, but the brackets jolt.

**Where interest caught.**
- Line 87, the first line in the back of the Log: *I think I'm borrowing something.* That is the hook of the movement, and it is on page one.
- Lines 153–165, the old man at the gate: "Before, they bet on you. Now they plan." Three lines, no explanation, and the whole social temperature of the district changes.
- Lines 417–455, Lira's room. "It isn't theft." The movement's emotional centre, and it lands because she goes to the desk first and puts it down.
- Lines 745–767, the eel woman. "Don't." The funniest beat in the movement and it is also the truest summary of what Pressure is.
- Lines 865–931, Corvane's yard. "Watch what doesn't move." A teaching scene I could *see*, with a drawing, a test, repeated failure, then one success at "two breaths", then "Once is nothing, boy."
- Line 1401, Feryn's "No." in front of forty-one people, and Vell's answer: "Then you should have written it better."
- Lines 1535–1547, the third exchange. The step comes, he goes *round* the air, he does not take the flank, and he does not know what he just did.
- Lines 1585–1587, the grip that stays one beat too long. I noticed it when he noticed it, and did not know why it mattered, and then at line 1767 I did.

**Where it slipped.**
- Lines 219–233 and 237–263 (chapter 41): four hours of watching a man hit a sack, then an hour on a frozen wall where "there was nothing", then the same wall again at 305–309 in chapter 42 with the same result. The second courtyard attempt tells me what the first already told me. This is the only stretch where I checked how much chapter was left.
- Lines 1175–1243 (chapter 45, "The district knew"): the vignette parade (errand boys, Renn, Marrow, Sarel, Dessa) is charming piece by piece, but five arrivals in a row at the board starts to feel like a montage, and I wanted the clock to move.
- Lines 1779–1799 (chapter 47): the arithmetic about watching versus touch is good thinking, and it is also the third long Log-analysis passage of the movement; by then I trusted him to reason and wanted him to stop writing and sleep.

**Which person matters.** Cael first; Lira a close second, and for the first time in this movement she gets interiority of her own (lines 417–525) and it is the best writing in the seven chapters. Vell rises sharply in chapter 46; the girl-turned-away-downriver story (lines 1425–1427) made her a person. Feryn is a strong one-movement guest: he objects, he reads the book before complaining, he settles, he buys dinner, he says "Fourteen" to the street. Corvane and the old man at the gate are the kind of minor characters that make a district feel inhabited.

**What I expect next.** The touch hypothesis gets tested on purpose (someone's hand, probably Lira's or the old man's woman-with-the-shoulder, deliberately). The Compact senior man with the thin file arrives (line 1941). Hesk gets the next letter. Dessa's "talk owing" (line 1241) gets collected. And at some point the cost of the step ("a thing that costs you something you can't measure is a thing that's going to cost you more than you think one day, at the worst possible time", line 635) comes due.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. The bout holds, the aftermath opens a second door, and the two people I care about are sitting on a wall not knowing together.

## 4. Apparent inconsistencies and plan-adherence notes (manuscript-internal only)

No canon was supplied, so none of these is a verified canon error.

1. **Bout counts (chapter 46 / 47).** Line 1411: "He's won two since, and four of his six before that" (eight). Line 1431: "she had watched him in a dozen bouts now". Line 1747: "Fifteenth. Feryn". These three numbers do not obviously agree. Medium confidence that a reader notices; this book invites counting.
2. **"Assessed" versus "unrated".** Line 651 and 1411: the boy "holds an assessed circuit rating... in my hand". Lines 1029, 1045, 1147, 1167, 1455: Vell announces and enters him as "unrated". If "assessed" is a distinct status from a declared rank, one clause anywhere in the movement would settle it. Medium confidence.
3. **"Three weeks less two days" (line 969)** is said on the Thursday after a Monday announcement (three days). Low stakes; low confidence anyone counts.
4. **Line 189:** "the shove that Petra had taught Cael at Copper 3, and that Dellin had nearly given him at Copper 3" — the repeated "at Copper 3" reads like a draft artefact.
5. **`[SHATTERED]` (lines 955, 1617).** If the brackets are a reserved-disclosure placeholder, that is plan adherence and not a manuscript fault; if they are meant to print, they read as redaction. I cannot tell which from the manuscript alone.
6. **Physical-state tracking is consistent:** left hip ache from the step (lines 73, 251, 615, 1121); left forearm over "the old burn" (lines 1501, 1587, 1679); ribs left then both sides (1471, 1589, 1675); right side from the fall (1587, 1835). The old burn and "the old place" on the left ribs are prior-book injuries I accepted on faith.
7. **Power limits are stated and held:** the step never comes when called (lines 251, 1131, 1575, 1713); it comes under threat (1123, 1537); the cost is logged (635, 1121). The second notice is explicitly "one data point" (line 1799). Nothing in the movement overreaches what the characters know.
8. **Knowledge boundaries:** Cael does not know what he is (lines 1617–1631); Feryn does not know what he fought; Corvane did not know the build showed at the deciding (line 1923). All consistent.

## 5. Movement centre — the bout (chapter 46)

**Does it hold me?** Yes, fully, from "No." at line 1401 to "I'm hungry" at line 1649. The pre-bout argument at Vell's table is as tense as the fighting, and the switch to Cael's POV at line 1459 is clean.

**Can I track it?** Yes, exchange by exchange, without rereading:

| Exchange | What Cael tries | What Feryn does | Outcome / cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (1463–1481) | Looks for the carried weight as Corvane taught | Strolls; carries nothing; releases | Flat on his back; up at eight; ribs |
| 2 (1489–1517) | Narrows to the shirt at the belt; reads the settling late | Rolls shoulders, half step, releases | Guard edge-on; sheds part of it; skids two paces, stays up; left forearm "paid" |
| 3 (1525–1553) | Reads the build at the deciding | Stands still, talks | The step comes unbidden; goes *round* it; open flank not taken |
| 4 (1569–1591) | Reads fine; calls the step (fails); trained slip | Comes fast; builds, does not release; hand where the boy will go | Thrown with his own speed; grip lingers one beat; incapacity |

Each exchange changes one variable on each side, the reader is told what was learned, and the call is argued from inside Cael's own ledger (lines 1597–1599). The one deliberate opacity, what the step *is* in the third, is a strength: the yard goes silent, Feryn says "I know what I saw. I've no idea what I was fighting," and the reader is in exactly the same position.

## Repair brief

Three priorities, in order. None asks for the bout to be shortened.

### Priority 1 — Reconcile the numbers at the peak (chapter 46 and 47)

- **Location:** lines 1411 ("won two since, and four of his six before that"), 1431 ("a dozen bouts now"), 1747 ("Fifteenth."), and the "assessed" / "unrated" pairing at 1411 vs 1455 (also 1029/1045, 1147/1167).
- **Observed issue:** three bout-counts that do not visibly agree, and a rating status that is "assessed, in my hand" at the table and "unrated" in the announcement, with no line saying those are different things.
- **Effect on the reader:** this book teaches its reader to audit ledgers, and the audit fails at the moment of maximum attention, in Vell's own mouth. The adult reader stops to do sums during the consent argument; the younger reader is simply unsure whether the boy has a rating or not.
- **Proposed scope:** two or three sentences, no structural change. Make Vell's two counts refer to the same set (or have her say which set each is); make the Log's "Fifteenth" match or name its counter; add one clause, anywhere between line 651 and line 1455, that distinguishes "assessed" from "rated" (e.g. in Vell's "Those are the terms" speech, which is already about reading the book carefully).
- **Strength to preserve:** Vell's consent speech (1421–1427), "Then you should have written it better" (1417), and Feryn reading the page with his finger before complaining (1413). Do not touch the argument, only the arithmetic inside it.

### Priority 2 — Remove the duplicated "nothing came" beat (chapters 41–42)

- **Location:** lines 237–263 (first courtyard attempt after Kestrel) and lines 305–309 (second courtyard attempt, same wall, same result, same simile of the sleeping dog).
- **Observed issue:** the second attempt re-reports the first ("the unfinished feeling was there again... perhaps a little nearer than it had been on Monday, or perhaps he only wanted it to be"). Together with the four-hour watch at 219–233, the movement's first ninety lines of Kestrel are three scenes of a boy standing still and feeling nothing, before the movement's real question is asked.
- **Effect on the reader:** this is the only stretch where both lenses slip. The younger reader checks the chapter length; the adult reader trusts the method but wants the half-heartbeat in the heels (line 317) sooner. Because it is the first chapter of the movement, it also sets the mid-book reader's expectation of pace lower than the rest earns.
- **Proposed scope:** cut or compress 305–309 to a single sentence folded into the end of the Thursday gate scene (303), so chapter 42 goes from Kestrel's "Morning" straight to Friday's grey half. Optionally tighten 227–233 by a third. Keep 237–263 intact; "not the Arbiter's kind of nothing" (line 247) is load-bearing and pays off at 1805–1809.
- **Strength to preserve:** the keys-and-locks passage (141–151), which is the movement's governing image and must stay exactly where it is; "Find out. Don't guess." (181); and the honesty of "One morning isn't three months" (263).

### Priority 3 — Thin the pause-beat and clear two draft artefacts (whole movement, line level)

- **Location:** "for a long time" at 28 sites (89, 177, 223, 279, 337, 351, 363, 375, 467, 497, 539, 573, 717, 793, 801, 811, 961, 1005, 1015, 1065, 1283, 1427, 1445, 1623, 1773, 1825, 1867, 1921), plus 14 "for a while", 6 "for some time", 9 "did not look up"; line 189 ("at Copper 3... at Copper 3"); lines 955 and 1617 (`[SHATTERED]`, if the brackets are not intended to print); line 1551 ("on the second Sunday").
- **Observed issue:** the same silence-beat is used for Cael thinking, Lira deciding, Vell writing, Corvane considering, Feryn weighing, Doss setting down a pipe. After the first dozen the ear hears the author rather than the character. Line 189 is a doubled phrase; line 1551's referent needs a reread.
- **Effect on the reader:** read-aloud cadence flattens in narration (both lenses scored it 7 while scoring dialogue higher); emotionally distinct silences (Lira at 497, Vell at 1427, Feryn at 1715) start to sound alike.
- **Proposed scope:** a line pass only. Replace roughly a third of the "long time" beats with the specific thing the character does while silent (the manuscript already does this well at 1005: "put the pipe down beside his bowl with great care"). Fix 189. Decide whether `[SHATTERED]` prints as a word or stays a token; if it prints, lose the brackets. Clarify 1551 ("the way his own eyes had stayed on Renn after he found the heel" or similar).
- **Strength to preserve:** the Log's voice, which is where the deliberate repetitions live and earn their keep ("Might. One data point.", line 1799); the intentional callbacks ("standing in my light", the quarter-turn of the cup, "Survivable"); and every silence in Lira's room (417–525), which should be left exactly as written.
