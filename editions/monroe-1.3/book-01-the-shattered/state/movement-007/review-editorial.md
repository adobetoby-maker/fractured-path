## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Chapter 41's keys at the mender's — a key that has lived in a lock carries the lock's shape "whether anybody meant it to or not" — is the movement's best image and it is earned twice over, in Cael's distrust of it ("comfortable thoughts were the ones that let you stop asking questions") and in the cash box of letters that pays it in ch45. The old man's "Now they'll plan for you" is the right size. Chapter 42 is the emotional centre and it lands: Cael asking to come to her room "because it's partly yours", Lira's cutaway putting the desk down before it can speak, "Stealing's when somebody's got less afterward. I haven't got less. I checked." Chapter 43's eel woman ("Don't.") is the funniest beat in the book and Lira having to stop walking is exactly timed. Chapter 44's Corvane lesson is a genuine learning encounter with a body in it: "And what doesn't move?" turns the whole method inside out in four words, and the bucket she sits on afterwards is the best piece of indirect characterisation in the movement. Chapter 46's bout: the first exchange's "the whole sky" after Corvane's "a hand laid on his chest", the forty-one-person intake of breath, Feryn learning Lira's own trick in one exchange, and the fourth exchange where "Feryn simply agreed with him." Chapter 47's "one data point" discipline and the old man's "Four."

**Where it slipped.** Chapter 45 is the loosest chapter: the "district knew" montage (Red Cap, Renn, Marrow, Sarel, Dessa) is good supporting-cast work but its chronology runs Saturday → Sunday → Monday and then the next section steps back to "the second Saturday" for Vell's card, so a reader tracking days stumbles; and the sentence "He did not tell anybody about the step" arrives two pages after Lira has watched him do the step and told him to log it, which reads as a contradiction before the reader works out it means "anybody else". Chapter 41's long italic Log entries are dense for a thirteen-year-old and for audio, though the content is the point. Vell's "Go away. You're standing in my light" is used twice in one movement.

**Which person matters.** Lira, then Feryn. Vell's reason for not overruling consent (the girl she turned away) gives her the book's best single page of backstory.

**What I expect next.** The senior evaluator's arrival; the Pressure letter to Hesk; Dessa's "talk owing"; Corvane's "come back".

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

## 2. Two lenses

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** The bout is completely followable: four exchanges, each with a plain change (couldn't find it / found it late and paid an arm / found it early and the step came / found it and it didn't matter). The science of it — watch what doesn't move — is the kind of trick a thirteen-year-old will try on a friend. The telling in Lira's room is readable as friendship; nothing in it needs explaining. The eel woman, the goat and the canal will be retold. Possible friction: the Log entries in ch41 (three long italic blocks in one chapter) and the word "inconclusive" doing a lot of work; "sat Bronze panels" is unclear to anyone.

**Adult genre reader.** The movement is unusually honest about mechanism — every hypothesis is hedged in Cael's own voice and the two "doors" are left as doors — and the fight is written as decisions, with the opponent learning twice, which is rarer than it should be. The adult will notice the near-paraphrase density of the bout and notice-night against source ch17 (events are canon; prose is new; the tool reports zero shared runs), and will notice a few repeated gestures (Vell's quarter-turn of the tea, now also Feryn's quarter-turn of his cup; the "stair that isn't there" image used in ch40 and again in ch46; "quickly and completely" twice). None of it costs the read.

## 4. The Feryn bout judged as a high-stakes fight (seat VOICE profile and VOICE_CHARTER §4)

- **Loss teaches (4.5):** *Reading the build is necessary and not sufficient; at a professional's speed the margin has to come from something other than the read.* Written in the Log in ch47. Its downstream use is owed to M9 (Darrow) — pair it there.

**Calendar — two wrong lines (concrete defect).** Assessed-Copper was given on day 97 (ch40, "come to my table on Tuesday"). **Feryn's ages — internal contradiction (concrete defect).** ch43 and ch46: Vell first saw him "Copper and nineteen"; he is "twenty-four now, or near it" (five years), yet she has watched him "over six or seven years". ch47: "his first Bronze evaluation, at nineteen". A Copper at nineteen cannot sit a Bronze evaluation at nineteen (Bible: ten ranks per tier, advancement "not instant", Iron between). One pass fixes it: make the first evaluation "at twenty-one" (or "twenty-two") and Vell's span "five or six years", or move her first sighting to "Iron and nineteen". Also ch47 "I sat Bronze panels for a year" reads as "served as an assessor"; the source has "I fought in Bronze evaluations for a year. Panels, months of it" and Book 2 calls Feryn a man who "taught himself" (B2 ch18). Prefer "I stood in front of Bronze panels for a year."
