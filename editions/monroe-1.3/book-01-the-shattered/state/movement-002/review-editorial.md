## 2. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** Ch 7's first page: the thumb on the card, fifty paces of readiness, and then "Nobody asked." The true note put in the wrong place (the gate was across the river) is a better opening lesson than any speech. Torvin's "Don't want it." Yeni's "Market, Cael." The old man in the yard who saw Cael look at the fourth position instead of the sixth. The board decoded as a load sheet with the headings torn off. Ch 9 all the way through: Lira is faster and funnier than I expected, and the exam-was-testing-obedience exchange is the movement's first real mutual recognition. Ch 10's Ressa with two loaves wrapped out of habit, and "That one was his." Ch 11's applause for the loser. Ch 12's terms said back in his own words. The bout. Ch 13's Vell writing eight lines more than the book needs and refusing to say what any of it means.

**Where it slipped.** Ch 8 is a long tour (seven scenes, 5.8k) and the pressure that carries it is only the pouch and curiosity until the board appears at the end; it is sustained by the quality of noticing, not by want. Ch 10 opens on Hesk for 3,500 words immediately after "Cinder House. Tomorrow… I won't be late," so the promised scene is deferred — rewarding for an adult reader, slower for a thirteen-year-old. The end of ch 11 and the second half of ch 13's walk both re-list what the reader has just been shown (ch 13 gives Renn's tells three times inside one chapter: Cael's speech to Lira, the narrative's recap, and Vell's entry). The bout itself is cut into five sections by rules, which makes a 2,400-word fight read in places like a report in paragraphs.

**Which person matters.** Lira, by a distance — and the book is right that she should. Then Vell, whose two scenes plus cutaway make her the movement's second discovery. Hesk's cutaway keeps Denvash alive without stealing the movement. Renn, in six lines after the bout, becomes a person worth meeting again.

**What I expect next.** Three weeks of losing on purpose; Lira's "tonight's answer" coming due; the two uncounted stones (cart, third exchange) forcing a word; Hesk's letter arriving; the Renn return at a month.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. This is a stronger movement than its budget overrun suggests, and the overrun is in the right places (the bout, the cutaways) rather than in padding — with the exception noted in Priority 1.

### 4b. Apparent inconsistencies (APPARENT — internal, not canon)

1. **Lira's academy arithmetic (ch 9, 701–709).** "Wind Path students learn a deflection in the second year" and her practical was "against a fourth-year." Kindling is at fourteen (LOCKED); Lira is "perhaps by a year" older than a boy who turned fourteen days ago; she has been in the district four months. A Wind student can only be a Wind student after Kindling, so she had at most eight to ten months at the academy — a first-year, not someone taught a second-year deflection. A thirteen-year-old who has just had "Kindling at fourteen" drilled into them in M1 may do this sum. Cheapest fix: drop the year-count ("Wind students learn a deflection early. Three parts." / "against a senior student"). Alternatively make her nearly two years older — but BOOK_MAP fixes her at 15, so the first fix is the safe one.
2. **"The book" referent in Vell's cutaway (ch 13).** At 2075 "the book did not care whether she knew. The book cared what had happened" — her ledger. Thirty lines later her private note reads "Lasted four. Book said one." — Marrow's book. The ambiguity is deliberate in Vell's spoken line to Cael (2023), where it works; in the cutaway's interior, a listener will hear "Book said one" as her own ledger predicting, which it cannot do. Fix: "Marrow had him at one." or "Slate said one."
3. **"Oh. That's actually smarter." (Tier B) is split by a tag** (ch 13, 2135: `"Oh," she said, in a different voice altogether. "That's actually smarter."`). The spoken words are intact and identical by ear, so I do not call this a violation; but Tier B says "keep exact; surrounding prose is free," and the surrounding prose has been put inside the line. If the coordinator reads Tier B as character-for-character, move the tag after "smarter."
4. **Lira's lodging (ch 13, 2203).** "This is me," pointing down a lane. BOOK_MAP §3c/§3d has Lira saving for and taking her own room in M5–M6 as a beat. The line does not say "my room", so nothing is contradicted — but M3–M4 must keep her lodging unspecified (shared, rented corner, anything but "her room") so the M5–M6 beat remains a change.
5. **Cael's own two rules dropped without acknowledgement.** His rise entry (ch 6, 257): "Find work. Watch everything. Show nobody anything. Be dull. Be competent. Be still here." Within four days he pays a week's rent, never looks for work, and puts his name down for a public bout. Hesk's Tier B instruction "Don't let anyone see you move. Not until you know what you're doing." (M1 ch 3, 251) is never recalled. His decision reasoning (ch 11, 1567: "staying out of it would cost him more") is good but is made against the Stone woman's lesson only, never against the two rules he is overturning. The event (Renn ~day 8) is canon; the weighing is the edition's job. This is Priority 2.

## 8. Rulings on the author's three flagged choices

**(a) Cael's name kept out of Vell's ledger until Darrow — SAFE, and better than the plan, with two conditions.**
BOOK_MAP §1 says Vell's ledger "lists him by name for the first time" after Darrow, and §7 Tier B gives the exact entry (*Cael Hesk-ward, assessed-Copper, def. Darrow Innes…*). The author's custom — debuts are entered by description; "a name went into the book when the yard had a reason to need it" (ch 13, 2049) — makes that ending earnable instead of arbitrary. Conditions for later movements: (1) the custom as written covers *debuts*; by M4 (Dessa, his first win) and M6 (assessed-Copper, a 4–2 run, Renn II booked by name) a reader will ask why a rated regular is still nameless. The planner should fix the rule one notch firmer in M3–M4 (for example: the book carries a name only when a bout is *booked against it* by another fighter or backed on a marquee — which is exactly what the Darrow wager does), and the M6 "For what it's worth. I do." assessment must stay a spoken judgment and a margin note, not a named ledger line. (2) Vell has never heard "Hesk-ward" — he gave "Just Cael" and she wrote something shorter than that. For the Tier B entry to exist, the full name must reach Vell between M5 and M9 (the summons, Ilsev's evaluation, or the wager terms are all natural carriers). Plant it; do not let the Darrow entry be the first time the book — or the reader — hears Vell use the surname.

**(b) The cart written in the notebook as bare fact, uncounted; the third exchange unexamined; M3's sparring to be his "first counted instance" — SAFE, with the arithmetic now owed to the planner.**
M1's plan said "unwritten until Lira's question." The author has him write it the night of Lira's question (ch 9, 915–917: "I told the carter I heard it coming. I don't think I did."), which pays Hesk's "write everything down" and is truer to a boy who copies archive entries so that he will not have to simply know them. It does not touch canon. But the pile now holds **two** uncounted stones (ch 13, 2197: "now there were two things in the corner, and he did not let himself count them"), and the cart is half-examined (he has concluded he did not hear it) while Renn stays wholly unexamined. BOOK_MAP §6's count ("sparring = first instance (M3); at Dessa he writes 'second instance' and sorts the Renn stone in honestly") was already muddled and did not include the cart. Rulings: M3's "first instance" must be written as his first *counted* instance with both uncounted stones visible to the reader — a conscious act of not-counting, not an author slip; and M4's honest sort must produce a numbered list that includes the cart (cart, Renn, sparring, Dessa) or the Book 3 plant's "first" becomes a continuity error for any attentive reader. The planner should write that list into the M3 packet now.

**(c) "Secondweek" — SAFE; add it to the edition's vocabulary.**
The source already supplies Thirdweek and Fourthweek (BOOK_MAP packet; ch 8, 553 and 563). "Secondweek" fills the same series and adds no new calendar system; it coexists with named weekdays (Tuesday is canon) the way "the second week of the month" coexists with "Tuesday." Its one use is a result notice dated before the upcoming Thirdweek card — internally consistent. I see nothing in the supplied canon that a numbered-week series could contradict. Recommend: enter First/Second/Third/Fourthweek in the edition's vocabulary so later movements use the series consistently and never improvise a "Fifthweek"; keep it to notices and circuit talk, where it already lives.

## 9. The reader's experience of the Renn bout (ch 12, 1807–1954; aftermath ch 13)

- **Trackable.** Every exchange has a readable problem and a readable change: probes → heel → flat on the shoulder (paid for the tell); moved on the heel → the sweep kept for clever beginners → the unused half-beat; the figure → an evasion he did not make → a punch "a child could have swatted"; no probes → down in four seconds. A thirteen-year-old can follow it with no prior fight-reading; an adult reader sees the fight's logic.
- **Cost is real and carried.** Shoulder, ribs, the forearm scrape he cannot account for (ch 13, 1969 — a fine small horror: an injury from the part of the fight he was not present for). The inventory on his elbow and knee is the right register.
- **Reveal discipline inside the bout is excellent.** "And then he was somewhere else." The mind "arrived at each new place after his body was already in it, and found it lit and waiting." He tries an explanation and sees it does not fit. In the fourth exchange he reaches for it and "his hand closed on air" — so the thing is shown to be undependable before Lira or Vell can wonder about it. This is the Second entry's instinct happening to a boy who refuses to watch it, which is the movement's engine.
- **Where it costs the reader.** Five section breaks inside one fight (1809, 1827, 1859, 1887, 1933) turn the pauses between exchanges into typographic stops; the bout is already structured by Renn walking back to his mark, so the rules are redundant and they are also the main reason this chapter has eight scene breaks. The one literary sentence mid-bout — "as a lid cut for a smaller jar does not fit" (1913) — is immediately excused by the text ("the middle of a bout was no place to stand doing arithmetic"), which is charming but is still two sentences of standing still. The narration's "he wrote it down in his head" device (1875, 1931) is right for Cael but used three times in one fight.
- **Aftermath.** Renn's six lines are the best Renn has ever been; "And I want the second look" gives him §3d's private want in his own mouth. Vell's "It's what happened." is a clean replacement for the source's line. Lira's "How?" / "Instinct." / "That's tonight's answer." lands exactly where the packet leaves pressure. The walk home is then over-extended by a narrative recap of the tells Cael has just spoken aloud (Priority 1).

Do not shorten the fight to save words. The repair is to remove the rules and one standing-still sentence, not exchanges.
