# Movement 002 review — chapters 7–13 (simulated cold read)

**Review type:** fresh-context simulated cold read. One model (Claude Fable 5.1), manuscript only. I read the wrapper and the compiled prompt (`review-cold-prompt.md`) and nothing else: no canon, no plan, no drafting history, no earlier chapters. This is not a real audience measurement and not a self-review. Where I report an inconsistency it is an *apparent* one found inside these seven chapters, not a verified canon violation.

**Coverage:** seven chapters supplied in declared order, 7 through 13. The movement reads as complete: it opens on arrival in Ardenmere and closes on the walk home after the debut bout. I judged it as a reader arriving mid-book.

**Locations** are given as chapter + anchor phrase, with line numbers from `review-cold-prompt.md` in brackets for the owner's convenience.

## Part 1 — Unscored reader response

### Is a mid-book reader oriented?

Yes, and faster than I expected. By the end of chapter 7 I know: the boy is Cael, fourteen, from Denvash, raised by a grandfather called Hesk who works at a bench; he has a registration card with something on its classification line that he expects to be challenged about; the world has Paths (Stone, Force, Wind, Blade, Iron, Shield, Ash) and a registry with tiers (Copper, Iron, Bronze, Silver) and ranks; Ardenmere has a walled Ranked core across the river and an Unranked District that has nothing to close; he keeps a notebook and counts things. Chapter 8 tells me the card carries "one word in square brackets, and nothing else" and that a notice forced a choice at home. Chapter 9 says the word. Chapter 10's Hesk section fills in the rest ("It's easier to make a family choose").

What I never learn and did not need: what exactly happened at Weaver's Row / the circle / the brass plate "that lit eleven seconds late"; what the bracket, the rig, the springs drawer and the washer were about; what Hesk has "not written in thirty-two years". These are carried as texture and plants. They read as deliberate withholding, not as gaps.

### Where interest caught

- Ch 7, "Nobody asked." [71] — a one-line answer to the dread the first page sets up. Good early beat.
- Ch 7, the nut-stall boy and his sister: "Third." / "Fourth on a wet day." [243–245]. First laugh. First people I wanted to see again.
- Ch 8, the bridge: the man with the sack turned back at the booth, and "it was not a table. It was a man with a sack walking back the way he had come." [437–445]. This is where the book's politics became a person.
- Ch 8, the board that advertises without naming its subject [547–579]. A genuine puzzle, read like "a load sheet with the headings torn off". From here on I was in.
- Ch 9, Lira from "You've been reading that board for eleven minutes." [633] onward. The movement's engine arrives.
- Ch 9, Cael's hand going to a strap that isn't there, and the dip between the hedges [831–839]. The turnip cart, planted in ch 7 as a thing he wouldn't write down, pays here. Very satisfying.
- Ch 10, Ressa's two wrapped loaves [951–971]. The best grief beat in the movement, done with bread.
- Ch 11, the first bout, "She let him cut her ... On purpose." [1379], and the crowd clapping for the woman who conceded [1437–1441]. "How you lose matters" lands as a rule I believe.
- Ch 11 ending, "Find out what it costs her before I let her spend it." [1579]. Best chapter close in the set.
- Ch 12, the whole debut [1829–1953]. The heel. The sweep. "And then he was somewhere else." [1897]. "Match." [1951].
- Ch 13, Vell's rule: "Write what you saw. Not what you understood." [2081] and "You didn't lose your name tonight?" / "No. Marrow did." [2213–2217].

### Where it slipped

- Ch 7's first six paragraphs are landscape and inventory before anything is at stake beyond the card. I kept reading because the prose is clean, not because I was pulled.
- Ch 8 is a tour: kitchen, strap, nut boy, carriers, bridge, workshops, yards, other market, board. Five of those are set pieces with the same shape (Cael watches a long time, someone says an epigram, he leaves), and three of them (the mangle-mender, the old man in the yard, the woman who sells answers) are not touched again in this movement. The bridge and the board are the two that had to be there. I felt the chapter's length.
- Ch 10 opens on Hesk with no signpost beyond the sentence itself. I relocated within a paragraph, but I noticed the gear change, and the chapter title "The Rules" belongs to the second half.
- Ch 13 after "Match." retells the bout four times: Cael aloud to Lira, Lira's explanation of the figure, Vell's ledger, then Cael's silent inventory on the walk. Vell's pass is essential. The silent inventory duplicates what Cael has just said out loud three hundred words earlier.
- Ch 9, the dray that nearly hits the skewer woman arrives at the exact moment Lira is explaining moving-before-deciding [799]. The carts "going through toward the yards" are seeded at the chapter's open, so it is not unprepared, but I saw the author's hand.

### Which person matters

Lira first. She is the one with something at risk that is not Cael's (her name, four months of it), and she is funny. Vell second: she becomes the movement's most credible witness, and her ledger is the place where the mystery becomes a fact rather than a feeling. Hesk, though he is only in half of one chapter, is the person I most wanted a letter from. Renn surprised me by mattering: "I want the second look" [1997] made him a person rather than an obstacle.

### Which event I expect next

Most immediately: Hesk's letter arriving at Torvin's (with the guild review in it) and crossing Cael's. Then the return bout against Renn at a month. Behind both: Yeni collecting her favour, the thing in the corner that Cael will not count ("now there were two things in the corner" [2197]) recurring, and someone, probably Vell or Lira, pressing past "Instinct". I also expect the old man in the yard and the answer-seller to come back; if they do not, chapter 8 will have spent more than it bought.

### Would I continue?

Yes, without hesitation. I would have kept going at the end of chapter 8 on the strength of the board alone, and from chapter 9 on the question was never open.

## Part 3 — Canon, physical state, power limits, knowledge boundaries, reserved disclosures

No canon or plan was supplied, so everything below is *apparent* inconsistency or confirmation found inside the seven chapters. None rises to a verified violation.

**Timeline — holds.** Cael's notebook dates (Day three cart [915], Day four arrival [263]) match "two days of the merchant road" [125] and "one night at the waystation" [329]. Hesk's side runs: depot day 3, Ressa day 4 ("He'll get to Ardenmere tonight" [979] matches Cael's arrival), guild letter day 5 ("second morning after the depot" [997]), Tuesday day 6 ("three mornings ago" [1023]; "six days" since Wednesday [1051, 1129]). Cael's days 5–8 (ch 8, 9, 10–11, 12–13) sit alongside. Lira's "I've known you a day" [1163] and "I've watched you for two days" [1615] are both right.

**Physical state — holds.** Left shoulder hit in exchange one [1845], right ribs in exchange four [1947], left forearm scrape of unknown origin [1969]. Renn pats "the right shoulder, the one that had not been hit" [1999]: correct. Ch 13 inventories all three consistently [1963–1971, 2185].

**Power limits — consistent as stated.** Lira's Copper Wind is defined as "slightly more negotiable round you, for about a second" [815] and her two displays (the dray [803–805]; walking backward without looking [871, 2217]) sit inside that. Cael's anomaly is never named or sized; Vell writes it as observation only [2085]. Nothing in the movement contradicts anything else in the movement about what a Path can do.

**Knowledge boundaries — holds.** The word is said once, to Lira [759]. Vell is told "Nothing to register" [1663] and explicitly chooses not to ask [2105]. Renn asks and gets the same phrase [1989–1993]. Lira at the table says "when I asked him what he was, he told me" [1705] without saying what. Lira's claim slightly overstates ch 9 (she asked about his declaration, not "what he was" [733–747]) but it is a fair gloss.

**Reserved disclosures — handled.** "The file he had watched opened at twenty-two" [1117]; "things he had not written in thirty-two years" [1131]; "the question had already been answered by someone who was not in the room" [719]; the two things in the corner [2197]. All withheld cleanly; none leaks.

**Apparent inconsistencies (low confidence, manuscript-internal):**

1. *Lira's academy years vs declaration age.* Lira reads as about fifteen [637, 893]. She left an academy four months ago having learned a second-year deflection [701] and sat a practical against a fourth-year [709], and she holds a registry tier. Lira also says a registry tier is "a word somebody stamped on you at fourteen" [1209]. If academies only follow declaration, two-plus academy years do not fit in the months between fourteen and now. If academies precede declaration this is fine. The manuscript does not say. Low confidence; may be plan adherence.
2. *Vell's end-word.* "Called." for the lock and the concession [1387, 1435]; "Match." for the knockdown [1951]; then the ledger reads "Called." [2047] and "Result stands as called" [2017]. Possibly a deliberate knockdown/concession distinction; not explained. Low.
3. *Renn's reserved figure.* Lira says Renn throws it "when he's decided you're worth throwing it at" [1743]; Vell has seen it "nine times since" the end of last season [2065] against "Eleven bouts this season" [1709]. Nine of eleven strains "reserved". Low; may be intended (it is his finisher, not a rarity).
4. *Marrow's loss.* "Everybody had him at one" [2041] followed by "That cost me" requires the reader to supply that some money went on the over at long odds and Marrow did not hedge. Lira's "He'd laid nothing off on the over" [2217] confirms it. Inferable; not an error. Flagged only because the thirteen-year-old lens stumbles here.

## Repair brief

Three priorities. Retain the developed action: nothing below shortens the debut or the chapter 11 bouts.

### Priority 1 — Chapter 13 retells the bout four times

- **Location:** Ch 13, Vell's ledger [2047–2091]; Cael aloud to Lira "He opens everything with the same two probes" [2137–2141]; Lira's account of the figure [2155–2159]; Cael's silent inventory "The probes and their rhythm. The heel. The shoulders..." [2187–2193].
- **Observed issue:** after "Match." the same four facts (probes, heel, shoulders, the sweep) are listed by Cael aloud, then by Vell in writing, then listed again silently by Cael on the walk home. The silent inventory adds nothing the reader has not just heard in Cael's own voice three hundred words earlier.
- **Effect on the reader:** the thirteen-year-old skims Vell to get back to the walk and then finds the walk repeating the yard; the adult reader notices the duplicate list and the chapter's momentum sags between two of its best beats ("Instinct" and "Marrow did").
- **Proposed scope:** compress the silent inventory [2191] to the one thing it adds, the unsorted item in the corner [2195–2199], and let the earlier spoken recap carry the list. Keep Vell's section whole. Optionally let Lira's explanation of the figure [2155] stand in for part of Cael's spoken recap rather than following it. Punctuation and paragraph-level work, not a rewrite.
- **Strength to preserve:** Vell's entire POV section, especially "Write what you saw. Not what you understood." [2081] and her private last line [2111]; the "Instinct" / "tonight's answer" exchange [2163–2177]; "You didn't lose your name tonight?" / "No. Marrow did." [2213–2217].

### Priority 2 — POV handoffs are unsignposted

- **Location:** Ch 10 opens on Hesk [931] under the title "The Rules", which belongs to the Cinder House half; return to Cael after a bare rule [1137]; Ch 13 switches to Vell after a bare rule [2033].
- **Observed issue:** the movement is Cael-close for three chapters and then changes head three times with no marker beyond a horizontal rule and a first sentence that names the new holder. Each handoff does land within a paragraph, but each costs a relocation, and the ch 10 one asks a mid-book reader to spend 1,900 words with a grieving adult before the chapter's titular material.
- **Effect on the reader:** the thirteen-year-old is most exposed in ch 10, where the Hesk half reads as a pause in the story they are following; the adult reader is briefly unsure whether the Fen Street house is a flashback. Neither is lost; both are slowed.
- **Proposed scope:** signpost, do not cut. Either split ch 10 at [1135] so the Hesk material stands as its own short chapter or titled interlude and "The Rules" begins at the Cinder House; or add a place/person head at each switch (ch 10 [931], ch 10 [1137], ch 13 [2033]). The Vell section could carry a similar head. No sentence-level change required.
- **Strength to preserve:** Ressa's two loaves [951–971]; Joren's "I read it standing up. Flat-footed." [989]; Garrik's washer [1085]; the unsealed letter left under the clock and the door left open a hand's width [1133]; Vell's POV entire.

### Priority 3 — Chapter 8's tour cadence and unpaid plants

- **Location:** Ch 8, the mangle-mender [453–477]; the old man in the yard [495–517]; the woman who sells answers [527–541].
- **Observed issue:** three set pieces in a row share one shape (Cael watches "nearly half an hour" / "a long time", someone delivers a line, he leaves) and none is touched again inside the movement. The mangle-mender and the old man also carry the same theme (watching and checking as craft), which the chapter title already states and the bridge scene has already shown.
- **Effect on the reader:** this is the stretch where the thirteen-year-old is most likely to stop; the board at [547] rescues the chapter, but only if the reader reaches it. The adult reader tolerates the length because the plants are visible, but notices the repeated cadence.
- **Proposed scope:** not a cut for word count. Either tighten one of the two "watching" set pieces (the mangle-mender's seven paragraphs could lose two without losing "IF IT'S STILL HERE" [467] or "Something of yours broken?" [473]), or give one plant a small live consequence inside the movement (for example, the old man's "Come back when you've got coin for yard time" [517] glanced at when Cael walks up past the yards in ch 12 [1595–1597] or [1783]). Either change is a paragraph, not a scene.
- **Strength to preserve:** the bridge-gate scene and "It was a man with a sack walking back the way he had come." [427–449]; "Watching's not nothing." [501]; the board from "In the middle of it, on a post, there was a board." through "it was easier" [547–609].
