## 1. Unscored reader response

**Where interest caught.** The board at the triangle and the list of fourteen names, five without names (ch 54) — a reason to care before any explanation. Vell's third condition and then "Has the girl been told?" asked in the same voice as the card (ch 54). The fourth stair, and Lira's "That's telling. Asking's when it isn't." Lira's cutaway (ch 55): the coal stairs at the academy, the term said to a paper-mended pane until it sounds like her, and the first coin ever taken out of the tin buying a lumpy mattress. The straw post that gives nothing back and the five chalk marks shrinking to a cup. The chalk-board woman turning out to be the week-two Stone woman. Hesk reaching into the dark and putting his hand on the word *drawer*. The washer bill with every road running to one drain, and the empty *Ruling* line held open until the bout. Coss at the gate, unshaven, on unpaid days. Then the bout: Darrow paying at the gate, reading feet first; Vell saying "Cael Hesk-ward" at the rope; the floor over the river; the slow sixth; Darrow leaning on a gate; the Wind spent in public; the market-gate carts; and the tenth strike thrown with half an arm of sand and nothing behind it, "a noise like a latch." Vell's dry pump. "I'd hate it to be free." The old man giving the one-degree nod instead of a number. Torvin and the chair.

**Where it slipped.** Ch 57's pre-bout is cut into five short scenes before the first exchange, and the morning after (ch 59) into three; both read as fragments of one continuous stretch. Ch 55's two short veteran scenes (Doss; the chalk-board woman) tell the same fact twice in a row — deliberately ("two was not three"), but the second one lands as repetition before it lands as pattern. Ch 59's market walk is a list. The Coss cutaway (ch 60) is the one place a younger reader may push through rather than be pulled — procedure, register, query form — though it is short and ends on the daughter's carried seven, which rescues it.

**Which person matters.** Lira, by a distance; then Coss; then Vell. Darrow is exactly as large as he needs to be and no larger.

**Which event I expect next.** The stranger and Coss's grey slip are two things that do not yet know about each other; I expect them to collide. Darrow's autumn hangs as an open offer.

**Would I continue.** Yes.

## 2. Two lenses and scores

Lenses, not demographics. Scores are editorial judgments (5 = understandable but inconsistent; 7 = engaging with located weaknesses; 9 = compelling with few substantial distractions).

**Thirteen-year-old reader.** The bout is followable because every piece was taught first: the straw, the bucket, the gutter drawing, "they do it themselves." The partnership mistake is legible — "You arranged this without asking me first" — and its repair is not a speech but a confession ("I didn't ask you because I was afraid you'd say no"). Lira's cutaway gives this reader the emotional key to the whole movement. Risks: the Coss cutaway's bureaucracy; the stranger is deliberately featureless and may read as a non-event rather than a chill (Lira's "that's not better" does the work).

**Adult genre reader.** The restraint is the pleasure: Vell not writing *atypical*; Coss's first empty page; Hesk's pride placed before the result; the notice that "wrote less than Vell." The crossing is built from evidence the reader watches accumulate, and the tenth strike is a choice made with a cost already arriving. Risks: the scene fragmentation above; dialogue tags thick in the talk chapters; one or two same-slot images that stand where the source's images stood (see §5).

### 3d. Seams found (each has a line fix in §7)

- **Knowledge boundary — ch 60.** Cael tells Lira the autumn pin man "was only borrowed." The lent pin and the bridge constable exist only in Coss's cutaway (ch 28, Halvern to Coss); M5 and the M8 rulings both hold that Cael does not know this. Fix.
- **Lira's cutaway vs the post — ch 55.** At the straw post Lira says "I asked Doss last night in your kitchen." Her cutaway has her in her room from Cael's leaving until she falls asleep, then at the widow's at first light, then straight up to the Cinder House. There is no night visit to Torvin's kitchen. Doss comes in off his watch at dawn (ch 59), so the ask fits the morning. Fix.
- **Coss's calendar — ch 60.** Coss came to the gate on Wednesday day 196 and left on the noon cart the same day (ch 56; author's calendar). From Wednesday day 203 that is a week, not "ten days"; "at his desk on the Monday after" would be day 201, which comes after the Sunday that the next sentence describes; and the return his section head put on his desk "on Monday" (ch 56, said on day 196 → day 194) is "Monday of last week" from day 203, not "a fortnight ago", and only one further return has come since, so "every week" overstates. Four fixes.
- **The count — ch 58.** At the top of the fourth exchange "every one of Cael's ten had gone in by" — nine have gone in; the tenth is the strike this exchange produces. Fix.
- **Attribution — ch 57.** "It was what Feryn had drawn on the wall." Feryn showed the stance with his heel; Cael drew the man on the washer bill. Fix.
- **Punctuation — ch 56.** Feryn's tannery line loses its opening quotation mark after "he said," — a true audio defect. Fix.
- **Minor — ch 58.** Vell's *assessed Cu (vouched)* has stood "for seven months"; assessed-Copper dates from day 97 (M6), about three and a half months before. The brackets are seven months old; the assessment is not. Light fix.
- **Optional — ch 56.** "It had been written the night after Coss's pie stall and the cooper's roof." M8: Hesk writes it the same night. The sentence can be read either way; a one-word change removes the doubt.
- **Noted, no fix proposed.** Ch 55: the chalk-board woman says "I heard you've got an Iron coming" on day 184, two days before the card goes up; district talk (Feryn at Torvin's wall, Lira asking Doss) makes it plausible. Ch 54: "Five of them without names" counts Torvin's wife and Red Cap as names; defensible. Ch 59: Hesk's "You tell me she says you're more like me than you know" assumes Cael's letter carried Lira's line; ch 56 says the letter held "Lira's room, and what he had said in it" — close enough.

## 5. Source distance — spot-check of the bout (ch 57–58 beside source ch 22–23)

Coordinator figures used: 8-word overlap 0 unprotected / 8 protected; skeleton probe 1% (author-run; ch 57–58 discarded and rewritten after the author caught them tracking source shapes). Read beside the source, the rewrite holds:

- **Entry points are new**: the board with HESK-WARD and a *W* that does not meet; the pie boy's new groove; sixty-three at two hours; Darrow paying at the gate and closing the boy's fingers over the coin; the empty *Ruling* line held up by Lira; the floor over the river; the gate he leans on; the market-gate carts; the ladder rung; the latch; Vell's dry pump; the stake at the edge of a marsh. - **Shared beats are spine, not sentences**: the free first exchange; Darrow noticing in the second and speaking under the crowd's hearing; the guarded crossing in the third; the Wind shown in public; the thrown-where-he'll-go fourth; force returned up its own road; the tenth strike; the call; rising alone; the question; Lira's fear after; the notice; the fence sentence. All are BOOK_MAP §2.23.
- **Same-slot images, different pictures**: source "sat down on the chalk the way a ledger closes" → edition "knelt… as a man kneels in a field to look at something in the grass"; source "touch his shoulder like bridge-stone" → edition "step back for a heavy cart they have seen come safely down a hill"; source "I felt the tape" → edition's feet-first reading; source Darrow "It's a wall, son… There's no door" → edition "There's nowhere on me that's further from the ground than anywhere else, son. All of it goes down." The last pair is the closest in function and the least close in wording; acceptable.
- **Two near-shapes worth a glance, not a finding**: Darrow at the mark "breathing as a man breathes in a chair after supper" (source: "the breathing of a man reading in a chair"); and the pattern *he did not decide → he would go over it many times → it never came out with a choice in it* (source: "What happened next, he did not decide. He would go over it that night, and for many nights…"). Both are under the gate and the second is the spine's required beat; I would not touch either in repair.
- **Verdict**: the bout is the edition's own. The one thing the rewrite lost against the source is the baker explaining to a stranger that the boy counts his change twice — a line of warmth the edition has elsewhere (her own loaf, "You had your chance"), so nothing to restore.
