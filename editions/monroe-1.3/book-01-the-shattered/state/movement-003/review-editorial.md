**Review seat:** Claude Fable 5.1, standing in for the Sol seat (out of usage). **Author of the manuscript:** Claude Opus 5.5 (seat `oconnor` 1.3.0). I have no drafting history with this text. This is a **fresh-context editorial read**, informed by the supplied canon evidence, the author's report and the measured metrics — not a blind cold read, and not self-review. I did not re-estimate any metric; the numbers below are the wrapper's (`tools/formula_metrics.py`).

**Coverage:** all seven chapters (14–20) read in order and in full; chapter 13 read for the seam; the packet (including the coordinator's drafting notes) and AUTHOR-REPORT.md read as the author's map, not as evidence. Canon evidence used: EDITION_BRIEF, BOOK_MAP (§§1–11), STATE_LEDGER (entry, M1, M2), CANON_RULES, UNIVERSE_BIBLE. For one finding I also checked the edition's own chapter 4 for the notice text (quoted in chapter 20).

**One-line verdict:** the movement does what the packet asked — three losses that each teach a different thing, the Log as method, a plant the reader can see, the courtyard, the shared weight — and it does most of it well. The problems are a rhythm regression (short, clipped narration between the beats that earn shortness), four small calendar/number slips in a book whose hero counts everything, one line in chapter 20 that misreads the notice, and a handful of repeated beats an audiobook listener will hear twice.

## 1. Reader response (unscored)

**Where interest caught.**
- Chapter 14, the Log's invention: he writes the Renn entry in sentences, reads back *In the third I got out of the whole figure*, sees that the sentence "did not know either," tears the page out, and rules three columns. "That was what sentences did… You put a true thing in one end, and a guess came out the other in the same coat." This is the best teaching passage in the movement because it dramatises the method instead of describing it.
- Vell at the table: "A name goes in when the yard comes to see it." And Cael's response — "It was a ledger he could check. It had no word in square brackets in it."
- Brenna's "*Stand.*" / "Stand and *fight.*" and the discovery that anger makes her slower, not faster.
- Amrit: the dry shimmer as bait, the breath that stops before the real heat, and the raised palm at the groove. "That was the right breath." The quietest and most satisfying of the three bouts.
- Petra's seventh exchange: "He did everything right… Her palm was there anyway." Then "I'm losing on speed now. Not on seeing."
- The plant itself, and Lira alone in the yard trying the drop eleven times: "You could only already be going."
- The midnight yard: "It was the place where he had been told a door would be, and there was no door there."
- Lira's "It always—" and the stop.

**Where it slipped.**
- Chapter 17's opening (the wager) carries a long conversation for a small payload, and the day-ordering after it wobbles (see §3).
- Chapter 19's ledger meditation (Vell's book, Marrow's slate, the Log, the Arbiter) is well built but told in a run of "He thought about…" sentences that flatten before the courtyard.
- The same emotional beat — he cannot speak because of what his voice would do — lands in chapter 17 after Vell and again in chapter 20 after Lira. The second deflates the first.
- The clipped narration between beats (not the beats themselves) makes the whole movement read younger than it is.

**Which person matters:** Lira, by a distance — and the movement earns it: her cutaway, her refusal to "be a desk," and the morning report. Vell second. Amrit, surprisingly, third.

**What I expect next:** the man at Torvin's door to turn into something (or not); the Compact pin the packet promises; Renn "at the month"; Red Cap's wager coming due; Cael doing "something besides last."

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation.

### 3b. Continuity findings (prose vs prose, verified)
1. **Ch 17, after the wager:** "Since then he had walked into a circle four times with a crowd round it." On day 21 he has fought Renn, Brenna and Amrit — **three**. Petra is four days away. → "three times."
2. **Ch 18, Lira's cutaway:** "Three weeks later he had told her about her own left foot." He told her on the third morning after Renn (ch 15 opens "On the third morning Lira let him use both hands… 'Your weight went onto your left foot'") — day 11, four mornings after he first sat on the crate (day 7). → "Four mornings later" or "Within the week." (The author's report lists "three weeks" among its calendar fixes; this one was applied to the wrong interval.)
3. **Ch 16, Amrit:** "a cook's fist, behind forty years of lifting stockpots" — Amrit is "somewhere near forty." → "twenty years" (or "half a lifetime").
4. **Ch 17, day order:** the old man's "Six" is dated "the next morning" (day 22) and sits *before* "'Petra Voss,' Vell had said, at the table, the morning after Amrit" (day 21) and "Lira said the same… on the cooper's roof that afternoon." A listener cannot tell which afternoon. Either move the old-man beat after the roof/sand-bag scenes, or anchor "that afternoon" ("the afternoon of the wager").
5. **Ch 19:** "He had been carrying two things in a corner for four weeks." The cart is day 3 (≈3½ weeks); Renn's exchange is day 8 (under three weeks). "For weeks" is safe; "four weeks" is right only for the Arbiter, and the chapter uses it correctly there.
6. **Ch 20, Lira on the notice:** "Nobody in here says you have to leave. Nobody orders your grandfather to put you out." The notice as written in this edition's chapter 4 *does* say so for the house: "residential access above Unranked District designation is revoked effective immediately. The bearer retains legal access to Unranked-designated residence." Lira's larger point — the *note* paragraph is "built" to make Hesk add up the cost — is exactly right and should stay. Her first sentence needs a half-line of precision: nobody in it says he has to leave *the city* or his *grandfather*; it says he cannot stay in that house, then tells Hesk what following him would cost. (Apparent inconsistency verified against the edition's own ch 4; not a canon error.)
7. **Ch 19, the pear tree** appears for the first time at midnight ("a stunted pear tree in the corner by the wall") though Cael has sat at the low wall by the pump in ch 14 and ch 19 by day. One clause in ch 14 would seed it. (Keep the tree — see §4.)

Everything else I could test holds: the nineteenth-day letter arithmetic (posted day 5, arrives day 12, reply same evening, arrives day 19); Thursday → Brenna → Sunday Petra; "three weeks" for Lira's watching (day 7 → day 28); the left forearm scraped, then burned over the scrape, then "You can't catch with the burnt one"; the right forearm catching Brenna and Petra; the Renn ribs re-bruised on the right; Dessa (Cu 5, Stone) over Brenna in the third exchange, consistent across Vell, the board and Brenna herself; Marrow's over/under 1→2→3→4; Hesk's letter matching the M1/M2 ledgers (Ressa's loaf, Joren flat-footed, Garrik's washer, the review).

## 5. The three losses as learning fights

The packet asked for three different problems, each loss longer and more informative, and Baro watched. Delivered, and the order is right.

- **Brenna (ch 15, 5 exchanges, ~1,300 words of bout).** Problem: pressure that never resets. Learned: the dip on the step-through (found in the second exchange, counted in the third), and that frustration makes her "bigger, slower and louder." Cost: forearm, then chest, then "Giving ground was not free either." Honest loss: he goes in on a real opening and is "collected." The bout risks monotony (four exchanges of giving ground) and the author breaks it correctly with the rope's "Rabbit," Brenna's "*Stand.*," and the rib tiredness. Brenna at the bucket ("Dessa found it in the third… Took you four… He's fourteen") gives her a beat of her own. Strength: the back section asks whether his own going-in was anger — the first time the Log turns on him.
- **Amrit Sole (ch 16, 6 exchanges, ~1,600).** Problem: a tell that can be shown on purpose. Learned: move on the shimmer; the shimmer can be held; the breath stops before the real heat (two of three — "two out of three is not a ruling"); *being sure is a tell*. Cost: the burn over the scrape. Result: the first act in the circle that is wholly his — the raised palm. Amrit's grave courtesy and the cold-water lesson make him the warmest opponent in the book so far. Strength: "I know you know. That's why it was the right one."
- **Petra Voss (ch 17, 7 exchanges, ~2,300 — the most room).** Problem: speed. Learned: seeing is not reaching; she learns him inside the bout; Brenna's and Amrit's tells are no use against her. Cost: hip, lip, the Renn ribs. The seventh exchange is the movement's best fight writing: he does everything right and is lifted anyway. Vell's "Then you'll have to do something besides last" is the correct hinge into M4. Strength: Petra's "Nobody is, the first time," and his refusal to tell her the lean — "*Never tell anybody their tell unless I'm sure I don't need it*" paid.

Progression across the three is legible: adapt-and-fail-to-use → adapt-and-be-baited-then-choose → adapt-and-be-outrun. Baro (watched, two bouts) is the right counterweight: "take only the openings I make myself" is a ruling he cannot yet live by, and the text says so. Do not shorten any of them for the word count; the fight beats' short sentences are earned.
