# Movement 006 — Cold read (chapters 34–40)

**Read type:** fresh-context simulated cold read. One model (Claude Fable 5.1), manuscript only: the compiled review prompt and the seven chapters it contains. No canon, no plan, no notes, no drafting history. This is not a human audience measurement and should not be used as one.

**Coverage:** 7 chapters supplied in declared order (34 Efficient, 35 The Second Look, 36 A Week Behind, 37 Nothing to Look Up, 38 A Door, 39 The Plan That Survives, 40 Tier Equivalent). The movement reads as complete: it opens on the first morning of the six weeks and closes on the forty-second day. Line numbers below refer to review-cold-prompt.md.

---

## Unscored reader response

**Arriving mid-book: am I oriented?** Mostly, and fast. Within the first two scenes I had: Cael, fourteen, from Denvash, lodging at Torvin's in Ardenmere; Hesk the grandfather back home; Lira, fifteen, Wind Path, his trainer and voucher, who failed out of an academy; Vell and her ledger; Renn who beat him on his first night; a Log; six Paths with visible signatures (Ember shimmer, Stone grit, Blade along the forearm, Shield plate, Force shove, Wind step). Something went wrong at his "Kindling" at Weaver's Row — a sigil rose, said one word nobody had heard, went out — and a Compact summons has been deferred six weeks. The chapters trust me to assemble this from use rather than restating it, and the trust is mostly repaid.

What I was *not* oriented on, and noticed: the causal link between "name in more mouths" and the Compact evaluation is never restated in this stretch. I inferred that a boy who is known, vouched and entered in an honest book is harder to "physically retrieve" or easier to defend, but it is an inference. One sentence from Cael or Lira under the pear tree (ch34, ~line 73) connecting the bouts to the evaluator would have closed it. Also unexplained for a newcomer: "the cart" (one of the four kept things), Coss (ch40 line 2765), Pellin's office, the "hand at the board that moved before it should have." These read as plants from earlier chapters and did not block me, but they are the places a mid-book reader feels the floor missing.

**Where interest caught.**
- Ch34, the hinge crate (lines 117–141): a boy sorting hinges that will either go into a door or be forgotten, under the sign IF IT'S STILL HERE, while thinking about six weeks. Quiet, exact, and it told me what the stakes felt like without a speech.
- Ch34, "Gone" (187–237): the chalk marks, the girls on the wall doing the solemn voice, Lira *becoming him* and showing him his private nod. This is the hook of the movement — a ridiculous drill that I immediately wanted to see pay off.
- Ch35, Renn (609–751): the best fight in the stretch. Five exchanges, each a different problem — the turn across the back, the false heel and the stumble that did not quite happen, the sweep into empty dirt, going *right into the blade*, then the front heel and the wedge. Renn's post-bout "I let you have that, you know" / "You didn't" / "I know I didn't" is the warmest beat in the movement.
- Ch36, Joren holding the level for fifty minutes (927–943) and Alis washing the cut four times (985–1011). I did not expect the Hesk chapter to be where I got most attached, but it was.
- Ch37, Sarel (1249–1413): the method itself fails, out loud, in two exchanges, and he moves without deciding. Her crouching to say "Two" and "Don't ask me for another one" is a character I would read a whole book about.
- Ch37, the right foot (1521–1601): "You put my fight in the back of your book." This is the emotional spine of the movement and it lands hard because the reader was there at the rope with him when he ruled it out.
- Ch38, the door (1689–1736): "listened to it be shut." Earned.
- Ch40, Corbin (2341–2521): truth, lie, wait, ribs, truth. A three-beat con that the reader can follow in real time, and the yard saying *past Corbin* afterwards is exactly the payoff the opening promised.
- Ch40, FRAGMENT ACQUIRED (2593–2615) and the five-not-four count at the end.

**Where interest slipped.**
- Ch38, Talis (1861–1890). This is where the fights blur. Exchanges one through three are the same beat told three times — Talis is suddenly somewhere else, planted, and a strike comes from there — with only the landing spot changing (upper arm, hip, touch). Coming directly after a six-exchange loss, with the same body already hurt in the same places, it is the one bout where I read faster to get to the end. The face-on-the-dirt discovery (1897–1905) rescues it completely, but the bout itself is the thinnest of the six.
- The injury refrains. "He could not find his breath anywhere. He found it." (ch35 707–709, ch37 1363–1365). "It took two tries" / "three tries" (ch35 775, ch37 1369). "Lira was standing at the barrel with both hands on the rope … and she was not biting her knuckle" (ch35 713, ch37 1369, ch40 2445). "The flat went from one shoulder blade to the other" (ch35 705, ch37 1363). By the third occurrence these read as template rather than as pain.
- Ch36, the guild review (1015–1065). Purposeful and quietly tense, but at adult tempo; it is the stretch a younger reader skims. The gauge-maker rescues it.
- Ch36 line 1097, the return to Cael ("Bronze," said Lira) after four Hesk sections, with no signal that the POV has changed back. I located myself within a line, but it is a seam.
- Ch38, Lira on the floor (1709–1733): a long run of interior summary that re-tells things I had just watched. It is earned, and the "different plant from the one on the packet" line is lovely, but the chapter pauses here.

**Which person matters:** Lira. Cael is the lens; Lira is the stake. Every chapter ending that pulled me involved her (walking out of the gate; "Yes?" at the door; "mine are mine"; the five things and the green door two streets away). Hesk is a close second, and the movement's smartest move is making Hesk's "a few years" and Cael's "four things" the same sin from both sides.

**What I expect next:** Cael telling Lira about the fragment, or failing to in time and being caught at it — the movement has loaded that gun twice ("you keep everything till you're sure"; "mine are mine"). Behind that, the senior evaluator arriving with a thin file. Vell's "come to my table Tuesday" was paid off inside the chapter, which I appreciated.

**Do the fights stay fresh across six bouts?** Four of six are genuinely fresh, and fresh for different reasons: the kiln boy by being short and *easy* and distrusted; Renn by escalation across five distinct problems; Sarel by the method collapsing in two exchanges; Corbin by the three-beat truth/lie structure. Dellin is deliberately flat ("Nothing happened") and short enough that the flatness is the point. Talis is where it blurs — and the blur is compounded by its position (immediately after Sarel's loss, same bruises) and by the recycled injury sentences. One more small copy: Sarel's "narrow, careful look … somebody who has found a thing on her bench that she did not put there" (ch37 1327) is Renn's craftsman look (ch35 691) applied verbatim, and it makes her reaction read as his.

**Would I continue?** Yes, without hesitation. I would turn the page for the Lira conversation alone.

## Apparent inconsistencies (no canon supplied; reported as apparent, not verified)

1. **Bout count vs Log numbering.** Ch34 line 81: "You've got five bouts in the book. One of them's a win." The kiln boy entry is then "*Seventh*" (357). After Renn, Vell says "three of seven in this book" (ch36 1109), but Renn's entry would be the Log's eighth. The Log runs one ahead of Vell's book throughout (Sarel ninth, Talis tenth, Dellin eleventh, Corbin twelfth). Possibly explained by an unofficial bout recorded in the Log but not the ledger in an earlier chapter; from inside this stretch it reads as an off-by-one. Confidence: low–medium.
2. **"Fought four times."** Ch38 line 1713: "In twenty-two days he had fought four times." The movement shows three (kiln boy, Renn, Sarel), and the sentence itself lists three. Confidence: high.
3. **Brenna bout timing.** Ch37 1209 and 1563: "three weeks ago." Ch38 1617: "two weeks ago." Ch38 1723: "three weeks ago." Confidence: high.
4. **Day arithmetic (checked, consistent).** Saturday = eighteenth morning since the board (ch37 1195); Sunday Sarel = 19; Wednesday = 22 (ch38 1711); Corbin Thursday = 37 ("last week," ch40 2343); Saturday = thirty-ninth night (2571); Tuesday = forty-second day (2757); Talis to Corbin = eleven days (2421). All reconcile. The "ninety-second day" (2343) sits on a different count (days in Ardenmere, cf. "fifty-first day" at 949) and reconciles if the board fell on day 55.
5. **Physical state.** Injury tracking is careful and consistent: left forearm over the old burn (Renn, then Sarel on "Renn's swelling"), right ribs (Sarel), left ribs (Talis, re-found by Corbin), back (Renn, then Sarel across it). Lira walks on the correct side each time (right/"rib side" after Sarel at 1429; left after Corbin at 2541). No contradiction found.
6. **Power limits / knowledge boundaries.** The drop-step done on purpose costs hip and breath and gets worse on repetition (ch40 2659–2671) — a cost is stated, not waved. Corbin's knowledge of the Talis bout and the sideways against Renn is plausible for a forty-fight regular. Hesk's knowledge of Lira comes only from letters and is kept to that. Nothing in this stretch asserts a reserved disclosure that an earlier chapter has not visibly seeded (the sigil, the one word, the green-book archive entry are all referenced as prior events).
7. **Reused description.** Sarel's reaction look (ch37 1327) is Renn's (ch35 691) verbatim, flagged by the text itself ("the way Renn had looked at him"). Not an error, but a copy where a distinct Sarel beat would serve.

If only the plan supports the "seventh" numbering, treat item 1 as plan adherence rather than a canon error.

## Repair brief

**Priority 1 — Give the Talis exchanges three different textures.**
- **Location:** ch38 lines 1861–1890 (first through fourth exchange).
- **Observed issue:** Exchanges one, two and three are the same beat — Talis is suddenly a stride away, planted, and a strike arrives from there — differentiated only by where it lands. Cael's interior search ("there should be something before it. A dip, a lean, a look") is stated once at 1881 and not dramatized as a narrowing. The bout follows a six-exchange loss on the same body, so the reader arrives already tired.
- **Effect on the reader:** This is the one bout of six where attention slackens; the fights blur here and only here. The ground-hum discovery (1897–1905) is strong enough to recover the reader, but it recovers them from a dip the bout caused.
- **Proposed scope:** Keep every exchange and the length. Give each exchange one thing Cael *rules out* on the body — shoulders in the first, hands in the second, the face and temper in the third (the touch, "good, let him," and nothing coming of it is already there; make it the third's whole job) — so that by the fourth the reader feels the body fully searched and empty a half-beat before the dirt answers. Vary the strike language (the "mallet on a stump" at 1871 is good; "door kicked shut" at 1879 is good; the fourth needs its own).
- **Strength to preserve:** The face-on-the-dirt discovery, Lira's hand on the iron stake, "That's why it's such a good place to keep something." Do not shorten the bout to fix it.

**Priority 2 — Retire the verbatim aftermath refrains after their first use.**
- **Location:** ch35 705–713 (canonical), then ch37 1363–1369, ch40 2443–2445; also "It took two/three tries" (ch35 775, ch37 1369) and the Sarel look at ch37 1327 copied from ch35 691.
- **Observed issue:** "He could not find his breath anywhere. He found it." / "the flat went from one shoulder blade to the other" / "Lira … both hands on the rope … not biting her knuckle" recur word for word across different bouts and different injuries.
- **Effect on the reader:** By the third occurrence the lines read as template and the pain stops registering as this bout's pain. The adult reader notices; the younger reader feels the fights "sounding the same" without being able to say why.
- **Proposed scope:** Line-level. Keep the Renn instance as the original. For Sarel and Corbin, write what is *different* about this breath, this back, this Lira — Sarel's blow lands on a bruise that already exists, Corbin's finds a catch that "had not quite healed." Lira at the barrel can do one new thing each time (she is already saying something he cannot hear at 1369 — that is the better line; keep it and drop the knuckle). Give Sarel her own narrowing look rather than Renn's.
- **Strength to preserve:** The italic "report voice" tallies after each exchange are structural, teach the reader how to watch, and should stay exactly as they are.

**Priority 3 — Fix the counts the book invites the reader to check.**
- **Location:** ch38 1713 ("fought four times"); ch38 1617 ("two weeks ago") vs 1563/1723 ("three weeks ago"); ch34 81/357 and ch36 1109 (Log "Seventh"/"Eighth" vs ledger "five"/"seven").
- **Observed issue:** Three small numerical disagreements in a movement whose protagonist counts bouts, days, chalk marks and kept things, and whose final beat is literally "It was five."
- **Effect on the reader:** The counting motif trains the reader to count; when the text's own numbers wobble, the reader's trust in the motif wobbles with them. Low cost, disproportionate return.
- **Proposed scope:** "four times" → "three times"; pick one of "two"/"three weeks ago" in ch38; confirm whether the Log's numbering is meant to run one ahead of Vell's book (if an earlier chapter supports it, leave it and consider a half-line nod; if not, renumber Seventh→Sixth through Twelfth→Eleventh and adjust "Six bouts" / "Four wins and two losses" at ch40 2577, which already reconcile).
- **Strength to preserve:** The day arithmetic (eighteenth morning, thirty-ninth night, forty-second day, eleven days since Talis) is consistent and should not be touched.

Deferred, not a priority: a one-line restatement in ch34 of *why* a name in more mouths helps against the Compact, for the mid-book reader; and a visible signal at ch36 1097 that the POV has returned to Cael. Both are seams rather than faults.
