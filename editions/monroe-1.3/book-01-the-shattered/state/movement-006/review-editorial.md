## 1. Unscored reader response

Interest caught at once: the frost, the post, Lira's arm still inside her coat, and a boy who has worked out that six weeks is five bouts. The mender's hinge crate is the quiet image of the movement — true hinges and scrap — and the author lets it do its work without naming it twice.

Where it held hardest: Renn's public rehearsal with a friend playing Cael, nod included (ch 35); the second exchange of Renn II, where the heel pulls and the word *gone* comes out loud in Vell's circle; the whole of Sarel, especially the moment on the mark where the watching itself is declared dead; Lira's "You *ruled*" in the grey half; the fourth step that talks and the door that locks from both sides; Corbin reading the feet again because tired boys do not lie with them, and being told the truth.

Where it slipped: the Denvash stretch of chapter 36 is five scenes long and the third of them (the Dessa letter read three times) and the last (the reply quoted) cover ground the reader already crossed in ch 35 and will cross again in ch 39. The "last weeks" montage in ch 39 is lovely but goes a beat past the point where it has said what it has to say. The crowd roll-call before Corbin is one list too many.

Who matters: Lira first — her anger, her room, her right foot. Then Hesk on the step. Then, surprisingly, Corbin, who is on the page for one chapter and is a whole person by the end of it.

What I expect next: Cael tells Lira, and it costs him, because she has already told him *mine are mine*. The senior evaluator. Dessa on the benches collecting a debt.

Would I continue: yes, without hesitation.

## 2. Two lenses

**Fluent thirteen-year-old.** Every bout is trackable: one problem each, stated in plain words at the mark (*he looks where he's going to hit*; *watching is too slow*; *a tell can live in the ground*; *built on what they are*; *he reads feet*). The *gone* drill with the girls on the wall is funny in the right way. The Corbin double-read is the kind of trick this reader retells. Drift risk: the guild review's procedural question-and-answer in ch 36, and the three verbatim letter passages that come round twice.

**Adult genre reader.** The movement's real subject is withholding: Hesk's neat columns, Cael's four-then-five, Lira's row of three, and the rule that lands on top of all of it. That spine is handled with restraint; nobody says the theme aloud. "Nothing happened" as a Log heading is a sophisticated beat. The cost of the length is felt mainly in repetition (letters quoted twice) and in dialogue-tag density, not in the fights.

## 5. Rulings on the items the coordinator flagged

**(1) Length, 45,106 against ~35,000.** Not a defect in itself (owner wants ~300k and developed fights; Book 1 projecting to ~315k is acceptable). Judged by earned/unearned:
- *Earned:* Renn II (+~800 over allowance plus aftermath), Sarel (+~200), Corbin (+~300 plus run-in). Every extra word in these is tactical development or recovery between exchanges. Do not trim.
- *Earned but repetitive at the edges:* the Hesk cutaway (~5,100 vs ~3,000). The scenes are all assigned by the brief and the packet (the summons letter a week late; Joren; Alis shown, not announced; the review). What is not earned is quotation: Hesk's "A win you built…" line appears verbatim in ch 35 and ch 36; the fear paragraph verbatim in ch 36 and ch 39; the Joren/Alis lines verbatim in ch 36 and ch 39. One of each pair is enough, and ch 39 (Cael reading) is the stronger placement. The Dessa-letter section reads the letter three times; two readings carry it.
- *Earned:* Lira's cutaway (~3,400 vs ~2,500). The board section restates ch 37 from her side — the brief asked for exactly that — but ~300 words of it re-narrate the confrontation rather than add her view. The shop, the stair and the room are exactly right.

Realistic total: 1,500–2,000 words. The movement would still sit near 43k, which is fine.

**(2) POV — Cael 81%; the Hesk cutaway's weight.** Knowledge limits: held exactly. ## Repair brief

At most three priorities. Same author, by reading, sentence by sentence; no scripted splitting or joining. Keep every decisive exchange, every speech line and every fight landing beat as they are.

### Priority 1 — Rhythm in the narrative between beats

- **Location:** narrative prose outside dialogue and outside the exchanges: ch 35 the lane walk and the Saturday drill; ch 36 Hesk's §(a) reading and §(f) writing; ch 37 the walk home and the Log aftermath; ch 38 the board section of Lira's cutaway; ch 39 the Log entry's surrounding narration, "The last weeks", the Dellin watching; ch 40 the pre-bout crowd and the notice's aftermath. Also the dialogue tags everywhere a paragraph already makes the speaker clear.
- **Observed issue:** sentence mean 11.43 (range 13–15.5), ≥40-word share 1.7% (range 2.5–4.5%), ≤5-word share 36.8% (ceiling ~34%), FK 2.95 (floor 3.5), population SD 9.8 against ~26; about 113 reporting verbs per 10k against ~41. Runs of three or four clipped statements that are one thought ("He read it. It was true. He folded it.") are the default texture rather than the landing beat.
- **Effect on the reader:** the fights' short beats lose contrast because the connective prose is cut to the same length; by ear, narration and impact sound alike, and the many "said"s add a tick to every line.
- **Proposed scope:** join clipped runs into one well-built sentence where they are one thought; give each chapter three to five deliberately long, readable sentences in walking, watching and letter passages where an action or a thought earns the length; drop "he said"/"she said" wherever the paragraph already shows the speaker. Aim at the working ranges, not the decimals. Expect a few hundred words to come out as a by-product. Leave the Log entries clipped; they are his voice.
- **Strength to preserve:** every short beat that lands a hit, a recognition or a turn ("Gone," said Cael, out loud. / He went over. / "Match," said Vell. / It was five.), and all one-to-three-word speech.

### Priority 2 — Trim where words repeat: the Hesk cutaway and the letter quotations
