## Where we enter

The morning of the day before Kindling, autumn. Cael is fourteen by a few days; the appointment at Weaver's Row was booked a year ago. Alis Kindled three days ago (Tide, Copper R1), Joren two (Stone, Copper R1). Cael has the registry summary's four-line [SHATTERED] entry memorised and has not written it down. Hesk has known for a few years and said nothing.

## What this movement is for

Make the reader feel expelled alongside Cael — and teach the Path system so thoroughly, through people, that every later chapter can lean on it. This is the densest progression-vocabulary stretch in the book (BOOK_MAP §6: first third ~83/10k).

Consequential turns:
- **The home worth losing.** The workshop and its load calculations ("never trust a number you can't trace back to where it came from"), the bakery's open door, the grey dog, the rooftop tea. Give the **Tuesday broadcast** (new): Hesk takes him to the community hall, they watch a recorded circuit bout, and Hesk shows him how to watch a stance — the origin of Cael's eye.
- **Joren at the low wall** (new learning encounter): the friend who was certain, now beginning; he shows off his first declaration (Bible standard format) and they tussle the way they used to. Cael watches a fresh Path from outside, before he has anything. Alis's line, starred.
- **Kindling.** Room to unfold: the sigil from behind the sternum, the count to eleven, the plate lighting late, Pellin's professional stillness, the side room and the angled tree, Hesk arriving prepared. "Don't let anyone see you move…" and "start paying attention to what you don't decide." Hesk may take him out by the back.
- **The record and the confession.** The notice's mechanism ("They're expelling you. From having me."), the archive's dispositions, Hesk at twenty-two — exactly as much as source Ch3 gives.
- **Exit and the road.** The farewell, the guard, the gate entry and its corollary, Hesk's notebook on the road (with the added "watch someone who does" line), and the cart Cael steps from before he has seen it — unwritten, unexplained. The waystation; the city from the rise.

## What the author owns

Chapter boundaries; how the Tuesday broadcast and the low wall are staged; which of the eleven descriptions get voices; the texture of Denvash (Outer District, northern quadrant, Weaver's Row, the canal gate); the road's incidents; how much of Hesk's notebook is quoted on the page versus felt. The tussle with Joren is friendly and small — let it be funny and a little sad.

## Where we leave pressure

On the rise above Ardenmere at dusk, a thousand cookfires, a boy who has read his grandfather's notebook and decided he will not be the fifth entry. The next movement opens at the open gate.
