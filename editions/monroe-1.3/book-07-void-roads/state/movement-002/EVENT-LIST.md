## The ground, as I fix it

- **Where the ford lies.** Movement 1 left it unplaced. It is the upper of the two shallow crossings of the valley's water that the party made on day 43 "at places with no name" (Cael has crossed it three times and never knew it was called anything). It lies below the mouth of the stretch, between two and three hours' walk below Thornwater's gate; the wheel broke a little above it on day 43. The pack that walked beside the cart on the stretch is the ford's pack: it "went home" down the valley.
- **The ford.** The river is about sixty paces from bank to bank there, spread over a ledge of grey rock: **the sill**, some ten feet broad, lying across the bed like a doorstep, with two wheel-grooves worn in it and green **slime** on all of it. Water slides over the sill shin-deep and fast. Above the sill is a long slow glide, thigh-deep. Below it the rock ends at a lip, and under the lip **the pools fall away**, over a man's head a stride out. In mid-river a **gravel bar, thirty paces by eight**, lies with the current: its blunt head at the sill, where the road crosses it, and its tail running down between the two pools to a point, **the tip**. So there are two channels to wade, twenty paces each, a **near** one on Thornwater's side and a **far** one, and a **near and a far shingle**. Above the near shingle is a cut bank, a man's height, with grass on top.
- **The lip stones** (mine; flagged): a row of white stones the hold sets along the sill's downstream lip to show a wheel where the rock ends. Resetting them after the thaw is the hold's work. That is what the two men went down for.
- **Brom's stone.** One slab on the sill in the near channel, five paces out from the bar, standing a hand clear of the water, an arm's length square, with a raised lip along its downstream edge. A heel set against that lip cannot be pushed off the sill. Anything coming along the sill from the near shingle must pass it.
- **Seln's pool** is the near pool, under the bar's cut flank, twenty paces down from the head and beside the tip.

## The third, and the lore

- Two men of the hold went down at noon on the 49th to reset three lip stones the thaw had shifted. The last stone went over the lip and pinned one man's leg, chest-deep. The other, a Force, got into the river beside him and lifted with his Path under the water for a long count, and nothing came. He got him up onto the bar, at dusk, and stood up on dry gravel with the lift still in his arms. Then the stones along the water got up. The pinned man was down; a hound came for his throat and **he got his hand between the jaws and his throat**, and the hand paid for it. When the Force shouted and used all he had, every hound left the silent man for him. One came up. (Reader Standard: told plainly, in a few sentences, by the survivor; no gore; it reads unmistakably as a man defending his throat with his hand.)
- **The lore**, as rules without reasons: the bowman, the first time Cael hears his voice, "Dusk. Never noon."; the survivor, "River's their colour."; the steading's woman, to Lira's "why does nobody know how they do it", "It's because you die,".
- **A hired crew may ask its hirer about the job** ("You're hired. That's different."). Cael asks on that footing. Karis notes the difference between that and asking a stranger why.

## Seln's seven (the loft)

- In his two hours in the grass he counts **seven**, coming to drink one at a time, never two together. Each drinks with its feet on dry stone and its neck stretched; not one will wet a foot. Six come and go by three trails in the far bank's grass. The seventh was on the bar the whole time, lying among the grey cobbles at the tip, twenty paces from where Lira stopped her runs; he had walked within four strides of it.
- The gravel of the 49th, read: six came in from the banks; the seventh stood at the tip throughout and moved only at the end, to meet the man who ran down the bar. In Seln's trade that is the cut man, placed where the subject will run. **The cut dog is never the one you are looking at.**
- He lays out, unasked, what he read at the white stone on day 45: a follower's distance, kept; a thing waiting upon a signal. This is the day it bears.

## Karis's hypothesis (the loft; a hypothesis until after the fight)

- Clause one: the stillhound hunts **discharge**, what a Path gives off in use. It hears, and may see; it does not act on either.
- Clause two: **water masks it**, at both ends. A Path used under water gives off nothing that reaches them; an animal standing in water takes nothing in.
- Evidence for: the country's rule (fire, voice and Path named together, and kept only on a road); the mile (it answered nothing, having been given nothing); the buckle (it heard, and only followed); the fan (it went round rather than wet its feet); seven drinking dry-footed; the Force who lifted in the river and was let alone, and stood up on gravel and was not; the hound that left a silent man for a shouting one; the ferry (a boy may shout on a river).
- Not accounted for: 110 yards; the hour.
- "Evidence against: none, which I don't trust."
- Cael's nine: a fragment answering is discharge as far as he can tell. He has held on that supposition for fifty-one days and holds on it now.

## The plan

- **Bait with one declaration, and kill with everything that isn't one.** Karis's point first: the smallest declaration there is, and she can put it out between two heartbeats.
- **"Never two at once, never the same person twice."** Two at once splits the pack and nobody can say which head goes where. The same person twice only tells the ones already walking that they were right.
- Order: Karis's point; then Brom's Iron Skin, from his stone, where the lip and the river are his; no burst from Lira (a burst puts her somewhere nobody chose, with every head after her); and if it comes to a third, Cael, and Wind, because Wind is the one thing he owns that a man on a bank would take for a Path and think no more about.
- **Seln takes the pool**, with his Path lifted under the water: clause two tested on his own body. Shadow hides him from eye and ear; the river hides the Shadow. Cael owns the same means and will not use it; the seal's logic is restated once, in the day-53 log (protected text).
- Seln asks for one thing: when they are all up, strike steel once, so that he can learn which of them looks.
- The hold lends its bowman as witness (he stands on the cut bank and does not come down) and the carter with his cart, to wait at the last bend: "Either way there'll be something to carry."
- Seln's case stays in the loft, between the second box and the third.

## The ford at dusk — seven hounds, in rounds a listener can count

Stations: Brom on his stone in the near channel. Karis at the bar's head, beside a boulder the size of a loaf. Cael and Lira at the far landing, one each side of the grooves. Seln in the near pool by the tip, to the chin, his Path lifted under water. The bowman on the cut bank. Cael counted the large stones on the far shingle at noon the day before; at dusk there are three more (see the amendments for the figures).

1. **The point.** Karis lights it. **The river stands up on three sides:** three on the far shingle, three on the near shingle behind them, one at the bar's tip. Brom strikes his knife's pommel on the bill. **Every head but one** stays on the light; the seventh, at the tip, turns to the sound.
2. **The far three.** One steps into the channel and two follow it. On the near side nothing has moved first, and nothing moves. Karis puts the point out when the three are ten paces into the water. They come on to where the light was: a hound in the river is blind and runs on a memory. Cael kills the first as it lands (the knife behind the shoulder). **Six.** The two behind it stand in the shallows with nothing to follow.
3. **The testing.** The two come up apart and worry the quiet bodies, snapping and springing back: they bite to be answered. Lira's draws her down the bar, a pace at a time. Cael's knife cuts air.
4. **Brom declares.** Iron Skin, on purpose, wrists to jaw. Every head turns. The near three come along the sill, one first and two after it. Cael's tester bolts for him across the bar's head; Lira's turns and runs up the bar. The first hound is on Brom's forearm, teeth on iron, and **he drops it like a coat**. *He was still large.* He turns his shoulder into it, bears it down over the lip, and pins its head and forequarters against the stone; **the current takes the hound's hindquarters**; he holds until it stops. The jaws open his forearm from wrist to mid-arm as the river pulls. **Five.** The two behind it arrive blind at a silent man; one snaps at his coat and gets no answer.
5. **Lira (her window, ≈1,800, two scenes).** The feinter; the Fenmark floor; Wind in her feet at every stride and refused at every stride; fifteen paces down the bar before she feels the cut dog eight paces behind her shoulder, looking past her. Brom goes grey; her hound turns like a weathercock and runs past her as past a post. Twenty-five paces of gravel she ran three times yesterday, without a burst, and she is fast; she takes it at the water's edge, where it checks; she stops dead on both feet, as she taught herself under a bowman's platform, and the edge rolls. **The ankle goes all the way.** Fast, with nothing at the end of it. **Four.** "I'm fine."
6. **The pack learns.** The two from the sill come up onto the bar and trot down it, passing silent people at ten feet without a glance: they have stopped testing quiet bodies. The last of the far three goes to the one person on the bar who ever answered.
7. **Karis.** The hound strikes her knife hand and the knife turns; she catches it up wrong-ended with no time to turn it, sets the hilt against her breastbone, and **holds it by its blade with both hands**. The hound's weight comes onto it. Her hands slide; she takes a second hold. A second point would end it and bring three more; Ember in Cael offers the same point and he looks at her hands instead. It dies on the knife. **Three left.** "I didn't light it."
8. **The seam.** Three wait: two in the middle of the bar, the grey at the tip. The light is going; Seln has been an hour and a quarter in the river. Cael's count of the rushes: each began with one animal, never the same one, and the rest went after it; when the mover died or lost its mark, the rest stood. **They follow the first mover, not a leader.** The grey has never moved first. **It is waiting** for something to come to it.
9. **The burst.** The rule (never spend a burst near an opponent whose single hit he cannot price) says yes for the first time: the price is one bite inside one count, in the leg he lands on, with a knife two strides behind the animal. He walks down the bar between the two, who let a quiet body pass. Six paces from the tip he lets Wind have the stride it has leaned for since the Line: **one burst, spent openly**, past the grey's flank, **downstream and away from Karis**, landing on the drowned tail of the bar, ankle-deep. **The landing lock with a dog in it:** for one count nothing he has can answer; the grey turns in its own length and takes his left calf. **Seln rises out of the pool behind the grey.** The two that followed the first mover arrive at the tip with nothing to follow, between two knives and a large man coming down the bar; they take the water.
10. **Five dead; two gone downstream**, logged as open. Cael feels the calf only when the water round it goes cold.

Deployment since the Line: **one**, from this dusk. Shadow unused, though it lifts at dusk through the whole wait. The Iron read stays shut. Companions' Paths (Karis's point, Brom's Skin, Seln's Shadow under water) are not his deployments.

## Thornwater, days 54–58

- **The cart-yard, day 58 (Karis's window, ≈1,400).** Three east carts come for grain. Lira asks a carter with a clouded eye where his road goes; he does not talk roads to paper. Karis is dictating notes on the carter's map to Cael: east of the gate, two days of ticks out, the double line leaves its straight, goes round bare hide and comes back; she calls it a quiet place on the map. The carter's wife, passing with a sack, a small boy on her cart's foot-board, says the protected three sentences and drives out. Four questions arrive and Karis keeps all four: that is the cost of learning the other way. She has Cael write the three sentences, read them back twice, and underline *everybody knows* twice. ## The road down, days 58–59

- **Lira on the cart's tail**, announcing that it was her decision.
- The ford in daylight: nothing on the bar; the lip stones reset, white, all in their row; two open.
- **Camp above the wash, the 59th.** The first thing the finding buys is a fire: a flint is not a declaration. Seln strikes it. The carter, twenty-two years on a cold road, sits down at it in the end. (Flagged.)
- **Lira at the fire:** Norhold told her to a tenth how far she was from Silver; a ladder was built to her height and she walked off it at full price; she ran the best thirty paces of her life at the ford and nothing says so. "So what am I becoming, exactly, if nobody's measuring?" Nobody answers. Cael does not either: the fact that set him free is the fact that starves her. He writes the question down and writes no answer under it.

## Lowmarch, day 60

- **Fourteen slips** again: the dry roads have taken crews and paper out.
- **Seln** reads the plank from post to post in the open and takes down the well's slip, which has hung since the ninth day. "I keep waiting for someone to ask for my report." Cael does not ask: the man's want is not to be asked.
- **Brom at the plank's end.** "What does the board pay a man with no Path at all." / "Same as anyone," … "What'd he do?" Brom says what he did. Pike rewrites the sheet by hand: **twelfth**. "Teague's still eleven up. He'll speak to you when you've cost him something. That's not rudeness. That's the ledger."

## The well, days 61–64 (new on the page)

- The slip has hung fifty-one days. Pike says why when he marks it: it went sour at the thaw; a carter's boy went down for a dropped bucket and was drawn up asleep. It is the well at the south road's fork, half a day out on flat ground, paid from the standing-money because it is everybody's well.
- **Seln walks it first** on the 61st and says nothing. At the curb on the 62nd there is fresh soot a fathom down and a candle stub on a knotted string under a stone. Cael reads it and lets him keep it.
- **The hazard is bad air.** Karis has one page that is right in both countries: foul air lies in a shaft like water in a cup; a flame shows its surface; it is bailed or stirred out. The candle dies at fifteen feet. Brom binds a faggot of thorn; Lira and Cael wind it up and down the shaft. Brom, forbidden to pull, calls the knots.
- **Cael goes down**, because the one whole pair of legs is wanted at the windlass. A candle hangs six feet under his boots; Karis counts aloud and he answers each ten. The fragments have nothing to say to air. He loses a count before the candle shows anything, and calls "Up" before he is sure. The candle is out at twenty feet when they lower it again. Shorter turns on the 63rd. The fallen course reset; the shaft drawn until it runs clean; Brom tastes it. A southbound carter drinks and scratches a wheel on the slip.

Each was re-composed by reading in the same session: related statements joined in narration and in the longer speeches of Seln, Karis and Pike, and a few over-long joins parted again. System vocabulary was then made plainer in the fight and its aftermath (declaration, discharge, Path, the fragment names) where figures of speech had been standing in for it.
