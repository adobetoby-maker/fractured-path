Oryn becomes the moral hinge. She gives care without demanding disclosure, tests the line for herself, and then becomes the person from whom Cael knowingly takes without prior consent.

I expect the next event to be the promised telling on the table, followed by Oryn establishing the rule and cost of the new fragment. I would continue. The ending at minute twenty-one is a strong hook because it converts a progression reward into a relationship debt immediately.

## Editorial verdict

The movement is structurally strong and canon-faithful. The Ring is a developed, legible action set piece; the recovery chapters deepen all six people; the two cutaways are purposeful; the tenth acquisition changes both capability and relationship. Geometry, dates, injuries, witness limits, fragment use, hound count, bolt count, and reserved disclosures track.

It is not ready to close unchanged. Four `[[TIER-B: ...]]` coordinator placeholders remain in the manuscript. There is also one direct logic error in the consent council and a related Reader Standard risk in the acquisition rationale. Numerically, scene spacing and the three primary working-range metrics are good, but sentence spread and readability remain materially easier/narrower than the owner-selected formula.

## Findings

### Release-blocking: four drafting placeholders remain

The following are visible production scaffolding rather than prose:

- `chapter-31.md:293` — Oryn's failed reading of Lira's bitten arm: “Nothing… Nothing in my hands.”
- `chapter-34.md:135` — Brom's fingers and the second mending: “String… That's two.”
- `chapter-34.md:191` — Oryn's route arithmetic at the stake: “Three in two days… That's a route.”
- `chapter-37.md:67` — Oryn's long-reading commentary, including “there's more water.”

These interrupt four consequential scenes, omit required action/sensation around protected partial wording, and expose process language to the reader. The manuscript cannot close while they remain. This is not a reason to compress the scenes: each missing unit should be supplied in Oryn's established practical register and fitted to the existing physical continuity.

### Major: the consent argument contains a factual reversal and a muddy decisive rationale

At `chapter-36.md:119-187`, Brom says “Then it's wrong,” Karis writes and speaks “wrong,” Cael says “I know that it is,” Seln says it is not consent, and Lira says “It's still wrong.” Yet `chapter-36.md:183` says, “nobody in that loft had called it wrong, least of all himself.” That contradicts the preceding page. The intended word is almost certainly “right,” or the sentence needs to say that nobody excused it.

The deeper issue appears at `chapter-37.md:157-175`. Cael can refuse the acquisition, but reasons that refusal would be “a lie” and a way to “keep his hands clean”; he then reaches and is rewarded with the tenth fragment. The movement has done substantial moral work before this point, and the canon outcome must remain, but this particular rationale risks telling a thirteen-year-old reader that openly admitting a selfish desire makes carrying it out more truthful than stopping. Honesty about intent is not consent and does not create an obligation to do the wrong thing. The immediate material reward and Oryn's dry closing line make the ambiguity sharper because accountability is deferred to Movement 6.

A narrow repair can preserve the hard choice: correct `chapter-36.md:183`, then let Cael recognize that refusing now would still be the right act even though it came late, before he knowingly chooses against that recognition. Preserve the covenant, the four companions' objections, the directed acquisition, and Oryn's agency in the final exchange.

### Moderate: one binding carry-in is not paid off at the opening

The Movement 4 handoff says Cael exceeded his two-hands limit by a quarter-hour, entered the error against himself, and left the party with half the sun; the packet explicitly assigns that cost to Movement 5's opening pressure. `chapter-31.md:3-9` establishes that the sun has gone under the hills while his book shuts, but never connects the lost light to his command error. There is no contradiction—the geometry and time of day remain correct—but a cause/effect promise is dropped. One unobtrusive reminder in the opening inventory would make the danger partly his responsibility and sharpen the leadership arc without delaying the hounds' entrance.
