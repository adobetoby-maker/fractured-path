## Fixed ground

- **The road from Oxhollow** comes down to the west bank of Lowmarch's river and over by the ferry (the keeper's carts "come round by my ferry"). Six days. Mine: a broad valley of alder and rough pasture; on the fourth day two hundred yards of wet moss crossed by a causeway of laid logs; halting-grounds with chock-stones.
- **Oxhollow's grain cart.** The keeper will send no wheel over the top, so his cart goes round to Thornwater for grain, eight days out. Its carter (unnamed: a long silent young man with very large feet, two mules) takes the three boxes on his bed, because the five walk his road in front of him as a hired crew would, for nothing. There is no paper in it and no line. He rests a day at Lowmarch and goes on to Thornwater with them on the 92nd. Two boxes go into Thornwater's loft; the middle box goes east on Brom's back.
- **Lowmarch** as the closed chapters have it: ferry and drum; lower inn, eave, plank, flat stone, horn pot, iron box, spike; the room and the sailcloth wall; the smith; the meadow and the ash. The landing broker's seal is a hook and a bar in dark wax; his clerk carries a ledger.
- **The east road, Lowmarch to Thornwater:** saddle, wash (three hours out), lower crossing, the ford (upper crossing), the stretch (walked at noon), the gate. Two days.
- **The Fallow road** leaves Thornwater by the east gate, goes up the valley and over a low place in the hills, and forks an hour out; the right-hand branch is the Fallow road. Two days of tall pale grass that no hold grazes; no fence, no smoke. It runs due east.

At its crest the ruts leave their straight line and swing out to the **north** (the left hand, going east), go round, and come back to the line on the far side. ## Chapter 23 — "Round by the Ferry" (days 84–89)

1. **Dusk, the 84th, Oxhollow's bank.** Present: Cael, the keeper; then the five. He has shut the book. The inventory at the start of a thing: ribs taped, twenty-one days from the 78th; the calf in her cloth and closing by itself; Lira's leg new, her ankle taped, her right foot bare; Brom's cuff, with three days of his week left; Karis filling; a hundred and sixteen and six owed; three boxes and no wheels; six days to a plank. The keeper comes down with his tally-stick: his cart goes for Thornwater's grain at first light, round by the ferry; "they go round" now. The boxes may ride if the five walk in front. What changes: the boxes have a way home that costs nothing, and he notices that a man offered it because of a line that said no.
2. **The ford, the 85th.** The carter and his two mules; the red oxen draw the cart through. The five wade a hundred yards in silence. On the south bank: the prints of four people and two mules, a day old, in single file a crew's length apart; one foot short on the left; the smallest prints deepest. He reads a crew he cannot see and never catches it. **Lira's boot:** the carter's spare pair, far too big; he will not split a pair; two marks; she packs the right one with linen and hangs the left on the cart's tail. "In hand, a hundred and fourteen."
3. **The 86th and 87th: the tape and the pace.** The tape is hated by noon, as he was told it would be. Learning: he has always walked in fits; he sets himself to the carter's pace beside the near wheel and finds that a breath taken to the tape and a stride taken to the mule are the same discipline. Lira, forbidden to run for a month, walks at the off wheel and counts nothing. Karis sleeps on the boxes. Seln finds the sleeping places. **Brom's hour:** at the noon halt on the 87th he stands a stick in the ground and watches its shadow; at its shortest, "That's the week." He picks up nothing. "She said the middle of the day. I've nothing here that wants lifting."
4. **The 88th, the moss.** Two hundred yards of black water and reed with a road of logs laid across it, some of them rotten. The carter will not take a loaded bed over. Lira walks the logs first, at a walk, and says which bear; the cart goes over empty with a man at each wheel; the load is carried. **Brom picks up the middle box in both hands, in front of him**, the first thing he has held so since the ford, and carries it over. Nobody remarks on it but the carter, who does not know what he is seeing.
5. **The 89th, evening.** The last halting-ground, with the river in sight. Karis awake and counting days to Thornwater: four. He does not open the book to more than the day's lines. The nine: held. Deployment since the Line: one.

Growth beats (Cael): a no that earned a favour; reading a crew by its feet without wanting to beat it; the pace; that Brom's week was Brom's to keep.

## Chapter 26 — "White Paper" (days 91–93)

1. **The mail, the 91st.** The ferry's sack. Two for "Cael, past the Line": a sheet folded small in a hand he knows from a wall of plaques, and a square of ledger paper. He opens neither in the meadow. One letter to a road: he means to have something in front of him.
2. **The wash at noon, the 92nd.** Ephram's letter, read aloud to the four (protected, whole, printed once). Lira: **"Good."** at the map reference to the Line and nothing past it. Brom hears Rooke quoted. Seln asks for the fourth paragraph a second time and says nothing. Cael sets a query from above a registry beside a posting written inside, and will not join them without a third thing.
3. **The printed sheet.** Above the wash Karis unrolls the registry's continental sheet on the ground for the first time since the shelf, and lays the calfskin beside it. East of the Line the sheet has **seven dashed roads that run out into white and stop**, with no name on any; by the river's bend **this is the third.** On the hide she has found, with Pike's "handful" in her head, **three round blanks**: the one the road walks round, and two more that she had taken for a carter's thrift (one in his hill-hatching to the south, one among his water-strokes to the south-east, with no road near either). She rings all three in pencil and can source one. The hide is a map of days and will not take a straightedge. **"Their map has no roads. His has no quiet ground. Between them you get the country."** (protected). Seln on why Pike says *void*: a void entry is one with no particulars written against it.
4. **Thornwater, the 93rd.** The stretch at noon. Under the platform, on a bench inside the gate, a square woman with a carter's hand between both of hers, before she has eaten. **"Fifteen days. Since the break."** (Oryn) / **"Breath stops at it."** (Cael) / **"Ninth day. I said nine. You've counted."** (Oryn). "That's one." He tells her where the crew is going and does not ask. Her sum aloud: whole on the 99th by the rule; the long reading no sooner than the 100th; she had meant to sit at this gate's table until then. **Two reasons:** she wants a patient whole and still for the long reading, and she has seen what this crew brings back from a road; and the carters have told her for thirty rounds that a healer's hands stop there, and she has never checked a thing she was only told about her own hands. **"You're a stop. I've added you."** … **"Don't make it a long stop."** Six gates wait the same days either way, and she knows who is at each.
5. **Her table at dusk, from the loft door.** The steading's woman gives her the back room, as always. One line for the bench. New: the lamp. Oryn to Karis: the week is out six days; light it. **Karis sets one point on a wick**, the first since the 78th. Lira and the bowman: two fingers.

## Chapter 27 — "The Rise" (days 94–95)

1. **The east gate, the 94th.** Two boxes stay in the loft with the reserve; Karis takes the chips out of the third box, twist by twist, down to the canvas with five thumbs in the wax, and moves them to the middle box. The middle box on Brom's back. Oryn on the mule's down-slope side. At the gate **the registry's sheet goes back into the box**; at the fork, an hour out, the printed road ends and the paper is white.
2. **Noon, the 94th: Vell's letter,** read aloud to Lira, who asked (protected, whole). Lira: **"She puts the thing that decides it last."** Cael: **"So does Ephram."** "The session still stands" is not explained to her; he says only that it is hers to say and his to keep.
3. **The Fallow road.** The country: grass to the knee and then the hip, pale; no stock; a wind with nothing to stop it. They walk quiet. A flint fire at dark on the 94th. Oryn says what a carter says of a healer's hands, and that not one of them has seen it. Karis does not ask her a fourth question.
4. **The rise, the 95th, with the sun low behind them.** The ruts swing north. Straight on, eleven grey stumps of different heights against the eastern sky, a long bowshot off. They leave the ruts and walk the old line abreast, slowly, six in a row. He attends to the place behind his breastbone where a sigil gathered once: dark, as it has been since a brass plate. Lira's hand goes up: **"It's like the air got thick."** Brom: **"Like a door closing behind me."** Oryn's hands half-raised, and silent. Karis: **"My Arbiter's gone."** Seln: **"Back up. Ten paces. Tell me when it comes back."** They step back; five voices say *back* on one stride. Karis: **"Same stride. All of us."** She sets a stone. **He felt nothing, either way, and says nothing:** he does not know what it means, and nobody asked, since four of them know that his has been dark since his Kindling and take it that a line has nothing in him to stop. The nine lie as they lay; Anchor asks about the stone; Shadow lifts at dusk, a stride inside as a stride out, and is told no.
5. **The log at the stone** (protected). Camp at the turn of the ruts, no fire. The floor takes the last light like water. Oryn stands at the stone with her hands held out over the line until the light is gone.

Growth beats: what he keeps of Vell's; the silence, and its two reasons, named as his; a place that reads nothing.

## Chapter 28 — "Sixty-Two Cords" (the night of the 95th; the morning of the 96th)

1. **Karis's window (≈1,400), headed: that night, at the stone.** No lamp. She calls her sigil as she was taught at fourteen, with the stone at her knee, and puts one foot over: gone; back: there. She does it nine times. She has been empty lately and knows the difference: empty, the sigil was there and had nothing to give; here it has everything and is not there. She writes a stride: where the stone is, how many of her strides from the turn of the ruts, that the line is narrower than one stride. She does not write why. Five rows. She is afraid of how tidy it is and enters that she is.
2. **The procedure, said once.** First light. Lira is the instrument, a stride inside, saying only *in* or *out*. Karis walks the line with the cord's end. Brom drives a peg at every cord with the back of the bill. Seln goes ahead and reads the ground. Oryn keeps the stone, with the mule. Cael writes the count. **The bend:** at each peg Karis sights back along the last cord, and the line has left it by the same four hands every time; she feels it in her wrist before she measures it.
3. **Iron at the third cord.** Brom's bill rings on something in the grass. A stake of iron, a hand out of the turf, a boot's width inside the cord's line; a stamp on its head, and a number: thirty-one. Anchor asks about it. Karis will not break a count. On.
4. **Round.** The north side between line and ruts; the east side, where Seln has found runs through the grass, as broad as a palm in the print, crossing the line both ways; the south. Lira drifts out twice and says so. It is all sign: no animal. Cael's ruling: in at noon, out with the sun two hands up.
5. **The close.** The last cord falls short of the stone. **"Sixty-two cords and twenty strides,"** … **"One thousand three hundred and seventy-four yards, and a piece."** The same bend at sixty-two pegs, and it has come home: ten cords to the middle. **"A circle is a made shape."** Said as geometry; nobody follows it. **The stake**, with Seln: the registry's stamp; **"Five years in the weather. A cold term either side."**; **"You drive them where you've measured to. Not where you've measured from."**

Growth beats: a count in his hand that somebody else's method makes true; refusing the Iron read for the bend, a last time.

## Chapter 29 — "A Stride Inside" (noon, the 96th)

1. **The order, and why.** At the stone, with the sun overhead. Karis sets it: smallest first, since Pike may be wrong and there are prints in the grass. Nobody declares outside the line. **Karis:** she looks for the places and finds every one; she can fill none. **"It costs nothing. There's nothing to spend."**
2. **Brom:** he calls it by name, aloud, and his wrists stay the colour of wrists. **"Skin."** **Seln** tries to lift his Path and has nothing to lift; then he is not there for a breath, by craft alone. **"I've a knife and fifteen years. It was never most of what I do."**
3. **Lira:** the whole burst, asked for on purpose for the first time since Norhold, and it is a step. **"It's in me. It's not in the air."** She steps out. `[[TIER-B]]` **the clock line** (the map gives it from the middle of a sentence) on its own line.
4. **Oryn's three crossings**, with Brom's left arm between her hands and a count running: in together (the count stops); out together (it is back at the stride); then she alone inside, with his arm reached over to her and the rest of him standing where his Arbiter is. **"Nothing. Not nothing in him. Nothing in my hands."** … **"A healer's hands stop working there."** What it means for six people going in: she can set and she cannot mend.
5. **The two private tests.** While every eye is on the healer he stands at the second peg, twenty yards north. He prices it: the hounds answer what goes out of a person; it is noon; the Iron read is the quietest thing he owns. He opens it a pace short of the line and walks. **It runs across the stride without a break.** He goes in twenty strides, as a man goes to look at ground he is about to lead people over, to a grey stone in the grass; and he lets Wind take him round it by a foot. From behind it is a man stepping round a stone. He shuts both. He has supposed, as the others suppose, that what was taken out of a Path would stop where Paths stop. **Karis, with a sixth row ruled:** "And yours?" **"It was dark before,"** … **"It's dark now."** Both halves are true. She writes it, and Lira calls that the sun is over.

Growth beats: the first thing he has kept from them; knowing that each half is true and the whole is not.

## Chapter 30 — "The Far Lip" (afternoon to dusk, the 96th)

1. **Six over.** The mule hobbled at the stone with the panniers and what is lashed on them. The cord laid east, cord by cord, and the grass trodden to a lane behind it. **Ten cords to the middle.** The Ring: eleven uprights round the rim of a grey floor, broken at eleven heights.
2. **The floor.** Twenty-six strides across. Level under the cord. The lip: a rim turned over like the rim of a dish, a hand above the turf. He kneels and puts both palms on it and opens the read: one mass, a pace and a half down and to every side, the uprights rooted in it without a joint. He writes two words: *no seam.* Karis's chips do not match, not one. The bands: lines cut level round every upright and round the lip. Lira asks whose hand it is. **"None I own. And I own more than the registry prints."** Karis takes a rubbing of each upright, a sheet to a stone: eleven.
3. **No instruments.** Not on the floor, not at the foot of any upright, not in the grass for forty yards. A hundred marks for a thing that is not there. The sun two hands up. Seln goes round by the far side to finish the circle of the search.
4. **The den.** Under the eastern lip the ground has gone from beneath the rim: a low dark mouth as long as a cart. Prints, this morning's, one upon another. The bones of deer, in one clause. Scat. A hollow of bare earth worn as smooth as a seat. Seln on one knee: **"They live in here because nothing that walks in can fight."** Everyone looks at the line, and at the sun, which is on the ridge; then at Oryn; then at him.
5. **The log at the far lip** (protected). He leaves off the line that has ended every entry since the Line. Shadow lifts, inside, as at any dusk. The box goes onto Brom's back. End: about to move.

Growth beats: the limit he set and overran; what the look at him asks; the missing line.

- **Karis's window** gives no figure for her own pace; it says only that her count from the turn to the stone is larger than his eighty and that two pacings differed by one.
- **Chapter 26:** the Oxhollow carter leaves Lira's left boot standing at the barn door, and she hangs it on Oryn's mule at the east gate (ch27). Oryn's sum at the long house door ends "by the ninety-ninth".
- **Chapter 27:** the bowman comes to the east gate; the mule grazes across the stone at dusk with two feet on either side.
- **Chapter 30:** the tenth cord ends at the middle of the floor and Karis checks it by pacing to the rim four ways. The rubbings: ten whole and the eleventh half done when Seln lifts his hand. Cael spends a quarter of an hour past his own limit and has it "entered against me". Seln's reading at the den: this morning's prints, eight or nine animals, all outward to the east.
- **The Teague marker** stands in the room scene before his window, as planned; the scene after it leans only on what the packet's own summary gives (read twice, put back).
- **The Fallow slip, Pike's traverse line, both letters and the three protected log entries** are printed once each; later references are narration (two partial re-quotations of the slip in chapters 24, 25 and 30 were recomposed after the first draft).
