## Unscored reader response

Interest first caught when the Oxhollow keeper offers the cart because the crew gave him an honest no: kindness becomes an earned progression reward rather than a pause between jobs. It caught harder at Lira’s enormous boot, Brom keeping the healer’s week to the hour, the two HOLD slips, and the cream paper under Pike’s stone. The strongest run begins with the line at the rise and builds through Karis’s night test, the perimeter, each person’s Pathless trial, Cael’s concealed tests, and the empty den.

Interest slipped slightly in the middle of the Lowmarch material. The wall standing, traverse accounting, Teague cutaway, five-person council, posting procedure, and witness count are all purposeful, but chapters 24–25 delay physical movement through several consecutive forms of paper arithmetic. The perimeter’s repeated cord procedure also risks feeling long to a younger reader before the iron stake pays it off. Three literal editorial placeholders cause a more serious break: they expose production scaffolding instead of story.

Cael matters most. His pleasure in being valued for an honest refusal, his refusal to turn Oryn’s choice into his request, and his growing habit of keeping technically true but materially incomplete records form one coherent moral pressure. Karis is the movement’s strongest secondary presence: she wants a primary source, designs a reproducible test, and refuses to turn fear into a finding.

I expect the missing pack to cut the cord-lane at dusk, forcing the six to retreat without five native Paths and forcing Cael to decide whether continued concealment is worth someone else’s injury. I would continue immediately.
