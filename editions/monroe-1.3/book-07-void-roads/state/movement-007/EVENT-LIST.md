## The ground at the Stair (one picture, used for chapters 48–52)

- **The ridge** runs north and south. The Stair road comes south along its top from the fork above Millrace and then drops off its western face. Where the slope eases there is a **brow**; at the brow the carters' live ruts turn north and go round the low ground, as they go round the Fallow Ring.
- **The low ground** is a shallow bowl, about five hundred and twenty yards from rim to rim. **The rim** is the line of the carters' ruts all the way round: two hundred and sixty yards from the middle, and **forty paces outside the line**.
- **The line** (the edge of the Quieting) is a circle two hundred and twenty yards from the middle. The old straight road, two grown-over ruts, runs on down from the brow, crosses the line and goes to the floor. Where it crosses stand **two iron stakes numbered thirty-two**, eight feet apart with the old ruts between them: the **old stake** on the north side (five cold terms of scale), the **newer stake** on the south side (one or two; the stroke and the bar under the number). Karis's **stone** lies between them. The camp is there, on the outside.
- **The floor**: a bare round plate of the fused grey, sixty of Cael's strides across, lying in the bottom of the bowl; no uprights, no ring. From the stone to the floor's eastern lip is a hundred and ninety yards of pale grass, down a gentle fall; from the lip to the hole is thirty. **The hole** is square, two strides a side, at the middle. The first step is on its eastern side and the stair goes down westward: **forty steps**, straight, three of them lit from the sky.
- **The chamber** opens westward from the stair's foot through a doorway (so the doorway is in its east wall). Ten strides square. Its roof is the underside of the floor. Walls carved from floor to roof in courses; a plain line between the second course and the third.
- **Overhead relation, to be stated once at the start of chapter 49 and re-anchored at each break:** six on the rim, outside the line, at four stations. **East (the gate, on the brow above the stone):** *the gate bow* (a crossbow) and *the empty-handed one* (no bow; the second Anchor hand, though Cael does not know which of the two is laying until dusk). **South:** *the south bow* and *the standing one* (no bow; never kneels, never loads; gives one sign). **West:** *the west bow*, alone. **North:** *the north bow*, alone. Four crossbows, six people, all hooded, in road grey, with a carter's dust-cloth across the face. No face, seal, name, office or voice at any point.
- **Distances that are said:** brow to line forty paces; the first bolt at forty-five yards (Brom five yards inside the line); stone to hole two hundred and twenty; rim to hole two hundred and sixty; chamber ten strides; forty steps; Anchor's sight sure at ten feet, faint at twelve, absent at fourteen.

## Chapter 45 — Eleventh (days 111–112)

1. **Day 111, Thornwater's west gate at first light.** Brom loads three boxes on the brown mule and learns her (the low side; the click). The bowman lifts two fingers to Lira. The first hard cold: ruts like iron. Down the stretch at a time of day when nothing comes up it; the ford below, where the mule will not cross moving water with anybody on her near hand, and Brom goes round her tail in the river. Lira walks the whole sole on ice. Cael's idle state: the listening lies quiet; one self-reading done, two owed, and he counts the days that he has let pass. Camp above the wash; a flint fire.
2. **Day 112, the saddle above Lowmarch, late afternoon.** The honest catalogue (the fourth time he has counted that town from high ground): the meadow down to a dozen fires, the ferry's cable white, the capstan's gelding blanketed, smoke from both inns, ice along the bank. Lira asks the old carter under the ash which gate the healer is late at. **The plain line:** Millrace is the next gate on her round after Thornwater, two days by the hill track from the northern wicket, and she came in there last night. So it is sooner than Thornwater. The boxes and the mule under the ash.
3. **The lower inn at dusk.** Pike, at the hearth bench with the book open, says the Fallow line as they come through the door, before it is shut (narrated; the document was printed in chapter 40), and says it again. The room updates without a word: the hide-woman takes her bundle off the bench end; the crew of three gone grey leave their dice lying; the old carter from the first week looks past them at the door; the standing-money boy looks at their boots. **Teague stands.** Pike: **"Eleventh. Teague's ten up."** Teague sits. Lira reads the sheet: the crew with the wooden foot is over them again; a job with no price costs a line.
4. **What the wall cannot say.** Pike, to the book: thirty years, forty-odd crews on that cloth, and not one over a line and back. **"Six in and six out, and the sixth a healer."** He has no column for it. Cael goes to the wall's corner and sees **the nail** where Pike hangs what has been read and returned: one sheet of cream paper.
5. **The second slip.** Protected text, printed once. ## Chapter 47 — Millrace (day 113, before light to early afternoon)

1. **Out of Lowmarch two hours before light.** A crew's pace on a carter's day: the south road past the well, onto the ridge by sunrise, frost ringing. The fork before noon; the branch down. The wheel heard before the roofs. Millrace: a weir, a race, a breast wheel, a mill of grey stone, a fence, a ford below. The ford-keeper at the fence, calling it in to the lean-to: **"Five off the ridge"**; and from inside, **"I know."**
2. **The table.** The lean-to against the mill's warm wall. Fifteen on the bench since first light. The five sit down at the bench's end and wait their turn, as she said they would. He watches her work: **a crushed hand** (a miller's man, between the sack-hoist's rope and its drum); **a child's chest**; **an old woman's knee with a ladder in it**, the knee that she had put an odd day by for, come down to her on a hurdle. A dozen small, one hand each.
3. **His turn.** "You." He stands at her stride and a half and asks: may he read her. **"No."** … **"You asked. That's the rule and you kept it. The answer's no. Next."** He sits down and enters it first, before anything else of that day. Karis: **"She was curious about you. Enter that somewhere."** Lira is next: the arm read, a count of ten; nothing said but "As it was."
4. **Her arithmetic.** The bench empty; she washes; he tells her where they will be. Finger by finger: sixteen here; seventeen at the ford with the oxen; a day south and a day back makes nineteen there, and a child who cannot be heard before the hundred and twenty-eighth. **"You're asking me to break the route."** / **"I'm not asking. I'm telling you where we'll be."** / **"That's worse. That's a healer's sentence."** **"I'm not going to be anybody's sixth."** She stands with it for a count of twenty. Then she sends word up the route by a carter who is loading meal: that she is late, how late, why, and where to send for her if the child's breathing changes.
5. **Terms.** (protected) "You're on the route now. Which means when I say lie still, you lie still, and when I say I can't help you, you believe me." Lira's line about the bowls (mine; the packet names it and does not give it). The mill's jenny, the thirty feet of three-strand, and the brown mule who looks at her old mistress and is not touched. Up to the fork: **the fork log** (protected). Two mules, six people.

## Chapter 50 — The Square of Grey (day 114, forenoon to dusk)
