## Unscored reader response

Interest first caught in Chapter 45 when Pike recites the crew's Fallow return and the whole inn silently revises its estimate of them. The cleared place on the hide-woman's bench, Teague standing through the recitation, and Pike admitting that his book has no column for what they did turn an abstract ranking into social consequence. The second contract then gives the movement its durable hook: it is obviously a lure, everybody knows it is a lure, and the real question is whether the trap's one false assumption makes entering it a rational choice.

Interest strengthened through the camp council because each vote exposes a different private want. Lira's refusal is practical rather than fearful; Brom's horse story makes the price itself suspicious; Karis admits that knowledge could make her walk in alone; Seln finally says that he wants a face; and Cael identifies the one fact the trap's designers do not possess. The strongest emotional material after that is Oryn's consent scene on the ridge, especially Cael's recognition that she is “built to arrive,” and Seln's overnight decision not to remove the choice from the crew by taking the case away himself.

The movement becomes most compelling from Brom's shooting through the day underground and Cael's dusk escape. The action is spatially legible, the enemy plan is intelligent, and restraint—not raw output—is Cael's hardest and most important act. The eventual declaration works because the preceding chapters have made every range, cost, delay, line of sight, and secret matter.

Interest slipped in three places. First, the opening road material is observant and humane but asks a cold reader to hold many unexplained prior references before the new pressure becomes visible. Second, four bracketed `[[TIER-B: ...]]` production notes replace prose at high-value emotional or explanatory beats; each one throws the reader completely out of the fiction. Third, after the rim confrontation resolves, Chapter 52 delivers several consecutive layers of measurement and classification. The discoveries are worthwhile, especially the unfinished “third site confirmed on bearing” line, but the sequence briefly feels like a second procedural climax after the dramatic climax has already discharged.

Cael matters most to me in this movement. His secret advantage drives the plot, but his attachment comes from the costs he accepts to keep that advantage secret and from the new discipline of asking permission, accepting “no,” and recording dissent honestly. Seln and Oryn are close behind: both are competent adults learning not to turn care into unilateral control.

I expect the next major event to be the daylight plotting of the bearing through the Ring and Stair, followed by a journey toward the third site. I also expect consequences from the western organization learning that its Quieting trap failed and that Cael can declare more than one kind of capability. I would continue. The movement pays off its immediate trap, changes how Teague and the road community understand the crew, and opens a larger geographical and institutional mystery.

Physical continuity is strong. Brom's bolt enters behind the right shoulder and exits below the collarbone; it remains as a plug while they are inside the Quieting, is cut and drawn only after Oryn's Tide returns, and the resulting carrying restriction is explicitly routed to his left side when he later bears the kit. Cael's Shadow cold, Storm lag, Compression impact, Ember burn, hunger, shaking, and delayed arm pain all survive the victory scene.

The ammunition account also closes: the shot into Brom, misses during the retreat and siege, the stair bolt, and the final shot at Cael total thirteen. Chapter 52 locates eleven shafts on or near the exposed floor, one on the stair, and Brom's divided bolt. The crew's own spanned bow is carried and guarded but never fired, consistent with Cael's log.

### Power limits

The Quieting's boundary and effect remain coherent within the supplied chapters: ordinary Paths stop at the edge without warning, while Cael's fragments continue to function. Anchor needs proximity to perceive the fence and touch from inside to unbind its knot. Shadow conceals Cael but exacts progressive cold and attention; the read helps him avoid loose objects but drops when his attention shifts; Wind has a one-count landing lock; Storm imposes ten counts of delay; Compression redirects a priced blow; and Ember supplies a small, tactically sufficient flame. Oryn's large mendings behave like a daily purse: three are spent at Millrace on Day 113, and two are used after the escape on Day 114. I found no internal power-limit contradiction.

### Knowledge boundaries and apparent inconsistency

The manuscript is unusually disciplined about the difference between observation and inference. Seln refuses to assign meaning to the punched mark; Karis distinguishes touching a wall from reading it; Cael separates what the rim watchers could see from what happened under cover; and Teague declines questions whose answers he could not safely carry. Those boundaries make one late certainty stand out.

Chapter 48 dates the old bar to roughly five cold terms and the newer marked bar to one or two. In Chapter 52, the unused bar in the abandoned kit carries the same added mark as the newer bar, and Lira calls it “the fellow of the newer one.” That evidence associates the kit at least as naturally with the later resurvey. Cael's final log nevertheless states, “Someone measured this place five years ago and wrote bearing and stopped.” The manuscript may have an unstated reason for selecting the older date, but it is not available in these chapters. As written, this is an apparent knowledge/timeline inconsistency rather than a verified canon error.
