## The ground at the Hall (one picture, used for chapters 53–56)

A shallow round basin, the third low place. From a rise on its north-western side the old track's last furlong runs straight down to the water. The basin is a marsh of reeds and black water, and the reeds stop all at one edge, which is the edge of the Quieting: two hundred and twenty yards and a stride from the middle (measured by cord on the 120th: C3). At the track's end, on the edge: Karis's grey stone, and one iron bar, old, scaled, *thirty-three*, with no second and no mark beneath the number. Along the water's edge the mud is heaved up from below in long ridges, and none of them leaves the reeds.

From the stake a **causeway** of the old grey runs straight in, a stride wide and a hand above the water, a hundred and eighty-five yards, to **the lip**: the kerb (the outer coping) of a round grey platform seventy yards across, a dish that holds a foot or two of clear moving water over a floor broken into great plates, old breaks, every edge round. In the middle stands **the Hall**: four walls of the grey, twelve strides square, roofless, a yard thick, broken down in places to chest height and standing three times a man's height in others; one door gap in the wall that faces the causeway, with a raised sill. Inside, the floor is a ledge three or four strides wide round a **breach**, the broken centre, where the spring comes up; in the middle of the breach one great plate lies aslant, a hand under water. The inner faces of the walls are cut in courses.

On the lip, a few yards from the causeway's head, a bar of the registry's iron leans, bent over at the height of a knee, from below. It is read from the stake (a hundred and eighty-five yards) by Lira's eyes.

**The wold-wyrm** (Oryn's word for it, from a salt carter she mended): long as three carts, blind, a blunt head like the bow of a skiff that opens in two halves; mud-dark above, pale beneath. It lives under the marsh and under the platform's plates, and has never been out of the circle. It is seen in pieces until the fight. It is not killed.

**Its rule, as the trials find it:** blind; it reads weight on the floor; it strikes where the weight is; it strikes what STAYS. A stone left lying is taken at a count of eleven; a skipped stone is let alone while it moves and taken where it stops, at fourteen; a swell (the bulge) goes round under the plates about once in a quarter of an hour; people walking along the causeway are followed and not struck. The kerbs and wall-tops come up out of the old work and are not "the floor".

## Chapter 53 — A Map of Days (dusk of day 115; days 116–118) · Cael ≈5,400

## Chapter 54 — What Stays (day 119) · Cael ≈5,200

1. **In.** First light, mist on the reeds. The file over the causeway. Oryn stops on the lip. Five cross thirty yards of plates without once standing; the door; the walls, cut inside in courses; the breach and the spring. Karis's wall is the eastern, chest-high where it has broken.
2. **Brom stops.** He bends his back for her, one-armed, and stands. The floor opens under him at a count of four. He puts her up before he goes sideways. Seln (protected): "It's blind. It reads the floor. Get *off* the floor."
3. **The coping as cover and trap.** Who is where: Karis and Lira on the east wall, Seln on the north, Cael on the north-east corner, Brom on the south wall by the door, alone (the wrong wall: the lowest, slick, and it leads nowhere). The thing goes round. Nobody can come down. Karis hangs head-down and rubs the wall she is kneeling on; the protected exchange (Lira / Karis / Lira), once. Twelve sheets begin.
4. **The shoulder.** Brom tries to work along his wall to the door's sill; the right arm comes out of its binding to save him; it opens; he slips OUTWARD, onto the platform's plates on the causeway side, and stays where he falls. **Oryn into the water, skipping** — never two counts on one plate — since she is the nearest person to a patient who has fallen. The second pass turns out under the door toward where she is going.
5. **Cael in, declaring.** *A thing that hunts what stays commits to the thing that stays hardest.* Off the corner onto the ledge at the broken edge, in its road; the read under water; the strike; **Compression** takes it standing. Then two strides into the breach, his palm on the aslant plate: **Anchor**. It comes up after the one thing on its floor that will not come away, and it comes up whole. "Brom," Cael said. "Wall. Your weight. Now." Oryn gets him onto the sill.
6. **Four bolts into the pale.** Lira from the wall-top, the stock across her knee and her shoulder against the coping: one; Seln spans; two. The thing rolls and her line is gone; Seln has it from the north wall: he takes the bow, spans and shoots twice. Cael lets the point go; the jaws close on water; it lets go and goes down, alive. Out, moving. `[[TIER-B: "Walls… Twelve sheets."]]`. It is left there.
