## Unscored reader response

Interest caught immediately in Chapter 38 when Cael says, “I opened it.” The fantastical mechanism is interesting, but the stronger hook is ethical: he knowingly used Oryn’s work without her consent. Oryn’s “You turned my table round” makes the violation intelligible without reducing either character to a position in an argument. Her decision to keep the answer he denied her the chance to give is a precise, painful consequence.

Interest rose again when the new Tide-adjacent fragment became useful rather than merely collectible. Brom’s forge analogy, the lagging ground, and Lira’s “Wait for the ground” turn an inward sensory change into visible training and a real efficiency gain. The birthday meal then supplies earned warmth without pretending the breach is settled. The Quieting investigation catches in a different way: apples teach Karis intellectual restraint, and that lesson becomes method rather than decoration.

Interest slipped most in Chapter 41’s chronology and across the middle of the research sequence. Chapter 41 moves from noon on day 104 back to the night of day 103, then forward again. The signals are present, but the reversal made me stop and reconstruct the order. Chapters 41–43 also restate Karis’s refusal to promote inference into finding several times. The repetition is thematically sound, but eventually I was admiring the method more than feeling the mystery advance. The unresolved production tokens caused the only complete breaks in immersion.

Oryn matters most to me in this movement. Her ethics, appetite for knowledge, refusal to forgive cheaply, care for Lira, gift of the mule, and lonely road afterward all belong to one recognizable person. Lira is close behind: paying for the birthday one-handed and learning to shoot from stillness make her adaptation emotionally legible.

I expect the crew to leave Thornwater, use the broker’s money to locate another Quieting, and discover that the western registry has begun moving in response to the report. I also expect Cael’s three self-readings and eventual report to Oryn to expose the unmoving thing inside him without resolving it cleanly.

I would continue. The strongest reason is not the abstract mystery but the way knowledge now has interpersonal costs: who may inspect whom, who gets told first, and who is already keeping records in the west.

## Continuity, limits, and disclosure check

### Canon

No editorial canon evidence was supplied, so I cannot verify canon contradictions. Any concern below is about the internal evidence of these seven chapters only. I found no apparent contradiction that rises above a clarity question.

### Physical state

Physical state is unusually well carried. Lira’s left arm remains tied and unused; the eighth mend is slower than the other seven; her shin may run while the foot must still land flat; and her crossbow technique is built around those exact constraints. Karis’s damaged voice improves by stages but is still restricted. Brom’s string marks harden as expected. Cael’s forearm and ribs cease to dominate once their bills are nearly paid. Oryn’s ordinary fatigue and expenditure remain distinct from loss of her Tide craft.

The only practical concern is reader load, not state logic: Lira’s arm, shin, ankle/foot, sling, and shooting stance are all tracked at once. The repeated healer checks keep them coherent, but a cold reader may briefly treat “the bone may run” as permission for the whole leg until Oryn immediately narrows it.

### Power limits

The movement establishes usable limits rather than convenient ones. Cael acquires a partial fragment only after sustained, costly, directed use with shared stakes; Oryn retains her ability; the new read begins as contact/self at arm’s length; it reveals current and cost but not instant explanation; and its first use destabilizes timing. Wind improves through sensory feedback and practice, not a free amplification. Inside the Quieting, five sigils fail at the same marked edge while Cael’s fragments do not. That exceptional result is stated as observation, not explained.

No internal power-limit breach was apparent. The most important safeguard is ethical rather than mechanical: Oryn’s rule that consent alone is insufficient without something to read *for*. That keeps the new ability from becoming casual surveillance.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully attributed. Karis separates what was seen, stated, guessed, remembered, and hypothesized. Cael tells Oryn the origin of the fragments, preserves the Shadow donor’s identity, gives Lira the Quieting result before the group, and withholds the self-reading because Oryn has first claim. Seln’s institutional inference is labeled as such. Oryn refuses to guess outside her trade. Vastin receives only the completion report and correctly records that he does not know why it reached him.

The packet’s missing protected lines prevent a full check of what those exact exchanges disclose. In particular, Oryn’s absent loft-leaving line falls immediately before her request to be present at future Quietings; its final wording could affect the apparent commitment of the sixth member.

### Reserved disclosures

Without canon or a disclosure schedule, I cannot certify whether any revelation is early or reserved. Within the movement, the disclosures are staged effectively: Cael’s exceptional function crosses from private knowledge to Lira, then to the six; Karis keeps her causal hypothesis out of the box; the west knows that Paths stop but not yet that Cael does not. The narrative closes on that asymmetry, which is the correct pressure to carry forward.
