## The ground, as I fix it

- **Which way they walk it.** The slip names the traverse from the hirer's end (Oxhollow to Thornwater). They are at Lowmarch, a day and a half from its eastern foot and six days from Oxhollow by road, so they walk it east to west and come down on the hirer's side with the map in hand.
- **The eastern foot.** At the lower of the two crossings on the Thornwater road (the first one met from Lowmarch; the ford of Movement 2 is the upper), a side water comes in from the north. An old track goes up it for two hours to where the valley shuts into a **gorge**. They do not pass Thornwater, and the stretch is not walked.
- **The fan.** At the gorge's mouth, on its south side, a fan of shale scree comes down from the end of a shelf: two hundred and sixty yards of slope, at the pitch of a barn roof, with a gully along its gorge-ward edge.
- **The bench.** A shelf along the gorge's south wall, high above the water, running west: thirty feet wide at best and six at worst, **pitched outward like a roof**, floored with slab, with aprons of scree where gullies cross it. The face of the mountain rises on the left hand; the gorge is on the right. The gorge **gives every sound back late**, off its north wall.
- **The unit.** A *hundred* is a hundred of Cael's strides, at a yard to the stride. Lira runs each hundred out and back before the cart is asked to go on it. At the end of each hundred Karis takes the **width** across the shelf with her knotted cord (in feet; the cord's whole length is never stated), the **pitch** (hands of fall to the yard, by a pebble on a thread hung from the brass straightedge) and the **depth** (a stone dropped and counted). Cael writes one line to the hundred: number, width, pitch, footing, water, and whether it takes a cart. Seventy marks on some sixty-odd lines: he is paid by the line.
- **The cart** is their own old cart, bought by the smith on day 12 and cut to a mule's gauge, hired back. It is drawn by hand: two in the shafts (Seln and Cael), Brom on a breast-strap on the up-slope side.
- **The herd's shelf** runs along the face three to four hundred yards above the bench. The herd drifts west along it at about the party's pace.
- **The narrowings**, numbered as they come: eleven feet, nine, nine and a half, seven, eight, six and a hand, five feet nine, and the eighth, which is wide enough and **broken clean across**: a slot two feet wide with edges like a sawn board.
- **The bay.** Beyond the eighth the bench opens for two hundred yards into a bay forty yards deep, floored with a scree apron. Seln's overhang and a seep of water are at its eastern end.
- **The fin.** In the bay's western third, a blade of pale hard rock stands out of the scree and runs straight down the fall line: fifteen yards long, head-high at its upper end (the nose), twice that in the middle, a yard or two thick. On its down-slope side there is a hollow of bare floor, where slides have parted before and not filled in.
- **The ledge.** From the bay's western end to the mountain's shoulder: three hundred yards of level shelf, six to eight feet wide. Its last part hangs under the shoulder. Beyond the shoulder the track goes down the western slope.
- **The lip.** What is left where the ledge went: a scar four or five feet wide, tilted outward, loose with plates, thirty-one paces long, above the gorge.

## Chapter 16 — the fan and the first of the bench (days 72–74)

The boxes go up by hand (Seln and Cael one between them, in nines; Brom one on his back in a strap: carrying is not pulling). Cost: the calf shuts three times on the slope.
3. **The haul (day 73).** The ferryman's rope over a rock horn at the fan's top; the cart on the upper end; the haulers walk *down* as she goes up, Brom in a chest loop as the counterweight, hands behind him. She floats sideways toward the gully on the second pitch; Lira, sitting braced behind a boulder with a tag line round her back, holds her head up. Karis at the horn calls the rope. Four pitches. Nothing is used: Anchor asks about the horn, Compression about the strap, and the Iron read offers him the whole weight of the slope, which is the one thing a surveyor would most like to know.
4. **The bench.** The first hundreds: Lira runs, Karis's cord, the pebble on the thread, the stone dropped and the count that comes back twice. He writes the first line and is told by Lira what he is being paid for it. Growth: the habit he was mocked for is a trade.
5. **Seln's overhang.** At dusk Seln is already there, with a floor swept and water found. Day 74 opens: hundreds nine to twelve; and above them, on a shelf of the face, a line of grey plated backs that he first takes for the mountain.

## Chapter 17 — the herd and Karis's day (days 74–75)

1. **Thirty.** What a shale-back looks like and sounds like (plates like roofing slabs, lichen on them; a low beaked head; a noise like a slate roof settling). He counts thirty, and looks for the one that moves first, as he found it at the ford, and there is none to find. They are below them, since the bench goes nowhere else.
2. **A cheap test.** That evening Karis asks for a fire: a flint fire in sight of the herd, which can be put out. No head comes up. Voices next: the gorge has been giving Brom's tail-board back all day. Two of the country's three are struck off. The third cannot be afforded.
3. **Karis's day (the learning encounter).** She asks for a day and gets it. A seat on a slab; Hesk's clock; Cael as her second pair of eyes and her hand when hers tires. (a) **Spacing:** a length and a half between each along one contour. (b) **The line moves** forty yards in an hour and she has not seen one animal take a step. (c) **A rock** comes down from above and goes between two of them, bounding: each takes one step aside, and no other head lifts (a step, not flight). (d) **The plate:** a slab lets go under the hindquarters of the easternmost; and all thirty, along three hundred yards, some of them round a shoulder of the face, step uphill together. No first animal. She saw them step, and counted, before the sound reached her.
4. **The page.** Laid beside the ford page (Lira's copy). The hounds turn all at once and then follow the first mover *toward*; the herd has no first mover and goes *away*. Whatever reached thirty animals at once, round a shoulder, ahead of the sound, is the thing seven hounds turned to. Same sense, opposite answer. Pike's word was *loud*. **"Derived. Not tested."** Lira asks whether a point the size of a seed would test it. **Protected: "On a slope, flight is a landslide with legs."** **"Write it in yours, too. I'd like it in two hands."** He writes it in the log. Growth: he learns a category his own catalogue lacked (derived, entered as such) and what it is to be asked to hold someone else's finding.
5. **The night of the 75th.** Brom on what he is carrying; Lira on running as work.

## Chapter 19 — the ledge and the stampede (day 78)

Positions at first light: the cart drawn out onto the scree apron thirty yards east of the fin, to wait for the ledge; Seln and Cael at the shafts; Brom on the strap, up-slope; Karis at the off wheel with the cord; Lira on the ledge, eighty yards out. The herd on its shelf, three hundred and fifty yards up. Objectives: five people and three boxes alive on the far side of what comes down. Limits: no declaration but the one that cannot be helped; Cael's calf; Brom's wrist; Lira's ankle.

1. **First light.** Frost. Lira goes out; he writes the number of the first ledge-hundred and does not count her strides.
2. **The ledge goes.** A length of it peels off under her; her body bursts before she chooses, and throws her back onto sound rock. The crack crosses the gorge and comes back late. **Thirty heads come up**, all at once. They go east along their shelf, away from the burst, and the shelf will not hold them at a run; it sheds, and they come down the face in a front eighty yards wide.
3. **"There. Thirty seconds. Lee side."** Cael, pointing at the fin. Seln takes Karis. Brom, out of the strap, looks at the scree creeping round the wheels: **"Cart's gone."** He drops the tail-board and takes two boxes, one to a shoulder; the wrist goes dark under the cloth.
4. **Karis at the nose.** The fin is a yard thick and thirty animals are coming; it will not turn them alone. She has a derived finding and nothing else. She begins to lay points up the face, points first, joins last. The third box is still on the cart. Cael goes back for it.
5. **The line.** A chevron of fire across the face, its apex eleven paces above the nose: a declaration the size of a road. The front splits at eleven paces. He is eight yards short, on the east side, with a box on his right shoulder. **Compression and Anchor want.** He prices both: either is a second declaration, on the lee side of her east arm, and animals caught between two loud things do not choose well; the split stands on her line and one more loud thing turns it. He holds. A plated shoulder takes him raw: **three ribs, left side, low; the calf opened on the shale.** He keeps the box. Brom and Seln drag him in.
6. **Eight seconds.** She sits down at the eighth, emptied; the last of them are by. **The cart floats** on the moving apron, turns once and goes into the gorge with its shafts up; the sound comes back late. Things heavier than a cart are heard to fall as well: **heard, not sworn.** Four in the hollow. He finds Lira three strides short of it on the west side, with her right leg under a plate.
7. **Lira's window (≈1,800; two scenes).** From first light: running as work; the ankle a beat behind; the ledge going; **the burst her body chose before she did**; the landing; her own crack coming back to her off the far wall; thirty heads; knowing whose noise it was. The run in: Wind says *now* at every stride and she refuses it, since a second burst is a second shout. The road of fire across the mountain. The plate. Lying under it while the west stream goes by. The boy's face over her, grey. **Protected: "That was me," … "The loud thing."** / "It was the ledge. The ledge went. Then it was you." / "Then it was me."

## Chapter 22 — Oxhollow (day 84), and two windows

## Amendments made while drafting (recorded after the prose; the list above is as first written)

- **The fin's geometry.** The list had the fin running down the fall line with a "nose" uphill. As drafted it lies **square across the fall of the ground**, like a wall across a field, halfway between the foot of the face and the rim: as long as a barn, a yard or so thick, waist-high at either end and twice a man's height in the middle, with shale banked chest-high along its upper side and a strip of bare floor twelve feet deep on its lower (lee) side. The wall stops stone; it would not have stopped thirty animals, which is why Karis's line is needed. Karis stands at its low eastern end. Her chevron's apex is eleven paces above the wall's middle; each arm runs down and outward to a little beyond the wall's end: **nine points and eight channels, about sixty yards from end to end.**
- **Who goes back for what.** All five reach the lee first. Karis says that the wall will stop the stone and not the herd. Brom looks back at the cart, says the packet line, and *then* runs back for two boxes. Cael goes after the third while she is laying points. The tail-board bangs this time, with nobody there to stop it.
- **Cael counts her set points** by the small dip of her hand and the direction of her eyes, as he has watched her lay them for two years. No fragment perceives anything. (A first draft gave this to the Ember fragment; it was taken out, so that nothing of his answers on the mountain, even passively.)
- **Where the shoulder takes him:** eight yards short of the wall's end and outside the foot of her eastern arm, in the edge of the eastern stream. He gives it his left side, as Lira told him at the ford, and is thrown toward the wall with the box.
- **The ledge.** The part that goes is the middle of it, from about eighty yards out, for thirty-one paces; the ledge is sound beyond that to the shoulder. **The lip** is the scar where that part was rooted: four feet wide, tilted outward, loose with plates. Seln scratches a mark on each stone that bears: **forty-four marks.**
- **The cart was drawn out onto the bay's frozen shale at first light** on Brom's reasoning (the frost glues the plates for an hour). It stands thirty yards east of the wall when the ledge goes.
- **The fan haul** is in **six** stages, not four, since the ferryman's rope will not reach farther between rock islands. The rope runs over a peeled log bedded in a notch (Brom's), not over bare rock. Karis signals with her arms (one up, walk; both, hold).
- **The hundreds** reach sixty-two at the bay. Line sixty-three is the ledge, written by Karis on the eighty-second evening: *Sixty-three. The ledge. Gone, for thirty-one paces. Cart: no.*
- **Seln's report at the fan's foot** carries two tales from Pike: a salt string on the cold road whose Force shifted a block with his Path under a ledge herd; and roofing-stone cutters who once came up this same side water with carts and stopped coming. The wheel-ruts on the track are theirs.
- **Karis's day, the fourth observation:** she starts a count when the plate tips, sees both ends of the line step in the same instant, and has said *one* and begun *two* before the sound reaches her. Cael, watching the westernmost animal beyond a rib of the hill, hears its own plates before he hears the slide. Brom's objection (the ground shaking) is answered by the noon rock, which struck the herd's own shelf and moved two. Lira supplies the word "frightened".
- **The night of the seventy-fifth** became its own short scene: Seln back with a report of the eight narrowings, the bay and the ledge; Brom's "So it needn't be on purpose"; Lira's promise to run under the wall.
- **Seln goes down for help at first light on the eightieth** and meets the healer already on the track below the shoulder; he walks in front of her mule across the lip. She has come because a carter at the foot of the hill saw the mountain come down and knew from the plank that a crew had gone up it.
- **Day 79 has no entry and day 78 has none.** The log resumes on the eightieth with the protected entry.
- **Oryn's rule for Brom** is given on the morning of the eighty-first, with the wrap: a week from the mending; "It'll take what sits on it. It won't take what hangs from it." The packet's "Carrying." / "Not pulling." is at the lip: Brom says the first and Oryn the second, agreeing.
- **The lip, crossing order:** Seln; Oryn and the mule with two boxes (and back); the mule with Lira up; Brom with the third box on his back; Seln back for Karis; Cael last. At the twentieth pace an unmarked slab that looks better than the marked stone turns over behind him.
- **The herd is counted at twenty-five** from the western ledge on the eighty-first. That is not entered as five dead.
- **The ankle** is refused at the noon halt on the eighty-second; the route is told at that night's fire, on the valley road. **The map is paid at the end of chapter 21** (the eighty-fourth morning, while the healer eats and sleeps); chapter 22 opens on her table.
- **Oxhollow's table:** a scald gone bad, an ox-driver's foot, a boy's collarbone; five others looked at and sent away. The stall is named once, in answer to Lira.
- **Oryn's window** is in two scenes (the hill out of Oxhollow, where she passes Teague's four; then the eighty-sixth to the ninety-second). The hold that gave the coat is the next north of Oxhollow; the child is at the gate after it and is no worse. The carter's hand is owed at Thornwater. She has added a stop three times before (the fifth, sixth and seventh holds).
- **What they have cost Teague:** he had timed his second slip to meet the healer's round at the upper hold on the eighty-first, for the bow's hip, and waited a day, and she did not come. He learns why from her own mouth on the hill and from Oxhollow's yard and keeper.
- **Windows as drafted:** Lira about 1,900 words in two scenes (chapter 19); Oryn about 2,100 in two (chapter 22); Teague about 1,250 in one (chapter 22).
- **Rhythm:** chapters 17, 20 and 21 measured short in first draft (means 11.0–12.0) and were re-composed by reading in the same session (17 and 21 rewritten whole); chapters 15, 16, 18 and 19 had too many sentences of forty words or more and were parted by hand. A vocabulary pass then put the series' own words (Path, fragment, declaration, reserve, mending, the surface and deep readings) where plainer ones had stood.
