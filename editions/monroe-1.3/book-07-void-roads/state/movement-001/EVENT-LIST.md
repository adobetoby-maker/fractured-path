## What I decided where the plan is open

- **The opening** is the end of the first watch at the shelf, the night of day 2: the same fire Book 6 closed on, burned down. One log line, then scenes. The skip is on the page.
- **Time** is kept by Hesk's clock, which loses, and which Karis ruled "right, by definition" in Book 6. The log counts days past the Line.
- **The courier** is a woman of fifty or so with a split lip long healed, a hat cut away on one side, and a hatchet in a saddle loop. She is coming west along the valley and meets them at the ford. She does not explain the ford; she rides its line (upstream cairn to downstream cairn) and they copy it. She waters the mule below the crossing. She asks how far the letters go and nothing else: not who, not what, not where they are bound. A copper each "to the turf fire". Distinct from Book 6's post-rider (a man, waiting for water to fall, who read the outside aloud and asked where she was to write to).
- **The three carts** (day 6): an elder carter with two mules; his sister's boy, about fifteen, on the second cart; a hide-woman with the third. They fall in behind and are not hailed. The boy raises a hand to show the strangers something his Path can do and the elder knocks it down: "Not on the road." The elder says the "paper" line to Cael at the evening halt. Cael answers "Yes." These three recur in the meadow (the elder is the carter under the ash; later plans call him "the carter from the first week").
- **"Paper"** is the edge word for people from the registered country: people with papers. What marks the five as paper: a shod horse, iron tyres, three roped boxes, boots from one maker.
- **The hold** gets its name from Cael's method. Claim: nobody out here uses a Path on a road, lights a fire they can do without, or raises a voice; everybody shows steel. Evidence: five items. Ruling: the reason is unknown; *hold*. From day 6 nobody declares anything on a road, and Cael holds all nine.
- **Lowmarch's ground** (mine; flagged): the road comes down to a wide slow river. The town is on the east bank. A cable ferry — a flat scow wound across by a capstan on the east bank, with a beast walking the capstan's circle. Above the landing, the lower inn (crews; the wall is inside it); on the rise, the upper inn (brokers, carters with money); the smith between. The meadow runs along the river below the lower inn, with one big ash in it. No wall anywhere. Four roads leave: the west road they came by; the north water road; the east road (Thornwater, two days); the south road; and a track off the north road that the meadow calls the cold road.
- **The board** is a tarred ship's plank on two posts under the lower inn's eave, facing the meadow. Slips are tacked to it. Pike calls it the plank. His ledger lies on it. **Pike's ink-pot** is horn, set in a hole bored in the plank so it cannot tip. **His stone** is a flat river stone that holds the page down, since he has no second hand to do it; postings waiting to go up sit under its edge. He reads everything twice.
- **Fourteen slips on day 9** (mine): the landing broker's six crossings (two marks); a fence-line at a steading on the north water (four and flour); a well on the south road (four; no detail given — Movement 2 owns the hazard); roof shingles at the upper inn; fleece carried down from a north hold; a strayed mule; a night-watch on the landing; stone-picking; a message to a hold four days off; and others. None says what tier or rank the crew must be. That is the missing thing Cael reads for.
- **Pike's lines:** "Copper, Force, thirty years on the roads." is his answer to Lira's "What are you?". "Walk, carry, climb, fight." is his answer to "What do crews do?"; Lira: "In that order?"; Pike: "Depends on the day." No captain is named, so the heading is five names. Cael says he is eighteen when Pike asks.
- **How a line is made** (mine; flagged): the hirer leaves the price with Pike when the slip goes up. A crew takes a slip at the plank and Pike marks it with their heading in front of whoever is there. The job's witness marks the slip when it is done. Pike reads the line aloud before he writes it and again after. Then he pays. The wall inside the lower inn is one sheet, one line to a crew, in the order Pike's book puts them, rewritten by hand when the order changes.
- **C1 / OWNER #23 — the sale** lands in chapter 3, days 10–12. Fodder arithmetic is Brom's (the meadow charges by the net; four nets a day). Seln's point: a shod horse on a mule road is a sign that says *paper*. Lira argues for the gelding. The joke: nobody ever named him ("You don't name what you sell." / "I didn't name him. I only called him things."), and the ferryman's capstan is a circle with no hill in it. **The gelding goes to the ferryman** to walk the capstan (his old mule is failing). **The cart goes to the smith**, who means to cut its axle to mule gauge and hire it out (a possible root for "the smith's cart" in Movement 3; flagged, not relied on). Brom keeps the hedging bill that came under the cart's seat board: **Brom's bill-hook**. **Lira's long knife** is the last thing the old purse buys, at the smith's, before the wax goes on. **Seln's case** goes back on its strap.
- **The reserve:** the sale and the rest of the Book 6 purse, sewn into canvas by Karis, waxed by Brom, five thumbs in the wax, in the bottom of the third box. Opened by five hands or not at all. **Brom's rule: we eat what the board pays.** The log's "in hand" counts the board purse only.
- **Where the boxes live:** part of the gelding's price is dry room in the loft over the ferryman's capstan-house. The boxes stay there for the nearer jobs. They ride the Thornwater carter's grain on day 42 because Karis left them three days once and will not learn whether she can do five.
- **The ferry escort** (day 14): the landing broker's consignment, six crossings, two marks. The job is to stand on the scow where both banks can see them. The broker's mark is a **hook and a bar**, in dark wax on the slip and burned on his bales: a plant only; nothing is implied about him. Cael has done this job before, "at a higher price and for worse people": standing on a carved number in a wool exchange. Small problem: on the fifth crossing a drift log comes down on the cable; Brom fends it with the sweep and does not harden.
- **The hold as a learning arc** (days 15–21, the gravel bar below the meadow at first light). Cael teaches, because two years of a managed band and a sealed fragment are the only practice anybody has. Lira runs the bar flat out and must stop on her feet where the burst would go ("a burst is a shout"); on the third run her body bursts without her. Brom carries a drift log over slick stones and must drop it when it shifts, since his skin comes up of itself; he has never dropped a load. Karis sets one point on a stone and does not light it, and it lights itself at the count of ninety. Cael's lesson: you do not refuse it at the end; you refuse it at the first stride and then again at every stride.
- **The nine as things held**, in the log, each with its rent, as the ledger prices them: Wind (the one-count landing lock); Pressure (gathers over most of a breath; shows); the Iron read (a pace and a half; mass and vector; cheapest and quietest); Compression (at contact; the bill comes after); Ember (one point; one channel); Shadow (sealed; costs from waking; drifts at dusk); Storm (Gold weight: air or body, not both; lanes billed ten, ten, twelve and clean at eleven, eleven, thirteen; everything answering a beat late is bill, not flag; untested past three; the heaviest thing he owns); Anchor (points, lines, a cage; one unbinding on purpose, one doorway held twelve seconds without his leave; sure at ten feet, faint at twelve, gone at fourteen; reach, rate and price unknown; wants every doorway since the Ostrand road); Blade (partial; asks *Edge?*). The anomaly, uncounted: *Still open. Still real. Patience.*
- **The fence-line** (days 22–24): a steading a day up the north water, held by an old man with a dog. Forty posts cut in the coppice and set. Brom cuts six hours with a borrowed axe and the bill and never once hardens when the axe kicks. Karis means to char the post-butts with a point, as a fencer chars them in a fire; the old man says "Not at my fence. Use the brands."; she does not ask why, and writes down the not-asking. He pays four marks and a sack of flour and says "Cheap." Brom and animals: the dog sits on his feet; a goat eats the tally.
- **Shadow drifting, twice, on a road with nobody on it:** day 5 (the valley) and day 24 (the north water road). Each time his footfalls go out from under him for a few strides before he catches it. Seln hears the absence both times. Neither is a deployment and the log says so.
- **The wash** (day 33): the hide-woman's four-wheeled wagon, coming west, has broken a front wheel on the east ramp and lies canted in the bed of the wash with a storm standing over the ridge. Hides out by hand first. A skid pole lashed under the broken corner (three wheels and a skid). The haul rope goes up the near (west) ramp to her two mules and Brom; the check line goes to Lira on the far bank, snubbed round a thorn stump, to stop the wagon rolling onto its broken corner. Cael and Seln at the wheels with a pole and the ash wedge from the Ostrand road. Karis reads the water on a notched stick. The wagon slips; Brom sits down in the rope and is dragged a yard and does not harden; the wedge goes in. The line fouls and Lira runs round by the bed and not across the air. The water comes down as the tailboard clears. Three marks. The hide-woman, who heard him say "Yes" on day 6: "You're not paper." / "I am."
- **The Thornwater road:** the carter is Thornwater's own, one cart and one mule, carrying grain in sacks up to the hold's loft. He posted the slip at noon and nobody took it. "The stretch" is a mile of valley short of the hold's fence, walked at noon by custom; he is a day late. He never says what is on it. The wheel on day 43 is the off wheel's linch-pin and a split felloe; Brom wires it; it costs an hour. They have forded the valley's water twice that afternoon, in the light. **I do not fix where Thornwater ford lies relative to the stretch** (flagged for Movement 2).
- **Positions on the stretch** (Seln sets them, having walked the first miles of this road alone on the twentieth day): Seln thirty paces ahead on the left verge; Lira and Cael abreast, ten paces ahead of the mule, one to a rut; the carter at the mule's head with his hand on its nose; Karis at the mule's off shoulder; Brom behind the tailboard with the bill.
- **The valley's noise** is frogs in the wet ground and crickets in the grass. **The hole in the noise** is a patch of silence, forty or fifty yards off, that travels at their pace: Lira, who listens to air for a living, hears it first and puts her hand up.
- **The five items** (Cael's observation, by count, naming nothing he cannot count): it keeps station at forty-five yards; it makes no sound; its head tracks the buckle (the breeching buckle on the mule's harness, the one thing in the party that ticks); it will not cross the gravel fan (a spread of wet gravel from a side gully; it goes round by the slope and comes back to its station); it reacts to nothing, because they gave it nothing. It stops a hundred and ten yards short of the fence, by his count of paces.
- **The exchanges on the stretch** are placed as the packet gives them. Brom "exactly as large as his body and no larger". Karis decides five times not to take out the notebook; he counts them by her hand.
- **Thornwater** (mine, within the plan): a grain hold. A fence of split posts across the valley's head with one gate; a yard; a barn with the grain loft over it; a well. The steading's woman meets them with a lantern. The bowman is on the gate's platform with an arrow on the string and the bow held low. The carter says the "First crew" line and "It's a stillhound. You shut up. It went home."; the woman says "Stillhound." first; she says "You're paper." and "Hm." She makes her mark on the slip. Karis has one word and underlines it once. She does not ask the second question.
- **Six days at Thornwater** (43–48): the crew works for its keep (sacks up the ladder; the fence walked). The carter goes down the road with the marked slip at first light on day 44 and is back on the fourth day, day 46, in the afternoon. He brings Pike's line copied on a scrap, so that they can say it is wrong before Pike says it for the meadow. Brom resets the off wheel properly; the carter pays for it with his own road map, drawn on scraped hide: roads, fords, holds, days between, and bare hide wherever a wheel has no business. **This is "the carter's map."**
- **Seln's window** is split in two (≈900 each) to keep scenes near the formula's length: part one at Lowmarch in chapter 4; part two at Thornwater in chapter 6. He walks every road out of a place before the others use it, alone, unasked, and composes the report in the old form by reflex. Nobody asks for it. He is waiting for the demand, and it does not come. At Thornwater he reads where the grey thing sat and turned: it kept station like something taught to wait for a signal, and he does not know what the signal is. He knows nothing of quiet ground, opens no case, decodes nothing, and says no word of affection.
- **The day-46 log** ends on the protected line. "Season" is the board's working season, which the carter says opens with the dry roads and shuts with the snow; "theirs" is every season Cael has lived in since his Kindling (the assessment round, the circuit, the tournament, the academic year).
- **Day 49, the board anatomized.** Pike reads the Thornwater line "after" and pays nine. The north-road woman captain's line is read before (she corrects it: the mule was lame when she took it), written, and read after. The eleven-bale captain claims twelve; the witness's mark says eleven; "Say it again and I'll write it"; he does not say it again. Pike's protected explanation. The ford slip: *Thornwater ford. Clearance. Forty marks.* with HOLD and one name. Pike on HOLD: anyone can ask; he has said yes to one. Tier talk at the plank: Lira's Iron, Rank One; Brom's Copper, for good ("Same as me, then"); Pike's guess at the top crew's captain (Bronze, if anything, and never walked to a station to have it written); the protected Bronze-and-Copper line.
- **The wall** (inside the lower inn, evening, over one jug): one sheet, thirteen lines, the top line one word, the bottom line five names. One crew is entered under its mule's name (the name is not given). "Did you ever hear of a ledger-keeper called Vell?" / "No." "Four lines. Nobody lost, nobody hurt, nobody knows you." Brom: "Eleven."
- **The map turned east:** the trigger is a slip on the plank that pays eleven marks to carry a broker's chest west to the turf-fire waystation, a road they know. Seln puts his finger on the west edge of the carter's map and says the protected sentence; Brom and Karis answer as protected. Said once. He turns the hide so that east lies toward them.
- **The first pole** is written at the inn table in the last light, under the money and the standing deployment line.
- **Teague's window** (≈1,500): he stops on the lower inn's sill. He counts the spear, the bow and the boy before anything else (the healer of no Path is at the upper inn, asleep; healers are off the count). Then the job: the cold road at first light, a week. Then the board: his HOLD on the ford, and whether a week is too long. Then the bottom line of the wall, and the room. Lira from her feet. Brom as a fact. Karis underlining something, with the beginning of his own smile. Four, and the wall says five; on the second pass, Seln. Then the fifth, who is reading him. He thinks of his people only as roles. His own paper says Copper and is eleven years old (flagged: the figure is mine). He knows nothing of any mechanism. He does not speak: they have cost him nothing yet.
- **The sill** (Cael, to close): he reads Teague back, item by item. Neither speaks. The last of the day comes past the man in the door and lies across the sheet: one word at the top, five names at the bottom, eleven lines of light between.

## Chapters and scenes

### Chapter 1 — The Valley With No Name (days 2–6)
1. **The watch ends.** Shelf, night of day 2. Cael and Brom on first watch. The fire down to an eye. He counts watchers from habit; zero. The quiet thing lifted at dusk and was held; the quick one asked about the scree's edge. Hesk's clock. He wakes Lira and Karis; Seln sleeps on, the fifth. Log: the standing line. *Present:* all five. *Changes:* the log gains its nightly line.
2. **Down.** Day 3. The lower switchbacks, boxes by hand. The valley floor: a river, trees nobody can name, two ruts. Karis will not name a tree without a source; Lira names it anyway. A halting-place with mule dung and no fire-scar. They light a fire all the same; nobody knows better yet. *Changes:* first evidence, unread.
3. **The ford.** Day 4. Two cairns. The courier rides the line and waters her mule below it. Lira takes the gelding over on the same line. The letters; the price; the hatchet; what she does not ask. *Changes:* the two letters go west. He writes that she did not ask.
4. **Empty road.** Day 5. The valley opens; ruts join. Brom and the gelding's feet. Dusk: his boots go silent for four strides. Seln: "Your left boot. Then both." Held. *Changes:* the first drift; Cael learns the rent can get as far as his feet.
5. **Three carts.** Day 6. Falling in behind. A day of quiet, steel and cold food. The boy's hand. "You're paper, aren't you" / "Yes." / "Then you know how to keep quiet. Keep it." Claim, evidence, ruling. Log. *Changes:* the hold begins. Protected: the paper exchange.

### Chapter 2 — Fourteen Slips (day 9)
1. **No wall.** Days 7–9 in a paragraph; then Lowmarch from the last rise: river, ferry, two inns, smith, meadow. Cael looks for a wall, a gate, a station board, and finds none. The crossing: the scow, the capstan, an old mule walking a circle.
2. **The meadow.** Where carts stand; the ash; the elder carter under it. The meadow-keeper's boy counts hay by the net. Tents going up and coming down. Lira hears "the board" and goes to find the fights.
3. **The board.** Fourteen slips read as he once read a board at a crossroads in Ardenmere: standing in front of it first. Every name on that one had letters and a number after it. Here: a job, a hirer, a price. Lira: no ladder. "Where do you go up?"
4. **Pike.** One arm, the ink-pot in its hole, the stone. "Copper, Force, thirty years on the roads." "Walk, carry, climb, fight." / "In that order?" / "Depends on the day." No captain; five names. "Eighteen." Five columns: Job, Hirer, Witness, Outcome, Price. He reads it upside down and looks for the missing column "the way you look for a stair in the dark, with a foot."
5. **Unranked.** Evening in the meadow. What each of them is on the far side of the Line, said round the pot, and what the book wanted of it: nothing. Karis writes the five column heads and underlines *Outcome*. Log. *Changes:* a heading exists with no line under it.
