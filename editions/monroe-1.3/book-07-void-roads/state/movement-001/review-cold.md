## Unscored reader response

The opening caught me with the contradiction between vigilance and absence: Cael counts a country that contains nobody, does not trust the answer, and is gently caught doing so by Brom. The first line of real propulsion is not an attack but a cultural error—the unused stone ring they mistake for a fire ring. From there, the road becomes an accumulating mystery whose evidence is physical and social: cairns that do not face each other, water drunk below a ford, visible steel, silent carters, and the boy whose hand is put down with “Not on the road.” I was fully committed once Cael ruled “Hold.”

Interest slipped slightly in two places. First, the opening asks a cold reader to absorb the Line, Paths, tiers, an Arbiter, nine fragments, Hesk, Shadow, Blade, Storm, Seln, and an unnamed hill before the immediate journey has fully taken shape. The scene remains attractive, but some of its emotional information arrives before the reader has enough framework to weight it. Second, the complete nine-fragment “rent” inventory in Chapter 4 is valuable setup, but it pauses the forward motion just after the excellent restraint drills begin. I wanted the information and still felt the catalog.

Cael matters most to me. His freedom does not come from becoming legible to a better institution; it comes from encountering a culture that records outcomes instead of classifications. That makes “I’m free. First time.” feel earned rather than declared. Brom is the movement’s emotional surprise: the horse sale, his insistence on earned money, his refusal to let Iron Skin answer for him, and the careful division of the jug make him lovable without making him soft. Lira is the liveliest immediate presence, and Seln’s gradual discovery that nobody is waiting for his report gives the quieter character arc equal weight.

I expect the next consequential event to join Teague, the held forty-mark Thornwater job, and the unanswered rule of the stillhounds. More specifically, I expect Teague either to test the five or to be forced to reassess who should take the ford clearance, while Cael’s group learns what a declared Path gives the road’s watcher permission to do. I would continue immediately. The movement closes on a true hinge: the crew has acquired a public record, a map, a rival or future mentor, and a dangerous question that has been demonstrated but not explained.

## Manuscript-only consistency check

### Canon

No canon evidence was supplied, so there are no verified canon violations in this review. References to the hill, Ostrand, Ardenmere, Hesk, Vell, the protected Shadow source, and the prior tribunal-like proceedings cannot be checked against earlier books. Any concern below is therefore an apparent manuscript issue only.

### Physical state and resources

Physical and logistical continuity is strong. The gelding’s dislike of hills leads to a credible flat-work sale; the cart is sold after Seln establishes that eastern roads are too narrow; the three boxes move to the ferry loft, back to camp for sealing, and later onto the grain cart. Lira’s palms are raw after the wash and skinned over by Chapter 5. Brom’s suppression develops from consciously stopping grey skin at the ferry to having no declaration at all when the ladder rung breaks. Earnings also reconcile at the story level: two marks for the ferry, four and flour for the fence, three for the wagon, and nine for the stretch make the stated eighteen, before ordinary spending.

The one apparent ambiguity is the repeated log line **“Deployment since the Line: none.”** In Chapter 1, Karis has already lit multiple fires with Ember after the Line, and Chapter 1 later explicitly counts four. The line appears to mean Cael’s own deployment, but later entries also mention offers from Brom’s Path and the group’s collective restraint. A cold reader can therefore read “deployment” as a group total and momentarily perceive a contradiction. This is a scope-label problem, not evidence of a broken power rule.

### Power limits

The presented limits remain coherent. Cael’s fragments offer rather than silently solve; Shadow’s leakage improves from four strides to one; Storm has specified attentional and timing costs; Anchor repeatedly identifies fixed points but is refused; Lira’s burst is fast but habitual; Brom’s Iron Skin is protective but can substitute for learned physical judgment; Karis distinguishes setting a point from lighting it. The stillhound’s trigger and the road’s reason remain unknown by design, and the manuscript does not use that unknown power to resolve a decisive problem.

### Knowledge boundaries

Knowledge is carefully localized. The five learn road customs by observation, Pike knows witnessed outcomes rather than ranks, Thornwater knows the stillhound’s practical rule without explaining its mechanism, and Seln withholds reconnaissance until it bears on a decision. His withholding is characterized and interrogated rather than used as an invisible convenience. Teague’s knowledge of the crew comes through publicly spoken lines and carter report, which the movement has established as available channels.

### Reserved disclosures

No list of reserved disclosures was supplied, so compliance cannot be verified. On the page, the movement preserves the central disclosures: why Paths are not declared on roads, what signal activates a stillhound, why gravel stops it, what the ford clearance entails, and what Cael’s anomaly means. The ending advances those mysteries through evidence rather than answering them prematurely.

## Repair brief

### 1. Clarify whose deployment the repeated log line measures

- **Location:** Chapter 1 opening and closing entries, then every recurrence of “Deployment since the Line: none,” especially the Chapter 3 ferry entry that also records offers from Brom’s Path and Cael’s fragments.
- **Observed issue:** The line reads like a group-wide operational total, but Karis has used Ember after the Line. The intended meaning appears to be that Cael has not deployed a fragment.
- **Effect on the reader:** A cold reader may stop to reconcile an apparent contradiction at exactly the moments when the repeated line should create mounting pressure and pride.
- **Proposed scope:** Make one early, minimal scope clarification—such as establishing that the heading belongs to Cael’s personal fragment log—and let later repetitions remain terse. No power event or scene needs changing.
- **Strength to preserve:** Preserve the incantatory repetition and the final emotional weight of forty-eight consecutive “none” entries.

### 2. Give the cold reader one firmer handhold before the opening system cascade

- **Location:** Chapter 1, from “It was an old habit, and he had it from a hill” through the first explanation of the Arbiter, Paths, tiers, and nine fragments.
- **Observed issue:** The prose introduces several institutions, people, powers, and pieces of prior history in quick succession while intentionally speaking around what happened on the hill.
- **Effect on the reader:** The atmosphere lands, but a reader without prior-book context may not know which facts are essential now, which are mysteries, and which are references meant to be recognized later. That weakens attachment during the first few pages without damaging the core hook.
- **Proposed scope:** Add or revise only a few orienting clauses around Cael’s immediate condition: he cannot use a normal Path, the nine fragments are dangerous borrowed abilities, and this journey requires him to keep them down. Do not add a glossary, a history paragraph, or an explanation of the hill.
- **Strength to preserve:** Preserve the eerie zero-count, the confidence that prior events were real, and the pleasure of learning the world through practical evidence instead of exposition.

### 3. Lightly streamline the nine-fragment rent inventory without reducing its tactical value

- **Location:** Chapter 4, the full entry beginning “Eighteenth day. The nine, as things held.”
- **Observed issue:** The inventory is important setup, but it arrives as a long static catalog immediately after the active restraint drills. Several entries combine source history, activation behavior, range, cost, and prior test results in one pass.
- **Effect on the reader:** The forward pull briefly flattens, and a younger or fast genre reader may skim precisely the constraints that make the later stillhound scene satisfying.
- **Proposed scope:** Keep all nine fragments and every limit needed for the stretch, but tighten repeated framing and consider deferring one or two nonessential source-history details until they become active. This should be a local compression, not a cut to the training sequence or the developed action.
- **Strength to preserve:** Preserve “rent” as Cael’s governing metaphor, the measurable costs, and the conversion of his old suppression training into knowledge he can teach the group.
